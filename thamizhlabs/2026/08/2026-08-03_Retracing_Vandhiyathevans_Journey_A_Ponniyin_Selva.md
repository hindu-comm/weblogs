### The Aadi 18 kickoff



Once upon a time, in the year **968 CE**, our story begins, on a fine Aadi 18 evening. Our hero, Mr. Vandhiyathevan was prodding along his horse near Veeranam lake, mighty pleased at seeing the fertile Chola lands, its enormous water bodies and more importantly, its beautiful women!

[](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!KQCu!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5849ab63-b795-4ada-9adf-f9b287484fdd_445x444.png)

![Image](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!KQCu!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5849ab63-b795-4ada-9adf-f9b287484fdd_445x444.png "Image")





The Chola citizens were celebrating the auspicious festival of Aadi Perukku. Women were singing folk songs which praised the Emperor. Kids were playing with *Sapparam*, a toy-on-wheels. Young girls and boys were giggling and running around flirting with each other. A gentle breeze carried the melodious songs of the farmers, resonating the atmosphere with joy. Vandhiyathevan stopped being tired of his 180km journey from Kanchi, and started enjoying his grand entry into the Chola kingdom. Vandhiyathevan was not sightseeing though, he was on a secret mission to deliver two scrolls to Thanjavur and Pazhaiyaarai. Thus kicks off the bestselling Tamil novel of all-time, our very own ***Tale of Two Cities- Thanjavur and Madurai*** - and the story of rivalry between the two great Tamil kingdoms - the Cholas and the Pandyas.

### The hero’s journey



Anyone who’s privy to the sight of Aadiperukku bringing fresh floods into the Veeranam lake cannot help but wonder at the foresight of the Chola kings. Prince Rajaditya, who was preparing for a battle in the northern banks, had some leisure time before the battle actually began. He used this window to construct this enormous lake, which shall store the Vadavaaru river water that would otherwise have emptied itself wastefully into the Bay of Bengal. This stored water can then be used for irrigation and drinking. Prince Rajaditya named the lake after his father and emperor Parantaka-1 whose alias name was Veeranarayanan. Sri Ramanuja was apparently struck enough by the sheer scale of this lake that he modeled the number of mutts he established, on the lake’s 74 sluices.

The hero continues southward, meets Aazhvarkadiyaan (a spy) who travel together southward to Veeranarayana Vinnagaram (Vishnu-Griha), a temple built by the same prince as per the traditional belief of Vishnu being the guardian deity of the lake bunds.

Here Vandhiyathevan and Aazhvarkadiyaan encounter a young Nathamuni, singing the ten pasurams known to his era: the very Nathamuni who would go on to rediscover the lost Nalayira Divya Prabandham. This is Kalki at his sharpest - dropping real historical figures into his fictional hero’s path without breaking stride.

By nightfall, the hero reaches the Sambuvarayar palace at Kadambur, intending only to rest but instead stumbles onto a secret conspiracy midnight meeting of small-kings and chieftains. This is where the primary plot point of the novel is revealed - who should be the next king - crown prince Aaditha Karikalan or his paternal uncle Madhurantaka? A comet/ meteor had showed up on the Chola skies for some days now. Considered a bad omen for the royal family, this spurred the urgency of succession planning in the empire.

Where is the Kadambur palace today? Unknown. Palace constructions with red bricks all crumble over time, which is why the foresighted Cholas revamped their temples with black stones - Katruli - that lasted longer. Bloggers suggest that this palace could have been near Melakadambur Amrithakadeswarar temple that’s 7km from Veeranarayana Temple. Kalki says that our hero on a horse reached from there to here in one naazhigai (24 minutes). Even assuming a tired horse to have cantered at 17kmph speed, not galloped at faster speeds, the math is math-ing.

### Crossing the Kollidam: A Detour With Aazhvarkadiyaan



Aazhvarkadiyaan the spy was also eavesdropping on themidnight meeting, who then trusts his instinct to follow a suspicious looking Pandya spy who leads him to his handler Ravidasan at a dilapidated place. The spot where the Pandya conspirators secretly convene, could have just been anywhere, but Kalki specifically mentions this as Thirupurambiyam, the place which hosted the battle that changed the course of South Indian history, resulting in Chola supremacy. This place still exists - Thirupurambiyam Pallipadai.

[](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Irw4!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F83592153-8233-45e1-9c5b-e2303aba2364_532x392.png)

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Irw4!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F83592153-8233-45e1-9c5b-e2303aba2364_532x392.png)





A Pallipadai, unlike a simple hero-stone Nadukarkoil, is a full temple built around an installed deity to honour a fallen warrior king Prithvipathi, who died in the Thirupurambiyam battle, fighting for the Pallava-Chola combine against Pandyas. The Pandya conspirators hence code-name this as “enemy’s place”. The plot thickens here - because Ravidasan and co were planning something far more sinister to take down the entire Chola clan.

So the plot had two parallel conspiracies:

1.  Internal conspirators who were planning to install a different Chola
    king than crown prince

2.  External conspirators who were planning to take down entire Chola
    clan in revenge for their fallen king Veerapandyan

### The Old Capital and The New



Cholas were moving their administrative capital from Pazhaiyaarai to the new fortified Thanjavur. Our hero Vandhiyathevan, after learning about the Kadambur conspiracy, leaves at once on his mission. He crosses the Kollidam river on a boat large enough to carry his horse, and continues south toward Kudanthai (Kumbakonam), to consult with a famous astrologer.

The astrologer checks our hero’s horoscope and says: “You’re lucky Vallam prince and you’ll always be on the move, as if there were wheels on your feet. You shall walk ; You shall ride horses; You’ll travel on the backs of elephants; You’ll sail in boats; You’re destined to cross the sea soon. Your mission shall succeed; About the Chola kingdom, for another hundred years, it will be on the rise, like the waxing moon. Despite temporary setbacks, *the golden period of the Chola kingdom is about to arrive soon*”

Hearing this happy news, the concerns over the comet and the dangers of his mission are temporarily forgotten by our hero. *Isn’t astrology part art, part science and part therapy?*

What’s lovely about Kalki’s writing is how he uses the astrologer as a foreshadowing device. This is literally a teaser for his part-2. Based on Kalki’s descriptions, the Kumbakonam astrologer’s house is in a desolated spot near a Kaali temple, close to the river Kaveri. I made an educated guess that this could be near the Paadal Petra Sthalam Kottaiyoor Koteeswaran Temple, referred to as Chozheeswaram in multiple inscriptions.

From Kudanthai the hero moves through Thiruvaiyaru and on to the new capital, Thanjavur, to deliver his first scroll to the emperor. It’s on this stretch that he first encounters Nandini, sweeping him clean off his horse the moment she reveals herself from her palanquin. The exact site of the Tanjore palace where his subsequent adventures unfold is, notably, lost to us.

From Thanjavur he presses on to Pazhaiyaarai, the earlier Chola capital, to deliver the second scroll to Kundhavai. The novel places two temples here by name Nathan Koil (a Divyadesam) and Vadathalli Koil (a Paadal Petra Sthalam).

Pazhaiyaarai once held three separate palaces: one each for king, queen, and princess - none of which survive; what remains of them lies in a village now called Chozhanmaaligai. It’s the same pattern as everywhere else on this route: the Chola palaces are gone, the Chola temples remain. And Pazhaiyaarai itself, for all its weight in this history, has largely slipped out of local memory.

It’s here, in a boat near the Pazhaiyaarai lake, that Vandhiyathevan finally meets Kundhavai and hands over the second scroll to complete his mission. But wait, Kundhavai sends him on another more dangerous mission - to bring back Ponniyin Selvan stationed somewhere in Lanka to deal with the dangers brewing in Chola kingdom. And that’s where Part 1 closes. Imagine writing an entire volume where the titular character Ponniyin Selvan is introduced only at the fag end! Kalki knew what teasers were, in 20th century itself. Its not for nothing that his books sell like hot cakes even after half a century of its publication.

### Conclusion



Nothing in the magnum opus Ponniyin Selvan happens by accident. It is all a carefully planned ploy by a master writer Kalki whose sole intent was to instill the greatness of Cholas into the heads of the common man via his historical fiction. Kalki, had carefully chosen his locations – spread across Tamilnadu and Sri Lanka, with almost every spot having a historic association with the Cholas. While the novel was published over a span of 5 years, the entire events of the novel take place within a 6 month period, from Aadi 18 to Thai 7 (early August to mid January). There are about 100 spots, peppered with UNESCO world heritage sites and temples and hiking trails.
