### The Problem You’ve Lived With for Years



It’s a lazy Friday evening. Dinner is done, the family is settled, and you scroll through your OTT platform looking for something to watch. You check Twitter or IMDB for a movie recommendation, find something well-rated, and hit play on an OTT.

Thirty minutes in, you realise what you are actually watching.

The villain chants Sanskrit shlokas before doing something terrible. Marxist violence is intellectualized and justified. Your family values, your community’ slang, your faith’s Gods, your nation’s sovereignty are all casually critiqued, slandered and mocked.

***You did not sign up for this. You just wanted to watch a film. You got a lecture instead.***

This has happened to you and a million others who consume cinema without any awareness of what ingredients the film has. It is not your fault because you had no idea what you were getting into, before you pressed play. You’ve Been Consuming Films Without a Nutrition Label. Until now.

### It’s just a film, What’s the Big Deal anyway?



“Watch films as just films” is a normal line the common man uses, but it is not true. Cinema is perhaps the most powerful medium capable of influencing the masses, placing ideas inside people’s heads through the powerful route of emotions, rather than the difficult route of logic. Nothing explains the power of cinema more than Tamilnadu, where right from CN Annadurai, M Karunanidhi, MGR, J Jayalalitha, to the current CM, Joseph Vijay, everybody has a cinema connection - either as writer or as actor. The lines between a fictional world on celluloid and reality have always been blurred.

If films were just films, the advertising industry would not spend billions every year placing their products inside them. If films were just films, the left would not have spent seventy years systematically building cinema as deliberate cultural projects. The left ecosystem knows that films are the single most powerful mechanism for shaping what a society considers normal, natural, and true.

Cinema works on a level of consciousness that argument cannot reach. While Substack articles and newspapers and Twitter posts appeal to your brain with logical arguments, a film asks you to feel emotions which bypasses the rational filters. Neuroscience calls this narrative transportation - we have now discovered that stories are fundamental to being human because stories literally shape how we see ourselves and the world around us. This reality is what makes cinema the most effective medium.

Millions of us got influenced into thinking “communism is a humanistic ideology” after watching **[Anbe Sivam](https://thamizhlabs.substack.com/p/decoding-dravidawood-chap-2-anbe)**, a deliberately designed film where the filmmaker manipulates you into thinking what he wants you think with a series of coincidences. Millions of Indians cried for Kabir Khan in *Chak De India* - and through that crying, absorbed the narrative of a minority unfairly suspected of national betrayal, rehabilitated through service. The real story of Mir Ranjan Negi was NOT told because the filmmakers decide what you want to feel, and then work backwards into the character design. **The feeling was subtly manufactured with industry grade ideological machinery**.

“It’s just a film, watch films as films” is EXACTLY what the ideological filmmaker is counting on you to say. Because if you are an empty bottle with such naïve unquestioning beliefs, they can then fill you up with their ideology wines before you leave the theatre. If you don’t carry your maps, you will get lost, and then whatever the filmmaker says would guide your journey. A map is most useful before the journey, not after you are already lost.

Culture Check site

CultureCheck is the Dharmic film database Indian cinema has needed for decades and never had. Not a boycott list. Not a political blacklist. Not a moral authority telling you what to watch and what to avoid. Something more useful than any of those things: a map.

Think of it as the nutrition label of Indian cinema. The label does not tell you whether to eat the food. It tells you what is in it. You have always had the right to watch whatever you want. You have not always had the right to know the ingredients. CultureCheck gives you that right. Ofcourse, we have a particular taste in our ingredients, so we certify only films that meet our criteria.

### The Ten Criteria



CultureCheck does not evaluate films on a vague left-right spectrum or a generic secular-neutral framework. It evaluates them across ten specific dimensions drawn from Bharatiya civilisational values. Each dimension has a clear definition, and each film is scored against all ten.

1.  **Dharma**: duty, moral responsibility, sacred obligation, and
    civilisational ethics. Does the film affirm or undermine the idea
    that individuals have responsibilities that extend beyond personal
    desire?

2.  **Civilisational continuity**: continuity across language, memory,
    family, faith, custom, and place. Does the film treat India’s
    civilisational inheritance as something worth preserving, or as a
    pathology to be escaped?

3.  **Rashtra**: national cohesion, sovereignty, territorial integrity,
    and collective confidence. Does the film affirm India as a nation
    with legitimate interests, or does it subordinate the Indian
    national frame to other allegiances?

4.  **Itihasa**: historical memory, invasions, colonialism, resistance,
    and inherited narratives. Does the film treat India’s historical
    experience honestly (including the suffering caused by invasion and
    colonialism) or does it suppress, distort, or morally equate what
    should not be equated?

5.  **Parampara**: inherited practices and inter-generational cultural
    transmission. Does the film treat tradition as a living inheritance
    or as a cage from which liberation is the only dignified response?

6.  **Local roots**: regional cultures, languages, communities, and
    sacred geographies. Does the film honour the specificity and dignity
    of rooted Tamil, Telugu, Marathi, or other regional identities, or
    does it flatten them into an ideological template?

7.  **Raksha**: protection of people, borders, social order, and
    civilisation from coercion or attack. Does the film affirm the
    legitimacy of protection - of family, community, nation? Or does it
    blindly categorize all protective instinct as aggression?

8.  **Social Dharma**: Please read this criterion carefully - it is
    Social Dharma, not the leftist Social Justice. This is about dignity
    of individual, responsibility of society, and need for reform of
    undignified unnecessary practices that affects social harmony. This
    comes from a place of understanding the enormous complexity of
    organizing any society, and acknowledges problems without branding
    our entire society as a pathology. This is the question we ask: Does
    the film engage with India’s social challenges honestly and
    constructively, or does it use genuine injustice as a vehicle for
    civilisational self-hatred?

9.  **Sacred regard**: treatment of Hindu and Bharatiya deities,
    temples, rituals, symbols, and sacred inheritance. Does the film
    treat Hindu sacred life with the same dignity it would extend to
    other traditions, or are Hindu symbols systematically deployed as
    markers of evil, oppression, or comedy?

10. **Contempt risk**: Whether the film’s dramatic force depends on
    degrading Hindu identity, Bharat, or a specific community as a
    community. The key word is depends: this is not about whether any
    negative character is Hindu, but whether the film’s emotional
    architecture requires Hindu identity to be the source of everything
    wrong.

These ten dimensions replace the vague impressionism of most film criticism with something specific enough to be contested, defended, and revised. When CultureCheck says a film fails on Sacred Regard or scores well on Rashtra, it means something precise - not a feeling, but an evidence-based finding against a declared criterion.

### How a Title Gets Rated: Research First, Verdict Last



The workflow behind every CultureCheck rating is a 3-step repeatable process:

1.   **Understand** **the** **film**: establishing the title, plot,
    characters, source material, and public context before any verdict
    is considered.

2.  **Research**: using film material, creator statements, reporting,
    history, court records, or archival material where relevant.

3.  **An adversarial pass**: This means actively searching for the
    strongest evidence that would make the first reading incomplete or
    wrong. The adversarial pass is **OUR COMMITMENT TO INTELLECTUAL
    HONESTY**. The reviewer is required to steel-man the opposing
    interpretation before arriving at a verdict.

Throughout, fact and interpretation are kept rigorously separate. What can be documented. What that fact means under the declared lens. Why a creator made a particular “creative choice” asserted only with evidence, never assumed. These three things are never blurred together.

The result is one of three outcomes:

1.  Publish, when the evidence supports certification and all review
    requirements are met.

2.  Hold, when the evidence is not strong enough for a durable verdict.

3.  Escalate or revise, when material historical, identity,
    social-critique, or perspective risk requires explicit resolution
    before the review can proceed.

### Challenge our Verdict



We understand that at the end of the day, art is subjective, and even with the same lens, we can view completely different pictures and derive different interpretations of what we saw. So unlike “My way or way”, we are open to challenges to our verdicts, provided you can make a legitimate argument in your defense.

At the heart of the methodology is a commitment to keeping three things permanently separate: fact, interpretation, and intent.

1.  **Fact** is what is real and does not change, unlike feelings, which
    change moment by moment. The real sub-inspector’s name was Anthony
    Sami that Jai Bhim distorted to Gurumoorthy. The real founder of Air
    Deccan was Captain Gorur Ramaswamy Iyengar Gopinath, not EVR-ist
    Nedumaaran Raajangam. The real coach behind the story of Chakh De
    India was Mi Ranjan Negi, not Kabir Khan. The real life brave forest
    office of Sherni was RM Abharna, not Vidya Vincent. These are facts.
    They exist regardless of what anyone thinks about them.

2.  **Interpretation** is what a fact means under the declared Bharatiya
    lens. Kerala Story film also changes names - Shalini Unnikrishnan,
    Nimah Matthew too are fictional names that represent real victims.
    The victims names were changed to protect their identity and
    dignity. Nimah Matthew’s representative name remains true to her
    real Christian name. This has to be hence interpreted not as a
    distortion, but a dignified change with no malintent. But the
    deliberate renaming of Anthony Sami to a Gurumoorthy, of Iyengar
    Gopinath to EVR-ist Nedumaaran is an interpretation question - it
    requires the lens to say what the factual distortion does to the
    film’s moral architecture, how it fits a documented pattern.

3.  **Intent** is why a creator made a choice - and CultureCheck asserts
    this only with evidence. A director’s stated interviews. A
    producer’s documented affiliations. A pattern across multiple films.
    In Sirai (2025) - a film inspired by real events that happened to an
    innocent Hindu boy - whose identity was changed to Abdul Rauf in the
    film - the film’s writer himself gave explanations for why they
    changed the identity. If the intent is not stated, it is inferred
    from how the distortion affects the perception of the film.

This trinity - fact, interpretation, intent - is what separates CultureCheck from both partisan cheerleading and partisan attack. It is what makes the reviews contestable and revisable. And it is what allows every reviewed title to have a structured challenge path: open the film page, select “Challenge this certification,” explain the specific factual, contextual, evidence-quality, or editorial issue, and include a supporting source. If the challenge holds, the review will reflect it.
