LIST OF FEMALE DUETS/MULTI-SINGER SONGS  IN HINDI FILMS



**LIST OF FEMALE DUETS/MULTI-SINGER SONGS**

**IN HINDI FILMS**

**Shrikant G. Talageri**

****

This article is intended to provide fairly exhaustive lists of the *all-female* duets and multi-singer songs (two singers, three-singers and four-singers) in Hindi films, arranged alphabetically and with an index of the singers at the end. Of course, the stock of Hindi film music is so vast that it is possible there may be many songs missing, especially in the very old and very new categories. And I hope there is a minimum of errors in the list.

Hindi film music (the golden age of which is now long over) is a vast ocean, and the number of singers and music directors who contributed to it is very large, and the record is often lost. *This article lists only the songs sung exclusively by female singers, and that too only by two-or-more female singers together, so it is a limited list*. People in general only know or remember the most prominent singers, but the list is a long one; and *each singer (even those with only one or two songs in this list) has contributed countless solos and has featured in countless male+female group songs*.

*There are also many many other prolific singers who have sung only solos and featured in male+female group songs but have not featured in all-female songs, and therefore will not be found in this list. Listing all those songs would be a daunting task*. 

While all the songs on the lists are all-female songs (and an article listing *all-male* songs will hopefully be my next to follow) male voices may feature in the choruses in many of the songs. And sometimes *rarely* a prominent male voice does feature in a short chorus-like situation at the beginning or end of the song, but does not alter the all-female nature of the song. For example, the extremely short appearance of the voice of Mohd. Rafi in the following Asha-Bhosle-Suraiya duet (no. 40 in the 2-singers list, at 1.16 to 1.23 seconds):

[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w16kwQd2yhg](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w16kwQd2yhg)

Likewise, one song (no. 60 in the 2-singers list) has a male tatkar .

The article however will only *list* the songs and not give youtube URLs of the songs, except for some of the few songs in Appendix 1.

**Appendix 2: Musings as a Chitrapur Saraswat**

While preparing the above list, I (as a person who is studying and writing on the Konkani language and on the history of the Chitrapur Saraswat community) was pleasantly surprised to find a number of Chitrapur Saraswat female singers in these duets. Apart from the well known **Suman** **Kalyanpur** (nee **Hemmady**) and **Krishna** **Kalle**, the list has duets by **Shyama** **Hemmady** (**Chittar**), **Shaila** **Gulvadi** (**Hattangadi**), **Usha Balsawar** and **Sushilarani** **Patel** (nee **Tombat**). *For a tiny connumity of less than 25,000 people worldwide, I found this impressive*. While researching for this article, I found another singer (who has sung solos or featured in male+female duets in an old Hindi film, Pamposh 1953), named **Sita** **Kalyanpur**. Also **Jaywanti** **Hirebet**, the legendary singer of Kannada songs, sang in Uday Shankar's famous 1948 Hindi film Kalpana(and in the 1946 super-hit Telugu film Tyagayya)! All this made me once more conscious, with some excusable pride, of the musical nature of my community: music seems to run in our blood. I believe the singer of a famous song from the 1949 film "Ek Thi Ladki" ("Dilli Se Aya Bhai Tingu") is also supposed to be a Chitrapur Saraswat named **Vinita** **Amladi** (who became Binota Chakraborty after marriage)! Additionally, the well-known singers **Anuradha** **Paudwal** (nee **Alka** **Nadkarni**) and **Anupama** **Deshpande** (nee **Kaikini**) and **Sandhya** **Rao** (**Badakere**) are also Chitrapur Saraswats. Indeed, singing is a common passion among Chitrapur Saraswats, and singers proliferate everywhere in school, college and local community programs, and in bhajan groups, orchestras and radio/TV programs. It is no wonder that the eminent Marathi litterateur PuLa Deshpande (*not* a Chitrapur Saraswat!), in one of his dramas (Batatyachi Chal), satirized a Chitrapur Saraswat lady who is obsessed with her harmonium and her singing.

The list of eminent female Hindustani Classical singers (Vidushis) includes (besides the above Sushilarani Patel nee Tombat) **Kausalya** **Manjeshwar**, **Saguna** **Kalyanpur**, **Lalith** **Rao**, **Lalita** **Ubhaykar**, **Vrinda** **Mundkur**, and many, many more. There is also a famous jazz singer who became prominent in the west: **Asha** **Puthli**.

On a personal note, our family as much as our community is steeped in music. My mother **Shaila** **Gangadhar** **Talageri** (nee **Sita** **Pandurang** **Taggarse**) was also a passionate singer all her life long (what broke her heart most in her last years −she expired in 2012 after two years of kidney problems and dialysis− was that she could not sing like before), and, after her family first moved to Mumbai from Mangalore in the mid-fifties, she also used to sing in programs in A.I.R., and, if her marriage and then my birth in 1958 had not cut short her singing and onverted her into a complete housewife, who knows, she would have probably progressed in that field and would have figured in this list of singers! My father was also a passionate music-lover (but only listener) devoted to buying and listening to classical and other forms of music (although his true identity was a passion for physical exercise and body-building). 

Our community has a long tradition of music: Vedic chanting and Sanskrit shlokas/songs as well as classical singing as part of the daily routine at our Chitrapur Math in Shirali (Uttar Kannada district, Karnataka), established lineages of kirtankaras, regular traditions of community singing of fixed collections of bhajans and songs during certain festivals (Datta Jayanti, Krishna Janmashtami, temple festivals, etc.), and so on. The community also regularly organizes Classical Sangeet Sammelans.

In the early twentieth century, when the famous Maharashtrian duo of V.D.Paluskar and V.N.Bhatkhande were establishing the modern traditions of Hindustani Classical music, the earliest Chitrapur Saraswats who had migrated to Mumbai from their Karnataka villages were also busy organizing and propagating Classical music. **Honawar** **Krishna** **Bhat**, teaching Classical music in Mumbai, counted among his disciples SN Ratanjankar, whose other eminent guru was Bhatkhande; and **Karnad** **Shankar** **Rao** was a c close associate of Bhatkhande. Later, two great giants were **Amembal Dinkarrao** (who was the head of All India Rado in the post-Independence decades, and who composed the signature tune of A.I.R. as well as the well-known signature tunes of most of the popular programs on A.I.R.) and **Vishnudass** **Shirali** (who composed music for the classic 1948 Uday Shankar film "Kalpana", and sang in it, and who wrote many books on Indian Classical music). After that there have been many eminent singers and instrumentalists of Hindustani Classical music. To name only a *few* prominent among them: vocal (e.g. **Chidanand** **Nagarkar**, **S**.**C**.**R**.**Bhat**, **Dinkar** **Kaikini**, **Mohan** **Chickermane**, **Bharat** **Balvalli**, etc.) and instrumental (both solo as well as in accompanying some of the greatest singers in the world of Hindstani music) on the *tabla* (**Pandit** **Taranath**, **Omkar** **Gulvady**, **Sadanand** **Naimpally**, **Gourang** **Kodical**, **Yogesh** **Samsi**, etc.), the *flute* (**Devendra** **Murdeshwar**, **Nityanand** **Haldipur**, etc.), the *harmonum* (**Anant** **Bellare**, etc.), the *sitar* (**Hattangadi** **Harihar** **Rao**, **Bhaskar** **Chandavarkar**, etc.). The list is a long one.

And, apart from music − and very eminent music critics in the media like **Mohan** **Nadkarni** and **P**.**G**.**Burde** − the world of performing arts and the film industry have many prominent Chitrapur Saraswat names like film directors (some of them also actors) **Guru** **Dutt** (**Padukone**), **Shyam** **Benegal**, **Girish** **Karnad**, **Anant** **Nag** (**Nagarkatti**), **Shankar** **Nag** (**Nagarkatti**), **Bipin** **Nadkarni**, **Anuya** **Palekar** (nee **Murdeshwar**), **Kalpana** **Lajmi**, **Amit** **Masurkar**, **Sundar** **Rao** **Nadkarni** (one of the early directors of Tamil cinema, and director of the record-breaking 1944 hit Tamil film "Haridas"), **Venkatrao** **Talageri** (who won the National Award in 1971 for his role in the Kannada film "Vamsha Vriksha") etc., and actresses like **Kamaladevi** **Chattopadhyay** (nee **Dhareshwar**), **Usha** **Nadkarni**, **Leena** **Chandavarkar**, **Isha** **Koppikar**, **Amrita** **Rao**, **Deepika** **Padukone**, etc. And of course, my own uncle **Pandurang** **Sitaram** **Talageri** who was one of the pioneer directors of the early silent film industry (and named by PuLaDeshpande along with Dadasaheb Phalke!), though now only known as the writer of the iconic Konkani drama “Chitrapur Vaibhav”.

Have I been using this appendix to my article for beating my own drum (or that of my community)? Perhaps, but it was a spontaneous addition to my intended article on female duets in Hindi films, and I must be excused for it. I will end my article on this note.

  

**Appendix 3 added 26 September 2025: Lata-Lata Duets?**

Today I just thought of adding two songs in which, in a sense, Lata Mangeshkar sings a duet with herself! That is, you hear two voices of Lata Mangeshkar singing simultaneously.

The first use of this technique was by R.D.Burman in the **1967** film **Baharon** **ke** **Sapne**, where the main song by Lata Mangeshkar has Lata Mangeshkar’s voice singing an alaap simultaneously in parts:

Baharon Ke Sapne (1967)- Kya Janun Sajan Hoti Hai Kya Gham Ki Sham

[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ADYDEx1pvEg&list=RDADYDEx1pvEg&start_radio=1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ADYDEx1pvEg&list=RDADYDEx1pvEg&start_radio=1)



The next use of this technique was by music director Vasant Desai in the **1968** film **Ashirwad**. In the beginning, and in parts in between, the voice comes from a gramophone record being played, during which the heroine does not move her lips. In other parts, the heroine starts singing with the record, and you hear two voices of Lata Mangeshkar singing simultaneously:

Ashirwad (1968)- Jhir Jhir Barse Sawani Ankhiyan

*https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=90w9lHPTbLo&list=RD90w9lHPTbLo&start_radio=1*

There may be similar other songs (with other singers) with dual voices of the same singer singing simultaneously.
