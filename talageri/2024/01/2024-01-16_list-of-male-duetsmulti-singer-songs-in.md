**LIST OF MALE DUETS/MULTI-SINGER SONGS**

**IN HINDI FILMS**

**Shrikant G. Talageri**

****

This article is a sequel to my article on female duets and multi-singer songs in Hindi films. This article lists the all-male songs with two or more singers. As I pointed out in the earlier article, my article intends to be an exhaustive list, but *due to the vast sea of Hindi film songs, it may have many missing songs*. As in the earlier article, I will append a list (not intended to be exclusive) of Marathi male duets. A few points to be made about this list:

1. Male duets and group songs fall into two distinct period-wise classes: an earlier (pre-multi-starrer) period when a majority of them tend to be either "comedy" songs or qawwalis, and also patriotic (or marching) songs, devotional songs and classical type songs, and also a few dialogue type songs; and a later (post-multi-starrer) period, in the seventies and eighties when, with the advent of multi-starrers, there was sudden fall in the number of *female* duets and group songs, and a sudden rise in *male* duets and group songs, these mainly being "friendship" songs and confrontation songs. Note the very large number of singers and groups in the list (*I may have missed out on other singers, groups and songs which I will continue to add into an addendum whenever they come or are brought to my notice*), and note that each of these groups could also have *other* songs with *female* voices added to the group: there is that phenomenal song from Love and God (1986, though it must have been recorded much earlier) with **six** male singers (Balbir, Hemant Kumar, Khan Mastana, Manna Dey, Mohd.Rafi and Talat Mahmood) − *but also a female singer* (Suman Kalyanpur) which precludes it from inclusion in this list, but not from special mention here:

<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9Bl7NastQrc>

As in the earlier article, I must point out that each singer who figures in this list of duets and multi-singer-songs (as well as *so many others* who don't) has countless solos and mixed (male+female) duets and group songs: these are categories which would run into tens of thousands or maybe lakhs, and so are not listed or even listable (by me at least).

2. As I also pointed out in my earlier article, it often becomes a tricky matter of *subjective* perception as to whether a singer's voice is to be taken as part of the song when it appears in it only for a few seconds or so without actually being an integral part of the duet/multi-singer-song. Sometimes, the actor/actress in the film also speaks (rather than actually sings) a few words in the middle, or interjects his/her voice in the middle of the song: for example, many songs feature Mehmood's voice in the middle of the song. In these cases, should that singer or the possessor of that voice be included as one of the singers in the song? *I have used my discretion or subjective feeling, which others may not agree with in every case*:  

For example, in my earlier list, I did *not* count Mohd. Rafi as one of the singers in the Asha-Bhosle-Suraiya duet from the film Shama Parwana (perhaps because it was the only such duet, and I wanted to include it in that all-female list!), and in this list I *am* counting Mohd. Rafi as a singer in the almost-exclusively Saigal song "Roohi Mere Sapnon Ki Rani" in the film Shahjahan because I want to include it in this list. See the mendicant singing in Rafi's voice at the end of the song (3.05 to 3.15):

<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TySZbAkJdzM>

It is a matter of judgment as to whether the extra voice should be counted or not. Mohd. Rafi's voice features as an extra touch in many songs. In one of them, for example, his only contribution is the phrase "Ho Ji Ho" repeated at various points during the otherwise Lata-Shamshad duet "Door Koi Gaye Dhun Ye Sunaye". But it is impossible to ignore it and to *not* count it as a Lata-Shamshad-Rafi song:

<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0A9YWtOGQOQ>

In the same film (Baiju Bawra), Lata Mangeshkar's voice appears for a few seconds (4.07 to 4.12) at the end of the Rafi solo "Tu Ganga Ki Mauj", but she cannot be counted among the singers:

<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j8jsd2DESbE>

Interestingly, the song had also been recorded as a Rafi-Lata duet, but was not used in the film because the situation demanded a solo. I have the song on my computer, but it seems to have been deleted from youtube.

4. In this list, it will be noticed that the same song (from the film Aadmi) features twice: as a Rafi-Talat duet and as a Rafi-Mahendra duet. The reason is that the song was recorded twice. The first time it was recorded as a Rafi-Talat duet and the song exists in audio, but Manoj Kumar (for whom the voice of Talat Mahmood was intended), insisted that he only wanted Mahendra Kapoor as his voice. So it was recorded again, and the actual version in the film features the later-recorded Rafi-Mahendra duet. The world of Hindi film music has countless such stories.

Indeed, the world of film music, and music in general, has countless stories of rivalries between great singers (though the above replacement of Talat by Mahendra Kapoor is not exactly one of them). There have even been attempts to destroy the careers of singers by putting things like sindoor in their food to destroy their voices: even Lata Mangeshkar had become the victim of such an attempt − fortunately (for all music lovers) an unsuccessful attempt. The most tragic case is that of a boy singer who would probably have become one of the greatest male singers in India if his career and life had not been cut short in this manner: **Master** **Madan** (Gurmukhi), a child singer of Jalandhar, who started singing at the age of *three*, recorded eight songs, and was poisoned to death (by persons unknown) in 1942 at the age of *fifteen*! Here is one of his recorded songs (sung in 1935 at the age of *eight*!):
