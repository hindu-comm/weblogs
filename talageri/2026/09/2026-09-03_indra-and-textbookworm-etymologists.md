“Indra” and Textbookworm Etymologists



**“Indra” and Textbookworm Etymologists**

**Shrikant G. Talageri**



I have written many articles referring in strongly critical terms to what I call “**textbookworm etymologists**” who claim to be having divine prophetic insights into mystical Laws of Nature which dictate that certain words in **Indo**-**European** languages can only change form according to **unalterable etymological rules**, and that words which do not seem to have forms fitting into those **unalterable etymological rules** are not **Indo**-**European** words at all but words borrowed from **non**-**Indo**-**European** sources, *even when there is **zero** **evidence** for such conclusions*.

Having just uploaded an article “**Parjanya and Indra**” today morning, I will stake my all on this subject by concentrating on just one single word: **Indra**.



According to **Witzel**, “**Proto-Indo-Iranians**” migrating from the **Steppes**, *on their way to **India** and **Iran** through **Central** **Asia***, jointly borrowed a number of words in common from an unknown and unrecorded “**BMAC** **language**” spoken by the inhabitants of the “**Bactria-Margiana-Archaeological-Complex**” then flourishing in **Central** **Asia**. I will concentrate only on the one word which constitutes *the most reckless, insolent and brazen of these claims about “**Indo-Iranian**” words “**borrowed**” from this hyper-hypothetical “**BMAC** **language**”: the word or name **Indra***.

These “**textbookworm etymologists**” are more fanatical about their claims about “**etymological rules**” than the most fundamentalist religious fanatics could be about their religious laws and beliefs.

*Nothing so utterly exposes the *extremely* *low* intellectual and (un)scholarly level of their claims, and of their **etymological** **rules**, and their vacuous formulations* *– or should we say the totally fraudulent nature of their “scholarship”* *− as this single word “**Indra**”*.



This whole issue seems to have been started by **A.Lubotsky** in his paper/article “**The Indo-Iranian Substratum**” (2001) where he declares that “***\*indra- shows irregular vocalization***” (LUBOTSKY 2001:304), and goes on to elaborate: “**\*indra- m. name of a deity: Skt. indra- name of a god; LAv. iṇdra- name of a daēva. Mayrhofer (EWAia s.v.) offers several etymologies, none of which is convincing, however. From a semantic point of view, the most plausible etymology is Slavic \*jędrъ \`strong, fresh', but the primary meaning in Slavic is clearly \`pit, kernel'. Note the “wrong” vocalization, *if this were an IE formation (form \*(H)indro- we expect IIr. \*\***ḭadra- )***” (LUBOTSKY 2001:311).

After uttering these mystic words, **Lubotsky** firmly decides that **Indra** is a **non-IE** word borrowed by “**IIr**” from the “**BMAC language**”.

**Witzel** takes up the refrain in various articles after **2001**: Just two examples from 2005 and **2019** will suffice:

“…**recently discovered evidence reveals a substantial body of loan words from Central Asia that have been preserved independently from each other both in the oldest Indian and Iranian texts. They reflect the non-indo-European, pre-Indo-Iranian language(s) spoken in the areas bordering N. Iran and N. Afghanistan, i.e. the *Bactria-Margiana Archaeological Complex*. These loans include numerous words from agriculture, village and town life, flora and fauna, ritual and religion. They were taken over and then exported to Iran and N. India by the speakers of the various Old Iranian and Old Indo-Aryan languages**” (WITZEL 2005:87).

“**The most prominent words are those of certain rituals, deities and priests: \**anc’u* “Soma plant”, \**yātu* “black magic”, *atharwan* “priest” (however see EWA I 60), \**ṛši* “seer”, \**uc’ig* “sacrificing priest”, \**c’arwa* “name of Rudra”, *\**indra**, \**g(h)andharw*/*b(h)a* “demi-god or demon”**” (WITZEL 2005:121). .

“**there was a major impact on IA by the (unknown) BMAC language of the Bactria-Margiana Archaeological Complex (BMAC). It included even *major*** ***Ṛgvedic gods such as Indra*, Śarva (Rudra), Gandharva etc.**” (WITZEL 2019:5).



In my earlier books and articles, I have already pointed out in detail that these so-called “**BMAC words**”, ****if**** they had entered into **proto**-**Indo**-**Aryan** and **proto**-**Iranian** as the speakers of those two languages moved *from* **the north** *through* **the BMAC areas of Central Asia** *into* **Afghanistan** and **NW** **India**, should have been found in the **oldest** parts of the **Old** **Rigveda**. However, they are found *only* in the **New** **Rigveda**, and many of them even *only* in **post**-**Rigvedic** **texts**. *This alone demolishes the whole **AIT**-**through**-**BMAC** theory*.

But here, in this article, I will deal specifically only with this *one* word or name “**Indra**”.

The utter *stupidity*, or *insolently* *fraudulent* *nature*, or *both*, of the “**Indra is a non-IE BMAC loan-word**” propaganda campaign becomes clear from every angle:

  

1. Not a single trace of this so-called “**BMAC language**” has been discovered anywhere to this date. There is no evidence that a single one of the so-called “**BMAC** **words**” was ever a part of any **non**-**IE** **language** ever spoken in the **BMAC** **area**.

In **1999**, **Witzel** had accused **NS** **Rajaram** of having pulled a “**Piltdown** **horse**” **hoax** by inventing an **Indus** **horse** on a seal where no horse existed. **Lubotsky** and **Witzel** can very definitely be accused of having pulled a “**Piltdown language**” **hoax** by inventing a **BMAC language** with *specific* *linguistic* *features* and *vocabulary* *which simply never existed*.



2. The word **Indra** is found in ***three*** ***distinct** **Indo**-**European** **branches***: **Indo**-**Aryan**, **Iranian** and **Anatolian** (**Hittite**).

Even if one tries to assume, with these fraudulent “scholars”, that there was indeed a **non**-**IE** **BMAC** **language** in **Central** **Asia** in which this word or name “**Indra**” originally existed, no-one would be able to explain why this word was “**imported**” only into three *distinct* **Indo**-**European** **branch languages** *and into no other language anywhere else in the world*. Even if this imaginary **non**-**IE** **BMAC** **language** of **Central** **Asia** became extinct itself, *why did it not leave traces of this word in *any* other language* other than three **IE** languages **Vedic**, **Avestan** and **Hittite**? \[Or, if one goes by their claim that the **Mitanni Indo**-**Aryans** migrated directly from **Central** **Asia** to **West** **Asia,** then that makes the word migrate in different directions into **four IE languages\]**

According to the brilliant study by **Johanna Nichols** quoted in detail in my books:

“**The vast interior of Eurasia is a linguistic spread zone** \[….\] **where** \[….\] **a single language or language family spreads out over a broad territorial range**” (NICHOLS 1997:122).

“**The central Eurasian spread zone (Figure 8.4), as described in Volume II, was part of a standing pattern whereby languages were drawn into the spread zone, spread westward, and were eventually succeeded by the next spreading family. The dispersal for each entering family occurred after entry into the spread zone. The point of dispersal for each family is the locus of its proto-homeland, and this locus eventually is engulfed by the next entering language** \[….\] **the locus is one of the earliest points to be overtaken by the next spread**” (NICHOLS 1997:137).

As per this “**standing pattern**”, *many different families* of languages moved through the **BMAC** **area**. So this word should have been found in *many* *different* *families*. Yet **the only languages ever recorded*,* which contain this so-called **BMAC** **word** “**Indra**”, are **three** **different** **IE** **languages** **only** and **no** **other** **language** (not even the so-called original **non**-**IE** **BMAC** **language** itself).



3. This word or name **Indra** is found ****throughout**** the **Rigveda**: **Indra** is the most important god in the **Rigveda**, and has ***over*** ***250** **hymns*** addressed to him or glorifying him (out of a total of **1028** **hymns** in the Rigveda). **Indra** is present in ****every**** ****part** **of** **the** **text**, **old** **and** **new*****,* and is mentioned (**by this name alone, not counting his other numerous special epithets**) ***2415*** ***times** **in** **538** **hymns***.

**Indra’s** ****negative**** presence in the **Avesta** as a *demon* rather than a *god* clearly shows an inimical attitude towards a **specific** **Vedic** **god** rather than to a god borrowed by the “**Indo**-**Iranians**” in common from elsewhere.

The presence of **Indra** in **Hittite** mythology also cannot be fitted into an **AIT** **paradigm** with a **Homeland** in the **Steppes** or in **Anatolia**. In the **Steppe** **Homeland** **theory**, **Hittite** would never have come into contact with the **BMAC** **area** in its migration from the **Steppes** to **Anatolia**, and this proves that **Indra** also has no original connection to the **BMAC** **area**.

*On the other hand, the presence of **Indra** in *these three languages* (**Vedic**, **Avestan**, **Hittite**) fits perfectly into the **OIT paradigm**, with **Hittite** moving from **India** into **Central** **Asia** and remaining there for many centuries still in contact with the **Indian** **area** as **Vedic** **culture** later expanded into **Central** **Asia** from **India***. 



The main point I want to make in this article is that these academic “scholars” can pull truly grand hoaxes on absolutely *zero* *bases* and *zero* *evidence*, and the “**Indra is a non-IE BMAC loan-word**” propaganda campaign is the best illustration of the fraudulent nature of
mumbo-jumbo “etymological” rules (i.e. “***\*indra- shows irregular vocalization***”).
