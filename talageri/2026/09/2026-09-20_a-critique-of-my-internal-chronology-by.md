“**In this post, I will attempt a brutal, honest, factual critique of Shrikant Talageri, who is unarguably one of the most important authors in the field of Indology. Despite making important contributions, he makes fatal errors in his reasoning that lead to faulty conclusions.**

****



**In Chapter 3 of his book “Rig Veda A Historical Analysis”, we read the following statement:**

***The Rigveda consists of ten MaNDalas or Books. And, excepting likely interpolations, these MaNDalas represent different epochs of history.*******

****



**Unfortunately, instead of giving actual evidence in favor of this hypothesis, Talageri opts for a selective reading of both the Rigveda and the Anukramani in order to support his absurd thesis. At the very outset, this statement seems illogical. If each mandala is written by a particular rishi family, and each mandala also corresponds to an ‘epoch’ of history, then it would imply that each ‘epoch’ was dominated by one and only one rishi family. It would imply that in each epoch, one rishi family held a near monopoly on composing Rigvedic hymns. At the very outset, this doesn’t seem very likely.**

****



**Further, we read:**

***The chronological position of MaNDala IX is equally beyond doubt: it is definitely much earlier than MaNDala X, but equally definitely later than the other eight MaNDalas.***

***MaNDala IX was meant to be a kind of appendix in which hymns to Soma, ascribed to RSis belonging to all the ten families, were brought together.*******

****



**Notice how these two statements contradict each other. If the IXth mandala consists of hymns composed by all the ten families, and each family composed hymns during a specific ‘epoch’, then it implies that IXth mandala should contain hymns from all the epochs. So while it may be true that the IXth mandala was organized at a later date, some of its hymns could be much earlier. By no means can IXth mandala be considered a ‘late’ mandala.**

****



**Further, we read:**

***The family MaNDalas can be divided into Early Family MaNDalas (VI, III, VII) and Later Family MaNDalas (IV, II, V). The Later Family MaNDalas have full hymns composed by direct descendants of RSis from the Early Family MaNDalas.*******

****



**The reader is left to presume that the second statement is supposed to be ‘evidence’ in favor of the the first statement. Unfortunately, the evidence is very flimsy. The fourth mandala is attributed to Vamadeva Gautama, and he bears no known relationship to the authors of mandalas VI (Bharadvaja), III (Visvamitra) or VII (Vasistha). However, there is one hymn in mandala IV, hymn 43, which is ascribed to the rishis Purumīḷha Sauhotra and Ajamīḷha Sauhotra. Supposedly, they descend from Suhotra Bharadvaja, and this is supposed to be ‘evidence’ that the entire IVth mandala is later than the VIth! But even if we accept the premises, the only thing we can safely conclude is that this one particular hymn is later to (most hymns of) VIth mandala. Just because one hymn in the IVth mandala was composed by a descendant of rishi Bharadvaja doesn’t mean the entire mandala is later than VIth. This unfounded conclusion could only be based on his unfounded hypothesis that each mandala is supposed to be an ‘epoch’!**

****



**He futher presents a series of similar arguments in which he uses the Anukramani to “logically deduce” the relative chronology of mandalas. I do not need to demonstrate in detail that all of those arguments are based on equally unfounded and flimsy premises. Let me just point out how he has selectively employed the Anukramani data. In mandala I, there are several hymns (166-191) attributed to Agastya Maitrāvaruṇi. As we know from hymn VII.33, Agastya was a contemporary of Vasistha, and was regarded as his brother. In fact, it was Agastya (according to VII.33), who introduced Vasistha to Sudas. This would imply that major portions of mandala I actually belong to the earliest period. But Talageri asserts that even the earliest parts of mandala I are later than VII. This is a serious contradiction, because according to Talageri, mandalas represent ‘epochs’ and therefore Vasistha and Agastya must be separated by ‘epochs’ (which can’t be true).**

****



**I can give another example. Hymns VIII.101, IX.62, IX.65, X.110, X.167 are attributed to rishi Jamadagni. Jamadagni was a contemporary of Visvamitra, and we know this from hymn III.53, where Visvamitra attributes his poetic skill (symbolized by Sasarparī) to Palastis and Jamadagnis. However, Talageri places mandalas VIII, XI, and X to be later than mandala III.**

****



**One may raise a point in Talageri’s defense. It could be that the Agastya of mandala I was a later Agastya. Likewise, the Jamadagnis of mandalas III, VIII, IX and X could all be different personalities belonging to different epochs. But this would open a can of worms. How, then, would we know that the hymns of mandala VI that are attributed to Bharadvaja were composed by the same Bharadvaja? In fact, we don’t. In all likelihood, the mandala VI was composed by many generations of Bharadvajas, and not all of their personal names were preserved in the Anukramani. This would mean that each family mandala would have early, middle and late portions, that would be roughly concurrent with the early, middle and late portions of other family mandalas. In other words, rishi families were (in some sense) “competitors” in a market rather than monopolists of their respective epochs.**”
