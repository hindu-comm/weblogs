Traditionally, the Smṛtis, Dharmasūtras and other such texts categorise people according to varṇa. Varṇa is not exactly the same as caste. The reason is that a specific varṇa is related to a particular kind of rituals, which could be performed by the person who possesses that varṇa. But at times, when the pre-requisite rules are not followed, a person, although still belonging to the same varṇa, loses the competence (adhikāra) to perform those rituals. The competence is to be regained by performing proper expiations (prāyaścitta). It implies a ritual gradation of high and low even in a single varṇa. Caste on the other hand does not possess such varied gradation. Varṇa not being something which can be perceived directly by one’s senses, its definition and application is stated by the aforementioned scriptures.

1.  **Varṇa as per Smṛtis**

In the tenth chapter of Manusmṛti, there appears a definition of the pre-requisite for the transfer of varṇa from the parents to the offspring. The verse and its translation by Sir Ganganatha Jha is as follows:

सर्ववर्णेषु तुल्यासु पत्नीष्वक्षतयोनिषु ।

आनुलोम्येन सम्भूता जात्या ज्ञेयास्त एव ते ॥ मनुस्मृति १०.५

Among all castes, those only who are born of consorts wedded in the natural order, as virgins of equal status, are to be regarded as the same (as their father). — Manusmṛti 10.5

If all the stated conditions are fulfilled, the transfer of the varṇa takes place at one’s birth. Exceptions to this rule are some cases, like that of sage Viśvāmitra, who was a Kṣatriya, but became a Brāhmaṇa through long penances. Except for such cases, the norm of transfer is the way as has been pointed out in the verse. This also suggests that a person who is born as belonging to a certain varṇa, cannot change, or upgrade his varṇa, except with a miracle. This might sound controversial, but this is what the usually Smṛtis say on this matter.

This is the perspective of the sages and humans. Beyond the sages, there are Devas or Gods. What do they think of this? Even if they say something on any matter related to Smṛtis, is their opinion authoritative? Let us explore this direction.

**2. Authority of Devas in the matters of Smṛtis**

In the eighth chapter of the Manusmṛti, an opinion of Devas has been cited. The issue is about who a Vṛṣala is. Is he a Śūdra, as people believe? or someone else? The verse and its translation by Jha is as follows:

वृषो हि भगवान् धर्मस्तस्य यः कुरुते ह्यलम् ।

वृषलं तं विदुर्देवास्तस्माद् धर्मं न लोपयेत् ॥ मनुस्मृति ८.१६

For Justice is the revered ‘Vṛṣa,’ Bull; and he who commits the violation, ‘alam,’ of it, him the gods regard as ‘vṛṣala,’ low-born; hence one shall not violate Justice. — Manusmṛti 8.16

In this way, a violator of justice is a Vṛṣala, as per the Gods, irrespective of the varṇa he belongs to. Commentator Medhātithi says that the Gods are more authoritative. His statement with Jha’s translation is as follows :

मनुष्यास्तु यदि जातिशब्दमेव मन्यन्ते कामं मन्यन्तां प्रमाणतरास्तु देवाः। ते चानेन प्रवृत्तिनिमित्तेन वृषलशब्दप्रयोगं मन्यन्ते।

The opinion that such a person is ‘vṛṣala’ is held by the gods; if it is taken as denoting a caste, it may be so taken; but the gods are more authoritative, and they accept the denotation of the term as here explained.

He further says:

देवग्रहणमर्थवादः। तस्माच्छ्राद्धकाले वृषलैर्न प्राप्तव्यं, हन्तव्यो वृषलश्चौर इत्याद्यासु क्रियासु मिथ्यादर्शी ब्राह्मण एव वृषलशब्देन ग्रहीतव्यः।

The mention of the ‘gods’ is only a commendatory exaggeration. For the reason here explained, in all such texts as — (a) ‘no vṛṣala should come in during the performance of a śrāddha,’ or ‘the vṛṣala thief should be killed,’ — the term ‘vṛṣala’ should be taken as standing for the Brāhmaṇa that perverts truth. (translation by Jha)

In this manner, a term which otherwise applies to a particular person by birth, is extended to be applied to someone different. The term Vṛṣala is generally used to denote someone who is a Śūdra by birth, but through this commendatory verse, it is used even for a Brāhmaṇa who perverts truth. In this sense, the term Vṛṣala as it appears in Smṛtis and such other texts, may not be limited to someone who is born in a Śūdra lineage. This extension has been validated by bringing in the Devas.

It seems for the reason of higher authority that we find certain verses or phrases in various Hindu scriptures speaking of what Devas think about the matter.

**3. Devas’ role in the formation of varṇas**

The Puruṣa Sūkta of the Ṛgveda states that the Devas and sages performed a yajña in which the persons of four varṇas were born. Following are the verses which indicate this:

तं यज्ञं बर्हिषि प्रौक्षन् पुरुषं जातमग्रतः।

तेन देवा अयजन्त साध्या ऋषयश्च ये॥ ऋग्वेद १०.९०.७

यत्पुरुषं व्यदधुः कतिधा व्यकल्पयन्।

मुखं किमस्य कौ बाहू का ऊरू पादा उच्येते॥ ऋग्वेद १०.९०.११

ब्राह्मणोऽस्य मुखमासीद् बाहू राजन्यः कृतः।

ऊरू तदस्य यद्वैश्यः पद्भ्यां शूद्रो अजायत॥ ऋग्वेद १०.९०.१२

In the seventh mantra, it has been told that with the Puruṣa as the paśu for the yajña, the Devas, beginning with Prajāpati, as well as the sages performed the yajña. In the eleventh mantra, a question arises about the various limbs of the same Puruṣa, and the answer to which is provided in the twelfth mantra, which says that Brāhmaṇa was his mouth (Brāhmaṇa was produced from his mouth), Kṣatriya was his arms, Vaiśya was his thighs and Śūdra was born of his feet.

In chapter six of the third canto of Bhāgavata Purāṇa (verses 11–33), it is told that before the becoming of the varṇas from the body of Virāṭ Puruṣa, Devas like Agni and so forth, entered various parts of his body. Here also, the Devas seem to have played a role in the origin of varṇas. Agni entered the mouth of Virāṭ Puruṣa (verse 12), Indra entered the hands (verse 21), Viṣṇu entered the feet (verse 22), etc.

**4. The case of a Cāṇḍāla and Viṣṇu’s guidance**

In the Padma Purāṇa, there appears a story of a Cāṇḍāla named Mūka. He was sincerely devoted to his parents. Viṣṇu resides in his house in the form of a Brāhmaṇa. He is seen in this form by a Brāhmaṇa named Narottama, who is in search of Mūka. Narottama had gone to pilgrimage without taking permission of his parents. Consequently he incurred sin. He is suggested, through an Ākāśa-vāṇī, to learn Dharma from Mūka. Towards the end of the story, Narottama asks Viṣṇu if the staying of a Brāhmaṇa near a Cāṇḍāla like Mūka led to sin, as indicated by the Smṛtis. Viṣṇu tells him:

कल्याणानां च सर्वेषां कर्ता मूको जगत्त्रये।

वृत्तस्थो योपि चण्डालस्तं देवा ब्राह्मणं विदुः॥१८६

मूकस्य सदृशो नास्ति लोकेषु पुण्यकर्मतः।

पित्रोर्भक्तिपरो नित्यं जितं तेन जगत्त्रयम्॥१८७

तयोर्भक्त्या त्वहं तुष्टः सर्वदेवगणैः सह।

तिष्ठामि द्विजरूपेण तस्य गेहोदरे च खे॥ १८८ — पद्मपुराण, सृष्टिखण्ड, अध्याय ५०

Mūka is the performer of all auspicious actions in the three worlds.

Even a Caṇḍāla who practises good conduct is known to Devas as a Brāhmaṇa. — 186

With regards to virtuous actions, there is none equal to Mūka in the three worlds.

He is forever devoted to his parents, he has won the three worlds. 187

Being satisfied with his devotion towards them, along with all the Devas,

I stay in his house in the form of a Brāhmaṇa, and also in the sky. 188 — Padmapurāṇa, Sṛṣṭikhaṇḍa, Chapter 50

Mūka’s devotion to his parents pleased Viṣṇu so much that he began staying at his home. Regarding the issue of a Brāhmaṇa staying with a Cāṇḍāla, the Smṛtis state discouraging statements. The reason behind it is that they believe that through such a contact, the Brāhmaṇa would incur sin. But Viṣṇu clarifies the purview of those statements. He goes to the extent of saying that for the Gods, a Cāṇḍāla of good conduct is a Brāhmaṇa.

Here there are two points worth noting. First is that it is Viṣṇu who has been depicted as the one who states this about what Gods think of such a Cāṇḍāla. Second is that, Viṣṇu further brings in the authority of the Gods to make Narottama understand the issues in the right context. For the Gods, it is good behaviour which counts, so much so that they would call Mūka a Brāhmaṇa. This could be considered a mere arthavāda or commendatory exaggeration. Just like the case of Manusmṛti discussed above, this could mean an extended application of the term Brāhmaṇa to anyone who is of good conduct.

**5. Viśvāmitra’s becoming a Brāhmaṇa**

Viśvāmitra, son of Kuśika, was a Kṣatriya by birth. Seeing the might of Vasiṣṭha, a Brāhmaṇa, Viśvāmitra pledged to become a Brāhmaṇa in that very life. He began his penance, which went on for a very long time. Then Brahmā arrived and said :

जिता राजर्षिलोकास्ते तपसा कुशिकात्मज ।

अनेन तपसा त्वां तु राजर्षिरिति विद्महे ॥वाल्मीकिरामायण १.५७.५

O Son of Kuśika, you have won the worlds of Rājarṣis. Through this penance, we know you as Rājarṣi. — Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, 1.57.5.

After this, realising that he has yet not been recognised as a Brāhmaṇa, Viśvāmitra says:

तपश्च सुमहत्तप्तं राजर्षिरिति मां विदुः ।

देवाः सर्षिगणाः सर्वे नास्ति मन्ये तपःफलम् ॥वाल्मीकिरामायण १.५७.८

I have performed a great penance, but all the Gods, sages and gaṇas know me as a Rājarṣi. I do not think that I have received the desired fruit of this penance.

We can clearly identify the pattern here. The typical sentences like ‘Devas know him as xyz’ (taṃ devāḥ … viduḥ), are found here in this form. This scene from the Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa where Brahmā, a Deva himself, says, “we know you as Rājarṣi”, and Viśvāmitra himself understanding this as ‘’Gods, sages and gaṇas know me as Rājarṣi” (implied that he had become a Rājarṣi), is a depiction of how the Gods would have communicated their opinion of such a nature.

Dissatisfied with the result of his tough penance, Viśvāmitra keeps performing penances. At the end of his penances, when due to various reasons, the Gods agree to declare him as a Brāhmaṇa, it too is a depiction of ‘Gods know him as a Brāhmaṇa’, because as shown above they declare that they know him as a Rājarṣi. One cannot declare something which he or she does not know.

It is further worth noting that even when Brahmā said that Viśvāmitra had attained Brāhmaṇya, the latter specifically prays that Oṅkāra, Vaṣaṭkāra and Vedas should accept him, i.e., he should get the competence of performing Yajña for others and of teaching Vedas (1.65.20). He then desires that Vasiṣṭha declares it (1.65.21), which he does. Although the Devas recognised his Brāhmaṇya, Viśvāmitra separately asked for the specific competence, usually assigned to someone who is at least a Brāhmaṇa by birth, to be given to him, and he gets it further confirmed by Vasiṣṭha.

**6. Śrī Bhagavān’s statement in Vṛddha Gautama Smṛti**

In the twenty first chapter of the Vṛddha Gautama Smṛti, Śrī Bhagavān is depicted as saying:

न जातिः पूज्यते राजन् गुणाः कल्याणकारकाः।

चण्डालमपि वृत्तस्थं तन्देवा ब्राह्मणं विदुः॥ वृद्धगौतमस्मृति २१.८

O King! Birth is not respected, qualities are the cause of welfare. Even a Caṇḍāla who is of good conduct, is known to Devas as a Brāhmaṇa. — Vṛddha Gautama Smṛti 21.8

The reason for such a statement is presented a little earlier in the chapter, which is that Devas feel contentment due to good behaviour, whereas Vedas and Agnihotra ritual are not that important. Śrī Bhagavān’s statement is as follows:

अग्निहोत्रं वृथा राजन् वृथा वेदास्तथैव च।

शीलो (शीलात्/शीले) देवास्तु तुष्यन्ति श्रूयतां तत्र कारणम्॥ वृद्धगौतमस्मृति, २१.५

Purposeless is Agnihotra, purposeless are Vedas in the same manner, Devas find contentment in good behaviour, hear the reason behind it. — Vṛddha Gautama Smṛti 21.5

In the verse which precedes this, it has been said that if the bhāva (mind/intention) is not pure, all rituals are purposeless or useless. Hence, says Śrī Bhagavān, that Devas find contentment in śīla or good behaviour. He further says:

क्षान्ती (क्षान्तं?) दान्ती (दान्तं?) जितक्रोधी (जितक्रोधं?) जितात्मानं जितेन्द्रियम्।

तमेव ब्राह्मणं मन्ये शेषाः शूद्रा इति स्मृताः॥

अग्निहोत्रव्रतपरान् स्वाध्यायनिरतान् शुचीन्।

उपवासरतान् दान्तांस्तान् देवा ब्राह्मणा(न्) विदुः॥ वृद्धगौतमस्मृति २१.६-७

Possessor of forbearance, possessor of patience, subduer of anger, subduer of mind and senses, such a person alone do I know as Brāhmaṇa, rest are remembered as Śūdras.

Those who are devoted to Agnihotra and vratas, those engaged in Svādhyaya, are pure, engaged in Upavāsas, and are patient, such are known to Devas as Brāhmaṇas. — Vṛddha Gautama Smṛti 21.6–7

It is at this juncture that the statement of a Caṇḍāla of good conduct being known to Devas as Brāhmaṇa appears. This too is a case of extended application of the term Brāhmaṇa to anyone who is of good conduct.

**7. Abhinavagupta and Jayaratha**

In his Tantrāloka, Abhinavagupta says that the one who has a calm mind is a Dvija or Brāhmaṇa, and further states that this has been analysed by Vedavyāsa in the portions like Mokṣadharma of the Mahābhārata. He says:

योनिर्न कारणं तत्र शान्तात्मा द्विज इच्यते॥५१३

मुनिना मोक्षधर्मादावेतच्च प्रविवेचितम्। — तन्त्रालोक, अध्याय १५

The cause (of Dvijatva or Brāhmaṇatva) is not birth (in a specific lineage), the one with a calm mind is called ‘Dvija’. 513



This has been analysed by Muni (Vedavyāsa) in Mokṣadharma (of Mahābhārata) and other such (portions). — Tantrāloka, chapter 15

In his commentary on this portion, Jayaratha quotes verses from the Mahābhārata where someone possessing certain qualities is known to the Devas as a Brāhmaṇa. He cites the following verses:

येन सर्वमिदं बुद्धं प्रकृतिर्विकृतिश्च या।

गतिज्ञः सर्वभूतानां तं देवा ब्राह्मणं विदुः॥

अभयं सर्वभूतेभ्यः सर्वेषामभयं यतः।

सर्वभूतात्मभूतो यस्तं देवा ब्राह्मणं विदुः॥

The one who knows all this, the ultimate cause and transformation,

the knower of condition/fate of all beings, such a person do Devas know as a Brāhmaṇa.

The one who has no fear from all beings, and all have no fear from whom,

the one who is the Self of all beings, such a person do Devas know as a Brāhmaṇa.

A little ahead, Abhinavagupta interprets the usual definitions of transfer of varṇas as stated by the Smṛtis, as the norm, i.e., in most cases, varṇa is transferred through the process mentioned by the Smṛtis. He says:

संविदो देहसंभेदात् सदृशात् सदृशोदयात्।

भूमाभिप्रायतः स्मार्ते द्विजन्मा द्विजयोः सुतः॥५१५

In this verse, as interpreted by Jayaratha, Abhinavagupta says that it is consciousness or the Self which has become a body, and from a certain kind of a body, a similar kind of an individual is born. It is in this sense of ‘most cases’, that the Smṛtis define a Brāhmaṇa as the offspring of a Brāhmaṇa and a Brāhmaṇī.

Abhinavagupta as interpreted by Jayaratha not only believes in the Brāhmaṇatva which is attained through lineage, but also in the one attained through one’s own qualities, like calm mind. To certify his opinion, he cites concerned statements of Vedavyāsa from the Mokṣadharma of Mahābhārata. For him, the opinion of the Devas are as authoritative as those of the sages who revealed Smṛtis.

**8. Interpreting the Devas’ perspective**

Devas or Gods hold an important place in the Hindus’ lives. All our rituals involve invoking some or other God. The Yajñas and Homas cannot be performed without a Deva. Because they hold an important position in Hinduism or Sanātana Dharma, their opinion on certain matters becomes very important. It is another thing whether we can act in accordance with them or not. The system of varṇa, from the perspective of the sages and humans is based on lineage and birth. It is also heavily dependent on various purificatory rituals and ceremonies or saṃskāras, expiations, and so forth, which are to be performed within a specific time limit in order to maintain the high gradation of varṇa and the resultant competence to perform rituals. But from the perspective of Gods, it seems that the more important component is conduct. It could be said that such a criterion is valid only in the abode of the Gods and not in the abode of humans. Nevertheless, it is important to hear from the Gods, through the scriptures, whom do they see as a Brāhmaṇa, Kṣatriya, Śūdra, so on and so forth. Medhātithi’s assertion that Devas are more authoritative than humans, is central to the importance of the opinion of Devas. He says that certain injunctions and prohibitions in the Smṛtis, which are to be applied to a ‘Vṛṣala’, are also applicable to a Brāhmaṇa who perverts truth. Just as the term Vṛṣala is extended to such a Brāhmaṇa, the term Brāhmaṇa is extended to even a non-Brāhmaṇa who is of good conduct and possesses other stated qualities. It seems that for Devas, active causes are more important than inactive causes, at least when it comes to varṇa. Birth is somehow not an active cause from the point of view of a person himself. It has to be followed by adherences to rules, regulations, timely performance of ceremonies, etc. In the absence of such rituals, even birth cannot come to one’s rescue.

Behaviour-based and nature-based classification or definition of varṇas is also found in the seventh canto of Bhāgavata Purāṇa. For instance a Brāhmaṇa is thus defined:

शमो दमस्तपः शौचं संतोषः क्षान्तिरार्जवम्।

ज्ञानं दयाच्युतात्मत्वं सत्यं च ब्रह्मलक्षणम्॥ भागवतपुराण, ७.११.२१

Calmness, absence of anger, cleanliness, satisfaction, ability to bear the uncomfortable, knowledge, mercy, being firmly devoted to God and truth, these are the marks of a Brāhmaṇa. — Bhāgavata Purāṇa, 7.11.21

At the end of this chapter, it has been clarified that in whichever person these marks are found, one should identify that person as belonging to that varṇa. It reads:

यस्य यल्लक्षणं प्रोक्तं पुंसो वर्णाभिव्यञ्जकम्।

यदन्यत्रापि दृश्येत तत् तेनैव विनिर्दिशेत्॥ भागवतपुराण, ७.११.३५

In this manner, we can see that the terms Brāhmaṇa, Kṣatriya, Vaiśya and Śūdra can also be employed based on a criterion other than birth. On this verse, the commentators Śrīdharācārya and Vīrarāghavācārya say that wherever the marks of a certain varṇa are found, one should identify the person with that varṇa, and not on the basis of birth. They say:

शमादिभिरेव ब्राह्मणादिव्यवहारो मुख्यो न जातिमात्रादित्याह।…यद्यदि अन्यत्र वर्णान्तरेऽपि दृश्येत तद्वर्णान्तरं तेनैव लक्षणनिमित्तेन वर्णेन विनिर्दिशेत्। न तज्जातिनिमित्तेनेत्यर्थः। — श्रीधराचार्य

The primary usage of the terms Brāhmaṇa and so forth is through calmness, etc., and not merely on the basis of birth… If that (mark) is seen elsewhere in (the person of) another varṇa, one should identify (person of) that varṇa with the varṇa on the basis of marks. Not (through the one which is determined) on the basis of birth, this is the meaning. — Śrīdharācārya

शमादिभिरेव ब्राह्मणादिव्यवहारो मुख्यो न जातिमात्रादित्याह …। यस्य पुंसो यल्लक्षणं वर्णाभिव्यञ्जकं प्रोक्तं “शमो दमस्तपः” इत्यादिना तद्यत् यद्यन्यत्रापि वर्णान्तरेऽपि दृश्येत तर्हि तद्वर्णान्तरं तेनैव लक्षणनिमित्तेनैव वर्णेन निर्दिशेत् न जातिनिमित्तेनेत्यर्थः। — वीरराघवाचार्यः

The primary usage of the terms Brāhmaṇa and so forth is through calmness, etc., and not merely on the basis of birth… If the stated mark which makes known a certain varṇa, by (the statement of) “calmness, non-anger, penance” etc., if that is seen in another varṇa, then one should identify that varṇa on the basis of the marks, and not (through the one which is determined) on the basis of birth. — Vīrarāghavācārya

Other commentators like Vaṃśīdhara, Gaṅgāsahāya, etc. say that what has been said is that if marks of a certain varṇa are found in the person of another varṇa, then that person should be given such respect. They say:

ब्राह्मणादितुल्यमादरमेव लक्षयति न तु तत्त्वेनार्चनादिकमिति। — वंशीधर

What has been indicated is the respect similar to that of a Brāhmaṇa etc., not actual worship, etc. — Vaṃśīdhara

ब्राह्मणवदादराभिप्रायेण न तु सन्ध्यावन्दनवेदपाठादिप्राप्त्यर्थं दानादियोग्यतार्थं वा व्यवहारविरोधात्। — गङ्गासहाय

Due to the intention of respect like a Brāhmaṇa, and not for attaining Sandhyāvandana, chanting of Veda, etc., or for the competence for receiving dāna etc., because of opposition with convention. — Gaṅgāsahāya

In both the cases, be it identification with a certain varṇa or be it giving respect like that varṇa, there is some truth behind the statements, which has been accepted. This could also be seen in the commentaries of Medhātithi and Nandana on Manusmṛti 10.122. The verse and its translation by Ganganatha Jha are as follows:

स्वर्गार्थमुभयार्थं वा विप्रानाराधयेत् तु सः ।

जातब्राह्मणशब्दस्य सा ह्यस्य कृतकृत्यता ॥ मनुस्मृति १०.१२२

He shall serve Brāhmaṇas either for the sake of heaven, or for the sake of both; when, he has attained the title of the ‘Brāhmaṇa,’ this implies the accomplishment of all his purposes. — Manusmṛti 10.122

Medhātithi, while interpreting ‘jātabrāhmaṇaśabda’, gives two meanings. He says:

तदाह जातब्राह्मणशब्दस्य ‘ब्राह्मणोऽयम्’ इति यदास्य शब्दो भवति तदासौ कृतकृत्यः कृतार्थो वेदितव्यः। अथवा जातब्राह्मणव्यपदेशस्येति व्याख्येयम्। ब्राह्मणाश्रितोऽयमिति यदास्य व्यपदेशो जायते। — मेधातिथि

Thus says, of the one who has attained the title of Brāhmaṇa, i.e., when the title becomes applied to him, ‘he is Brāhmaṇa’, in this manner, then he should be regarded as having attained all his ends. Or, it should be explained as, ‘when he has come to be known as dependent on the Brāhmaṇa.’ — Medhātithi (translated by Jha with changes)

जातब्राह्मणशब्दस्य दास्यादिति शेषः। ब्राह्मणाराधनेन ब्राह्मणवद्भवतीत्यर्थः। — नन्दन

Of the one who has attained the title ‘Brāhmaṇa’, the remaining portion is ‘by service’. By worshipping the Brāhmaṇa, he becomes like a Brāhmaṇa. — Nandana

The essence of what both Medhātithi and Nandana have said is that by serving a Brāhmaṇa, a Śūdra is either addressed as ‘Brāhmaṇa’, or he becomes like a Brāhmaṇa. Being addressed like a Brāhmaṇa, a Śūdra would be possessing Brāhmaṇa-like qualities. This seems to echo what the Devas intend to say and what is found in the Bhāgavata Purāṇa, as presented above. To call someone a ‘Brāhmaṇa’ is not based on birth alone. Similar is with the titles of other varṇas.

**9. Four kinds of jāti related to varṇa**

In the sub-commentary on Mitākṣarā called Bālambhaṭṭī, we are told that the jāti related to varṇa is of three kinds. It reads:

कर्मजन्यतावच्छेदिका विश्वामित्रादौ।…ऊढब्राह्मण्यां वोढृब्राह्मणजन्यतावच्छेदिका सर्वेषु ब्राह्मणेषु। देवरजे तु शास्त्रबोध्या। — याज्ञवल्क्यस्मृति १.९० बालम्भट्टी

The three kinds are; i.) delimitor of being born of action, like that of Viśvāmitra and such others, ii.) delimitor of being born of duly wedded Brāhmaṇa and Brāhmaṇī, like that of all Brāhmaṇas, and iii.) that which is to be known from the scripture, like that of the one born of brother-in-law of a woman.

The third kind of jāti related to varṇa is what seems to be related to the preceding discussion. It is through the scripture that it is known to exist. Although generally, varṇa and jāti are to be known only through scripture, it is unlike the Brāhmaṇya received through duly wedded Brāhmaṇa parents, because the latter is already known to people, so much so that they wouldn’t consult the scripture for it. Regarding that of Viśvāmitra and the like, it is known through stories in the Itihāsa texts, and also that they are about only what has happened in the past and not the present times.

Śeṣakṛṣṇa in his Śūdrācāraśiromaṇi tells us his opinion about the case of Viśvāmitra and Paraśurāma. He says:

एवं च परशुरामस्य ब्राह्मणत्वात् (ब्राह्मणात्?) क्षत्रियायामुत्पन्नत्वान्मूर्द्धावसिक्तत्वेऽपि स्वतपोऽतिशयादीश्वरानुग्रहाद्वा विश्वामित्रादेरिव ब्राह्मणत्वं ब्राह्मणधर्ममात्रमेव। जातेरुत्पत्तिनिवृत्त्ययोगात्।

\- शूद्राचारशिरोमणि, प्रथम परिच्छेद, जातिविवेक

He takes Paraśurāma to be born of a Brāhmaṇa father and a Kṣatriyā mother, making him a Mūrddhāvasikta. Yet, says Śeṣakṛṣṇa, that the Brāhmaṇya he received due to his great penance, or due to the mercy of God, is only Brāhmaṇatva, which is an attribute residing in Brāhmaṇas. The example he gives is that of Viśvāmitra and says that the varṇa related to jāti cannot be created or destroyed. We could take the fourth kind to be the one effected due to mercy of God.

An example of this is found in the story of Mataṅga as narrated in Skanda Purāṇa. It is told that he, a Cāṇḍāla by birth, performed severe penances to gain Brāhmaṇya, but he could not. Indra tells him to ask for a boon other than that of Brāhmaṇya, to which Mataṅga asks for the method by which he could gain Brāhmaṇya. Indra tells him to go and see a Mahākāla-liṅga. He does so, and Śiva grants him Brāhmaṇya.

The verses related to this episode are thus:

महाकालवने लिंगं स्थापितं ब्रह्मणा पुरा।

दिव्यमूर्त्तिधरं दिव्यं श्रीसिद्धेश्वरपूर्वतः॥३८॥

तस्य दर्शनमात्रेण विप्रत्वं समवाप्स्यसि।

वासवस्य च वाक्येन मतंगो गतवांस्तदा॥३९॥

महाकालवनं रम्यं सिद्धक्षेत्रमथापरम्।

ददर्श तत्र तल्लिंगमशेषफलदायकम्॥४०॥

दृष्ट्वा संपूजयामास पुष्पैर्नानाविधैस्तथा।

पूजितः प्रत्युवाचेदं मतंगं देवसत्तमः॥४१॥

अहो महान्सभाग्योसि यत्त्वया तोषितोऽस्म्यहम्।

मत्तः सर्वं समुद्भूतं ब्रह्मांडं भूर्भुवादिकम्॥४२॥

वरदोऽस्मि वरार्हाणां शापदोऽस्मि दुरात्मनाम्।

ब्राह्मण्यं मत्प्रसादाच्च अक्षयं ते भविष्यति॥४३॥

ततोऽसौ विप्रतां यातो मतंगो लिंगदर्शनात्।

पुनः पूजाप्रभावेण ब्रह्मलोकं गतो द्विजः॥४४॥

— स्कन्दपुराण, आवन्त्यखण्ड, अवन्तीस्थचतुरशीतिलिङ्गमाहात्म्य, अध्याय ६०

**Conclusion**

Through the scriptural references and analysis presented above, it is clear that regarding the identification of a person with a varṇa, there seem to have been two criteria; birth and qualities. Because varṇa is otherwise non-empirical, its meaning and application is to be stated by and understood from the attested Hindu scriptures. In the scriptures, along with the criterion of birth, there is also a criterion of qualities. Devas have been at the forefront of certifying the latter. The typical statements regarding this, which are found in the scriptures, are of the form of “Devas know abc as xyz” (taṃ devāḥ … viduḥ). Such statements are depicted to have been said by the Devas themselves in the episode of Viśvāmitra’s transformation from a Kṣatriya into a Brāhmaṇa. Even after being declared as having attained Brāhmaṇya, he asks separately for the competence of performing yajña for others and teaching Vedas. Abhinavagupta’s interpretation of such statements also attest the depth of them. In this manner, it can be said that the birth-based identification of varṇa is only one part of the concept of varṇas, which is specifically meant for ritualistic competence, whereas the other is the quality-based identification of varṇa. The extended application of a certain varṇa-title to a person of a different varṇa, in certain cases, may have stuck with the person, and it may be that his offspring and lineage eventually came to be identified with that varṇa. The question which remains is this; do Devas, who are at the centre of rituals, by recognising a person of a certain varṇa as belonging to another varṇa, extend ritualistic competence of that varṇa to him or not? We know that someone can lose such competence due to non-adherence to the concerned rules and regulations, which can be regained by performance of suggested expiations. Being within the purview of the scriptures, would it be completely impossible that extended usage of varṇa-titles would not lead to extension of ritualistic competence? These titles may come under the third and fourth types of the four types of jātis related to varṇa, i.e., those known through scripture and those effected by the mercy of God.
