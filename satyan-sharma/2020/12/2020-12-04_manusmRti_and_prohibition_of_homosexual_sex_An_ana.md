## **Verses ‘against’ Male homosexual sex**

*brāhmaṇasya rujaḥkṛtyaṃ ghrātir aghreyamadyayoḥ /*

*jaihmyaṃ ca maithunaṃ puṃsi jātibhraṃśakaraṃ smṛtam // Manusmṛti 11.67*

*maithunaṃ tu samāsevya puṃsi yoṣiti vā dvijaḥ /*

*goyāne ‘psu divā caiva savāsāḥ snānam ācaret // Manusmṛti 11.173*

In 11.67 (or 11.66), it is said that having sex with a man (maithunaṃ puṃsi) causes one to fall from his jāti (jātibhraṃśakaram). This verse is being used to show here how great an offence it is that it causes one to fall from his jāti. In verse 11.174 (or 11.173), the prāyaścitta of it is mentioned. It is said that if a dvija (any one of the upper 3 varṇas) has sex with a man (maithunaṃ tu samāsevya puṃsi), he should take a bath while wearing clothes (savāsāḥ snānam ācaret). So, the prāyaścitta of this ‘great offence’ which causes one to fall from his jāti, is merely taking a bath while wearing clothes.

The mention of dvija (11.174) implies the exclusion of Śūdras and others, while only including Brāhmaṇas, Kṣatriyas and Vaiśyas. Secondly, even in the case of a dvija male committing such an ‘offence’ of having sex with a male, the prāyaścitta is way too simple. May be the author of Manusmṛti did not think of it as something extremely bad, which is why he has stated such a simple prāyaścitta.

## **Verses ‘against’ Female homosexual sex**

*kanyaiva kanyāṃ yā kuryāt tasyāḥ syād dviśato damaḥ /*



*śulkaṃ ca dviguṇaṃ dadyāc chiphāś caivāpnuyād daśa // Manusmṛti 8.369*

*yā tu kanyāṃ prakuryāt strī sā sadyo mauṇḍyam arhati /*

*aṅgulyor eva vā chedaṃ khareṇodvahanaṃ tathā // Manusmṛti 8.370*

The verse 8.369 mentions of a kanyā (virgin) committing a ‘sexual offence’ on another kanyā, where the committer of offence is to be punished both physically and financially. Why only one of them is to be punished? It is because, as almost all major traditional commentators agree, it is about damaging a kanyā’s vagina. Medhātithi mentions a reason why a kanyā would do that to another; being jealous of the kanyā because of her looks (rūpa) etc.

Other commentators like Govindarāja, Maṇirāma and Kullūka explicitly mention kanyā being destroyed by the insertion of finger (aṅguliprakṣepeṇa nāśayet). Commentator Nandana adds ‘aviṣahya’ as an adjective of the offended kanyā, suggesting that the offender kanyā cannot tolerate her, may be because of jealousy as suggested by Medhātithi.

Same goes for the next verse (8.370), where a woman does that to a kanyā. Why the offended in both cases is a kanyā only? The indication is given by Sarvajñanārāyaṇa (8.369); because there would be a clear suspicion of a sexual intercourse (due to a clear physical change in the vagina), she wouldn’t be able to be married to another man.

So as suggested by majority of commentators and basic logic, also as these 2 verses involve kanyā alone as the offended, the case discussed is not about sambhoga or sexual intercourse, but about disturbing/destroying the vagina of a virgin, which would lead to the destroying of her status of virginity (of those times).

## **Conclusion**

The aforementioned verses which are being popularly cited to assert that Manusmṛti prohibits homosexual sex or considers it as a great offence, do not seem to defend that assertion. I request those who cite these verses, to find other verses from this text to defend that assertion.

Also, in any case, Manusmṛti is not the only authoritative text for ‘Hinduism’ or ‘Sanātana Dharma’. Hence, any such equation between the former and the latter does not hold true, or else all other similar texts would become useless.

But as far as this text is concerned, I request the respected scholars to cite other verses from it, which clearly call for some serious punishment and not merely bathing in the case of a man having sex with a man. In the case of female homosexual sex, there’s a need of an actual verse even mentioning it, let alone that which calls for a serious punishment, with which even the majority of the traditional commentators agree.
