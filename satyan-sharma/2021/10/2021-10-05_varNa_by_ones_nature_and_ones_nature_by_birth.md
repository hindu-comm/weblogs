*An analysis of system of varṇa based on Manusmṛti, Bhagavadgītā and other texts*

Is the system of varṇa based on birth? Or is it based on karma? Such questions are asked and answered variedly by scholars and laypersons. My curiosity about the same led me to explore various parts of traditional Sanskrit literature, from Vedas to Dharmaśāstras. One thing that I was struggling with was the consistent idea of birth-based varṇa system. I wondered if it were logically justifiable to call someone a Brāhmaṇa, a Kṣatriya, a Vaiśya or a Śūdra, based merely on their birth? It seemed to me like a hereditary property which was passed down to a person, irrespective of whether they deserve it or not. I read Manusmṛti along with its traditional commentaries, but could not figure out the actual idea behind birth being the basis of one’s varṇa. When I began reading Bhagavadgītā to explore this, it also did not lead me to a satisfactory answer, which I think was due to my own inability to comprehend the meaning of its verses. One thing which was clear in my mind was that Śāstra does not state something arbitrarily. Anything stated in it has to have a basis, which it may or may not have mentioned explicitly. So, if I am not able to find an explicit mention of the birth-based varṇa system, I have to try finding an implicit mention of the same. Fortunately enough, I did find it in Manusmṛti. I again went back to Bhagavadgītā. I read the related portions along with the traditional commentaries. Moving back and forth between both the texts, I happened to find congruence of the idea behind varṇa system (of course by birth). The idea which Bhagavadgītā expresses explicitly, is implicitly mentioned in the Manusmṛti. Similar ideas, instead of being merely a philosophical abstraction, find mention in old Sanskrit texts of Āyurveda. In this article, I shall share all those ideas. I shall also attempt to draw some conclusions, which shall be subject to scholarly review.

**Who is a Brāhmaṇa as per Manusmṛti?**

In the 5th verse of the 10th chapter of Manusmṛti, we find the definition of who shall be known by the varṇa of their parents. The definition can be used to identify a Brāhmaṇa, Kṣatriya, Vaiśya and a Śūdra. The definition is as follows, along with Ganganatha Jha’s English translation:

सर्ववर्णेषु तुल्यासु पत्नीष्वक्षतयोनिषु ।

आनुलोम्येन सम्भूता जात्या ज्ञेयास्त एव ते ॥

Among all castes, those only who are born of consorts wedded in the natural order, as virgins of equal status, are to be regarded as the same (as their father).

In this way, if all the other conditions are fulfilled, the child of a Brāhmaṇa father and a Brāhmaṇī mother is to be known as a Brāhmaṇa. Similar is the case with people of the other three varṇas. At the first sight of it, the definition looks perfect and seems to solve the issue of identifying certain people with certain varṇas on the basis of their birth. But when you come across other important details in the Manusmṛti itself, this definition begins to look not so perfect.

In verse 40 of the chapter 10, Manusmṛti tells about those people, who were born of parents of different varṇas, but were not publicly known to be so. Following is the verse with Jha’s translation:

सङ्करे जातयस्त्वेताः पितृमातृप्रदर्शिताः ।

प्रच्छन्ना वा प्रकाशा वा वेदितव्याः स्वकर्मभिः ॥

These castes, proceeding from mixtures, have been described in relation to their fathers and mothers; and whether manifest or unmanifest, they may be known by their occupations.

Similar is expressed in verse 57 of the same chapter, which says:

वर्णापेतमविज्ञातं नरं कलुषयोनिजम् ।

आर्यरूपमिवानार्यं कर्मभिः स्वैर्विभावयेत् ॥

The man of impure origin, who is devoid of caste, unknown, a non-Aryan, even though having the appearance of an Aryan, — one shall discover by his acts.

The number of such people grew to such an extent that in Mahābhārata, one finds Yudhiṣṭhira expressing how difficult it was to examine one’s jāti. He says:

जातिरत्र महासर्प मनुष्यत्वे महामते ।

सङ्करात् सर्ववर्णानां दुष्परीक्ष्येति मे मतिः ॥

सर्वे सर्वास्वपत्यानि जनयन्ति यदा नराः।

वाङ्मैथुनमथो जन्म मरणं च समं नृणाम्॥ (3.177.27–28)

Yudhiṣṭhira’s statement tells us that during his time the number of children reproduced by parents of different varṇas grew so much, that for him, telling someone’s jāti with certainty was very difficult. In the next verse, he provides a basis for peeping through the jāti of a person, which is śīla or character/behaviour. With this, let us move to Manusmṛti.

**Varṇa and Śīla in Manusmṛti**

In verse 59 of chapter 10 of Manusmṛti, the real reason behind the preference to marriages between a man and a woman of the same varṇa is hinted at. The verse and its translation by Jha are as follows:

पित्र्यं वा भजते शीलं मातुर्वोभयमेव वा ।

न कथंचन दुर्योनिः प्रकृतिं स्वां नियच्छति ॥

The base-born man bears the character of his father, or of his mother, or of both; he can never conceal his origin.

Although the verse is related to someone born of an incompatible varṇa combination, the reason for which it is being despised in the verse, can be the reason behind why the marriages between a couple of same varṇa is preferred. The reason is that the child inherits the śīla or character/behaviour of either or both the parents. So mixed-varṇa marriages were seen as marriages of mixed śīla, which was then seen in the child reproduced.

This is probably the reason why in the Chāndogyopaniṣat story (4.4.4), Hāridrumata Gautama declares Satyakāma Jābāla as a Brāhmaṇa, because he infers that his Brāhmaṇa like nature has come from a Brāhmaṇa father. During his commentary on this, Ādi Śaṅkarācārya states that only Brāhmaṇas are straightforward by nature. He says:

ऋजवो हि ब्राह्मणा नेतरे स्वभावतः

On this statement of his, Ānandagiri comments that people of other varṇas i.e., Kṣatriya etc., aren’t like this, and so Satyakāma was declared to be a Brāhmaṇa. Thus are his words:

क्षत्त्रियादीनामपि केषाञ्चिदार्जवमस्तीत्याशङ्क्याऽऽह नेतर इति

On the Brahmasūtraśāṅkarabhāṣya on sūtra 1.3.37 (apaśūdrādhikaraṇa), the sub-commentary of Ānandagiri adds one more point. It says that a Śūdra is deceptive and because Satyakāma spoke the truth, it proved that he was not a Śūdra. He says:

तदीयसत्यवचनेन शूद्रस्य मायावित्वयोगात् तदशूद्रत्वे सिद्धे

Similar is the point taken by Rāmānandācārya in his commentary on the same passage of Chāndogyopaniṣat, with the only difference that he takes the word ‘Brāhmaṇa’ to denote Kṣatriya and Vaiśya also. He says:

यद्यपि ब्राह्मणपदं ब्राह्मणस्यैव बोधकं तथापि त्रैवर्णिकानां वेदाध्ययनेऽधिकारात् ब्राह्मणपदं त्रैवर्णिकस्योपलक्षणम् अत्रैवर्णिकः शूद्रः एतादृशमार्जवादिवचनं वक्तुं नार्हति शूद्रस्य कुटिलस्वभावत्वात्

This too points to the fact that the basis of varṇa was not merely a distant lineage, but something far more recent and better perceivable i.e., one’s nature. In the absence of information on one’s lineage, one’s behaviour and conduct can act as a proof of it.

In the said incident of Chāndogyopaniṣat, gotra was to be used to determine the varṇa of Satyakāma. But even if one knows one’s gotra, it cannot itself tell one’s varṇa. As explained in the Mitākṣarā commentary of Yājñavalkyasmṛti (1.96), if one (along with his descendants) does not leave the āpaddharma (jobs to be taken in times when one cannot survive by performing the prescribed jobs) even in the absence of the time of distress, then the fifth generation becomes that very (lower) varṇa, the dharma of which the family had taken up during the time of distress. The commentary reads:

तस्मिन् व्यत्यये सति यद्यापद्विमोक्षेऽपि तां वृत्तिं न परित्यजति तदा सप्तमे षष्ठे पञ्चमे वा जन्मनि साम्यं यस्य हीनवर्णस्य कर्मणा जीवति तत्समानजातित्वं भवति। तद्यथा। ब्राह्मणः शूद्रवृत्त्या जीवन् तामपरित्यजन् यदि पुत्रमुत्पादयति सोऽपि तयैव वृत्त्या जीवन् पुत्रान्तरमित्येवं परम्परया सप्तमे जन्मनि शूद्रमेव जनयति।

In this way, one may as well find a Brāhmaṇa gotra in a Śūdra family. For example, as per Harivaṃśapurāna (29.7–8), the descendants of Ṛṣi Śunaka are of all 4 varṇas.

पुत्रो गृत्समदस्यापि शुनको यस्य शौनकाः॥ 7½

ब्राह्मणाः क्षत्रियाश्चैव वैश्याः शूद्रास्तथैव च॥ 8½

Hence, inferring the varṇa of a child by simply looking at his/her parents’ known varṇa (usually through gotra or memory, even in the absence of varṇadharma through many generations) is ineffective.

**Karma in accordance with Śīla**

In the verse 30 of chapter 1 of Manusmṛti, through a basic illustration, it has been suggested that living beings naturally perform the actions assigned to them. The verse and Jha’s translation are as follows:

यथर्तुलिङ्गान्यृतवः स्वयमेवर्तुपर्यये ।

स्वानि स्वान्यभिपद्यन्ते तथा कर्माणि देहिनः ॥

Just as on the approach of the turn of the Seasons, each season, by itself, acquires its own seasonal characteristics, — so in the same manner living beings take up their respective lines of action.

On verse 40 of the 10th chapter of Manusmṛti mentioned previously, the commentator Sarvajñanārāyaṇa comments that the reprehensible work which the people born of parents of different varṇas perform, is due to their śīla or nature to opt such work. He says:

स्वकर्मभिर्गर्हितकर्मवर्तनशीलतया वेदितव्याः प्रच्छन्ना अप्रच्छन्ना अपि

Similar is the interpretation of Kullūkabhaṭṭa, who says:

तथा गूढाः प्रकटा वा तज्जात्युदितकर्मानुष्ठानेन ज्ञातव्याः

Govindarāja takes such work to be impelled (codita) due to one’s jāti, which again is similar to the idea of śīla. He says:

एताश्च गूढाः प्रकटा वा तज्जातिचोदितकर्माचरणदर्शनेन बोद्धव्याः

Bhāruci says that one should infer the jāti as mentioned in the śāstra, by looking at the work one does. He says:

कर्मणा शास्त्रोपदिष्टजातिरनुमातव्या

These interpretations seem correct because if someone is not known as being born of a mixed-varṇa marriage and isn’t known to belong to a certain jāti, they may not be given the job prescribed for them by the śāstra. Hence it seems that a person opted for work as per their natural inclination, due to which their jāti could be inferred. It is to be further noted that as per the verse and its commentaries, there is no difference between such a person being known or unknown by their jāti from the point of view of the work they perform, which implies that what the śāstra did was that it merely formalised an already existent practice and did not make a whole new practice from scratch. This may give a new perspective on how to interpret the list of jobs prescribed for the offspring of such mixed-varṇa marriages is in Manusmṛti.

**Bhagavadgītā and Varṇa**

Verse 41 of chapter 18 of the Bhagavadgītā also sheds light on the issue of varṇa. It is as follows, along with an English translation as per Gita Press’s English edition.

ब्राह्मणक्षत्रियविशां शूद्राणाञ्च परन्तप।

कर्माणि प्रविभक्तानि स्वभावप्रभवैर्गुणैः॥

The duties of the Brāhmaṇas, Kṣatriyas and the Vaiśyas, as well as of the Śūdras have been assigned according to their inborn qualities, Arjuna.

In the various traditional commentaries on this verse, ‘svabhāvaprabhava’ is explained variously. Ādi Śaṅkarācārya takes three meanings of ‘svabhāva’, which are as follows:

- स्वभावः ईश्वरस्य प्रकृतिः त्रिगुणात्मिका माया सा प्रभवः येषां गुणानां ते स्वभावप्रभवाः
  तैः शमादीनि कर्माणि प्रविभक्तानि ब्राह्मणादीनाम्।

Svabhāva is the Prakṛti of Īśvara which is of the form of the three guṇas. Hence, the guṇas, of which it is the prabhava (cause), are svabhāvaprabhava guṇas. The karmas of Brāhmaṇas, etc. are categorised as per these guṇas.

- ब्राह्मणस्वभावस्य सत्त्वगुणः प्रभवः कारणम्, तथा क्षत्रियस्वभावस्य सत्त्वोपसर्जनं रजः
  प्रभवः, वैश्यस्वभावस्य तमउपसर्जनं रजः प्रभवः, शूद्रस्वभावस्य रजउपसर्जनं तमः प्रभवः,
  प्रशान्त्यैश्वर्येहामूढतास्वभावदर्शनात् चतुर्णाम्।

The prabhava (cause) of the svabhāva (nature) of Brāhmaṇa is sattva guṇa, that of Kṣatriya’s svabhāva is rajas mixed with sattva, that of Vaiśya’s svabhāva is rajas mixed with tamas, and that of Śūdra’s svabhāva is tamas mixed with rajas. The reason for this is that in Brāhmaṇas is seen praśānti (calmness), in Kṣatriyas is seen aiśvarya (nature of ruling), in Vaiśyas is seen ceṣṭā (action/endeavour), and in Śūdra is seen mūḍhatā (dullness).

- जन्मान्तरकृतसंस्कारः प्राणिनां वर्तमानजन्मनि स्वकार्याभिमुखत्वेन अभिव्यक्तः स्वभावः सः
  प्रभवो येषां गुणानां ते स्वभावप्रभवाः गुणाः गुणप्रादुर्भावस्य निष्कारणत्वानुपपत्तेः।

Svabhāva is the saṃskāra of the previous birth, which becomes known in the current birth through its effect i.e., the guṇas. Hence those guṇas are svabhāvaprabhava.

In all these explanations, one thing which is common is that the guṇas are expressed in the current birth. The other commentaries like those of Madhusūdana Sarasvatī, Vallabhācārya, etc., also have this thing in common. Hence, you can examine if those, who claim to be or are known to be of a certain varṇa, actually belong to it or not. Even though the proportion of guṇas may be determined by the karma or saṃskāras of the previous birth, the guṇas become manifest in this very birth. In the end of his commentary on this verse, Ādi Śaṅkarācārya states that even the division of karmas of varṇas mentioned by Dharmaśāstras, has been done on the basis of these very guṇas.

It is in this sense that the following verses (from 18.42–44) are to be understood. As are the guṇas, as are the natural tendencies, so are the karmas prescribed for the people of different varṇas. It is in this sense that the svadharma is always better paradharma (18.47). Also, it seems reasonable to divide karma as per the different personalities of all humans, because to tell a human to do something which is no where near their natural ability, will lead to all sorts of practical problems.

From verses 18.26 to 18.28 of Bhagavadgītā, the characteristics of sāttvika, rājasika and tāmasika persons have been stated. Not only this, in the previous and following verses, jñāna (knowledge), karma, buddhi and dhṛti, as per their similar categories based on sattva, rajas and tamas, have been described. Following are the verses with the Gita Press’ translation.

मुक्तसङ्गोऽनहंवादी धृत्युत्साहसमन्वितः।

सिद्ध्यसिद्ध्योर्निविकारः कर्ता सात्त्विक उच्यते॥ (18.26)

Free from attachment, unegoistic, endowed with firmness and zeal and unswayed by success and failure — such a doer is said to be Sāttvika.

रागी कर्मफलप्रेप्सुर्लुब्धो हिंसात्मकोऽशुचिः।

हर्षशोकान्वितः कर्ता राजसः परिकीर्तितः॥ (18.27)

The doer who is full of attachment, seeks the fruit of actions and is greedy, and who is oppressive by nature and of impure conduct, and is affected by joy and sorrow, has been called Rājasika.

अयुक्तः प्राकृतः स्तब्धः शठोऽनैष्कृतिकोऽलसः।

विषादी दीर्घसूत्री च कर्ता तामस उच्यते॥ (18.28)

Lacking piety and self-control, uncultured, arrogant, deceitful, inclined to rob others of their livelihood, slothful, despondent and procrastinating — such a doer is called Tāmasika.

In the following verses, such a division of buddhi (understanding) and dhṛti (firmness) also has been stated. This shows that the theory of relation between guṇas and behaviour was a well developed one.

As said by the commentaries on the verse 18.41 of Bhagavadgītā, the guṇas in a person are manifest in this birth and with the range of data which is available on the issue, the persons can be examined in detail as belonging to a certain proportion of guṇas. For example a Brāhmaṇa would need to be sāttvika, a Kṣatriya would be mainly rājasika with some sāttvika features, a Vaiśya would be mainly rājasika with some tāmasika features, and a Śūdra would be tāmasika with some rājasika features. In case a person who is known by lineage as a Brāhmaṇa, but is mainly tāmasika with some rājasika features, then as per the commentaries, he should be known as a Śūdra. A person who is known as a Śūdra by lineage, but is sāttvika, then as per the commentaries, he should be known as a Brāhmaṇa. It may be in this sense that the 4th sūtra of Āpastamba Dharmasūtra has been stated, which says that among the four varṇas, the earlier is superior by birth than the one which follows. Because it is at one’s birth that the svabhāva or sahaja bhāva (literally ‘the bhāva which is born along with oneself at birth’) becomes manifest, and the sattva guṇa is the most revered of all, whereas tamas guṇa is the most despised of all, the individual who has a sāttvika personality is taken to be the most superior of all others. The others who have lesser sattva and more rajas, would be the second, and so on and so forth. This could be the same reason why in Mahābhārata (13.131.6) Maheśvara says to Umā that it is by nisarga (svabhāva) that a Brāhmaṇa is a Brāhmaṇa, a Kṣatriya is a Kṣatriya, a Vaiśya is a Vaiśya, and a Śūdra is a Śūdra. His statement is as follows:

ब्राह्मण्यं देवि दुष्प्रापं निसर्गाद् ब्राह्मणः शुभे।

क्षत्रियो वैश्यशूद्रौ वा निसर्गादिति मे मतिः॥

The said verses of Bhagavadgītā redirect our attention from the lineage to the individual. The definition of, for example, a Brāhmaṇa as provided in Manusmṛti 10.5, is not practically applicable, keeping in mind thousands of years of mixed-varṇa marriages, and also the issue of continuance of āpaddharma even after the time of distress is over. It does not matter by what varṇa your lineage is known in today’s world, what matters is the characteristics or personalities as described in the śāstras.

Here, it is important to state that even in the case put forward in Bhagavadgītā, the varṇa is still by birth, although not necessarily the varṇa by which one’s lineage is currently known. There should be a distinction between the varṇa by which one’s parents are currently known and the varṇa which they actually belong to. It is still true that the person will inherit the characteristics of their parents, but to merely take the parents’ varṇa to be X or Y and then to stick it on their child like a tag without examining their characteristics, shall make that varṇa to be an artificial one. It is possible that the varṇa which many people identify themselves with today, is only artificial and not their real varṇa.

As said earlier, the basic idea behind not promoting mixed-varṇa marriages was incompatibility of śīla or nature. Hence, a marriage between a man of Śūdra nature and woman of Brāhmaṇa nature, and vice versa, were seen as incompatible combinations from the perspective of natural tendencies of the person. Merely matching the unexamined known varṇa inherited from one’s family isn’t going to help.

**Is there a connection between Guṇas and Varṇas in Manusmṛti?**

In chapter 12 of Manusmṛti, the effects of guṇas have been stated. Following are the verses and Jha’s translation.

सत्त्वं रजस्तमश्चैव त्रीन् विद्यादात्मनो गुणान् ।

यैर्व्याप्यैमान् स्थितो भावान् महान् सर्वानशेषतः ॥ 24

Know Sattva, Rajas and Tamas to be the three qualities of the Self, by means of which the Great One completely pervades all these beings.

यो यदेषां गुणो देहे साकल्येनातिरिच्यते ।

स तदा तद्गुणप्रायं तं करोति शरीरिणम् ॥ 25

Whichsoever of these qualities wholly predominates in a body, it makes the owner of that body abound in that quality.

सत्त्वं ज्ञानं तमोऽज्ञानं रागद्वेषौ रजः स्मृतम् ।

एतद् व्याप्तिमदेतेषां सर्वभूताश्रितं वपुः ॥ 26

‘Sattva’ has been declared to be Knowledge, ‘Tamas,’ to be Ignorance, and ‘Rajas,’ to be Love and Hate; — such is the nature of these, all-pervading and interpenetrating all beings.

तत्र यत् प्रीतिसंयुक्तं किञ्चिदात्मनि लक्षयेत् ।

प्रशान्तमिव शुद्धाभं सत्त्वं तदुपधारयेत् ॥ 27

Whenever one perceives in himself something full of bliss, calm and pure, — he should know it to be ‘Sattva.’

यत् तु दुःखसमायुक्तमप्रीतिकरमात्मनः ।

तद् रजोऽप्रतिपं विद्यात् सततं हारि देहिनाम् ॥ 28

What is mixed with pain and brings unhappiness to the Soul, — know that to be ‘Rajas,’ imperceptible and constantly attracting embodied beings.

यत् तु स्यान् मोहसंयुक्तमव्यक्तं विषयात्मकम् ।

अप्रतर्क्यमविज्ञेयं तमस्तदुपधारयेत् ॥ 29

What is mixed with stupefaction, undiscernible, of the nature of sensual objects, incapable of being reasoned about and uncognisable, — one should recognise as ‘Tamas.’

वेदाभ्यासस्तपो ज्ञानं शौचमिन्द्रियनिग्रहः ।

धर्मक्रियाऽत्मचिन्ता च सात्त्विकं गुणलक्षणम् ॥ 31

Vedic study, austerity, knowledge, purity, control over the organs, practice of virtue and meditation on the Soul, — are the characteristics of the quality of ‘Sattva.’

आरम्भरुचिताऽधैर्यमसत्कार्यपरिग्रहः ।

विषयोपसेवा चाजस्रं राजसं गुणलक्षणम् ॥ 32

Proneness to undertake work, impatience, commission of improper acts, constant addiction to sensual objects are the characteristics of the quality of ‘Rajas.’

लोभः स्वप्नोऽधृतिः क्रौर्यं नास्तिक्यं भिन्नवृत्तिता ।

याचिष्णुता प्रमादश्च तामसं गुणलक्षणम् ॥ 33

Avarice, drowsiness, irresolution, cruelty, disbelief, bad character, habit of begging, and inattentiveness are the characteristics of the quality of ‘Tamas.’

After some more verses, the states of existence with regard to guṇas are mentioned. These verses categorise various beings, humans and animals, as per guṇas.

स्थावराः कृमिकीटाश्च मत्स्याः सर्पाः सकच्छपाः ।

पशवश्च मृगाश्चैव जघन्या तामसी गतिः ॥ 42

हस्तिनश्च तुरङ्गाश्च शूद्रा म्लेच्छाश्च गर्हिताः ।

सिंहा व्याघ्रा वराहाश्च मध्यमा तामसी गतिः ॥ 43

चारणाश्च सुपर्णाश्च पुरुषाश्चैव दाम्भिकाः ।

रक्षांसि च पिशाचाश्च तामसीषूत्तमा गतिः ॥ 44

Inanimate beings, worms, insects, fishes, snakes, tortoise, cattle and wild animals, — represent the lowest state due to the quality of ‘Tamas.’ — (42)

Elephants, horses, despised *Śūdras*, Mlecchas, lions, tigers and boars — represent the middling state due to the quality of ‘Tamas.’ — (43)

*Cāraṇas*, *Suparṇas*, hypocritical men, *Rākṣasas*, and *Piśācas* — represent the highest state among those partaking of the quality of ‘Tamas.’ — (44)

झल्ला मल्ला नटाश्चैव पुरुषाः शस्त्रवृत्तयः ।

द्यूतपानप्रसक्ताश्च जघन्या राजसी गतिः ॥ 45

Stick-fencers, wrestlers, actors, men subsisting by the use of weapons, those addicted to gambling and drinking, — represent the lowest state among those partaking of the quality of ‘Rajas.’



राजानः क्षत्रियाश्चैव राज्ञां चैव पुरोहिताः ।

वादयुद्धप्रधानाश्च मध्यमा राजसी गतिः ॥ 46

Kings, *Kṣatriyas*, priests of kings, and leading wranglers and warriors represent the middling state among those partaking of ‘Rajas.’

गन्धर्वा गुह्यका यक्षा विबुधानुचराश्च ये ।

तथैवाप्सरसः सर्वा राजसीषूत्तमा गतिः ॥ 47

Gandharvas, Guhyakas, ‘*Yakṣas*,’ the attendants of the gods, and all the Apsaras, represent the high state among those partaking of ‘Rajas.’

तापसा यतयो विप्रा ये च वैमानिका गणाः ।

नक्षत्राणि च दैत्याश्च प्रथमा सात्त्विकी गतिः ॥ 48

Ascetics and hermits, *Brāhmaṇas*, celestial beings, lunar asterisms, and Daityas represent the first state partaking of ‘Sattva.’

यज्वान ऋषयो देवा वेदा ज्योतींषि वत्सराः ।

पितरश्चैव साध्याश्च द्वितीया सात्त्विकी गतिः ॥ 49

Sacrificers, sages, gods, vedas, luminaries, years, *Pitṛs* and *Sādhyas* represent the second state partaking of ‘Sattva.’

ब्रह्मा विश्वसृजो धर्मो महानव्यक्तमेव च ।

उत्तमां सात्त्विकीमेतां गतिमाहुर्मनीषिणः ॥ 50

*Brahmā*, creators of the universe, Dharma, the Great One, Unmanifest, — these the wise ones describe as representing the best state partaking of ‘Sattva.’

Śūdras are mentioned in the category of madhyama tāmasa (12.43), Kṣatriyas are mentioned as madhyama rājasa (12.46), while Vipras are mentioned as prathama sāttvika (12.48). Although Vaiśyas aren’t mentioned in this, but this is enough to show that the relation between guṇas and varṇas was known in some sense even during Manusmṛti. Jha connects the word ‘garhita’ (despised) with Śūdras, but no traditional commentary on the related verse specifies this.

**Āyurveda and categorisation of personalities as per Guṇas**

The categorisation of sāttvika, rājasika and tāmasika personalities or minds is also mentioned Āyurveda texts like Suśrutasaṃhitā. At the end of the 4th chapter (garbhavyākaraṇa) of Śārīrasthāna of Suśrutasaṃhitā, a detailed mention of such persons is found, where 7 types of sāttvika, 6 types of rājasika and 3 types of tāmasika temperaments are mentioned. In the 28th chapter of Kāśyapasaṃhitā’s Sūtrasthāna, there is a mention of 8 types of sāttvika, 7 types of rājasika and 3 types of tāmasika temperaments. This is to show that the categorisation of personalities as per guṇas isn’t merely a part of śruti and smṛti, but is also accepted by Āyurveda texts for medical purposes.

Following are some of the verses from the 4th chapter of Śārīrasthāna of the Suśrutasaṃhitā, which state the features of the Sāttvika, Rājasika and Tāmasika personalities, along with a translation by Kaviraj Kunja Lal Bhishagratna:

Sāttvika features -

शौचमास्तिक्यमध्यासो वेदेषु गुरुपूजनम्।

प्रियातिथित्वमिज्या च ब्रह्मकायस्य लक्षणम्॥ (81)

The features of a Brahma-kāya person are cleanliness of person and conduct, belief in the existence of God, a constant reader of the Vedas, a worship and reverence of elders and preceptors, hospitality and celebration of religious sacrifices.

माहात्म्यं शौर्यमाज्ञा च सततं शास्त्रबुद्धिता।

भृत्यानां भरणं चापि माहेन्द्रं कायलक्षणम्॥ (82)

Those of Māhendra-kāya person are valour, command, constant discussion of the Śāstras, maintenance of servants and dependents and magnanimity.

Rājasika features -

ऐश्वर्यवन्तं रौद्रं च शूरं चण्डमसूयकम्।

एकाशिनं चौदरिकमासुरं सत्त्वमीदृशम्॥ (88)

Asura-Sattva men are affluent in circumstances, dreadful, valorous, irascible, jealous of other men’s excellence, gluttonous and fond of eating alone without sharing with anyone else.

तीक्ष्णमायासिनं भीरुं चण्डं मायाविनं तथा।

विहाराचारचपलं सर्पसत्त्वं विदुर्नरम्॥ (89)

A Sarpa-Sattva man is irritable, laborious, cowardly, angry, double-dealing, and hasty in eating and sexual intercourse.

Tāmasika features -

दुर्मेधस्त्वं मन्दता च स्वप्ने मैथुननित्यता।

निराकरिष्णुता चैव विज्ञेयाः पाशवाः गुणाः॥ (95)

The features of a Pāśava-Sattva man are perverseness of intellect, parsimoniousness, frequent sexual dreams and incapacity of ascertaining or discerning anything.

अनवस्थितता मौर्ख्यं भीरुत्वं सलिलार्थता।

परस्पराभिमर्दश्च मत्स्यसत्त्वस्य लक्षणम्॥ (96)

The features of Matsya-Sattva man are unsteadiness, stupidity, cowardice, fond of intermissive quarrel and oppression and a longing for water.

This shows that the relation between guṇas and personalities is not a mere abstraction, but something which is practically applicable in Āyurveda. Also, with the data available from such texts, one can test the guṇa of a person’s temperament.

**Tridaṇḍī Svāmī’s commentary on Puruṣasūkta**

In his commentary on the 18th mantra of the Puruṣasūkta of Śuklayajurveda, Śrīmadviṣvaksenācārya Tridaṇḍī Svāmī mentions the lakṣaṇas or marks of a Brāhmaṇa. The lakṣaṇa which he quotes from the Vajrasūcyupaniṣat does not take into account the lineage. The major points in the lakṣaṇa are:

- A Brāhmaṇa knows his Self to be devoid of jāti, guṇa and kriyā.
- He is devoid of the faults of kāma, rāga, etc.
- He possesses qualities like śama, dama, etc.
- His mind is far away from dambha, ahaṃkāra, etc.

The lakṣaṇa ends by saying that such lakṣaṇas of a Brāhmaṇa is what is intended by Śruti, Smṛti, Purāṇa, etc.

He then quotes Āhnikasūtras, which talk about similar attributes. One of the verses is as follows:

शान्ताः सन्तः सुशीलाश्च सर्वभूतहिते रताः।

क्रोधं कर्तुं न जानन्ति एतद्ब्राह्मणलक्षणम्॥

Nārāyaṇa Śarmā in his Āhnikasūtrāvalī for Śuklayajurvedīya Mādhyandinas and Vājasaneyins cites the lakṣaṇa of one sāttvika personality (Brahmakāya) as mentioned in the Śārīrasthāna of Suśrutasaṃhitā (verse 81, chapter 4). He then mentions the same verses from Āhnikakārikas, as done by Tridaṇḍī Svāmī. Nārāyaṇa Śarmā quotes verse 6.20 from the Vasiṣṭha Smṛti, which gives similar sāttvika qualities, as a mark to identify a Brāhmaṇa. The verse is as follows:

योगस्तपो दमो दानं सत्यं शौचं दया श्रुतम्।

विद्या विज्ञानमास्तिक्यमेतद्ब्राह्मणलक्षणम्॥ ६.२०

Yoga, penance, control over senses, donation, truth, cleanliness, mercy, Śāstra, knowledge, Vijñāna (knowledge of the Self?), belief in Vedas etc., are the marks of a Brāhmaṇa.

Similar is done by Mādhavācārya while commenting on verse 1.38 of Pārāśarasmṛti (ṣaṭkarmmābhiratonityaṃ). He states the marks of a Brāhmaṇa, who is pointed to in the verse, from the Mahābhārata. Some of the verses are as follows:

सत्यं दानं तपः शौचमानृशंस्यं दमो घृणा।

दृश्यन्ते यत्र विप्रेन्द्र स ब्राह्मण इति स्मृतः॥

जितेन्द्रियो धर्म्मपरः स्वाध्यायनिरतः शुचिः।

कामक्रोधौ वशौ यस्य तं देवा ब्राह्मणं विदुः॥

These also are sāttvika. Mādhavācārya states that by these marks, those who haven’t done agnyādhāna are also included. These verses could also be taken to be an emphasis on good conduct. Then we must ask, why is there a need to emphasise good conduct in this manner? It may be the case that because of a lot of inter-varṇa marriages, the definition of who the actual Brāhmaṇa (and likewise the actual Kṣatriya, Vaiśya, Śūdra) is could not be taken for granted. If one were to believe on the basis of gotra and lineage (which may be dubious) that a certain person was an actual Brāhmaṇa, then even though he may not have the nature of a Brāhmaṇa by birth, he would still be considered an actual Brāhmaṇa and taught the skills of a Brāhmaṇa, ultimately leading to an artificial Brāhmaṇa.

Just as Vasiṣṭha Smṛti gives a lakṣaṇa of Brāhmaṇa, it gives the lakṣaṇa of a Śūdra too, which is:

दीर्घवैरमसूया च असत्यं ब्रह्मदूषणम्।

पैशुन्यं निर्दयत्वं च जानीयाच्छूद्रलक्षणम्॥ ६.२३

One who holds enmity against someone for a long time, finds faults with others’ qualities, speaks false, finds faults with Brāhmaṇas, is cruel, doesn’t have mercy, know that to be the lakṣaṇa of a Śūdra.

The tāmasika nature of Śūdra has been mentioned by Mādhavācārya in his commentary on Parāśara Smṛti. Following are the verses he cites from the Anuśāsanaparva of Mahābhārata:

रागो द्वेषश्च मोहश्च पारुष्यञ्च नृशंसता।

शाठ्यञ्च दीर्घवैरत्वमतिमानमनार्ज्जवम्॥

अमतञ्चातिवादश्च पैशुन्यमतिलोभता।

निकृतिश्चाप्यविज्ञानं जनने शूद्रमाविशत्॥

In these verses it has been said that the tāmasika attributes like rāga, dveṣa, etc. entered a Śūdra during his birth. The last verse cited by Mādhavācārya along with these verses says that it is for the purpose of destroying these tāmasika attributes that a Śūdra has been told to serve a Dvija. So the question arises, if you know someone who is known as a Śūdra in his public life, but you have never observed any such tāmasika attribute in him, would you still call him a Śūdra? Yes you may say that his tāmasika attributes may have been destroyed by serving Dvijas, but what if you know him since his birth and have never observed any tāmasika attribute in him? I would call it a case of wrong identification, and would conclude that though he is not a Śūdra by birth or nature, but has been mistaken for a Śūdra via what is popularly known about his lineage. I would then infer that the absence of tāmasika attributes in him need to have some cause either before his birth, or in his birth. If after examining his family and his nearest ancestors I do not find any such tāmasika attributes, I would conclude that most probably his family is not of Śūdras. Similar would be in case of other varṇas.

Because ultimately, the idea of varṇa is based on one’s inherent nature and because mixed-varṇa marriages were already happening in good numbers at the time of the authorship of the texts being mentioned, the description of such qualities was done to identify the real Brāhmaṇa, the real Śūdra etc. Merely looking at the lineage to identify one’s varṇa may also goes against Manusmṛti’s verse 10.60 which says that a case of varṇa-mixture in a mukhya kula also would reflect in the nature and/or behaviour of the offspring. Following is the verse with Jha’s translation.

कुले मुख्येऽपि जातस्य यस्य स्याद् योनिसङ्करः ।

संश्रयत्येव तच्छीलं नरोऽल्पमपि वा बहु ॥ ६०

Even when one is born in a great family, if there be a confusion regarding his parentage, he surely imbibes the traits thereof, to a greater or less extent.

The said verses of the 18th chapter Bhagavadgītā provide a stable basis for such an identification.

**Is it possible to ascertain the real varṇa of a child before Saṃskāras like Upanayana?**

Hāridrumata Gautama ascertained the Brāhmaṇa-ness of Satyakāma during a basic conversation. In case it does not seem possible in a general manner, there could be one way in which an Ācārya or Guru could determine such a nature of a child, for which he could get ample time. The saṃskāra called Vidyārambha (also known as Akṣarasvīkāra) is open to all varṇas and is performed for a child when he or she attains 5 years of age. The earliest Upanayana saṃskāra usually happens when the child is 8 years old (in the case of a Brāhmaṇa). In that case, from the day of Vidyārambha, upto Upanayana, an Ācārya or Guru of the child has at least 3 years to ascertain the nature of the child, from which he could determine the actual varṇa with which the child is born. In case a child who belongs to a family, which is currently known as Śūdra, exhibits clear signs of being a Brāhmaṇa, the Ācārya could ascertain that the child is not a Śūdra and further infer similarly for his family. Ascertainment and revision of one’s real varṇa makes it better possible for one to marry someone of a similar real varṇa, leading to an offspring which is equal to both, who shall not only be known as a Brāhmaṇa, but shall also be an actual Brāhmaṇa. In this way, the predictability of one’s real varṇa from one’s parentage or lineage becomes better.

One may wonder what would happen to the hereditary occupation of the lineage in which the child is born? The answer is a small ceremony-like event which happens before Cūḍākarma (tonsure ceremony). It is usually called ‘Jīvikāparīkṣā’ (examination of the means of livelihood). It is mentioned in many texts on saṃskāras like Saṃskāramayūkha. The authority on the ceremony is Mārkaṇḍeya. As per the procedure, clothes (or books), weapon, pen (ink pot, paper etc.), etc. are kept in front of the child. The thing which the child touches first is known to be his source of livelihood. The verses are as follows:

देवाग्रतोऽथ विन्यस्य शिल्पभाण्डानि सर्वशः।

वस्त्राणि\* चैव शस्त्राणि ततः पश्येत्तु लक्षणम्॥ (\*also शास्त्राणि)

प्रथमं यत्स्पृशेद् बालस्ततो भाण्डं स्वयं तथा।

जीविका तस्य बालस्य तेनैव तु भविष्यति॥

If the occupation of a child is fixed by lineage, why would this ceremony be mentioned? The mention of this ceremony shows that the occupation of a child, as per Śāstra, need not be fixed by lineage. So in case a child’s varṇa is ascertained to be other than the one which is known popularly based on his lineage, his change of profession is not an issue as per the aforementioned ceremony. A child not following his ancestor’s profession, is not an issue.

**Can one change their varṇa by karma?**

As has been said, varṇa is based on śīla/svabhāva. To change one’s varṇa would then mean changing one’s nature. The problem with it is that it is called one’s nature or svabhāva for the reason that it cannot be changed, except in cases of miracles, etc. One may move up and down the staircase which is called nature, but one cannot, in most cases, change the staircase itself, unless there’s some miracle, etc. In case one does see a change, it could be inferred that it was through ignorance of one’s own nature that one was acting in opposition to it earlier. One acts as per one’s nature, which implies that one’s nature comes before one’s action.

**Concluding remarks based on Bhagavadgītā**

In Bhagavadgītā’s chapter 3, verse 33, Kṛṣṇa tells something very basic and yet important to the ongoing discussion. The verse with Gita Press’ translation is as follows:

सदृशं चेष्टते स्वस्याः प्रकृतेर्ज्ञानवानपि।

प्रकृतिं यान्ति भूतानि निग्रहः किं करिष्यति॥

All living creatures follow their tendencies; even a wise man acts according to the tendencies of his own nature. Of what use is any external restraint?

Ādi Śaṅkarācārya defines this Prakṛti as the saṃskāra of dharma, adharma, etc. of previous birth, which becomes manifest in the present birth.

On this verse, Ānandagiri notes:

प्रकृतिं यान्ति प्रकृतिसदृशीं चेष्टां गच्छन्त्यनिच्छन्त्यपि सर्वाणि भूतानीत्यर्थः

“The meaning of ‘follow Prakṛti’ is that all beings act as per Prakṛti even when they don’t desire so.”

Rāmānujācārya says on this verse:

ज्ञानवान् अपि स्वस्याः प्रकृतेः प्राचीनवासनायाः सदृशं प्राकृतविषयेषु एव चेष्टते

“Even the wise acts, in matters of Prakṛti, according to one’s Prakṛti, i.e., the vāsanā of previous life.”

Vallabhācārya notes:

अतो भूतानि सर्वाणि सात्त्विकराजसतामसानि स्वभावमनुवर्त्तन्ते वायुं तृणवत्। तत्र निग्रहः शिक्षणं ऐन्द्रियो वा किं करिष्यति प्रकृतेः प्रबलत्वादित्यर्थः।

“Hence, all beings, who are sāttvika, rājasa and tāmasa, follow svabhāva, just as a twig follows wind. In that case what can control, be it of the form of teaching, or sensory control do? Because Prakṛti is powerful.”

The aforementioned excerpts from the commentaries are clear about one idea, i.e., the physical activities (including mental and intellectual) involuntarily happen according to one’s own previous saṃskāra or vāsanā. So if karmas of varṇas are divided according to svabhāva alias Prakṛti, they are divided for each varṇa according to their natural tendencies. The persons of a varṇa cannot naturally act otherwise, unless deluded for a while, just like Arjuna. Hence, the varṇadharma is not some ruling forced upon humans of different varṇas, but is rather a regulated system in accordance with their natural abilities (mental and physical), following which one shall attain Puruśārtha.

Bhagavadgītā 3.33 bears some similarity of idea mentioned in Manusmṛti 1.30, which has been cited earlier in this article.

In verse 18.60 of Bhagavadgītā, Kṛṣṇa says to Arjuna that although he is refusing to perform his Kṣatriya dharma due to moha, he shall be made to do it through the force of prakṛti. The verse and its translation by Gita Press is as follows:

स्वभावजेन कौन्तेय निबद्धः स्वेन कर्मणा।

कर्तुं नेच्छसि यन्मोहात्करिष्यस्यवशोऽपि तत्।।

That action, too, which you are not willing to undertake through ignorance you will perforce perform, bound by your own duty born of your nature.

What Vedānta Deśika notes, in his sub-commentary on this verse, is worth noticing. He says:

शौर्यं निर्भयप्रवेशसामर्थ्यं तेन तन्मूलं कर्मात्र वासनावशाद्रुचिविषयतया बन्धकत्वेनोक्तम्

He says that śaurya etc., which have been implied to be Arjuna’s svābhāvika karma, are the objects of Arjuna’s interest because of vāsanā. Hence, in all normal conditions, Arjuna would be naturally interested in expressing śaurya, etc.

The reason why Arjuna would perforce perform his svābhāvika karma is stated in the next verse, i.e., 18.61. The verse along with the Gita Press’ translation is as follows:

ईश्वरः सर्वभूतानां हृद्देशेऽर्जुन तिष्ठति।

भ्रामयन्सर्वभूतानि यन्त्रारूढानि मायया।।

Arjuna, God abides in the hearts of all creatures, causing them to revolve according to their Karma by his illusive power (Māyā), as though mounted on a machine.

On this Vedānta Deśika says:

नहि जीवमीश्वरो भूतावेशन्यायेन प्रवर्तयति अपितु सत्त्वादिगुणमयान् भावान् पुरस्कृत्य पूर्वसिद्धवासनाविशेषजनितसङ्गद्वारेणेति

He says that Īśvara does not impel the Jīva by possessing him like a ghost, but by making entities made of sattva guṇa, rajas guṇa and tamas guṇa at the forefront and connecting the Jīva with them through its vāsanā of the past life.

This also makes it clear that one is naturally impelled to perform certain actions, which are one’s svābhāvika karmas, against which one should not go. Even though one may feel like not doing one’s svābhāvika karmas due to ignorance, one will inevitably end up doing those very karmas being impelled by Īśvara via his Māyā.

This also is consistent with the ideas in Manusmṛti about beings taking up their respective lines of actions naturally, and about finding the jāti of a person born to parents of different varṇas, by looking at what profession they choose even when they are not known to be an offspring of such parents.

From all the data cited in this article, I draw the following conclusions:

- Varṇa by birth technically means varṇa by natural tendencies (śīla),
  where the latter is said to become fixed at birth.
- One’s śīla comes first, and action happens in accordance with it. So
  changing one’s varṇa by action would require a miracle, at the least.
- One may be mistaken in identifying their actual varṇa, which may be
  different from the varṇa by which their parents and ancestral lineage
  are known.
- Keeping in view the prevalence of mixed-varṇa marriage since a really
  long time, and keeping in view the issue of a lineage practising
  āpaddharma even in the absence of āpat, one cannot simply take one’s
  popularly known varṇa to be their actual varṇa.
- Statements of behavioural characteristics of each varṇa cannot be
  simply taken to be a mere emphasis on good conduct.
- Bhagavadgītā provides ample data to help conduct a basic investigation
  of one’s actual varṇa.
- Finding one’s actual varṇa and performing the actions prescribed to it
  by Bhagavadgītā and other Śāstras, shall provide both personal and
  societal stability.
- Marrying a person with similar actual varṇa (svabhāva/śīla) could lead
  to a better and stable marriage and the offspring resulting from the
  marriage shall be equal in svabhāva to both. Such a practice could
  lead to better predictability of behaviour and abilities even after
  generations.
- Śāstra does not start a societal practice from scratch, but formalises
  and regulates an already existent societal practice.
- Performing the karma prescribed by the Śāstra based on one’s varṇa,
  which is basically one’s natural characteristics, one can reach the
  highest goal of Puruṣārtha viz., mukti.
