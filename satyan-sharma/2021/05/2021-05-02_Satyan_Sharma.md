Thank you for the response. It is true that shraddha plays a very important role in the life of a shishya. I also have realised that these great sages do not care about whether their views conform to the society or not. All they care about is the truth and due to kripa, they communicate it to us laypersons. I have tried to be very careful in reaching conclusions here, and tried my best to not fall prey to my biases.

It is further interesting to find an ancient recorded interpretation of Purushasukta by a great sage like Yajnavalkya, and that instead of deriving a hierarchy out of it, he derived unity.



I agree that for a mumukshu, it does not at all matter where the knowledge comes from. A traditional commentary on the Manishapanchaka has clarified that the incident of Adi Shankaracharya and Chandala happened so that those around Adi Shankaracharya gave up their caste pride in such matters.
