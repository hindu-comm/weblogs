# Could Śūdras read Vedas?

As per Śāstras, a Śūdra is not eligible for the study of Vedas. In this previous statement, ‘the study of Vedas’ is the translated version of the original Sanskrit expression ‘vedādhyayana’ or the adhyayana of Vedas. To understand the ineligibility of Śūdra in the said case, we would need to first understand as to what counts and what does not count as adhyayana of Vedas. As the erstwhile pontiff of the Śriṅgeri Pīṭha Śrīmad Abhinava Vidyātīrtha Svāmī has opined in the 23rd chapter of his book ‘Exalting Elucidations’, “Vedādhyayana is characterised by a śiṣya listening to his Guru reciting the Veda and then repeating the same.” He further says, “It is not learning with the aid of recorded sound.” In chapter 24 of the same book, he cites Kumārila Bhaṭṭa to say that all study of the Veda is preceded by learning from a Guru (vedasyādhyayanaṃ sarvaṃ gurvadhyayanapūrvakaṃ).

The 14th century Vedic scholar Sāyaṇācārya, in his Ṛgvedabhāṣyabhūmikā says that the adhyayanavidhi (svādhyāyo’dhyetavyaḥ) expresses that Vedic study (svādhyāya) becomes sanctified by adhyayana through the exclusion of other methods of study like reading books, etc. (*adhyayanavidhiśca likhitapāṭhādivyāvṛttyā adhyayanasaṃskṛtatvaṃ svādhyāyasya gamayati*). Śrīmad Abhinava Vidyātīrtha Svāmī excluded the learning of Veda from audio recordings, because the questioner asked about it directly. Sāyāṇācārya’s statement that adhyayanavidhi excludes reading of books, etc. for Vedic study can be combined with Śrīmad Abhinava Vidyātīrtha Svāmī’s statement of exclusion of audio recordings. Hence, methods other than the formally learning of Vedas from a Guru, via Upanayana saṃskāra etc., is not recognised as adhyayana of Vedas.

What does this imply? It implies that there are two ways of studying the Vedas; one is learning it from a Guru via Upanayana, etc., and the other is learning it through reading books, audio recordings, etc. The former is termed as adhyayana of Vedas, whereas the latter is not. This is why in his Bhāmatī commentary on Brahmasūtra-Śāṅkarabhāṣya (1.3.34), Vācaspatimiśra considers the option of Śūdras learning Vedas via reading books, etc. and hence him becoming eligible for Brahmavidyā. This option is however refuted by the commentator, not by citing scriptural passages prohibiting application of such methods, but by stating that such methods wouldn’t lead Śūdras to receive the fruits of adhyayana, namely abhyudaya and niḥśreyasa. Such fruits shall be received only by those who study Vedas via Upanayana, etc. (*pustakādipaṭhitasvādhyāyajanyo’rthāvabodho na śūdrāṇāṃ phalāya kalpate*)

On the same passage of Brahmasūtra-Śāṅkarabhāṣya, Ānandagiri comments that although sin would be incurred on the part of a Śūdra studying Vedas via reading books, etc., he would still do it because the knowledge gained by it would destroy that sin (*na ca niṣiddhādhyayanād duritodayabhayānna pravartate likhitapāṭhādijanitavidyayā tannibarhaṇāt*). He too does not cite any passages from scriptures prohibiting such an endeavour by the Śūdra. In Rāmānanda Sarasvatī’s commentary on Brahmasūtra (1.3.34), the same option has been considered. Its effectiveness towards brahmavidyā has been rejected by saying that only the Vedic adhyayana which is done with the Guru is effective towards brahmavidyā (*na ca śūdrasyāpi likhitapāṭhādinā vedaprāptiriti yuktam/ gurūpasadanapūrvakādhyayanasaṃskṛtavedasyaiva brahmavidyādyupakārakatvāt*). Here too, a Śūdra’s study of Vedas via reading books, etc. has not been said to be prohibited or punishable as per some scripture, but is merely rejected from the point of view of its effectiveness.



You may ask about the punishment or expiation regarding a Śūdra learning Vedas as mentioned in Gautamadharmasūtra (2.3.4 or 12.4). The commentaries of Maskari and Haradattamiśra on that specific passage throw some light on how to interpret it. The word ‘Veda’ in the said passage of Gautamadharmasūtra is understood by Maskari as the ‘Veda along with its supporting limbs’ (*vedaṃ sāṅgam*). First of all, such a study or learning of the Veda would have to be done via hearing it (*upaśrutya*). The word ‘udāharaṇa’ is understood by both Maskari and Haradattamiśra to be ‘reciting Vedas along with Dvijas’. The word ‘dhāraṇa’ is understood by both to be ‘reciting Veda independently (of Dvijas)’. The purview of this passage is the hearing of Vedas’ recital from a person with a desire to learn Vedas, and the recital of Vedas. It seems, that because of this line of interpretation, at least a section of learned Vedic scholars like Vācaspatimiśra, Ānandagiri and Rāmānanda Sarasvatī did not mention that a Śūdra performing study of Vedas via reading books, etc. is prohibited. They seem to simply take it as an open option, which is not prohibited as such. They reject it only from the point of view of its effectiveness. It is also worth noticing that they do not refute it also by saying that Śūdras aren’t educated enough and hence cannot learn Vedas even by reading books.

Aśvaghoṣa in his Vajrasūcī, mentions that there were Śūdras who were learned in Vedas (dṛśyante ca kvacit śūdrā api vedavyākaraṇamīmāṃsāsāṃkhyavaiśeṣikalagnājīvakādisarvaśāstrārthavidaḥ). It could be that they learnt Vedas via reading books, etc. This could also be the reason why Bhāsarvajña in his Nyāyabhūṣaṇa rejects the idea that Śūdras learnt scriptures of Jainas, etc. because they weren’t allowed adhyayana of Vedas, although they desired it because they knew of the positive effect it had on Dvijas. It seems that he took it for granted that they could access the Vedas from books, etc., and mere exclusion from adhyayana (systematic study of Vedas from a Guru via Upanayana, etc.) does not mean a complete ban on accessing Vedas. This is why he cites another reason for Śūdras (and others) learning the scriptures of Jainas, etc. The reason he gives is that there is no rule regarding adhyayana and adhyāpana of those scriptures.

Such being the case, it seems that at least some of the ancient and medieval scholars of Vedas and other Śāstras thought that a Śūdra could study Vedas via reading books, etc. and that such a method was not scripturally punishable or banned.

*Addendum* : Certain old traditional scholars like Ānandagiri, Rāghavānanda, Advaitānanda, Rāmakiṅkara, Śrīdeva and Appayya Dīkṣita take the so-called punishment of Gautamadharmasūtra (2.3.4 or 12.4) to be prāyaścitta (expiation) and not a punishment. It means that the so-called punishment merely tells that a Śūdra should not indulge in traditional Vedādhyayana, or else he would incur a great sin. Hence, these traditional and chronologically old scholars, did not see it as a punishment at all.

References to the statement of these scholars:

Ānandagiri (on Ādi Śaṅkarācārya’s comm. on Brahmasūtra 1.3.38), Rāghavānanda (on Manusmṛti 1.91), Advaitānanda (Brahmavidyābharaṇa 1.3.38), Rāmakiṅkara (Brahmāmṛtavarṣiṇī 1.3.38), Śrīdeva (Kātīyaśrautasūtra 1.1.6), Appayya Dīkṣita (Nyāyarakṣāmaṇi 1.3.38).
