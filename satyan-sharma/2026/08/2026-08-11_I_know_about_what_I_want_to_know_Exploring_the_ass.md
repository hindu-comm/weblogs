To know that one does not know about a certain thing in a certain way, is the door to the quest for truth. To assume about the manner in which an unknown thing exists, is not looked upon as a good act. In this article I argue that both curiosity and assumptions go hand in hand, and that without assumptions, one cannot even begin to ask questions about things of the higher order, like time, space and fundamental reality. Hence, I shall suggest, through my arguments, that in order to be curious and ask questions about such things, assumption is a prerequisite. For those who may not feel good about this, I have equated curiosity and the act of questioning with a sport activity.

**What is curiosity?**

To be curious about a thing has two sides, one is to know that you do not know the thing in its entirety, second is to desire to know it in that manner. The first is the prerequisite for the second. The desire to know could be an effect of various causes. I could desire to know because of some promised or probable reward which the said knowledge could lead me to. I could also desire to know because to not know a particular thing is a situation I do not want myself to be in. This could be for a variety of reasons. I may think that I must be a knowledgeable person, and not knowing something in its entirety puts a question mark on my personality. I may also think that not knowing something in its entirety equals not knowing the thing at all. There could be other reasons as well. The stated as well as unstated reasons must culminate into the desire for knowing something in its entirety, or even knowing some other or newer aspects of it, in order to become something what we could call curiosity. I am not going to explore the second side anymore. I am much more concerned with the first. How do I know that I do not know something in its entirety? This is what I will be exploring now.

**Division of the whole world into two categories**

I divide everything that exists in the world into two categories; knowable and unknowable. The first, that is, knowable, can be either known or unknown, whereas the second will forever be unknown. To understand what knowable and unknowable means, let us briefly look into what it means to know. Getting down to our routine experiences, we will find that to know something is to be aware of its existence and the manner in which it exists or has existed. For example, you have moved to a completely new town, a lot of areas of which you are not aware of. Ten old residents of the town tell you about a unique tree which exists in some corner of it, which you do not know about. You already know what a tree is, generally, but have no awareness about this unique tree. Now you are aware of its existence, because the old residents have told you about it. But you still do not know what is so unique about this tree. One day one of them leads you to the said tree. Now you see it in front of you, adorned with the uniqueness. This is the second part of knowing, that is, knowing exactly the manner in which the thing exists.



The tree is a knowable thing because you can be aware about both its existence in general, as well as the specific manner in which it exists. The temporal distance between both the general and specific awarenesses may be short or long. A building, a human, an animal, so on and so forth about which you can gain both kinds of awareness are knowable. These knowables are known to possess general and specific attributes or features, where the former unites various knowables into one category, whereas the latter differentiates one knowable from another knowable belonging to the same category. Take the example of a tree again. The word ‘tree’ points to something which is has a trunk, has branches, has leaves, gives shade, stands inside muddy ground, so on and so forth. These are the general attributes of what we call trees. One could view one attribute at one time, and the other attribute at another time. Then come the specific attributes, such as what kind of trunk, leaves, fruit, what species, specific location, so on and so forth. One may say that a specific location in which a specific tree exists, is not the attribute of the tree, because it is not inherent in the tree. The reason why I included it in the attributes, is because it acts like one, that is it differentiates the specific tree from all others, general and specific. I could further specify that attributes could be both internal and external, where the latter could include specific space and time.

**What about the attributes themselves?**

If a thing is to be specifically known on the basis of its attributes, both internal and external, we must first talk about the attributes themselves. If time is also a specifying attribute of a knowable thing, what is time? Is it the time which we see or infer through the clocks and watches? Or is it what the physicists talk about when they say that time is a dimension or whatever? Or is it both? Or is it neither? What is time really? This is the question which I will now analyse. It goes without saying that I cannot see time, the way I see the tree. I cannot also experience time like I experience happiness. Time, whatever it may be, is not directly available to my experience. So what do I mean when I ask ‘what is time really’? The first dimension of the question, I think is that I am expressing a doubt regarding whatever I have been told or I have understood about time upto this point of time. The second dimension of it would be to accept that if there is time, I do not know it in its entirety. The third dimension would be that I desire to know it in its entirety.

**Asking about what ‘time’ really is**

I know the word ‘time’ and what all it signifies in the popular usage. When someone asks me, “what time is it?”, I do not get confused what the person is asking or he wants to know. I look at my watch and tell him the time being shown in it. I asked the question about the reality of time because I am sure that this kind of time is not the entire story of time. Why? Because the time being shown in the watch differs due to various reasons. If it were time, we would have had times and not a singular time. This is another point which is to be addressed, that is, singularity of time. Why do I believe time to be singular? May be because the popular usage of it is based on and expresses its singularity. Yet its popular understanding and usage has not satisfied me. I do not even know if the popular usage of the word ‘time’ signifies the so-called ‘real time’ or not. Further, having not known time in its entirety, how do I know that I know it only partially? Not only this, to ask about how time exists really, implies that there is an inherent way in which it exists, whereas it appears in another manner, the way in which I know it. But how do I know all of this? I think, I do not know.

To be able to assume so much about a thing like time, can be explained in the way of superimposition. I think that time is like a tree or something like that. There are various parts of it, some of which I know, some of which I do not. I have a habit of understanding a certain thing, in a manner through which I had understood a completely different thing. Not just trees, other objects which I can make myself aware of through my sense organs, act as a ground for me to ask and understand about things which are not within the purview of my sensory capacity. Such is with the case of my curiosity with regards to the reality of time. I think of it as having various attributes, various parts, just like a tree, or box, so on and so forth. If such is the case, am I asking a correct or justifiable question? The time, about which I am asking the question, has been already defined by my own self, in a way. The time I am asking about is singular, has multiple attributes, exists in a certain manner but appears in another, and last but not the least, it is fully knowable.

In this way, the object of my question is not what time really is, but the time I believe exists. So, it is not an idealistically correct question, because I have assumed a lot, even before knowing about time. But is it wrong to ask such a question? Both the philosophical and scientific pursuits of truth are based on such questions. I cannot ask such a question without assuming a lot of things. I will have to assume, in order to know the truth, if it at all is knowable. If being curious, being inquisitive, are good qualities, how could the prerequisite assumptions be wrong? Although this line of thinking, if true, may have undesirable repercussions on the truth of our understanding of the fundamental reality, which is called fundamental because it is thought that its appearance is different from the manner in which it actually exists. Let there be those repercussions. I can see this act of myself assuming and asking questions about things which are not completely knowable, in the sense stated earlier, like a game or sport. Any kind of sports has to have some ground rules, in the absence of which it cannot be played. Although we know that we have cooked up those rules, they are fictional, yet the emotional experience we have when we play the game, is not fictional. I for one, will keep asking questions about things such as time, space, fundamental reality, because I see it as a good indoor sport. Making the rules before the actual game begins, is not unfair. Assuming before enquiring is a necessity, but should definitely not be overdone. I would not like to watch a sport event where the result is pre-determined.
