In my previous [post](/vedic-roots-of-minimalism-in-knowledge-1c939a4f4fb8), I talked about the Vedic roots of knowledge-minimalism, which favours only purpose-oriented practical knowledge.

This is reflected in textual minimalism.

Textual minimalism is about writing only that which is necessary.

*A smart writer composes the text and a smart reader understands it.*

Indian scholars have favoured this style by authoring two kinds of texts : i) ultra-brief, and ii) short.

Ultra-brief texts are in the sūtra-style. Sūtra is a brief yet comprehensive sentence. This style was popular in very ancient times.

Following are a few of such texts:

Aṣṭādhyāyī by Pāṇiṇi.

Mīmāṃsāsūtras by Jaimini.

Brahmasūtras by Bādarāyaṇa.



and so forth.

When people could not understand such texts directly, the tradition of commentaries and detailed texts began.

Reading the huge corpus of such texts was time-consuming, and still is.

Hence short texts were created, which contained only essential information.

Following are a few of such texts:

Nyāyasāra by Bhāsarvajña.

Vedāntasāra by Sadānanda.

Sāṅkhyasāra by Vijñānabhikṣu.

Tarkasaṅgraha by Annaṃbhaṭṭa.

and so forth.

*Specific purpose → Specific communication*
