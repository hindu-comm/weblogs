The right to choose rituals and pills  
2026-09-21  
Source: [here](https://satyan-sharma.medium.com/the-right-to-choose-rituals-and-pills-ad3845b90de2)

If you are not a physician, it is strongly advised that you yourself must not choose medicines to treat your disease. You could mistake the complexity of a medicine with its efficacy and reject an efficacious yet simple medicine. A physician may prescribe you a pill, or may not even prescribe one. He or she may simply tell you to make certain changes in your daily routine, following which you will be benefitted. The end result is benefit and everything else works for producing it. A physician uses the related scientific database to ascertain the kind of treatment which will benefit a specific patient who suffers from a specific disease.

In the field of rituals, the ritual database is used to ascertain which ritual will produce a certain benefit for a certain kind of a person. If one believes that rituals work, one would also believe that the ritual database, which mentions them, is true. This database is found in the form of various texts. In Sanātana Dharma, texts such as Vedas, Brāhmaṇas, Āraṇyakas, Kalpasūtras, etc. are the database of this sort. Experts in the field of rituals ascertain how a particular ritual mentioned in the database with all its nuances and details is to be performed in order to achieve the desired result. You may yourself aspire to become such an expert, for which you will have to spend years in training and practice. You may also want to spend a lot of your time in trial and error. There are people who value journey more than the destination. For others, it is better to invest their time and effort in activities which would surely produce the desired results for them. In this case, they should consult an expert. It is also possible that an expert could give you the wrong advice, due to which you would fail to attain the desired result. Still it would be better to get a second opinion from another expert, than to indulge in trial and error.

Another way is to perform such rituals which work for each and everyone. Just as the ritual database mentions complex rituals, it also mentions simple ones which will produce the desired result for all those who perform them. For instance, the database mentions a simple and powerful ritual, i.e., chanting the names of Bhagavān. From Vedas to Purāṇas, the database is replete with statements which promote this ritual and praise its efficacy. If you believe that the ritual database is true, you will have no doubt about the efficacy of this ritual and will perform it. If you doubt this, why do you not doubt the entire database and give up the desire to perform any ritual whatsoever?

If your physician advises you to make certain dietary changes instead of prescribing you a pill, do you fight for your right to consume a pill? If you doubt the efficacy of those dietary changes, which are based on the same scientific database as is the pill, why do you not doubt the entire scientific database and give up the desire to take any pill whatsoever? Even if your physician prescribes you a pill, do you tell him or her to prescribe you the pill which you like and demand that the prescription be changed? In case the physician’s advice does not lead to the desired result for you, do you consult another physician or indulge in trial and error? Ultimately, we have to ask ourselves this: am I really seeking a solution to my problem, or merely pretending to do so? The one who really seeks a solution to his or her problem, assumes the role of a learner and humbly asks the experts for it. Even if the experts are not within reach, one would resort to solutions which have been accepted by the experts as generally and easily applicable for all. Honesty and humbleness pave the way for long-lasting benefits.
