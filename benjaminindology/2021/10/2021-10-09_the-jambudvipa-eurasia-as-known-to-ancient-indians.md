Origin of Mahabharata – Tracing the Evolution of An Oral Epic

Did the ancient Indians map out Eurasia? Ancient Indians used the term ‘Jambudvipa’ to describe Eurasia. Jambudvipa, based on Puranic texts, may be mapped out as follows:

![](https://benjaminindology.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/jambudvipa-identified.jpg?w=1024)

At the center is the mount Meru, located in the region called Ilavrita-varsha, corresponding to parts of Kyrgyzstan/Tajikistan. The region is bounded to the north by Nila mountains (Almaty region of Kazakhstan), to the east by Gandhamadana mountains (Tian Shan), to the south by Nishadha mountains (Kunlun), and to the west by Malyavan mountains (Pamir).

Four rivers emerge from Meru, and flow in the four cardinal directions all the way to the salt sea. The northern river is Bhadra (Ob-Irtysh), the eastern is Sita (Aksu-Tarim), the southern is Alakananda (Ganga) and the western is Vankshu (Oxus).

It is to be noted that Meru is a mythical mountain. The four rivers do not originate from Meru, but from the mountains that surround it. The Vankshu originates from Malyavan, Sita from Gandhamadana, Alakananda from Himalaya and the Bhadra from Shveta.

The region bounded by Gandhamadana (Tian Shan) and the eastern sea is known as Bhadrashva-varsha, corresponding to the Xinjiang. The region bounded by Malyavan (Pamir) and the western sea is known as Ketumala-varsha.

Further south of Nishadha (Kunlun) mountains are the Hemakuta (Karakoram) mountains, and these two comprise the boundary of Hari-varsha (Tibet). Further south of Hemakuta mountains are the Himalayas. These two comprise the boundary of Kimpurusha-varsha (Gilgit-Baltistan). The land bounded by the Himalayas and the southern ocean is Bharata-varsha (India).

North of Nila mountains (of Almaty region) are the Shveta (Altai) mountains, and these two comprise the boundary of Ramyaka-varsha (East Kazakhstan Region). North of the Shveta mountains are the Shringavan (Ural) mountains, and these two comprise the boundary of Hiranmaya-varsha (Western Siberia). Beyond the Shringavan (Ural) mountains, lies the region of Kuru-varsha (Eastern Europe), bounded by the northern sea.
