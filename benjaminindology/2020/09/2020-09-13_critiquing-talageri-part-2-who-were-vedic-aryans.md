Critiquing Talageri: Why he gets almost everything wrong

This will be Part 2 of my critique of Talageri. I will now tackle another hypothesis of Talageri:  
  
***And, in fact, the particular Vedic Aryans of the Rigveda were one section among these PUrus, who called themselves Bharatas.*** (Chapter 5, THE RIGVEDA – A Historical Analysis)

Talageri seems to assert that “Vedic Aryans” were only Bharatas. All other tribes and peoples, therefore, cannot be called “Vedic Aryans”. To begin with, the word ‘Aryan’ does not appear in the Rig Veda at all. It uses the word ‘Ārya’.

According to the Rig Veda, the descendants of Manu include “five peoples”:

***What (manly power) is in the midspace, what in heaven, what through the five peoples of Manu, confer that manly power (on us), o Aśvins. (RV, VIII.9.2)***

In the above verse, it is made clear that people descended from Manu included all the five peoples, and not just Pūrus. It is generally agreed that the five peoples included Anu, Druhyu, Yadu, Turvaśa and Pūru. So what has this got to do with Ārya? Everything! Rig Veda consistently identifies Āryas with the peoples of Manu. Here are a few samples:

***You did the gods beget as god, Vaiśvānara, as light just for the Ārya (I.59.2)  
…  
You are king of the communities belonging to the sons of Manu. (I.59.5)***

***O Aśvins, wondrous ones, scattering barley by a “wolf”, milking out refreshment for the race of Manu, blasting sound at the Dasyu with a bagpipe, you made wide light for the Ārya. (i.117.21)***  

***Indra aided the Ārya sacrificer in battles, affording a hundred forms of help in all contests—in contests whose prize is the sun. Chastising those who follow no commandment, he made the black skin \[tvácaṃ kr̥ṣṇā́m\] subject to Manu. (i.130.8)***

***He \[…\] alone became the victorious rouser of the communities of the sons of Manu. It was just you who tamed the Dasyus, and who alone vanquished (their) communities for the Ārya. (VI.18.2)***

From the above references, it is clear that Ārya is a word applied to “sons of Manu”, which includes “five peoples”: Anu, Druhyu, Yadu, Turvaśa and Pūru.

Nor it is the case that only Pūru were important to Rigvedic priests. There are many instances of other peoples being praised, lauded, sacrificed for, and wished well for. I have tried to prepare an exhaustive list for all the friendly references to other peoples. These are mostly focused on Yadu and Turvaśa (in 16 verses from different hymns). Yadus and Turvaśas were allied with Kaṇvas. Two friendly references to Anu are also found. There are none in the case of Druhyus. The list of these references can be found at the end of the post.

Furthermore, in Book 6 of Rig Veda, which Talageri identifies as the oldest, there a reference where Bharadvaja supports Turvaśa against Divodāsa!  
  
***This deed done by you stands out today—that for his \[Tūrvayāṇa’s\] sake (you ground down) Kutsa, Āyu, and Atithigva; many thousands did you grind down to earth. You led Tūrvayāṇa up boldly. (VI.18.13)***

In the above verse, Atithigva refers to Divodāsa. It says that “many thousands” (including Divodāsa) were ground down by Indra for Tūrvayāṇa. As the name suggests, Tūrvayāṇa is likely to be a Turvaśa king. This would also explain why Bharadvaja wishes for the well-being of Yadu and Turvasa in VI.20.12, despite having been hostile to them in past (VI.27.7).

**List of Friendly references to Yadu, Turvaśa and Anu in Rig Veda:**

I.36.18, Kaṇva Ghaura, “With Agni we call Turvaśa and Yadu from afar, also Ugrādeva.”

I.54.6, Savya Āṅgirasa, “You helped the manly Turvaśa and Yadu”

I.174.9, Agastya Maitrāvaruṇi, “When you (Indra) will cross the sea, o champion, make Turvaśa and Yadu cross to well-being.”

IV.30.17, Vāmadeva Gautama, “And these two, Turvaśa and Yadu, who could not swim, did the lord of powers, the knowing Indra, bring to the far shore.”

V.31.4, Avasyu Ātreya, “The Anu people fashioned a chariot for your (Indra’s) horse”

V.31.8, Avasyu Ātreya, “You brought the waters, the good milkers, to rest for Yadu and Turvaśa, (when you were their) transport, Indra.”

VI.20.12, Bharadvāja Bārhaspatya, “When you will cross the sea, o hero, make Turvaśa and Yadu cross to well-being.”

VI.45.1, Śaṃyu Bārhaspatya, “Who with good leading led Turvaśa and Yadu here from afar, he is Indra, our youthful comrade.”

VIII.1.31, Kāṇvas, “When with trust in the winning horses I mount on the chariot, of the valuable goods what will stand out is the livestock coming from Yadu.”

VIII.4.7, Devātithi Kāṇva, “A great deed of you the bull should be witnessed. Might we see Turvaśa and Yadu.”

VIII.4.19, Devātithi Kāṇva, “At the gifts of the vibrant king (Kuruṅga), bestowing good fortune, we thought ourselves to be among the Turvaśas.”

VIII.6.46, Vatsa Kāṇva, “At Tirindira’s I received a hundred and at Parśu’s a thousand as the bounties of the Yāduvas.”

VIII.6.48, Vatsa Kāṇva, “The prominent one has reached up to heaven, giving camels yoked in fours, and by his fame (has reached) the Yāduva people.”

VIII.7.18, Punarvatsa Kāṇva, “With which (aid) you aided Turvaśa and Yadu, with which (you aided) Kaṇva, who gained the stakes, may we receive (a portion) of this for wealth.”

VIII.9.14, “Here are oblations set out for you; here are soma-drinks for you at Turvaśa’s and Yadu’s (sacrifice) and here also among the Kaṇvas.”

VIII.45.27, Triśoka Kāṇva, “This is real: having obtained at Turvaśa’s and Yadu’s what is not to be spurned \[=soma\], he came through to victory by his labor.”

VIII.74.4, Gopavana Ātreya, “We have come to the best smasher of obstacles, preeminent Agni belonging to the Anu, before whose face lofty Śrutarvan, the son of R̥kṣa, flares up”

X.49.8, Indra, “I made Turvaśa and Yadu to be famed through my power.”

X.62.10, Nābhānediṣṭha Mānava, “And also two barbarian-slaves to perform service, each with his allotted (gear), along with an abundance of cows, have Yadu and Turva (each) magnanimously bestowed.”
