Does Brahman have a default form?

In the Srimadbhagavatam there is the instruction for the practice of dhaarana, dhyana and samadhi culminating in the realization of the ultimate Truth. The verse in consideration, for samadhi, is:

2.1.10:

मनो निर्विषयं युक्त्वा ततः किञ्चन न स्मरेत् ।

*पदं तत्परमं विष्णोः मनो यत्र प्रसीदति*॥ १९ ॥

\[ Having made the mind free of any objectification, one should not think of anything (else). This is the supreme state of Vishnu, where the mind attains tranquility.\]

Sridhara Swamin says: समाधिमाह – निर्विषयं मनो युंक्त्वा समाधाय स्थिरीभूते मनसि स्फुरत् परमानन्दमात्राकारं कृत्वेत्यर्थः \| प्रसीदति उपशाम्यति \|

\[samadhi is being stated: the mind is to be made free of all objectification and once there is firmness in this state, the supreme bliss manifests in it. Then the mind subsides.\]

We find a similar verse/practice in the Bh.Gita 6.25:

शनैः शनैरुपरमेद्बुद्ध्या धृतिगृहीतया।  
आत्मसंस्थं मनः कृत्वा न किञ्चिदपिचिन्तयेत्॥ २५ ॥

[](http://advaitasharada.sringeri.net/display/bhashya/Gita?page=6&id=BG_C06_V25&hlBhashya=%E0%A4%9A%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%A8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%AF%E0%A5%87%E0%A4%A4%E0%A5%8D#bhashya-BG_C06_V25)

शनैः शनैः न सहसा उपरमेत् उपरतिं कुर्यात्। कया ? बुद्ध्या। किंविशिष्टया ? धृतिगृहीतया धृत्या धैर्येण गृहीतया धृतिगृहीतया धैर्येण युक्तया इत्यर्थः। आत्मसंस्थम् आत्मनि संस्थितम् ‘आत्मैव सर्वं न ततोऽन्यत् किञ्चिदस्ति’ इत्येवमात्मसंस्थं मनः कृत्वा न किञ्चिदपिचिन्तयेत्।एष योगस्य परमो विधिः ॥ २५ ॥

Shankara too says that the mind must be freed of all objectification and the thought ‘Atman alone is everything; there is naught other than that.’ This is the pinnacle of Yoga.

Hemadri, in his commentary to the Muktaaphalam of Vopadeva, for the expression*पदं तत्परमं विष्णोः* of the verse of the Srimadbhaagavatam says:

पदं विष्णोः निराकारस्य परमं पदं स्फूर्तिस्थानम् \| …प्रसीदति सत्त्वमात्रशेषमवतिष्ठते \|
….असम्प्रज्ञातसमाधिना*निर्विशेषचैतन्यलाभः \|*…पुरुषः स्वरूपं प्रविष्टः शुद्धः केवलो मुक्त इत्युच्यते \|

The above words of Hemadri are no different from what Shankara has said for the Kathopanishad mantra:

विज्ञानसारथिर्यस्तु मनःप्रग्रहवान्नरः।  
सोऽध्वनः पारमाप्नोति तद्विष्णोः परमं पदम् ॥ ९ ॥

किं तत्पदमित्याह — विज्ञानसारथिः यस्तु यो विवेकबुद्धिसारथिः पूर्वोक्तः मनःप्रग्रहवान् प्रगृहीतमनाः समाहितचित्तः सन् शुचिर्नरो विद्वान् , सः अध्वनः संसारगतेः पारं परमेव, अधिगन्तव्यमित्येतत् , आप्नोति, मुच्यते सर्वसंसारबन्धनैः। तत् विष्णोःव्यापनशीलस्यब्रह्मणः परमात्मनो वासुदेवाख्यस्य परमं प्रकृष्टं पदं स्थानम् , सतत्त्वमित्येतत् , यत् असावाप्नोति विद्वान् ॥

While Shankara has said ‘vyapanashiila’ (all-pervading) for the term ‘Vishnu’, Hemadri says ‘niraakaara’ (formless) for the same word in the Bhagavatam. This is because, as both contexts, the upanishad and bhagavatam, are about jnanam for moksha, it cannot be of any deity. If there is a default form for Brahman, it cannot be ananta, infinite.

Ayyanna Dikshita of 17CE has in his book ‘vyasa tatparya nirnaya’ said that if there is a default form for Brahman, it cannot be said to be ananta, all-pervading for there is always a contour for a form that defines, limits, the entity, and the entity cannot be said to occupy the space that is outside the form/contour.

Hemadri of the 13 CE reflects this idea in his commentary to the Muktaphalam:

p.8: अनवच्छिन्ने वस्तुनि अवच्छेदक उपाधिः आकारः \| तेन रहितो निराकारः \| \[In an object that is devoid of limitations, a limiting factor is upaadhi and that is form. That which is without that upadhi is niraakara, formless.\]

Thus, just as Sureshwara too has stated, Shankara, Sridhara Swamin, Hemadri, all admit that the Brahman of the Vedanta is not any deity by default and that such Brahman is formless by default. A form is assumed only for upasana purposes and such a form is not by default any particular form. The Yogasutra ‘yathaabhimata dhyaanaad vaa’ \[Samadhi is attained by concentrating on a form of one’s liking/where one’s mind finds rest.\] This is taken further to culminate in the asamprajnata samadhi where even the form is given up and the formless alone remains. And this is not any saguna entity but nirvishesha chaitanyam, as Hemadri explicitly states. Shankara has dealt with the ‘saakaara and niraakara’ aspects in the BSB 3.2.14, etc. and shown that the description of Brahman as endowed with form is for upasana purpose and those passages that teach Brahman as bereft of form are for jnana, enlightenment, purpose. And that the passages about Brahman being formless alone are the true purport of the teaching of Brahman.

Om Tat Sat
