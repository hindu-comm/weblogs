Nididhyasanam on Brahman and the resultant ‘sarvaatma bhaava’ in the Kaivalyopanishad.

The Kaivalyopanishad is of the Atharva Veda. It has two known ancient commentaries of: Sri Narayanashrama (a relatively short one) and Sri Shankarananda, the Guru of Swami Vidyaranya. These two Acharyas belong to the 13 – 14 CE. Swami Amalananda, the famous author of the Kalpataru on Bhamati, belongs to the post-lineage of Sri Narayanashrama.

The Kaivalyopanishad is a short, crisp teaching on the various aspects of Vedanta, with all the necessary aspects. In the sequel is given a selection of the Upanishad, along with the commentaries, on the aspect of nididhyasana on Brahman and the resultant realization of Being the Infinite, Self-of-All Brahman, sarvaatma bhaava.

The first image below is about the Brahman that is to be meditated upon by the aspirant. A rough translation of the mantra 6 of the first section is:

6\. (Who is) unthinkable, unmanifest, of endless forms, the good, the peaceful, Immortal, the origin of the worlds, without beginning, middle, and end, the only one, all-pervading, Consciousness, and Bliss, the formless and the wonderful.

The study of the commentary, in its original, given in the image, will give the best understanding, though.

The second and third images below is of the 7th mantra with the commentary:

7\. Meditating on the highest Lord, allied to Uma, powerful, three-eyed, blue-necked, and tranquil, the holy man reaches Him who is the source of all, the witness of all and is beyond darkness (i.e. Avidya).

\[The commentary brings out the nature of Brahman of this Upanishad. ‘Umaa-sahaayah’ is explained as ‘the one for whom Uma = Brahmavidya, is the consort. This reminds one of the commentary of Shankaracharya for the Kenopanishad mantra where the word ‘Umaa Haimavati’ occur in the context of Indra beholding her: तस्येन्द्रस्य यक्षे भक्तिं बुद्ध्वा विद्या उमारूपिणी प्रादुरभूत्स्त्रीरूपा । Recognizing Indra’s devotion, Vidyaa, of the form of Uma, appeared in front of Indra…..अथवा उमैव हिमवतो दुहिता हैमवती नित्यमेव सर्वज्ञेनेश्वरेण सह वर्तत इति ज्ञातुं समर्थेति = or Uma alone is Haimavati, the daughter of Himavan, who constantly resides with the Omniscient Ishwara…\] These words of Shankara are for the ‘pada bhashya’ of the Kena Upanishad. In the ‘vaakya bhaashya’ Shankara says, for Uma: अभिप्रायोद्बोधहेतुत्वाद्रुद्रपत्नी उमा हैमवतीव बहु शोभमाना विद्यैव । \[She is the consort of Rudra, verily Vidyaa (liberating knowledge).\] There is the gloss of Anandagiri for both the bhashyas of the Kenopanishad.

\[Or Uma sahayam means: as Ardhanaari svarupa, in the left half, as Uma, Bhavani…with her as his consort…\]

The 8th mantra onward the realization of Brahman by the aspirant is described. The fourth and fifth images are of this.

8\. He is Brahma, He is Shiva, He is Indra, He is the Immutable, the Supreme, the Self-luminous, He alone is Vishnu, He is Prana, He is Time and Fire, He is the Moon.

\[The commentary has already identified the pronoun ‘sah’ = ‘he’ of this mantra as the aspirant denoted by the word ‘muni’ in the previous mantra. The muni is the aspirant who has performed the nididhyasanam as per the earlier two mantras. So, this aspirant, upon realization, is spoken of as having attained the self-of-all status, sarvaatma bhaava. He identifies with everything in creation, including gods and celestial entities: Brahmaa, the first-born, Shiva (the commentary says: Umaa sahaaya, the consort of Uma, thereby identifying Shiva with the Umaa sahaaya of the 7th mantra), Indra, ..The aspirant alone is.Vishnu, the holder of conch and discus.

9\. He alone is all that was, and all that will be, the Eternal; knowing Him, one transcends death; there is no other way to freedom.

\[The aspirant realizes himself to be ‘all, everything’, of the past, present and future.\]

The commentator Shankarananda gives a nirguna Brahman interpretation too to the 7th mantra umaa sahaayam…This is given in the sixth image below.

As means to become purified, the Upanishad specifies the constant recitation, japa, of the Sri Rudram. The commentator concludes by saying: Thus, by the meditation on trinetra, the three-eyed and by the chanting of the Sri Rudram…one attains the liberating knowledge and moksha, kaivalya. This is given in the seven, last, image. The commentator has already identified the saguna Ishwara of this Upanishad with Shiva in unmistakable terms as Bhavani pati in the 7th mantra and as ‘umaa sahaayah’ while giving the meaning of ‘shivah’ in the 8th mantra where a list of gods like Indra, Brahma, Vishnu are given. Thus, the Advaitin commentator of the pre-Vidyaranya and pre-Appayya Dikshita period has not ‘converted’ the deity of this Kaivalya Upanishad to any other than Shiva. It goes without saying that anyone, whether Advaitin or other, by attempting a conversion will be going against the ancient sampradaya, the purvacharya, of the Vedanta tradition and thereby will be asampradaya vit.

There are many other sadhana-friendly aspects in this Upanishad that are worthy of a detailed study.

Om Tat Sat
