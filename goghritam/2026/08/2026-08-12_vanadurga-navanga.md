devAmR^ita-varShiNI – देवामृत वर्षिणी

## vanadurgA navA~Nga?

[![](https://goghritam.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/gemini_generated_image_a6o2zna6o2zna6o2-1.png?w=755)](https://goghritam.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/gemini_generated_image_a6o2zna6o2zna6o2-1.png)

There seems to be a widespread fascination with enumerating the *navāṅga-s* (nine limbs) for various Śākta deities, which is a feature particularly prominent in the traditions of Kerala, though likely prevalent in other regions as well. While the actual practice of a *navāṅga-sādhana* must be formally learned under the guidance of an authentic Guru, a brief overview is provided here for **Vanadurgā**.

It is worth noting a technical detail: the source text from which this sequence is drawn does not explicitly mention a *navāṅga*, referring instead only to *ṣaḍaṅga* (six limbs). Nevertheless, without introducing any external elements, we will strictly extract and highlight the nine distinct limbs embedded within the *mūla* text itself, while taking into account two levels/layers of potential objections.

The navA~Nga would be 1) vanadurgA hR^idayaM 2) pUrvA~Nga mantra-s i.e. mantra japa-s that are done prior to the main mUla mantra japa of vanadurgA 3) mUla mantra japa 4) uttArA~Nga mantra-s mantra japa-s done after mUla mantra japa. 5) the nine vanadurgA mAlA mantra-s 6) mantra varNAvalI stavaM 7) kavachaM 8) aShTottaraM 9) sahasranAmaM.

The pUrvA~Nga and uttarA~Nga mantra-s for vanadurgA which were [listed here](https://goghritam.wordpress.com/2023/10/12/durga-sahasranama/) earlier are reproduced below:

“The pUrvA~Nga mantra-s for vanadurgA are guru, gaNapati, vaTuka (or svarNAkarShaNa bhairava in some tradition), mahA-vArAhI and brahmAstra-bagalAmukhI while the uttarA~Nga mantra-s are shiva pa~nchAkSharI, shrI-kR^iShNa mahAmantra (aShTAdashAkShari), mahAlakShmI, nArAyaNa-aShTAkSharI and the last one is daxiNa kAlI (or rAjashyAmalA in some tradition). daxiNA kAlI makes more sense here. The original text specifying the uttarA~Nga mantra-s says shyAmA and clarifies it to be dakShiNa kAlI. There is a record of a slight variation of this tradition, though no known text specifies this. The a~Nga-s are similar to above but have kirAta vArAhI instead of mahAvArAhI, gopAla sundarI instead of kR^iShNa mahAvidyA , baDabAnala-nIlakaNTha-aghorAstra instead of pa~nchAkShari and the famed nArAyaNAstra instead of nArAyaNa aShTAkShari.” — This earlier note missed out on an important pUrvA~Nga mantra which is sharabheshvara, the japa of which is done right after bagalAmukhI and before the main mUla mantra japa of vanadurgA.

If one prefers not to count the *pUrvA~Nga* and *uttarA~Nga* as part of the nine auxiliary steps, citing them as later additions and adding that the focus there is on those specific deities instead of vanadurgA, they might count the *vanadurgA mUrti pa~njara nyAsa* along with the recitation of the long *mahAvana mantra* to arrive at the count of nine. If one is looking for nine a~Nga-s without counting the mUla mantra japa, one might add the shorter vanadurgA AvAhana mAlA mantra to this list and it will be still be nine.

While adhering strictly to the lineage teachings passed down by one’s guru is paramount, special circumstances do arise where deities themselves inspire subtle shifts. These contextual adjustments, when meaningful and effective, are precisely how many of our established contemporary traditions evolved over time. This should not be misunderstood as some free suggestion to replace pUrva and uttara a~Nga-s with pa~njara nyAsa and mahAvana. The suggestion here is only to consider the later two to arrive at the count of nine for those who have the upadesha and enthusiasm. One risk in saying the above is, enthusiastic sAdhaka-s making unauthorized and even harmful changes, claiming them as directives from bhagavatI herself!

Even without additional steps, this sequence remains elaborate and time-intensive, and this is before taking the *vanadurgA pa~nchashatI (or VD saptashati) pArAyaNa* into account. Certain lineages incorporate the *dasha mahA vidyA mantra-s* as part of the *VD pa~nchashati*. Although this may represent a later development, it remains a highly beneficial practice, especially given that two popular *mahAvidyA-s* already function as *a~Nga vidyA-s* of *vanadurgA*. It is noteworthy that these two *Mahāvidyā-s*, often considered diametrically opposed in *prayoga* practice by certain traditions, are seamlessly synthesized under the overarching domain of the supreme *Mahāvidyā*, *Vana-Durgā*.

The aShTottara mentioned below, starts with the nAma **mahAvidyA**. To understand the full context and depth of this nAma of vanadurgA one may re-visit [this note on the mahAvidyA tradition.](https://goghritam.wordpress.com/2020/10/20/two-streams-of-vanadurga-upasana/) This is an important and a defining name for this devata, as the authentic (some Telugu publications enthusiastically give one of the several generic durgA sahasranAma-s as VD 1008) vanadurgA sahasranAma also starts with the nAma **mahAvidyA**. The first shloka of the sahasranAma goes: **mahAvidyA** mahAlaxmIrmahAmahiShavAhanA, mahAdevapriyA… This is further reinforced by the fact that the gAyatri of this particular stream of vanadurgA upAsana also refers to the deity as mahAvidyA with the gAyatri as: oM mahAdevyai cha vidmahe vanadurgi cha dhImahi tanno **mahAvidyA** prachodayAt. The same gAyatri is deployed at multiple places in the tAntrika vanadurgA sandhyA vandana vidhi of this system, along with the mUla mantra.

ईश्वर उवाच ॥

“Listen, my son! I shall declare to you the auspicious hymn (*Stotram*) of Goddess Vanadurga, consisting of 108 names, which destroys all sins and evil deeds.  
राज्यदं पुत्रदं चैव सर्वसौभाग्यवर्धनम्‌ ।  
स्तवसास्य ऋषिर्ब्रह्मा गायत्री छन्द ईरितः ॥

It bestows kingdoms, grants progeny, and enhances all forms of good fortune. For this sacred hymn, Lord Brahma is the Seer (*Rishi*), and *Gayatri* is stated as the Meter (*Chhandas*).  
वनदुर्गा देवता ऽ स्य विनियोगीष्टसिद्धिषु ।  
षट्स्वरैर्न्यास उद्दिष्ट आद्यान्तप्रणवैस्सह ॥

Goddess *Vanadurga* is the presiding Deity (*Devata*), and its ritual invocation (*Viniyoga*) is for the fulfillment of all desired desires and spiritual attainments. The ritual placement of energies (*Nyasa*) is prescribed using the six primary musical notes/vowels (*ShaTsvaras*), prefixed and suffixed with the sacred syllable OM (*Pranava*).”
