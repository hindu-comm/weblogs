In the history of India’s freedom struggle, particularly in the wars fought for freedom in Tamil Nadu, June 16 holds a place of great significance. It was on this day that Chinna Marudhu Pandiyar, the younger of the Marudhu brothers, issued the famous Jambudweepa Proclamation. This was not an isolated event that occurred suddenly on a single day. The proclamation emerged at a crucial stage in a long and spirited struggle waged by the Palaiyakkarars against British domination. To understand the significance of this proclamation, it is essential to examine the circumstances in which it was issued by Marudhu Pandiyar.

**Background**

The eighteenth century was a period when the British and the French, who had originally come to India for trade, were making determined efforts to establish their political authority in the country. At that time, an intense war of succession was taking place within the family of the Nawab of Arcot, who ruled as the representative of the Nizam of the Deccan. Of the two claimants to the position of Nawab, Chanda Sahib was supported by the French, while Muhammad Ali was backed by the British.

In the wars fought between the two sides, Muhammad Ali emerged victorious and ascended the position of Nawab of Arcot. Taking advantage of this victory, the British obtained from Muhammad Ali the right to collect revenue from the Palaiyakkarars. They subsequently imposed heavy taxes and began collecting them from the local chieftains. Many Palaiyakkarars resisted these unjust and oppressive demands. Among them was Muthu Vaduganatha Thevar, the ruler of the Sivaganga Samasthanam.

At one point, the British launched a military campaign against Sivaganga and treacherously killed Muthu Vaduganatha Thevar. Thereafter, his wife, Queen Velu Nachiyar, raised an army with the support of the Marudhu brothers, defeated the British, and recaptured Sivaganga. After the period of Velu Nachiyar, the administration of Sivaganga passed into the hands of the Marudhu brothers.

The Marudhu Pandiyars, who placed the welfare of the Sivaganga region above everything else, did not initially adopt a confrontational attitude towards the British. They maintained cordial relations with several British officers. Colonel James Welsh, in particular, was a close friend of Chinna Marudhu. In his military memoirs, Welsh recorded that Chinna Marudhu regularly sent him fine-quality rice and delicious fruits of a kind that could not be found elsewhere in India.

The Marudhu brothers, however, understood that this friendship would not last forever. They were well aware that the British were ultimately interested in establishing political domination. Anticipating that Sivaganga would sooner or later be forced into a confrontation with British forces, they began taking precautionary measures and preparing themselves for such an eventuality.

They were also deeply distressed by the execution of their friend, Kattabomman, the ruler of Panchalankurichi, who had resisted the oppressive and arbitrary taxation imposed by the British. Chinna Marudhu believed that a united confederacy of the Palaiyakkarars would possess sufficient strength to resist British power. He therefore began making determined efforts to bring such an alliance into existence.

The forts at Kalaiyar Kovil, Tirumayam, and Tiruppattur were repaired and strengthened. At Aranmanai Siruvayal, Chinna Marudhu built a palace for himself and converted it into a formidable fortified stronghold. He also created a small naval force at Thondi, consisting of a fleet of boats. Arrangements were made to use these boats to bring food supplies for the troops from Jaffna.

While these preparations were under way, Chinna Marudhu sent emissaries to several Palaiyakkarars and began building an alliance against the British. He held discussions with Gopala Nayak, the ruler of Virupakshi; Mayilappan, Singam Chetti, and Muthu Karuppa Thevar, commanders associated with the Ramanathapuram Samasthanam; Krishnappa Nayak of South Canara; and Dhondia Wagh of northern Karnataka. Sivaganga, Dindigul, and Mysore emerged as the principal centres of this confederacy. Several diplomatic missions worked to coordinate the military forces of these territories. Since there was constant danger from British spies and the possibility of betrayal by informers, communication among the Palaiyakkarars was conducted largely through oral messages.

Despite all these carefully planned precautions, the British sensed that a major resistance movement was taking shape. In June 1800, Collector Hurdis sent local agents to spy on the activities of the Marudhu brothers. Although they were unable to discover the full extent of the preparations, the British suspected that the Palaiyakkarars might create serious trouble and therefore intensified their repressive measures against them.

Angered by this growing oppression, Gopala Nayak convened a meeting of the Palaiyakkarars at Dindigul in April 1800. At the meeting, the following resolution was adopted:

*“The British have betrayed the trust of the people of this land and have begun to destroy the traditions of our country. They have reduced the people to servitude and have started imposing their authority upon them. The only remedy is to break British power and compel them to remain merely as traders. This objective can be achieved only through an armed struggle. We must therefore wage such a struggle and defeat the British.*

*Victory in this endeavour will transform the people into free human beings. It will also inspire the people of other parts of India, enabling everyone to improve their livelihoods and live in prosperity.”*

As the first step in this struggle, it was decided to launch an attack on Coimbatore.

However, even before the appointed date, fighting broke out in Mysore between the British and the insurgents. This unexpected development threw those leading the resistance in Tamil Nadu into confusion.

Meanwhile, after the death of Kattabomman, his brothers Sevathaiya and Oomaithurai, who had been imprisoned in Tirunelveli, escaped from prison and resumed the struggle against the British. The British force sent against them under the command of Macaulay initially suffered heavy losses. A reinforcement force under Colonel Agnew was then dispatched to Panchalankurichi. After a fierce battle there, the British emerged victorious. Oomaithurai escaped from Panchalankurichi and sought refuge in Sivaganga.

Because of these unexpected conflicts in both the north and the south, Gopala Nayak postponed his planned offensive. The delay proved costly. The British forces that had secured victories in Mysore and Panchalankurichi joined together and attacked Dindigul. Gopala Nayak was defeated in the ensuing battle.

Following this initial setback, the forces of several Palaiyakkarars took refuge in the forests between Virupakshi and Sivaganga. They concealed themselves in the wooded regions around Kalaiyar Kovil, Keelanellikottai, Siruvayal, Paganeri, and Cholapuram. Chinna Marudhu brought together the forces stationed in these areas and began planning a renewed attack against the British.

The British somehow learned of these preparations and dispatched a military column under Colonel Agnew towards Sivaganga. Thanjavur, Pudukkottai, and Travancore also sent troops to assist the British.

As Colonel Agnew prepared for the assault on Sivaganga, he issued the following proclamation to the people of Sivaganga and to Periya Marudhu on June 12, 1801:

*“Chinna Marudhu, the Servaikarar of Sivaganga, has failed in the duties he owes to the Company’s government and has disregarded its orders. He has been supplying men and weapons to the insurgents of Panchalankurichi. He has also provided refuge to those who attacked the Company’s forts, killed its servants, and plundered towns.*

*“The people of Sivaganga, as well as the rest of the world, are aware that under the Company’s rule they have enjoyed every comfort and protection. The world also knows of the protection extended by the Company to Chinna Marudhu, notwithstanding the acts of cruelty he had previously committed while serving the Rani. … Though he is not the lawful heir, he has been exercising illegitimate authority over Sivaganga. He has now taken up arms and is fighting to usurp the throne of Sivaganga.*

*“Enraged by these actions, the British government has dispatched Colonel Agnew with an army to punish Chinna Marudhu and his supporters and to install the lawful heir on the throne of Sivaganga. Therefore, anyone claiming the right of succession must approach Colonel Agnew with the relevant documents. Adequate protection will be provided to such claimants. After the rebels have been suitably punished, the claims of succession will be thoroughly investigated, and the title will be conferred upon the rightful heir.*

*“Colonel Agnew has learned that Vellai Marudhu, the brother of Chinna Marudhu, does not approve of his younger brother’s actions. While seeking to punish the guilty, Colonel Agnew also wishes to protect innocent people. Should Vellai Marudhu surrender, arrangements will be made for him and his family to proceed to Madurai and live there in peace.”*

It was in response to Colonel Agnew’s proclamation, and also to inspire the people of the country to join the struggle for freedom, that Chinna Marudhu issued the Jambudweepa Proclamation. Some portions of it are reproduced below:

***“His Excellency Nawab Muhammad Ali foolishly allowed the Europeans a place among you and has now been reduced to the condition of a widow. The Europeans, acting without loyalty, have fraudulently appropriated the government for themselves. They treat the inhabitants like dogs and exercise their authority over them.***

***“There is neither friendship nor unity among you. The communities mentioned above failed to understand the duplicity of the Europeans. Not only did they continue to slander one another, but they also surrendered their government to them. The people now ruled by such wretches have been reduced to poverty, and even water has become as scarce and precious as jaggery. Although they suffer, they do not know how to free themselves from their misery.***

***“Even if a man lives for a thousand years, he must one day die. It is the fame he earns that will keep his memory alive for as long as the sun and the moon endure….***

***“It has therefore been resolved that, hereafter, everyone must enjoy his rightful hereditary inheritance. Accordingly, the Nawab of Arcot, Vijaya Raghunatha Tirumala Nayak, the ruler of Thanjavur, and others must immediately regain the territories that rightfully belong to them, without any injury to the established order of succession. The Europeans must serve under the Nawab of Arcot. Once European authority has been destroyed, we shall all enjoy happiness without tears.***

***“Therefore, O Brahmins, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas, Shudras, and Muslims—all among you who possess manly courage—whether you serve as civilians or soldiers, labour in the fields or elsewhere, or work under those contemptible Europeans as subedars, jamadars, havildars, nayaks, or sepoys, and are capable of bearing arms, prove your strength in the following manner: wherever you encounter those wretches, destroy them.***

***“May whoever removes this proclamation from the wall incur the guilt of committing the five great sins. Let everyone read it and prepare copies of this proclamation.***

***“Thus declared Marudhu Pandiyan, servant of the great rulers and the implacable enemy of the contemptible Europeans.”***

The words of this proclamation convey several significant messages. Chinna Marudhu did not seek merely the liberation of the Sivaganga Samasthanam or that of the Palaiyams allied with him. Watching the whole of India gradually fall under British domination, he understood that unless Bharat was liberated swiftly, its civilisation and culture would face grave dangers. It was with this larger vision that he named his declaration the “Jambudweepa Proclamation.”

The vast land of Bharat, known in the Tamil tradition as Navalantivu, is referred to as Jambudweepa in our ancient texts. By deliberately invoking this name, Chinna Marudhu made it clear that his objective was the liberation of the entire country from British rule.

He had also recognised that the British were consolidating their power by exploiting the absence of unity among the various communities of the land and among the rulers who governed its different regions. Through this proclamation, Chinna Marudhu emphasised the need for unity among them and declared that victory could be achieved only through such collective solidarity.

Most importantly, he did not place himself at the centre of this struggle or seek power for himself. Instead, he stated that the territories should be restored to those who had ruled them before the arrival of the British. Without saying it explicitly, Chinna Marudhu Pandiyar conveyed a profound truth: no great victory could be achieved except through a united and collective effort.

The proclamation was affixed to the gateway of the Tiruchirappalli Fort and to the outer wall of the Srirangam temple. At the conclusion of the proclamation, Marudhu Pandiyan declared:

***“Marudhu Pandiyan bows at the feet of all the elders and acharyas residing in Srirangam. Kings built these forts and strongholds. Yet those very kings and their people have now been reduced to poverty by these contemptible men. I seek your blessings to fight against them!”***

These words reveal the deep respect and reverence that Marudhu Pandiyan had for our traditions and spiritual heritage. They also make it clear that our rulers regarded nationalism and spirituality as their two eyes.

Today, we are no longer under foreign rule. Yet the enemies of the nation continue to trouble us in many different forms. Just as in those times, there is no shortage of traitors even today. Some internal elements, working in collaboration with foreign forces, continue to create obstacles to the emergence of a strong and united Bharat.

Therefore, even in the present age, every line of this proclamation remains relevant to us. These forces cannot be defeated without unity among the people, without the conviction that India is one, and without regarding nationalism and spirituality as two inseparable guiding principles. That is the enduring message the Jambudweepa Proclamation continues to remind us of.
