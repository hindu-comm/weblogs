viśadavaak

The Sriramayana-purushas are four – Sri Rama, Bharata, Lakshmana and Shatrughna. Each of their avataram is for a specific purpose. Of those, Shatrughna (Shatrughnazhwan) is born to show us the greatness of bhagavata sheshattvam and paaratantryam (or Acharya bhakti, as our Acharyas have shown).

His bhagavata paaratantryam is exemplary. Our Acharyas show that Shatrughna did not know another God than Bharatazhwan. “Shatrughna never knew another thing than Bharata, who in turn did not know anything other than Sri Rama”!! The lives of Shatrughna and Bharata entirely revolved around Bharata and Srirama, respectively. Shatrughna is actually taken as the role model for Madhurakavi Azhwar, who knew no other God than Nammazhwar – his Acharya. Madhurakavi Azhwar says “tevu matrariyen” (I don’t know another God than Nammazhwar). Our Acharyas invariably draw parallels with Shatrughnazhwan’s nature.

This is also shown in the commentary on the first verse of Ayodhya Kanda (Ayodhya kanda – 1-1). This is a popular sloka, quoted by our Acharyas very often to show bhagavata paratantryam. The verse reads as

“गच्छता मातुलकुलं भरतेन तदानघः । शत्रुघ्नो नित्यशत्रुघ्नो नीतः प्रीतिपुरस्कृतः ॥”

In this sloka, Shatrughna is called ‘anagha’. agha means sin. Anagha is the one without sin. What sin is this referring to? Our Acharyas show that it is ‘ramabhakti’. His mind was so firmly set on Bharatazhwan that he was never carried away even by Rama’s beauty! This is the state in which a charamaparvanishta (one who has their Acharyan as everything) should be.. This is how Madhurakavi Azhwar was, to Azhwar. This is how Vaduganambi was, to Emberumanar! We see this from the many anecdotes mentioned in the various works of our Acharyas.

If Ramabhakti should be called a ‘sin’, we can only imagine the intensity of Shatrughna’s devotion towards Bharata, more than Rama himself!

We are mostly taught that Shatrughna never thought about Rama, from all these commentaries. But wait, is this true? And to what extent, if it is true?

We can find a beautiful answer in the Ayodhya kanda, 89th sarga. Bharatazhwan takes the entire population of Ayodhya to Chitrakoota. His intention is simple. “If Rama cannot withstand the tears of Bharata alone, then he can never withstand the tears of so many people. So he will definitely return.”

But it didn’t work out. That’s a different story. Anyway, on the way to Chitrakoota, Bharata stayed overnight at Shrungiverapuram, where Guha lived. Having spent the night in contemplation upon Sri Rama, Bharata goes to wake up Shatrughna, saying “Oh Shatrughna! You are still sleeping! Wake up. Go and get Guha here, so that he can get us all across this Ganga with his boats”. That is when Shatrughna gives a wonderful reply, which is not generally heeded to. He says,

“जागर्मि नाहं स्वपिमि तमेवार्यं विचिन्तयन् । इत्यॆवमब्रवीद्भ्रात्रा शत्रुघ्नॊपि प्रचॊदितः ॥”

It simply means that Shatrughna – upon being woken up (and provoked to speak) – said this: “I am not sleeping; I am awake. I am also contemplating upon that Sri Rama (whom you are contemplating upon)”! Shatrughna, who did not know another God than Bharatazhwan, says he was contemplating upon Sri Rama! How interesting!

The commentator says there is a paathaantaram (an alternate version), where तमेवार्यं is replaced with तथैवार्यं. Then, it means “I am contemplating upon Sri Rama in exactly the same way you are doing”. In either way, Shatrughna’s contemplation upon Sri Rama is shown in this sloka.

But if Shatrughna was so deeply attached to Bharata, why would he contemplate upon Sri Rama? The answer is simple. He did that also for the sake of Bharata!! Would Bharata be happy if Shatrughna totally ignored the person whom he loved the most? Not at all! So Shatrughna contemplated upon Sri Rama, talked to him, hugged him, etc – ONLY for the sake of Bharata’s happiness!

He did not do this when everyone were happily living in Ayodhya (probably he did, but no account of it). He did this when Bharata was burning in the fire of separation from Sri Rama! Such beautiful kainkaryam!!!

What better role model can we have for Acharya bhakti?

So, yes – charamaparvanishtas also have to contemplate upon Bhagavan. Tiruvaradhanam to Perumal has to be done. There is no escape saying “I am not looking at Perumal, as I know only my Acharyan”. But this contemplation is not because they are carried away by the beauty and auspicious qualities of Bhagavan, but ONLY because their Acharyan would be happy if they did it.

Madhurakavi Azhwar also says “devapiraanudai kariyakola ttiruvurukkanban naan” (I see the beautiful form of Bhagavan with all his alankaram). Vaduganambigal also had Srinivasa Perumal as his nitya tiruvaradhana perumal, and later Emberumanar also gave him a tirumeni of Lakshmi Narasimhar, just as any other simhasanadhipati was given. We can find these two tirumenis in his tirumaligai even today.

**PS**: I was wondering why our Acharyas have not quoted this sloka in the commentary of “tiritandaagilum” pasuram (#3) of kanninun siruttambu. But Azhagiya Manavala Perumal Nayanar has beautifully answered this question for me in his commentary. He says, “this means, Madhurakavi Azhwar got to see Devapiraan because He came in front of Madhurakavigal Himself; and this is only because of Azhwar’s prasadam”. So the context is slightly different. He doesn’t say, “I shall go and see perumal for the sake of Azhwar”, but rather says “I am seeing perumal because of Azhwar”. So this sloka doesn’t fit in that context. If his intention was the former one, then I am sure our Acharyas would have quoted this sloka of Ayodhya kanda as well!
