viśadavaak

SriRamayanam is replete with Sri vaishnava sampradaya meanings. One such beautiful meaning can be seen from two slokas of Sriramayanam with the phrase “agratastē gamiśyāmi”. Let us look at those meanings here.

When Kaikeyi sought a boon from Dasharatha to send Srirama on an exile for fourteen years, Srirama readily accepted and prepared to leave. In the pursuit, he came and informed about this change in his status to his mother, brother and wife. Kausalya could not help, but stayed back in Ayodhya as per Srirama’s wish. However, Seetha and Lakshmana had other plans on their minds. Both of them were of a firm determination to accompany Srirama on his exile of fourteen years.

Seetha tried, in various ways, to convince Rama to take her along with him. In that context, she says this:

> यदि त्वं प्रस्थितो दुर्गं वनमद्यैव राघव।  
> ****अग्रतस्तॆ** गमिष्यामि** मृद्नन्ती कुशकण्टकान्।।

She says, “if you are going to the dense forest today itself, then I shall go before you, smoothening the pricky grass and thorns”.

After a few more words, Rama accepted to take Seetha along with him to the forest. Then, Lakshmana was bent upon going along with them to the forest. He surrendered to Rama, and said these words:

> धनुरादाय सशरं खनित्रपिटकाधरः ।  
> **अग्रतस्तॆ गमिष्यामि** पन्थानमनुदर्शयन् ॥

He says, “I shall walk in front of you, holding the bow and arrow, and also the spade and basket, showing you the way”.

In the same context of pleading to go with Rama to the forest, both – Seetha and Lakshmana – talk alike. They use the same phrase – “agratastē gamiśyāmi”. Upon reflecting on these two slokas, a beautiful meaning is revealed.

We should first know the real sense in which Seetha says “I shall walk before you, smoothening the pricky grass and thorns”. Is Seetha capable of it? Rama’s divine body is so soft and supple that he is himself called “puśpahāsasukumāravigrahaḥ”, meaning, the one who possesses a divine body that is as beautiful and tender as a just blossoming flower. If a *raṇakarkaśa* Rama (the one who is very fierce in the warfield) has such a tender body, then what to say about Seetha’s divine form? It is much more tender than that of Rama himself. So there is no way that we can even imagine that she would go about smoothening the pricky grass and thorns, physically. So there must be some other meaning in this metaphor. We need to see what it is.

Rama holds a vow of protecting those who surrender unto him. The only thing he expects is that we do not interrupt him by trying to put effort in our own protection. There are many ways of trying to protect ourselves. Protection basically means “removal of undesirable outcomes and bestowal of all goodness”. Most often, we do not realize what is really good for us. We tend to assume that something is good for us, while it might not be so, in reality. In such cases, although Bhagavan tries to give us what is good for us, we tend to move away from it, and try to seek what we think is right for us. This becomes a great hurdle in Bhagavan’s act of protection. This is what Seetha calls as kanTaka or a thorn in the way of Rama. This sprouts up from the innumerable sins that we have accumulated since time immemorial. Here, when she says that she would smoothen the thorns and pricky grass, it means that she would bless the jīvātmās such that their pāpabuddhi is lost and they turn towards Perumal for protection. This grace of Piratti is what Azhwar calls as “பங்கயத்தாள் திருவருள்” in his tiruvāimozhi 9-2-1.

Alright, but then she should have said just “thorns”. Why did she include “pricky grass”? Self protection is of two types. One – the jīvātmā yearns for material benefits, because it has confused this body to be the soul. Two – the jīvātmā yearns for kaivalya, which is a kind of moksha, but does not have bhagavat kainkaryam as the end result. This stems up because of the jīvātmā’s wrong assumption that it is independent in nature. That is why Seetha talked about two pricky things – a thorn and a grass, and said she would smoothen them out, so that Rama could walk freely in his way, blessing the jīvātmās. This goes on to show the influence of Piratti in our protection. In other words, she is the puruśakāra, who influences Bhagavan in such a way that He would not leave us unprotected at the end of the day, come what may! For that, she prepares us to be ready to receive that supreme grace. This is shown in her words in the Sriramayana.

Further, Lakshmana also utters the same phrase, but for a different reason. He says, he would walk before Rama in order to show him the way. Where would he lead Rama to? The answer is in his subsequent avatāra as Bhagavad Ramanujacharya. He brought Rama to each one of us. When Bhagavan came down as Rama, Krishna and others, he could not attract the jīvātmās in as many numbers as Ramanujacharya brought to Perumal during his avatāra. So, Lakshmana is indicating that he would stand as the Acharya and bring Perumal to each one of us – the distressed souls. The line “धनुरादाय सशरं खनित्रपिटकाधरः” shows that he comes down in the form of an Acharya, who would be a role model for all our kainkaryas. He would hold all the tools required to serve Perumal and Piratti at all times in a befitting manner.

“Alright, but the entire episode is when Rama is leaving on exile. He is going to the forest. How are these words applicable to us today?”, you may ask. The key to answer this question is in Tirukkacchinambigal’s work devarajashtakam. He says that this samsara or this material world is itself a scary dense forest. He says this one sloka –

> संसारमरुकान्तारॆ दुर्व्याधिव्याघ्रभीषणॆ  
> विषयक्षुद्रगुल्माढ्यॆ तृषापादपशालिनि

So, the inner meaning of these slokas in our context is that Perumal comes down to this dense forest called samsara just in order to uplift the distressed souls, and Piratti becomes the purushakara and Ilaya perumal becomes the Acharyan. With the help of Acharyan and Piratti, we get the supreme grace of Bhagavan himself!
