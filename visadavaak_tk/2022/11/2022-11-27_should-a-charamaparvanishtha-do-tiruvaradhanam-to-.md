viśadavaak

First of all, a charamaparvanishtha is an one who is totally devoted to their Acharyan. The Acharyan is the means of liberation as well as the destination to reach, for that person. So it makes sense only to do tiruvaradhanam to one’s own Acharyan. After all, Madhurakavi Azhwar – a role model for charamaparvanishthas – said “tevu matrariyen” – meaning, “I do not know of another God than Nammazhwar”. That being the case, he would have done tiruvaradhanam to his Acharyan Nammazhwar alone, is it not?

But no – that’s not true! Tiruvaradhanam is to be performed to Perumal at any cost. There is no exception to this. Even a charamaparvanishtha will do tiruvaradhanam to perumal, because that’s how the Acharyan likes it. Acharyan is not happy with the shishya doing kainkaryam to him alone. Rather, he is happy only when the shishya serves Bhagavan. So, a charamaparvanishta would do tiruvaradhanam to Perumal also.

But wait! does a charamaparvanishtha even identify a separate entity called Bhagavan?

Periyavacchan pillai says that the word “Guru” or “Acharya” befits only Jagadacharya Sri Ramanujacharya. There is no one else who fits the definition of that word. So when we say “Acharya”, it is really referring to Sri Ramanujacharya. He says, “तस्मिन्रामानुजार्यॆ गुरुरिति च पदं भाति नान्यत्र”. Then why are we calling all other Acharyas as “Acharyas”, when they are not Acharyas in the first place? For this, he gives a beautiful justification in the next line, where he says – “तस्माच्छिष्टं श्रीमद्गुरूणां कुलमिदमखिलं तस्य नाथस्य शॆषः”; meaning, “therefore, the remaining Acharyas in the lineage are all subservient to Ramanujacharya” (and hence called ‘Acharyas’). For us, they are the very manifestations of the Acharya Sri Ramanuja. The swami who performs panchasamskaram to us is called as “Acharya”, only because he is the direct manifestation of Sri Ramanujacharya for us. We call all others as “Swami” only because we visualize our own Acharya in every Srivaishnava. In this way, it is just Sri Ramanujacharya who shows himself in so many ways (i.e., in the form of so many Acharyas) to a true Sri vaishnava – a charamaparvanishtha. All Acharyas post Sri Ramanujacharya’s period attained moksha due to his tiruvadi sambandham. All Acharyas prior to Sri Ramanujacharya’s period attained moksha due to his tirumudi sambandham. This is evident from the fact that all prior Acharyas worshipped Emberumanar in the form of “Bhavishyadacharyan”, who can be seen even today at Azhwar Tirunagari.

For a jeevatma who has realized this truth, there is no separate entity called ‘bhagavan’, for – even Bhagavan is the sheshabhuta of Ramanujacharya! Behold the beauty here. Bhagavan is called ‘prathamāchārya’ or the first Acharya in our guru parampara. Our guruparampara starts with “lakshminatha” and comes down up to our own Acharya. That being the case, we revere Bhagavan also only for being an Acharya in our glorious lineage!

So, coming back to the question – should a charamaparvanishtha do tiruvaradhanam to perumal? The answer is a resounding ‘yes’. But the only difference being – a charmaparvanishtha never sees a different entity called bhagavan in that place. Rather, he sees the ‘prathamāchārya’ in that place, and therefore performing tiruvaradhanam to “Acharya lineage”, starting from one’s own Acharya, and going up to Lakshminathan!

This is the beauty of comparing “tiruvaradhanam to Acharyan, Mamunigal, Emberumanar, Azhwar, Nityasuris and Perumal” and “tiruvaradhanam to asmadacharyan, and other Acharyas namely Mamunigal, Emberumanar, Azhwar, Nityasuris and Bhagavan”!!

PS: One person asked me, do nityasuris also fall under the category of Acharyas? Yes, of course! They are called “kainkaryadeshika”s, meaning – the ones who teach us how to do kainkaryam to Bhagavan!
