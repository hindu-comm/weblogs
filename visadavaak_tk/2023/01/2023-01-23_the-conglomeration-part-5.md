viśadavaak

The ‘taaratamya’ that is referred to in the Vishnupurana sloka “तया तिरोहितत्वाच्च शक्तिः क्षेत्रज्ञसंज्ञिता । सर्वभूतेषु भूपाल तारतम्येन लक्ष्यते ॥” is explained by Sri Vishnuchittar thus: तारतम्येन – ज्ञानानन्दतारतम्येन

This differentiation is further illustrated with specific names and forms in the subsequent slokas, starting with ‘apraaNavatsu’. तारतम्यमेव दर्शयति – अप्राणवत्स्विति । शिलाकाष्ठादिषु सा – क्षेत्रज्ञशक्तिः स्वल्पा – ज्ञानानन्दादिना, न तु स्वरूपेण ।

In this context, Engalazhwan shows how apraaNavat objects are understood as shilaa or rock, kaashtham or wood, and others. Nowhere does he lay down a condition that the kshetrajnashakti or knowledge and bliss of a jeevatma is limited in these bodies IF they are taken due to special event. Rather, it talks about the availability of these bodies to jeevatmas at large due to karma. If this is not taken in this spirit, and if one assumes conditionality of this statement, then we should also assume similar conditions in every other entity mentioned in this context, such as plants and herbs, reptiles, birds and so on. This is why we made it clear in the beginning that the anvaya of this sloka goes with the three preceding slokas, and should not be read/interpreted seperately. When our Acharyas have not shown such a condition, we cannot assume that condition on our own.

Therefore, considering how Emberumanar talks about “karmanimittasharira”s and how Engalazhwan understands such shariras to be ‘shila, kashta and others’, we can confidently say that the purvapakshi’s claim that “only a few objects can be indwelled by a jeevatma due to its karma” is much against the philosophy of our Acharyas, who in turn followed the scriptures.

Further, let us examine the real meaning of ‘abhimani jeeva’ or ‘abhimani devata’. We need to examine this in detail because the locus standi of the purvapakshis is the claim that abhimani devata identifies itself with all such inanimate objects, and therefore not indwelled by a jeevatma individually.

So who is an abhimani devata? The popular notion of abhimani devata is “the one who is born in the deva yoni, and identifies themselves with a certain entity in this creation”. For example, we see the sun in the sky. We also worship the sun god by the name Suryadeva. Is the physical orb called ‘sun’ same as the deva called ‘surya’? NO! We say that the sun god or Suryadeva is the abhimani devata for this physical orb called ‘sun’. So whatever we do to worship sun god is directly offered to this visible sun, but in reality the worship reaches the Sungod who sits elsewhere and not really indwelling this physical orb.

However, this definition assumes two things – one, that the abhimani devata is not indwelling the entity but sitting elsewhere and claiming sole ownership in that physical body; and two – that the abhimani is a deva only, and not belonging to other yonis. We need to examine the truth of these two statements to know the validity of this definition.

Let us examine the first assumption that the abhimani devata is not indwelling the entity but only claiming sole ownership on that physical body. By saying ‘sole ownership’, I mean ‘not allowing other jeevatmas to claim ownership simultaneously’. In other words, this means to say that only one jeevatma should be identifying itself with a certain achit at any given point in time. To validate this assumption, we need to take a pramana from Vishnupurana, 1st amsha, 22nd chapter. This is popularly called the astrabhushana adhyaya. Let us see if this assumption fits the Vishnupurana philosophy.
