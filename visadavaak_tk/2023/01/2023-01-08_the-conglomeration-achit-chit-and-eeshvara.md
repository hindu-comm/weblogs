viśadavaak

We are in the middle of determining whether the archaa forms are made of pure sattva or mixed sattva. As a subtopic to this, we contextually came across the concept of anupravesha. The question was: what happens to the jeevatma who resides in the prakrta achit, when Bhagavan takes that same achit as His divine body? For this, we have to first get convinced that there should be jeevatma inside the prakrta achit before it becomes the ‘divine body’ of Bhagavan. Hence this contextual post.

The shruti vakyas show that Bhagavan is the primordial creator. He transformed moola prakrti into mahaan, and that in turn into ahankara, and so on. This way, the twenty four achit tattvas – starting from moola prakriti all up to the panchabhutas – were created. After that, Bhagavan mixed these five elements in a process called pancheekaranam. Thus, the elements needed for individual creation were setup initially. Up till this point, the process of creation is called ‘samashti srshti’ or common creation. This samashti srshti includes all twenty four achit elements. They are all directly pervaded by Bhagavan.

Thereafter, Bhagavan creates the andas and lokas, and appoints a four faced brahma to carry forward this creation process. Brahma, as his duty, continues this process by giving every jeevatma a body according to its own karma. This process is called ‘vyashti srshti’ or individual creation. This is where specific things are created with an individual name and form (naama and rupa). For example, a cow has the name as ‘gauH’, and the form that is unique to the cow species. Similarly, a human being has the name as ‘manushya’ and the form that is unique to that species. Therefore, the name and form are uniquely associated with entities that can be categorized by speicies (jaati) and qualities (guna). This is what we call as “naama-rupa-bhaaktvam” or the eligibility to be identified with a name and form.

The shruti vakyas clearly show that the vyashti srshti (the individual creation) happens through a jeevatma, and hence called ‘sadvAraka’ (through a via medium); whereas the samashti srshti (the common creation) happens without the help of a jeevatma, and hence called ‘advAraka’ (without a via medium). The chadogya upanishat statement “anena jeevena aatmanaa anupravishya naamarupe vyaakaravaani” clearly shows this. Even the taittiriya upanishat statement “tadevaanupraavishat tadanupravishya satcha tyatchaabhavat” etc. shows the same thing more clearly.

NOTE: In his tatparyadipika, shrutaprakashikacharyar shows that vyashti srshti can be both advaaraka as well as sadvaaraka. We will come to the reconciliation of this point a little later, or in subsequent posts. For now, for all practical purposes, let us hold that it is sadvaaraka, as the shruti vakyas have shown this very explicitly.
