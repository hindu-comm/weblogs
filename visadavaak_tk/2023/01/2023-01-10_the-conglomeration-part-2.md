viśadavaak

We saw how the vedas talk about Bhagavan using jeevatma as a via medium to pervade the achit entities for the second time (first time being the swarupa vyapti in order to sustain those entities), which we call as “anupravesha”.

Keeping these vedic statements as the basis, Emberumanar writes in his vedartha sangraha thus: “जीवात्मा तु ब्रह्मणः शरीरतया प्रकारत्वात्, ब्रह्मात्मकः । “यस्यात्मा शरीरम्” इति श्रुत्यन्तरात् । एवंभूतस्य जीवस्य शरीरतया प्रकारभूतानि देवमनुष्यादिसंस्थानानि वस्तूनि इति ब्रह्मात्मकानि तानि सर्वाणि । अतः देवः मनुष्यः यक्षो राक्षसः पशुः मृगः पक्षी वृक्षो लता काष्ठं शिला तृणं घटः पटः इत्यादयस्सर्वे प्रकृतिप्रत्यययोगेन अभिधायकतया प्रसिद्धाः शब्दाः लोके तत्तद्वाच्यतया प्रतीयमानतत्तत्संस्थानवस्तुमुखेन तदभिमानिजीव-तदन्तर्यामिपरमात्मपर्यन्तसंघातस्यैव वाचकाः – इति । “  
The summary of this is: Jeevatma is a mode to paramatma (mode of manifestation), and therefore has paramatma as its antaryami. The vedas also say “jeevatma is the body of paramatma”. Similarly, all the insentient entities that we see, such as deva, manushya, etc., are also modes for the jeevatmas, and therefore the body of paramatma. Therefore, all the objects that we see with a name and form in this world such as deva, manushya, yaksha, rakshasa, pashu, mrga, pakshi, vrksha, lata, kashta, shila, trnam, ghata, pata, etc. are actually identifying the conglomeration of achit forms that are popularly seen with those respective names, the jeevatmas that identify themselves with those achit forms, and the paramatma who resides within those jeevatmas.

In his tatparyadipika commentary to this paragraph, Shrutaprakashika bhattar opines thus: Every single entity known by name and form has a jeevatma that identifies itself with that entity. It could be anything from deva, manushya etc, up to a heap of mud (loshta), a log of wood (kashta), etc., as it is shown in scriptures such as Vishnupurana (aprANavatsu savalpA sA, tatO hi shailAbdhidharAdibhEdAn, and other such slokas). The exact words of Shrutaprakashika bhattar are as follows: “देवाद्यवान्तरजातीयानामपि ब्रह्मपर्यन्तत्वं दर्शयितुं यक्षराक्षसादय उपात्ताः । “अप्राणवत्सु स्वल्पा सा”, “ततो हि शैलाब्धिधरादिभेदान् जानीहि विज्ञानविजृम्भितानि”, “भूतानां प्राणिनः श्रेष्ठाः” इत्यादिभिः लोष्ठकाष्ठादेर्जीवाधिष्ठितत्वश्रवणात् काष्ठं शिला इत्याद्युक्तम् । छिन्नद्रुमो हि काष्ठम्? तत्र द्रुमावस्थाधिष्ठातृजीवापगमेऽपि काष्ठावस्थाभिमानी जीवो विद्यत एव नामरूपभाक्त्वादिति भावः ।”

Here, Emberumanar is taking the names of objects that can be identified with a name and form, as popularly known in this world. In that context, he lists out deva, manushya, yaksha, rakshasa and other forms that we call sentient, and also kaashta, shilaa, ghata, pata and other forms that we popularly call insentient. A question might arise at this point, as to whether these objects like the stone, log of wood, etc can also be identified with a jeevatma. Shrutaprakashika bhattar clarifies this with a pramana from Sri Vishnupurana and Manusmriti.

The sloka sarting with aprANavatsu from Sri Vishnupurana goes thus:  
अप्राणवत्सु स्वल्पा सा स्थावरेषु ततोऽधिका ।  
सरीसृपेषु तेभ्योऽपि ह्यतिशक्त्या पतत्रिषु ॥

पतत्रिभ्यो मृगास्तेभ्यस्तच्छक्त्या पशवोऽधिकाः ।  
पशुभ्यो मनुजाश्चातिशक्त्या पुंसः प्रभाविताः ॥  
तेभ्योऽपि नागगन्धर्वयक्षाद्या देवता नृप ॥

शक्रस्समस्तदेवेभ्यस्ततश्चातिप्रजापतिः ।  
हिरण्यगर्भोऽपि ततः पुंसश्शक्त्युपलक्षितः ॥

In these slokas, Parashara maharshi goes on to show how the kshetrajna shakti (chaitanya or dharmabhuta jnana) varies from body to body. In bodies that do not have well defined prana shakti, the dharmabhutajnana is extremely limited, whereas it is slightly higher in the sthavara bodies with slightly defined pranashakti (such as plants). The reptiles and similar creatures have a little higher knowledge, whereas the birds have even higher chaitanya. This way, he goes on showing the ranking of knowledge in various bodies, and says that the four faced brahma has the highest dharmabhutajnana in this creation. In his commentary to this section, Vishnuchittar (Engalazhwan) writes thus: “शिलाकाष्टादिषु सा क्षेत्रज्ञशक्तिः स्वल्पा ज्ञानानन्दादिना, न तु स्वरूपेण । सरीसृपेषु तेभ्योऽप्यधिकेति शेषः । अतिशक्त्या – ज्ञानशक्त्याधिकेत्यर्थः । ” – meaning, the kshetrajna-shakti or dharmabhutajnana is extremely limited in stone, log of wood and other materials where prana is not defined. The chaitanyam is said to be limited due to contraction in dharmabhuta jnana and ananda, and not limited by nature itself. A little higher than these are plants (with defined prana) and even higher are reptiles and so on. The word atishakti means the one which is placed higher due to more knowledge and bliss. This goes on to show how a jeevatma is present in all of these things that we see around us, with varied degrees of dharmabhutajnana.

While mentioning the shilaa, kaashtam, etc. Sri Vishnuchittar never mentions any exceptional conditions such as “the jeevatmas taking a stone form due to a curse” and so on. This wording of “शिलाकाष्टादिषु” is a general statement and encompasses every stone and every log of wood and such materials, and not limited to some random instances of those materials. Otherwise, we should be able to show pramanas for curses that made jeevatmas as logs of wood (we have examples for jeevatmas turned into stone, but no popular examples of logs of wood). Further, we should also apply the same logic to all the animals and say all animals do not have an abhimani jeeva either, because Gajendra was an elephant born out of curse! So, by the same logic, we should be able to say that the birth as an elephant is also a rare event that occurs by a curse. The same holds for a tree, as Nalakubara and Manigreeva were cursed to be born as trees! So, in effect, every single birth can be ruled out and considered as an exceptional case only. This finally defies Vishishtadvaita! Where are we headed?  
Also, the word “sarvam” cannot be explained (“etatsarvam idam vishvam” mentioned in the same context) if all the bodies are not taken into consideration.

Why am I talking about curse in this context? How should we understand “abhimani jeeva”? Let us see in the forthcomoing posts.
