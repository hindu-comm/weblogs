viśadavaak

The purvapaksha interpretation of Emberumanar’s words in the vilakshanatvadhikaranam is not fully acceptable. Their claim is that Ahalya and others got the body of a rock, wood and other materials out of their karma. What they fail to understand is that every single jeevatma taking such a body is bound to take such bodies only due to its own karma. So the phrase “कर्मनिमित्तशिलाकाष्ठादिशरीरेषु” applies not only to Ahalya and others but also to every other jeevatma that takes on such bodies. The focus of Emberumanar’s words is not on “ahalya and others”, but on “shareera”, and the fault in such bodies. Ahalya and others are named only because we can easily relate to how a jeevatma can also take rock/wooden/other bodies that is otherwise considered as not indwelled by a jeevatma (by purvapakshis). We know how Ahalya sinned, and as a result of that sin she got the body of a rock. Similarly, Sriramayana also talks about the famous apsara named Rambha being turned into a stone by Vishwamitra. So the reason why Emberumanar named Ahalya and others is that we can easily relate how a certain karma can result in the body of a rock as well. It is not to show that only a few rocks or woods are indwelled by a jeevatma, as claimed by the purvapakshis. Therefore, the birth as a rock, mud, wood, pot, cloth etc. is also quite possible for a jeevatma, and every such instance is indwelled by a jeevatma as per the vedic statement “anena jeevena…” (since each of those things are identified with a name and form). This is amply clear from Shrutaprakashika bhattar’s commentary, where he generically elucidates the term ‘indriyaashrayatvam’ from Emberumanar’s sribhasyam, and nothing about special cases of Ahalya and others. If Emberumanar’s focus was on “ahalya and others”, then that would have been clearly shown by Shrutaprakashika bhattar. Also, the same pramana is shown more generically in the same context, without taking the names of Ahalya and others, in Emberumanar’s two other works called Vedantasara and Vedantadeepa.

The exact words of Sudarshana suri are as follows:  
(अहल्यादीनामिति) इन्द्रियाश्रयत्वम् – ज्ञानप्रवृत्तिजननपटुतरेन्द्रियाश्रयत्वम् । इन्द्रियसद्भावमात्रविवक्षायां पूर्ववदव्याप्तिरभिप्रेता ।  
Even while showing that he is commenting on the statement “ahalyaadeenaam…”, he doesn’t speak a word about Ahalya or others, but clearly shows how indriyashrayatvam is referring to mere presence of indriyas but lacking the capacity to facilitate knowledge in such bodies. This is seen in rock, wood and all other insentient bodies. So there is no reason to limit this to Ahalya and others.

Furthermore, we shall also see how Engalazhwan understands the Vishnupurana slokas in this context. In the 6th amsha 7th chapter of Sri Vishnupurana, Parashara Maharshi narrates a beautiful conversation between Keshidhwaja and Khaandikya. This is a beautiful conversation related to yoga, and Keshidhwaja clearly shows how one must meditate upon Bhagavan. In order to do so, one must know how Bhagavan pervades the entire universe. That is the context where Keshidhwaja highlights how Bhagavan pervades all these things using his three shaktis or powers – namely, Vishnushakti, Kshetrajnashakti and avidyaashakti (respectively called eeshvara, chit and achit tattvas, in other words). Unless we understand the three shaktis of Bhagavan as the three tattvas, we will not be able to appreciate the real meaning of the following slokas from that chapter.

विष्णुशक्तिः परा प्रोक्ता क्षेत्रज्ञाख्या तथापरा । अविद्याकर्मसंज्ञाऽन्या तृतीया शक्तिरिष्यते ॥  
यया क्षेत्रज्ञशक्तिस्सा वेष्टिता नृप सर्वगा । संसारतापानखिलानवाप्नोत्यतिसन्ततान् ॥  
तया तिरोहितत्वाच्च शक्तिः क्षेत्रज्ञसंज्ञिता । सर्वभूतेषु भूपाल तारतम्येन लक्ष्यते ॥  
अप्राणवत्सु स्वल्पा सा स्थावरेषु ततोऽधिका । सरीसृपेषु तेभ्योऽपि ह्यतिशक्त्या पतत्रिषु ॥

Here, the slokas must be read together as their anvaya is same. They make fully sense together, and therefore they should not be read separately. They say – “Vishnushakti is the superior shakti (since it is verily the eeshvara tattva), whereas the kshetrajna shakti is inferior to it (since it indirectly shows Bhagavan through a jeevatma). The third shakti is called avidyaa, with which the kshetrajnashakti or the jeevatma in every body is covered, and which causes that jeevamta to suffer the heat of samsara. Since the jeevatma is thus covered by avidyaa, it shows itself with differences in different bodies. It is very low in bodies lacking life force (such as rock, wood etc.), and slightly more in plants and herbs. It is even more in reptiles and more than that in birds”, and so on.  
Keshidhwaja extends this difference upto the four faced Brahma.

In this context, the word “kshetrajnashakti” clearly refers to the knowledge and bliss of a jeevatma. A jeevatma is called kshetrajna in vedantic terms, as it is the indweller of a body and controls it.

The vishnuchitteeya commentary in this context is thus:  
तत्र परा विष्णुशक्तिः प्रत्यस्तमितेत्यादिनोक्ता, क्षेत्रज्ञाख्या अपरा हिरण्यगर्भादिनोक्ता ।  
The reason why Vishnushakti is called ‘superior’ is because it is as said in the sloka “pratyastamita…”. Whereas, the kshetrajnashakti is called ‘inferior’ because it is as said in sloka ‘hiranyagarbha…’. So, here Engalazhwan clearly refers to four faced brahma (hiranyagarbha), prajapati, maruts, vasus, et al. when talking about kshetrajna. In other words, it clearly shows jeevatma only.

This jeevatma is said to be ‘sarvagaa’, meaning it is present in every body which is taken due to karma. This is the exact statement of Engalazhwan: सर्वगा – कर्मनिमित्तसर्वशरीरगा

Here, the focus is on ‘sarvasharira’ and not ‘karmanimitta’. Meaning, this statement is not focusing on the phrase “obtained due to karma”, but on the phrase “all the bodies”. This is how it has to be taken as per context (since it is talking about the entire creation, and not about some specific objects). So, we cannot limit this to a few bodies (such as Ahalya and others) and claim that they are obtained only due to some karma. Since Engalazhwan clearly shows it is true with all bodies, it applies to every single piece of rock, wood, pot, cloth, etc. All these bodies are clearly taken due to karma of a jeevatma.
