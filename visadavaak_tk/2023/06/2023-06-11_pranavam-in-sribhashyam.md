viśadavaak

प्रपद्यॆ प्रणवाकारं भाष्यं रङ्गमिवापरम् ।  
परस्य ब्रह्मणॊ यत्र शॆषित्वं स्फुटमीक्ष्यतॆ ॥

This is a very fine sloka that we recite while studying the Sri Bhashya. It likens Sribhashyam with Srirangam, where the mastership of the Supreme Brahman is shown very clearly. The basis for such likening is in the ‘pranavaakaaratvam’. The vimana gopuram in Srirangam is called ‘pranavaakaara vimaanam’. Perumal lies on the serpent bed under the pranavaakaara vimaanam, showing his long crown, which is a mark of his sheshitvam. Sribhashyam, on the other hand, is verily in the ‘form of pranavam’, where the mastership of Bhagavan is shown empirically with words. This writeup is about how Bhagavan’s sheshitvam is shown in the Sribhashyam, just as in the pranavam. Let us see.

A disclaimer that I would put upfront is that this post is not to educate the uninitiated. This is just for the enjoyment of initiated people (who have learnt the meanings of pranavam under the able guidance of an Acharya). So, you may find some things not explained sufficiently, or some details missing. My request to you is to please go to your Acharyan and learn those meanings.

Pranava shows us the absolute mastership of Bhagavan. It does this by splitting the word into three syllables and a declension case. They are ‘a’, chaturthi vibhakti, ‘u’ and ‘ma’. These four entities correspond to the four chapters of the Sribhashyam. The first two chapters of Sribhashyam constitute the ‘siddha dvikam’ or the duo about Bhagavan. The last two chapters of Sribhashyam constitute the ‘saadhya dvikam’ or the duo about the means (to attain Bhagavan) and result (of such attainment). The siddhadvikam is further split into two chapters called ‘samanvaya-adhyaaya’ and ‘avirodha-adhyaaya’. The saadhyadvikam is also further split into two chapters called ‘saadhana-adhyaaya’ and ‘phala-adhyaaya’. These together constitute the four chapters of Brahmasutras. Of these, the ‘a’kaara shows the first two chapters. The ‘u’kaara shows the third chapter. The chaturthi vibhakti and ‘ma’kaara together show the fourth chapter. Let us examine this further.

Bhagavan is shown as the creator, sustainer and destroyer of all worlds. This is shown in the first chapter of the brahmasutras, starting with the sutra ‘जन्माद्यस्य यतः’ (Brahmasutra 1-1-2). This is expanded upon in the rest of the chapter. The ‘a’kaara also shows this meaning in the pranava. ‘a’kaara is the source of all words, thereby connoting Bhagavan – who is the creator of all worlds. Similarly, the root ‘av’ means to protect. Therefore, ‘a’ means the one who protects. This includes the period of great deluge as well. So, essentially, Bhagavan is connoted with the word ‘a’. In this way, Bhagavan is attributed with the responsibilities of creating and protecting all entities. This is what the ‘samanvaya-adhyaaya’ (the chapter of attribution) does, as we saw above.

Further, the second chapter is called ‘avirodha-adhyaaya’. When the Acharyas thus attributed so many qualities to Bhagavan, there were many objections to them. The objectors said that these qualities do not necessarily apply to Bhagavan (or Bhagavan alone), but to other devatas (as well). The author of brahmasutras (Sri Vedavyasa) had to write an entire chapter to reinforce his claims in the first chapter. Since this chapter is full of such strong defense against wrong attributions, it is called ‘avirodha-adhyaaya’. This is also shown in the pranavam in the ‘a’kaara itself. The attribution of creation of all worlds, along with its protection was done to Bhagavan. But we the jeevatmas (commoners, without sampradaya knowledge) started opposing it. We said ‘why only Brahman should have these? Why not the others?’. So the pranavam had to give a unique trait of Bhagavan, by which it would be clear that these responsibilities do not lie with anyone other than Him. That unique trait is ‘being the consort of Sri Mahalakshmi’; or ‘shriyahpatitvam’, in short. Bhattar says this beautifully in the Srigunaratnakosham, thus:  
यद्भ्रूभङ्गाः प्रमाणं स्थिरचररचनातारतम्यॆ मुरारॆः  
वॆदान्तास्तत्त्वचिन्तां मुरभिदुरसि यत्पादचिह्नैस्तरन्ति  
He says, Sri Mahalakshmi is the basis for the different creations of Bhagavan. She is also the one who distinguishes Bhagavan from everyone else by the marks of her feet on His chest (showing her eternal presence with him). These go on to show how Bhagavan’s creatorship and protectorship are unique to Him. This comes to Him due to His association with Sri Mahalakshmi. Pillailokacharyar says, “இவள் தாயாய் இவர்கள் க்லேஶம் பொறுக்கமாட்டாதே அவனுக்கு பத்நியாய் அபிமதவிஷயமாகையாலே கண்ணழிவற்ற புருஷகாரம்”. Piratti never fails in mediating with Bhagavan and ensuring that He accepts the jeevatma into His fold. Therefore, Bhagavan’s protectorship is also beyond all doubts. This is how the objection to His possession of creatorship and protectorship are resolved. This is essentially the gist of ‘avirodha-adhyaaya’ (the chapter of resolving objections). This association with Sri Mahalakshmi is shown in the ‘a’kaara itself by our Acharyas. “ரக்ஷிக்கும்போது பிராட்டி ஸந்நிதி வேண்டுகையாலே இதிலே (அகாரத்திலே) ஶ்ரீஸம்பந்தம் அநுஸந்தேயம்”.

Thus, ‘a’kaara in the pranavam shows us the meanings of the first two chapters of Brahmasutras. ‘a’kaara, as Bhagavan’s name, shows the first chapter, and his shiryahpatitvam shows the second chapter. Together, it shows the ‘siddha’dvikam.

Further, the third chapter is called ‘saadhana-adhyaaya’. This majorly speaks about the means of attaining Bhagavan for the sake of eternal service. Although Swami shows in his vedanta granthas that bhakti is the means for attainment (in order to debate with the scholars of other schools of thought), he does carefully mention his philosophy in one place, that Bhagavan himself is the means of attaining him. In the paraadhikaranam of the brahmasutras, the purvapakshi claims that the means to attain Bhagavan should be something different from him, because the means and goal cannot be the same. That is where Sri Vedavyasa had to establish with sufficient proof that the means as well as the goal can be same, and they are verily Bhagavan himself. This is shown in the sutra ‘उपपत्तॆश्च’ (3-2-34), and explained by Bhagavad Ramanujacharya with vedantic texts as proof. So, although Bhakti is expounded contextually, Swami also establishes that Bhagavan himself is the means to attain him. This is shown in the ‘namas’ in the tirumantram, which is in turn an explanation for the ‘u’kaara in the pranavam. “உகாரத்தை விவரிக்கிறது நமஸ்ஸு”. ‘u’kaara essentially shows the ananyArhatva of a jeevatma. This shows his belongingness to Bhagavan alone, and not even to himself! Therefore, the means for his protection is also Bhagavan alone, which is what is shown by Swami in the third chapter of the brahmasutras.

The fourth chapter of the brahmasutras is called ‘phala-adhyaaya’, and it speaks about how a jeevatma takes the archiraadi gati and reaches the supreme abode Sri Vaikuntha. Reaching that place, a jeevatma begins his eternal servitude to the supreme lord, and that is shown at the end of the brahmasutras. The fourth quarter of the fourth chapter deals with how the jeevatma serves the supreme lord eternally, starting with the sutra ‘सम्पद्याविर्भावः स्वॆन शब्दात्’ (4-4-1), and ending with ‘अनावृत्तिः शब्दादनावृत्तिः शब्दात्’ (4-4-22). This is where Sri Vedavyasa shows how the jeevatma attains his essential nature of servitude unto Bhagavan, and engages in such eternal service. There are two things that manifest in that state, which are – knowledge and servitude (jnaatrtva and sheshatva). Both of these are shown in the ‘braahmaadhikaranam’ and ‘jagadvyaapaara-varjaadhikaranam’, respectively, in the brahmasutras. These are shown in the pranavam with ‘ma’kaara and the chaturthi vibhakti, respectively.

Thus, the ‘u’kaara in pranavam shows the meanings in the third chapter of the Brahmasutras. The ‘ma’kaara and chaturthi vibhakti (aaya) show the meanings in the fourth chapter. Together, they show the ‘saadhya’dvikam.

All these meanings are shown at once by Periya Perumal, who reclines on the aadishesha serpent bed, under the pranavaakaara vimaanam. He stretches one hand towards his feet, and folds the other hand and shows his crown. His long crown decorated with Tulasi leaves is a symbol of his mastership over all worlds (ஆதிராஜ்யஸூசகமான சூழ்த்த துழாய் மன்னு நீண்முடி). With his right hand showing that crown, Perumal shows us that He is the master of all worlds. The bimbam of Sri Mahalakshmi on his chest shows his shriyahpatitvam. His feet are our sole refuge and the means to attain him. And those very feet are indeed the receivers of our eternal service. Together, they show the meanings of the pranavam (the meanings of the four chapters as detailed here).

This was shown by Aalavandaar to his disciples as well, during his last moments in this leelaa vibhuti.

Therefore, it is very apt to equate the Sribhashyam with Srirangam (pranava vimaanam), where the mastership of Bhagavan is shown very clearly.
