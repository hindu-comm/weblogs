viśadavaak

Sri Manavala Mamunigal’s book – jeeyarpadi tiruvaradhana kramam – needs a deeper study to understand the nuances of tiruvaradhanam that we offer on a daily basis.

As seen in the previous post, this procedure was authored primarily to include the uttara vakya from the Ramanuja rahasyatrayam. We have to see how ‘charamaparva nishtai’ is incorporated into the tiruvaradhana kramam in jeeyarpadi kramam. Let us examine this now.

If you ask a layman, ‘who do you offer tiruvaradhanam to?’, he would obviously say ‘perumal’. If you ask him ‘why?’, he would say ‘because that servitude is my real identity’. But if you ask a charamaparva nistha the same questions, the response would be slightly different. He would say that his tiruvaradhanam is only to his acharyan. Only for the sake of pleasing the Acharyan, he would perform tiruvaradhanam to perumal as well, and that too – being a limb to the Acharyan for that entire time period. This indeed is his real identity.

Mamunigal also shows this beautifully in the jeeyarpadi tiruvaradhanam. The entire procedure is broken down into several parts – worshipping one’s own Acharyan, then Emberumanar, followed by Azhwars and then the nityasuris; only then, one worships Perumal and Pirattis. As we see here, it starts with the worshipping of our own Acharyan before anyone else.

First, the kovilazhwar is opened during vishwarupam, and a prayer is made to Perumal that he should kindly accept the upacharas or offerings, while being present along with his devis and nityasuris. Once this is done, then the archaka (one who is performing tiruvaradhanam) consecrates the holy waters of Viraja and Tirukkaveri, and then begins the tiruvaradhana procedure. The first (and only) person to be worshipped by the archaka directly is his Acharyan. The archaka washes the feet of his Acharyan (while uttering the taniyan), and the tiruvaradhanam to Acharyan is over! The holy water that washed the feet of his Acharyan is later taken as Sripaada teertham.

Then, he routinely begins with Emberumanar tiruvaradhanam. From here onwards, the archaka invariably prays to each one – viz., Emberumanar, Azhwars, Nityasuris and Bhagavan himself – that they should accept the tiruvaradhanam from the hands of his Acharyan. Nowhere does the archaka say that he offers the upacharas on his own. The phrase ‘ஆசார்யன் திருக்கைகளாலே’ can be seen in every step of the tiruvaradhanam, without fail. This is a clear indicator that the archaka is only being a limb of his own Acharyan while the latter performs tiruvaradhanam to Perumal with utmost love.

Now a question might arise: why perform tiruvaradhanam as a limb to my Acharyan? Why not perform on my own? It is because the Acharyan is free from blemishes, and therefore fit to perform tiruvaradhanam. He is also the one who is more dear to Perumal, and also placed above me in all aspects. So it makes a lot more sense to allow him to do the tiruvaradhanam, while I stand behind him, assisting him in his worship. Also, when we look at our own identity, it is rather ‘absolute servitude’ and ‘total dependence’ on the Acharyan, or being a tool in the hands of the Acharyan for his use alone. Therefore, I serve my own Acharyan who does the actual tiruvaradhanam, befitting my true identity. I let my Acharyan be the wielder and I become the tool in his hands. This makes Bhagavan happier. Isn’t this pleasure of Bhagavan the final goal of our tiruvaradhanam?

Alright, we let our Acharyan perform the tiruvaradhanam, and being his limb, we offer the upacharas to Perumal. But who all accept tiruvaradhanam in our daily worship? Some people proudly claim that they have the tirumenis of all azhwars and major acharyas to whom they perform tiruvaradhanam. Some claim that their kovilazhwar is very small, and just has azhwar and emberumanar tirumenis. Some offer tiruvaradhanam to Vedantacharyar, and some to Manavala Mamunigal. If we are asked, “Do you have a tirumeni for Pillai lokacharya” or “do you have a tirumeni for Marudur nambi or Mazhuvur nambi (or any of the other 74 simhaasanaadhipatis, randomly)?”, how many of us would happily claim that we do, and that we offer daily tiruvaradhanam to them all? Further, how many Acharyas’ tirumenis can we have in our kovilazhwar? Does tiruvaradhanam include worshipping all of these azhwars and acharyas as well?

We shall answer these questions in the forthcoming posts.
