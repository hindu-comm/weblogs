viśadavaak

We saw how Nampillai’s words are interpreted differently in the seyyatamarai decad of Nammazhwar. Let us examine Nampillai’s words further.

In concluding the pravesham of seyyatamarai decad, Nampillai tells us a beautiful thing. He says, when a devotee offers a tirumeni to Bhagavan, made out of any material of his own liking, Bhagavan accepts it with great affection. He shows the same affection in that tirumeni as he showed towards the butter offered to him by yashoda and other gopikas in gokulam. The purvapakshis interpret this statement as saying Bhagavan just “likes” the forms to a great extent, but does not change them into shuddhasattvamaya tirumeni. In fact, this is the basis for their interpretation of ‘abhimanam’ as ‘liking’. But the real thing in this statement is – it just shows “how much” Bhagavan likes the tirumeni we offer. This liking comes because his dear devotees have offered it with so much desire and love. Just because Bhagavan has this liking towards such tirumenis, he does the abhimanam of shuddhasattvamaya sva-asaadhaarana divyamangala vigraha in these tirumenis. So, clearly, “liking” and “abhimanam” only go hand in hand, but not the same in this context. That means, abhimanam does not directly mean ‘liking’ alone, but the abhimanam comes as a product of the initial liking towards the tirumeni.

Further, the ninth pasuram in this decad “tanjamaagiya tandai taayodu” is to be examined. In fact, this ninth pasuram itself is the basis for attributing this entire decad to archavataram. The pasuram goes thus:  
தஞ்சமாகிய தந்தைதாயொடு தானுமாய் அவையல்லனாய்  
எஞ்சலிலமரர் குலமுதல் மூவர் தம்முள்ளுமாதியை  
அஞ்சி நீருலகத்துள்ளீர்கள் அவனிவனென்று கூழேன்மின்  
நெஞ்சினால் நினைப்பான் யவன் அவனாகும் நீள்கடல்வண்ணனே

In this pasuram, we need to see Nampillai’s commentary on the last one and half lines, starting from ‘avan ivan enru koozhenmin’, to know the greatness of archavataram.

Nampillai explains the pasuram thus: the word ‘avan’ refers to paramapadanathan, who is beyond all boundaries of place, time and objects, and having an asadharana vigraha (an exclusive shuddhasattvamaya tirumeni). He is always enjoyed by the nityasuris . The word ‘ivan’ refers to the archavatara, which is bounded by time, place and object. He takes as his own divine body any form that we offer to him, which is made of any material of our liking. Thus, he remains accessible to our knowledge at all times. So, ‘avan’ is beyond all boundaries, and ‘ivan’ is bounded by many factors. So saying, do not doubt the greatness of archavatara! Whatever you offer as his divine form heartily, Bhagavan – whose greatness is actually beyond all boundaries – takes that itself as his asadharana vigraha and desires that form very much. This is as said in the pramanas.

The pramanas that Nampillai quotes in this context are:  
1) यॆ यथा मां प्रपद्यन्तॆ (Bhagavadgita)  
2) मनसाभिक्लृप्तः (Narayanopanishat)  
3) स्मृतं सङ्कल्पनामयम् (Vishnupuranam)  
4) सुवर्णरजतादिभिः (Vishnudharmam)

Nampillai’s words at this place are as follows:  
“(நெஞ்சினால் நினைப்பான் யவன்) மநஸ்ஸாலே யாதொன்றைத் திருமேனியாகக் கோலினிகோள். (அவனாகும் நீள்கடல் வண்ணனே) அபரிச்சேத்யமஹிமனான ஸர்வேஶ்வரன் அத்தையே தனக்கு அஸாதாரண விக்ரஹமாகக்கொண்டு விரும்பும்.  
…  
यॆ यथा मां प्रपद्यन्तॆ என்றும், मनसाभिक्लृप्तः என்றும், स्मृतं सङ्कल्पनामयम् என்றும், सुवर्णरजतादिभिः என்றும் சொல்லுகிறபடியே. “

The first thing we have to notice here is the phrase “அத்தையே தனக்கு அஸாதாரண விக்ரஹமாகக்கொண்டு விரும்பும்”. One cannot ignore the words ‘attaiye’, ‘tanakku asaadhaarana vigrahamaagakkondu’ etc, and highlight only ‘virumbum’ and conclude that Bhagavan is only displaying a liking in this dravya akin to his asadharana vigraha.

He clearly shows the pramanas and says, “as said in these” (என்றும் சொல்லுகிறபடியே) and stops at that. It is only when this statement is read along with the arumpadam that we get the real picture of how Nampillai treats archavatara in line with the said pramanas. The adaiyavalaindan arumpadam specifically highlights a point. The arumpadakarar explains the second pramana “manasaabhiklrptaH”. The actual statement goes thus: “हृदा मनीषा मनसाभिक्लृप्तॊ य एनं विदुरमृतास्तॆ भवन्ति \|”  
The adaiyavalaindan arumpadam is thus: “मनसाभिक्लृप्तः = इच्छानुगुणं निर्मितमित्यर्थः । अयं मनसाभिक्लृप्तॊ भवति । यॆ एनम् = एवंविधं विदुः । तॆ अमृता भवन्ति इत्यर्थः ।  
“अजॊऽपि सन्नव्ययात्मा भूतानामीश्वरॊऽपि सन् । प्रकृतिं स्वामधिष्ठाय सम्भवाम्यात्ममायया ॥  
जन्म कर्म च मॆ दिव्यं एवं यॊ वॆत्ति तत्त्वतः । त्यक्त्वा दॆहं पुनर्जन्म नैति मामॆति सॊऽर्जुन ॥” என்று தானே இத்தை உபப்ரும்ஹித்தானிறே. “

Manasaabhiklrptah means that which is made as per one’s desire. This is referring to the archaa forms that are offered by the devotees to Bhagavan to accept as his divine forms. Therefore, Bhagavan also takes that form. Those who understand the greatness of Bhagavan thus shall become immortal. This has been expounded by Bhagavan himself in the Bhagavadgita as well, in the fourth chapter, when he says “I descend with all my greatness intact; those who know my births and deeds in their true sense thus, oh arjuna! they shall reach me upon giving up the body, and not cycle back in this samsara” (ajo’pi and janmakarma slokas). This is specifically the explanation for “ye enam viduH amrtaaste bhavanti”.

So, Nampillai is essentially showing manasaabhiklrptaH as a pramana for archaa forms (grihaarchaa, as per context), and the arumpadakarar says that this vedic statement is explained by Bhagavan in his Bhagavadgita with the slokas ajo’pi and janmakarma. The sloka “ye yathaa” is also quoted by Nampillai to supplement this fact in the same place. Also, we know from Bhagavadgita bhashyam that the sloka ajo’pi shows how Bhagavan takes shuddhasattva tirumenis in all his births. This removes all doubts in how archavatara is perceived by our Acharyas. Archa vigrahas are purely shuddhasattvamaya only, be it in a temple or a matham or a house, or anywhere for that matter.
