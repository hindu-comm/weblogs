viśadavaak

अग्रतः प्रययौ रामः सीता मध्यॆ सुमध्यमा ।  
पृष्टतस्तु धनुष्पाणिः लक्ष्मणॊऽनुजगाम ह ॥

This is a popular Sriramayana Sloka that shows how Rama walked in the front, Seetha in the middle and Lakshmana at the end, while traversing the forest dandakaaranya. While this order of Rama, Seetha and Lakshmana is very enjoyable in terms of visualizing the pranava, this sloka set me thinking as to why they walked in that same order, and not any other way.

Lakshmana would not have walked in the front or middle for two reasons.

One – Lakshmana walked in the front, followed by Rama, and Seetha at the end, while walking to the Mandakini river to give tarpanam to their deceased father Dasharatha. Rama says, this is the order in which brothers should walk to give tarpanam to their parents – the younger ones in the front, followed by the elder ones. But this is a lower level of reasoning.

The second, and the actual reason is that Lakshmana walked at the end to show his servitude to the divine couple. He would always have both Rama and Seetha in front of him, because his essential nature is to serve the divine couple, and not either of them individually. But then, couldn’t have Seetha walked in the front and let Rama walk in the middle? Lakshmana could always follow them. After all, she is the one who said “अग्रतस्तॆ गमिष्यामि मृद्नन्ती कुशकण्टकान्” (I shall walk in front of you, Oh Rama, and I shall soften out the thorns and blades of grass to make your path smoother). But no, she walked in the middle as seen in the aforesaid Sriramayana sloka.

The reason for this is – Rama’s swarupam (as Narayana) is to accept service and protect his dear devotees; Seetha’s swarupam (as Sri mahalakshmi) is to accept service from other jeevatmas, on the one hand, and also to enhance their service towards Perumal on the other. Lakshmana’s swarupam (as a jeevatma) is to serve this divine couple. So, essentially, Sri mahalakshmi is the one who enhances the servitude of jeevatma. This is also shown by our Acharyas when they say “அவன் ப்ராப்யனாமிடத்தில் தான் ப்ராப்யையுமாய்க் கைங்கர்யவர்த்தகையுமாயிருக்கும்”. Now the question is how does she enhance kainkaryam? One way – she can instigate the jeevatmas to perform certain services. Another way – she can remove the obstacles in the path of servitude so that the jeevatma performs such kainkaryams in an uninterrupted way.

The most popular explanation to this is that she instigates the jeevatmas to perform certain services to Perumal and herself, as said in ‘தேவிமாராவார் திருமகள் பூமி ஏவ மற்றமரராட்செய்வார்’. But there is also another way in which she enhanced the kainkaryam of Lakshmana.

Generally, it is said and understood that Perumal’s beauty itself is a big obstacle in serving him (ஸௌன்தர்யம் அந்தராயம்). It is because, the jeevatma gets immersed in the beauty of Perumal and therefore forgets everything else, including kainkaryam! That doesn’t serve the purpose, because the jeevatma’s essential nature is to serve, and not enjoy! Lakshmana had gone to the forest to serve Rama and Seetha for the entire period of exile. Rama’s beauty was more exquisite from behind than it was from the front (முன்னிலும் பின்பழகிய பெருமாள்). In fact, Sumitra warns her son Lakshmana to refrain from being mesmerized by Rama’s beauty (which could lead to disruption in his service to Rama) while going to the forest.

सृष्टस्त्वं वनवासाय स्वनुरक्तः सुहृज्जनॆ \|  
रामॆ प्रमादं माकार्षीः पुत्र भ्रातरि गच्छति \|\|

She says, “Oh son! You have been created to go to the forest to lovingly serve your brother Rama. Never get carried away by his transcendental beauty, especially while he walks, lest you disrupt your service unto him”.

Rama is more beautiful from behind than from the front; and also has extremely gracious and beautiful walking styles (गजसिंहगती वीरौ शार्दूलवृषभॊपमौ). So it is very easy for anyone to get carried away by that beauty and miss their kainkaryam.

Lakshmana is a nityasuri (aadishesha’s incarnation). The undesirable state for him is disruption of service. Since Rama’s beauty is a major factor in distracting him, Seetha walked in the middle, thereby stopping Lakshmana from enjoying Rama’s beauty. In this way, Lakshmana could do more service without being distracted. This is how Seetha acted as kainkarya-vardhakai, or the enhancer of servitude.

Now a major question looms over us. Would Lakshmana not get carried away by Seetha’s beauty? This is all the more dangerous for the swarupam than getting carried away by Perumal’s beauty! It does so much harm to the jeevatma! Or did Seetha lack that beauty which Rama possessed? Of course not! Seetha was more beautiful than Rama, as seen in the sloka “तुल्यशीलवयॊवृत्तां तुल्याभिजनलक्षणाम् \| राघवॊऽर्हति वैदॆहीं तं चॆयमसितॆक्षणा \|\|”. Then the only other possibility is that Lakshmana could get carried away by Seetha’s beauty.

But, that never happened. Lakshmana’s brahmacharya was so steadfast that he even failed to identify Seetha’s other ornaments such as keyura, kundala, etc (worn on the upper parts of the body), but readily identified just the anklets or nupura at Kishkindha. नाहं जानामि कॆयूरॆ नाहं जानामि कुण्डलॆ । नूपुरॆत्वभिजानामि नित्यं पादाभिवन्दनात् ॥ This shows how Lakshmana never raised his head to look at Seetha even in the eyes, but always served at her feet at all times. So, if Seetha would walk in front of Lakshmana, there would be no question of Lakshmana raising his eyes and enjoying the beauty. If he would thus walk looking at the ground, or turning around, then there would be no question of being distracted by Rama’s beauty either. Thus, Seetha protected Lakshmana’s servitude from disruption due to distractions. It is pertinent to note here that Rama would instigate Lakshmana to do specific works, while Seetha would protect such service from disruptions due to distraction! Thus, both of the divine couple would essentially enhance Lakshmana’s kainkaryam!

Why am I saying so much? It is because I saw this beautiful photograph of Chellappillai, Kalyani tayar and Emberumanar returning from the Kalyani after tirukkalyanotsavam. It is common for people to quote the ‘agrataH prayayau’ sloka for this event. But that set me thinking, why this order and not anything else?  
  
In fact, since Piratti enhances servitude of Lakshmana (Emberumanar here), he performs kainkaryam by being all of those items – the umbrella, the palanquin, the beautiful robes, the divine jewelries, the supporting pillows, etc, even while retaining his original form of being Emberumanar. This is just as he would do in Sri vaikuntham (senraal kudaiyaam irundaal singaasanamaam etc.)

How beautiful kainkaryam!!
