viśadavaak

Manavala Mamunigal further writes in the commentary of Acharyahrdayam – sutram 221 – that Bhagavan takes on the forms offered by his dear devotees itself as his asaadhaarana vigraha. This is in line with how Nampillai explained in the Eedu.

Further, Mamunigal’s jeeyarpadi tiruvaradhana kramam is a nice place to know how grihaarchaa forms must be seen. For all who say grihaarchaa forms are indeed praakrta in nature, Mamunigal’s jeeyarpadi tiruvaradhana kramam comes as a thorn in the throat. In every step of the tiruvaradhanam to Perumal, Mamunigal shows this phrase without fail – “समस्तपरिवाराय सर्वमङ्गलविग्रहाय श्रीमतॆ नारायणाय नमः” . People who are conversant with Jeeyarpadi kramam will vouch for it. The word “samasta-parivArAya” is used to include all the intimate servitors of Bhagavan who are inseparable from him at all times (e.g, aadhaarashakti and other devatas in the dharmaadhipeetham, chaamaragraahinis, dvaaraadhyakshas, et al). The word ‘sarva-mangala-vigrahAya’ is used to indicate all the divine forms of Bhagavan present in the kovil azhwar.

Ideally, as per the Agama practice, every tirumeni should be given an upacharam every day. However, the grihaarchaa is simplified, and our Acharyas have shown us an easier way to achieve this. It is done by uttering the word ‘sarvamangalavigrahAya’. Just one set of upacharas, at once, serves all the tirumenis or forms taken by Bhagavan in our kovil azhwar.

In this place, if we had to believe that Mamunigal and other Acharyas were of the opinion that grihaarchaa forms are praakrta in nature, then he wouldn’t have used the term ‘mangala’ (sarva-mangala-vigraha). It would have sufficed to just say ‘sarvavigrahAya’. After all, we are boldly accepting that Bhagavan takes on praakrta forms, right? Then why hesitate to call him just ‘sarvavigraha’? Why confuse with ‘sarva-mangala-vigraha’? But Mamunigal and all other Acharyas have always shown that it is ‘mangalavigraha’, or even better – ‘divya-mangala-vigraha’. They deliberately use this phrase in all places. It is very rare to find the word ‘vigraha’ without adjectives while showing the divine body of Bhagavan. This ‘maangalyam’ in the vigraha’s adjective is the indicator for shuddhasattva dravya. This is also the reason why it is called ‘tiru-meni’ in tamil.

There are even more number of pramanas from the Acharyas’ srisuktis to show how they believed the tirumeni of Bhagavan in his archaa forms as shuddhasattvamaya. But I stop here, fearing the length of posts. Enough said about the shuddhasattvamaya tirumeni in the archaavataara till now. However, I may be posed another question thus: “Can Bhagavan not take praakrta forms at all? In fact, archaa forms are an embodiment of his supreme compassion and simplicity. This is also shown by our Acharyas in umpteen number of places. Is there another simplicity than taking on praakrta forms (with blemishes) just because a pure devotee offered them?”. Let me answer this question now.

Let us not assume on our own any particular act as Bhagavan’s simplicity. The simplicity in archaa forms is explained by Mamunigal in the Srivachana bhushanam. He says, Bhagavan descends in the archaa forms so as to be worshipped by the lowest soul also. He descends in those forms and waits for even those people who ignore his divine presence, or even worse – try to disprove his presence totally (தன்னை அநாதரிக்கிறவர்களையும் ஆதரித்துப் போருகை). Mamunigal asks, “is there a higher simplicity than this one?” This is how our Acharyas experienced simplicity of Bhagavan in the archaa forms. Simplicity does not mean he takes on praakrta forms as his divine body.

Also, Bhagavan does not take praakrta forms because he is hailed as ‘akhila-heya-pratyaneeka’ by the Vedas. Not only is he pure (without blemishes) himself, but also removes the blemishes of all those who surrender to him. That being the case, how can the praakrta form be the divine body of Bhagavan? Isn’t our praakrta body taken on due to karma? What karma does Bhagavan possess to be inflicted with a praakrta body? That way, I can also ask in return – “can Bhagavan not accept karma, and enjoy their fruits? after all, he can get rid of those karma at his will, right? Just as he can get away with the praakrta forms! None of them can bind him in this samsara, just as much as the rajas and tamas in the praakrta form cannot affect the divyaatma svarupa of Bhagavan! So, tomorrow, if a true devotee prays that Bhagavan also gets some karma and stays with them in this samsara for company, then Bhagavan should come down accordingly! But, no! none of the scholars accept this argument. They say this is a stupid argument. They say Bhagavan is never affected by karma. “न मां कर्माणि लिम्पन्ति न मॆ कर्मफलॆ स्पृहा” says Bhagavan in the Bhagavad Gita. Just like that, my friend, I say that Bhagavan does not take on praakrta forms because he has no karma affecting him. His divine forms are always shuddhasattvamaya in nature. It is his viruddha-ghatanaa-saamarthyam that he transforms these prakrta materials into apraakrta by removing rajas and tamas.

Then, one may argue that shuddhasattva dravya has no shortcomings, and hence should not be subjected to any shortfalls. But we see that archaa forms act very much like praakrta materials by not showing themselves in absence of an external source of knowledge, being damaged, etc. How is this justified? This is also answered by Pillai lokacharyar in the tattvatrayam – achit prakaranam. He says that shuddhasattva is believed to be jada by a few Acharyas, and ajada by few other Acharyas. Pillai lokacharayar goes with the latter set of Acharyas, and endorses their view by saying that it is indeed ajada, and it shows itself to nityas, muktas and eeshvara, even without external aids for knowledge (svayam prakasha). But it does not show thus to the samsaris.  
அஜடமானபோது நித்யர்க்கும் முக்தர்க்கும் ஈஶ்வரனுக்கும் ஜ்ஞாநத்தையொழியவும் தோற்றும். ஸம்ஸாரிகளுக்குத் தோற்றாது.(sutras 5 and 6 of achit prakaranam, in tattvatrayam).

That is how, we – the samsaris – fail to identify the shuddhasattva forms of Bhagavan. It is a shortcoming on our end, and not on the shuddhasattva dravya.

Further, we fully accept that Rama was shot by arrows, wounded badly, lost consciousness in the warfield, etc., even when we know that vibhava avataras are always with shuddhasattva tirumenis only. Similarly, Krishna had to face so many difficulties. He was hit by hunger and fatigue (வேர்த்துப்பசித்து வயிறசைந்து pasuram). How can all these be accepted for apraakrta tirumeni? Our Acharyas show that it is his deep involvement in that avatara (அவதாரத்தில் மெய்ப்பாடு). When he descends amongst us, he wishes to live like us; not by taking the praakrta forms that come due to karma, but by showing the same activities and responses like us, even with his apraakrta divyamangala vigrahas. Same is the case with archaa forms as well. It is not a shortcoming, and certainly not a reason to brand his tirumeni as ‘praakrta’. It only shows his deep involvement in that archaa form, and his absolute simplicity even while being supreme! It shows his sincerity in projecting as “archaka-paraadheena-akhilaatmasthithi”. It is such a beautiful form of Bhagavan, where he shows not just his divine form but also his divine qualities, with just a shuddhasattvamaya body!

With this, I conclude this series of posts with a trust that we all accept Bhagavan’s tirumeni in archaa forms as being shuddhasattvamaya only, and thereby be uplifted. Thank you.

(concluded)
