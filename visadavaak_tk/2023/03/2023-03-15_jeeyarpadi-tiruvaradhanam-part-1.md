viśadavaak

श्रीशैलॆशदयापात्रं धीभक्त्यादिगुणार्णवम् ।  
यतीन्द्रप्रवणं वन्दॆ रम्यजामातरं मुनिम् ॥

As a Srivaishnava, one gets initiated into panchasamskaram by an Acharyan. The five samskaras are – tapa, pundra, nama, mantra and yaga. Of these, tapa samskara is when one gets the shankha and chakra marks on their shoulders. pundra is adorning with the twelve/five tiruman kappu (depending on gender). nama is getting the dasya nama indicating their relationship with Emberumanar. mantra is when one gets initiated into the three rahasya mantras viz., tirumantram, dvayam and charamashlokam. The fifth samskara is called yaga. It comes from the root ‘yaj’, meaning worshipping the Lord. Therefore, yaga essentially is a samskara wherein one gets the eligibility to worship Sriman Narayana. The Acharyan teaches his shishya to do the tiruvaradhanam regularly.

There are several sources from where one can learn the tiruvaradhanam procedures. For example, Emberumanar himself has written a book called nityagrantha, where he shows the detailed procedure for a complete tiruvaradhanam. Similarly, there are procedures that were followed by Sri Parasara bhattar, Sri Somasiyandan, et al. There are procedures outlined in the Pancharatra Agama to perform a complete tiruvaradhanam to Perumal. Many Srivaishnavas choose different sources, and do their daily tiruvaradhanam as instructed by their Acharyan.

However, most tengalai tirumaligais and mathams instruct the jeeyarpadi tiruvaradhana kramam, which is authored by Sri Manavala Mamunigal. This is an ancient practice of performing tiruvaradhanam to Perumal and his parivaram along with all Nityasuris, Azhwars and Acharyas. In this series of posts, let us delve deeper into the jeeyarpadi tiruvaradhana kramam and see why it becomes imperative for every srivaishnava to follow this procedure of tiruvaradhanam.

First, we should understand why worshipping of Bhagavan is called ‘tiruvaradhanam’. The word ardhanam comes from the root ‘raadh’, which means to increase (राध् वृद्धौ). This gets the ‘aa’ upasarga, indicating completeness of the action. This is a way of increasing the pleasure of Bhagavan multifold by serving Him in all ways as desired by Him. Since this aradhanam is performed to Sriyahpati or the Lord of Sri mahalakshmi, who is the supreme entity, this procedure is called ‘tiruvaradhanam’ by our elders, and not just ‘aradhanam’. This is in line with the usage of ‘tiru’ for indicating respect in the Srisampradayam, such as tiruvarangam, tiruvengadamudaiyan, tiruppallandu, tirukkarthikai, tirumangai azhwar, tiruppadi, etc.

Next, we should understand why Manavala Mamunigal wrote this procedure when so many other procedures already existed. At least, he is not the one who writes something more than what Emberumanar has already written about the subject. Yet, we see both nityagrantha as well as jeeyarpadi tiruvaradhanam existing together. Why was this done? The reason is – jeeyarpadi tiruvaradhana kramam is for antimopayanishtas. Antimopayam or charamopayam is an upayam or means to attain our goal (nityakainkaryam) where an Acharya’s abhimanam uplifts the soul and grants the fruits. Here, the word ‘Acharya’ refers to Emberumanar alone. It is his relationship and abhimanam that uplifts each and every soul and grants the nityakainkaryam at his lotus feet. There is no higher means than this one. Therefore, it is called ‘antima-upaaya’ or ‘charama-upaaya’. For people who are in this state of mind, Emberumanar becomes everything. Emberumanar is the ultimate goal to attain, as well as the means for attaining him. Therefore, they are initiated into the ‘Ramanuja rahasyatrayam’. Just as we use the uttaravakya (of dvaya mahamantra) for all tiruvaradhanam purposes, Manavala Mamunigal showed us the procedure to worship Emberumanar with the uttaravakya in Ramanuja rahasyatrayam. Therefore, it becomes imperative for every Srivaishnava to follow this procedure in order to worship Emberumanar. Since this procedure was not shown in any of the other extant granthas, Mamunigal authored the jeeyarpadi to show the same.

Not just that; every Srivaishnava looks upon his/her own Acharyan as verily the living Emberumanar. So it becomes important to perform tiruvaradhanam to one’s own Acharyan also. This is also shown in the jeeyarpadi tiruvaradhana kramam.

In view of all these points, the jeeyarpadi tiruvaradhanam holds a special place in our Srisampradayam.

Let us examine these points in more detail in the forthcoming posts. Jeeyar tiruvadigale sharanam.
