viśadavaak

After having seen several pramanas such as artha panchakam, arumpadams of srivachanabhushanam and mumukshuppadi, eedu and its arumpadams, and bhagavadgita bhashyam (and therefore the Vedas), now we need to see how this subject is dealt with in tattvatrayam.

The ‘tattvatrayam’ of Pillai Lokacharyar has three prakaranas or divisions – viz., chit, achit and eeshwara prakaranas. Of these, the eeshwara prakarana deals with Bhagavan’s svarupa, rupa, etc. in great detail.

In dealing with Bhagavan’s unique characteristics, Pillailokacharyar says that one of the primary functions of Bhagavan is to create the worlds. Thereby, he also becomes the protector and the destroyer of the worlds. In the 40th sutram, Pillai lokacharyar says: இவன்றான் முந்நீர் ஞாலம் படைத்தவெம் முகில்வண்ணன் என்கிறபடியே ஸவிக்ரஹனாய்க்கொண்டு ஸ்ருஶ்ட்யாதிகளைப் பண்ணும். Meaning, Bhagavan carries out the creation, sustenance and destruction of these worlds with a divine body. Later, in the next sutram, he describes that divine body of Bhagavan in great detail. He says, ‘ விக்ரஹந்தான் ஸ்வரூபகுணங்களிலுங்காட்டில் அத்யந்தாபிமதமாய், ஸ்வாநுரூபமாய், நித்யமாய், ஏகரூபமாய், **ஶுத்தஸத்வாத்மகமாய்**, ……., ஸர்வாஶ்ரயமாய், அஸ்த்ரபூஷணபூஷிதமாயிருக்கும்’. In commenting on this big sutra, Manavala Mamunigal explains shuddhasattvatmaka thus: ‘ ஶுத்தஸத்வாத்மகமாகையாவது – குணாந்தர ஸம்ஸர்கமில்லாத ஸத்வத்துக்கு ஆஶ்ரயமாயிருக்கிற அப்ராக்ருத த்ரவ்யமே வடிவாயிருக்கை. ந தஸ்ய ப்ராக்ருதா மூர்திஃ என்னக்கடவதிறே.’

The position of our Acharyas is very clear in this statement of Mamunigal. He says that there is no mixing of rajas and tamas, and it is only pure sattva which is present in that divine form. The divine body itself is made of such supernatural material (apraakrta dravya) (அப்ராக்ருத த்ரவ்யமே வடிவாயிருக்கை), which has only shuddhasattva in it (குணாந்தர ஸம்ஸர்கமில்லாத ஸத்வத்துக்கு ஆஶ்ரயமாயிருக்கிற). He also quotes a pramana from the Mahabharata, where Bhishma says that there is no praakrta body for Bhagavan, in order to support this claim. “na tasya praakrtaa murtiH”.

If it was just this much, then one could have argued that this could be true, but there is no proof to show that this applies to archaa forms. This description could only apply to the other four forms – para, vyuha, vibhava, and antaryami. After all, their intention is to establish archaa forms as praakrta only, right?

But Mamunigal summarizes this 41st sutra and contextually shows the link with the subsequent sutras in this short passage at the end of the sutra:  
ஆக, விலக்ஷணவிக்ரஹயுக்தனாய் என்றத்தை யுபபாதித்தார் கீழ். அநந்தரம், **இந்த விக்ரஹயோகப்ரயுக்தமான வீஶ்வரனுடைய பரத்வாதி பஞ்சப்ரகாரத்தையும்** தனித்தனியே ஸுவ்யக்தமாக தர்ஶிப்பிக்கவேணுமென்று திருவுள்ளம் பற்றி, ப்ரதமம் அதுதன்னை யுத்க்ஷேபிக்கிறார் – “ஈஶ்வரஸ்வரூபந்தான் பரம், வ்யூஹம், விபவம், அன்தர்யாமித்வம், அர்ச்சாவதாரமென்றஞ்சு ப்ரகாரத்தோடே கூடியிருக்கும்.

This passage is setting a context between the ‘vigrahayogam’ (having a divine body) and ‘parattvadi pancha prakaram’ (taking five forms). Without mincing words, Mamunigal says “Being associated with such a divine body, he takes on the five forms such as para, vyuha etc.” We should notice the usage of the phrase ‘இந்த விக்ரஹயோகப்ரயுக்தமான வீஶ்வரனுடைய பரத்வாதி பஞ்சப்ரகாரத்தையும்’. The same divine body (shuddhasatvatmaka aprakrta divyamangala vigraha) is associated with all five forms of Bhagavan.

In the 42nd sutra starting with ‘ஈஶ்வரஸ்வரூபந்தான்’, Mamunigal further clarifies this with his pristine words: ஈஶ்வரஸ்வரூபம் என்கிறவிடத்தில் ஸ்வரூபஶப்தத்தாலே சொல்லுகிறது – **ஸ்வாஸாதாரண விக்ரஹத்தையாதல்**, விக்ரஹவிஶிஷ்டமான ஸ்வரூபந்தன்னையாதல். He shows the meaning of ‘svarupam’ as both ‘divine body’ as well as ‘divine nature’. When it means ‘divine body’, it clearly is his unique apraakrta form. He shows that this is as told by Pillai lokacharyar himself in another abridged version of the tattvatrayam. ‘ஸங்க்ரஹேண இட்டருளின மற்றையிரண்டு தத்வத்ரயப்படியிலும் “திருமேனியும் அஞ்சுபடியாயிருக்கும்; அதாவது- பரத்வமும், வ்யூஹமும், அந்தர்யாமித்வமும், விபவமும், அர்ச்சாவதாரமும்” என்றும்… இவர் தாமே யருளிச்செய்கையாலே.’

So, Pillai lokacharyar first speaks about the glories of Bhagavan’s divine body, and says that Bhagavan takes on five forms with such a divine body. They are para, vyuha, vibhava, antaryami and archaa. He doesn’t mention about the nature of divine body in those individual forms subsequently (except for vibhava, and that too for a different reason); the reason being, it is already discussed in the 41st sutra as being common to all the forms. Considering all these points, it becomes amply clear to us that Pillai lokacharyar and Manavala Mamunigal have also clearly accepted archaa forms as having made of shuddhasattva dravya only.

Even then, one may argue that archaa forms can be of two types – one with a shuddhasattvamaya tirumeni, and one with praakrta body itself. So, the archaa forms that are discussed here (and in most places seen previously) are talking about the first type, where the divine body is made of shuddhasattvamaya dravya. However, Nampillai’s eedu alone talks about the second type of archaa, which is said to have a body made of praakrta dravya only. That is mostly grihaarchaa. But this argument cannot be accepted. After all, our Acharyas who clearly show that antaryamitvam is of two types (அந்தர்யாமித்வம் இரண்டுபடிபட்டிருக்கும் : arthapanchakam 21st sutra) would not have avoided showing the two types of archaa forms as well, if they existed. But nowhere have our Acharyas talked about two types of archaa forms. There is only one type of archaa, and that archaa murthy is made of shuddhasattvamaya dravya only – be it an archaa form worshipped at home, matham or temple.
