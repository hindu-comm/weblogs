viśadavaak

In the arumpadam for “tanjamaagiya tandai taay” pasuram, arumpadakarar says that the shruti vakya ‘hrdaa maniishaa’ is explained by Bhagavan in the ‘ajopi’ and ‘janma karma’ slokas of the Bhagavadgita. Let us have a quick look at how Emberumanar comments on these slokas.

In the ajopi sloka, Emberumanar’s bhashyam goes thus: प्रकृतिः – सभावः, स्वमॆव स्वभावमधिष्ठाय स्वॆनैव रूपॆण स्वॆच्छया सम्भवामीत्यर्थः । स्वस्वरूपं हि ’आदित्यवर्णं तमसः परस्तात्’, ’क्षयन्तमस्य रजसः पराकॆ’, ’य एषॊन्तरादित्यॆ हिरण्मयः पुरुषः’, ’तस्मिन्नयं पुरुषॊ मनॊमयः अमृतॊ हिरण्मयः’, ’सर्वॆ निमॆषा जज्ञिरॆ विद्युतः पुरुषादधि’, ’भारूपस्सत्यसङ्कल्प आकाशात्मा सर्वकर्मा सर्वकामस्सर्वगन्धस्सर्वरसः’, ’माहारजनं वासः’ इत्यादिश्रुतिसिद्धम् । …  
अतः अपहतपाप्मत्वादिसमस्तकल्याणगुणात्मकत्वं सर्वम् ऐशं स्वभावम् अजहत् स्वमॆव रूपं दॆवमनुष्यादिसजातीयसंस्थानं कुर्वन् आत्मसङ्कल्पॆन दॆवादिरूपः सम्भवामि ।

Here, Emberumanar clearly shows that all of Bhagavan’s descents are devoid of blemishes and replete with auspicious divine qualities, and that Bhagavan descends in this world without giving up on his supremacy.  
In this passage, svarupa means the divine form.

After a couple of slokas in between, he says ‘जन्म कर्म च मॆ दिव्यमॆवं यॊ वॆत्ति तत्त्वतः । त्यक्त्वा दॆहं पुनर्जन्म नैति मामॆति सॊऽर्जुन ॥”. In commenting on this sloka, Emberumanar makes it very clear with his words that Bhagavan’s tirumeni is apraakrta divyamangala vigraham only. His commentary for this sloka is as follows –  
एवं ***कर्ममूलहॆयत्रिगुणप्रकृतिसंसर्गरूपजन्मरहितस्य*** सर्वॆश्वरत्वसार्वज्ञ्यसत्यसङ्कल्पत्वादिसमस्तकल्याणगुणॊपॆतस्य साधुपरित्राणमत्समाश्रयणैकप्रयॊजनं ***दिव्यम्*** – ***अप्राकृतं मदसाधारणं मम जन्म***, चॆष्टितं च, तत्त्वतॊ यॊ वॆत्ति, स वर्तमानं दॆहं परित्यज्य पुनर्जन्म नैति, मामॆव प्राप्नॊति, ***मदीयदिव्यजन्मचॆष्टितयाथात्म्यविज्ञानॆन*** विध्वस्तसमस्तमत्समाश्रयणविरॊधिपापः, अस्मिन्नॆव जन्मनि यथॊदितप्रकारॆण मामाश्रित्य मदॆकप्रियॊ मदॆकचित्तॊ मामॆव प्राप्नॊति ।

Now, one may ask how this applies to archavatara, since this sloka is talking about deva, manushya and other avataras of Bhagavan. The answer to this question is in the eleventh sloka – ‘यॆ यथा मां प्रपद्यन्तॆ तांस्तथैव भजाम्यहम् । मम वर्त्मानुवर्तन्तॆ मनुष्याः पार्थ सर्वशः ॥’. This is a sloka that is usually attributed to the archa forms. Although neither Krishna nor Emberumanar explicitly show that this is for the archavatara, our Acharyas have shown this sloka for archaa forms without fail in their respective commentaries. The basis for this is in Emberumanar’s bhashyam, which reads thus:  
न कॆवलं दॆवमनुष्यादिरूपॆणावतीर्य मत्समाश्रयणापॆक्षाणां परित्राणं करॊमि, अपि तु यॆ मत्समाश्रयणापॆक्षाः यथा – यॆन प्रकारॆण स्वापॆक्षानुरूपं मां सङ्कल्प्य प्रपद्यन्तॆ – समाश्रयन्तॆ, तान् प्रति तथैव – तन्मनीषितप्रकारॆण भजामि – मां दर्शयामि । किमत्र बहुना, सर्वॆ मनुष्याः मदनुवर्तनैकमनॊरथाः मम वर्त्म – मत्स्वभावं सर्वं यॊगिनां वाङ्मनसागॊचरमपि स्वकीयैः चक्षुरादिकरणैः सर्वशः स्वापॆक्षितैः सर्वप्रकारैरनुभूयानुवर्तन्तॆ ।  
(I not only protect those who wish to surrender unto me by descending in deva, manushya and other forms, but also show myself in those very forms that such pure devotees desire; what is more, all humans who desire to reach me shall stay focussed on my divine nature, which is otherwise not known even to the great yogis, and constantly experience my divine forms and actions with all their senses.)

In this passage, Emberumanar makes it clear that Bhagavan is not just taking avataras in deva, manushya and other forms, but also in any form that a devotee so desires. When he says ‘दॆवमनुष्यादिरूपॆणावतीर्य’, the word ‘aadi’ itself suffices to show the other two types of yonis – tiryak and sthaavara. So this word itself covers all the possible yonis. Then what other forms is Bhagavan talking about in this present sloka? That is where our Acharyas show that it is the archavatara. Bhagavan doesn’t just stop at descending in the form of deva, manushya, tiryak or sthaavara (in other words, vibhava avatara), but also descends in this world in the form of archaa forms, taking up any form that a devotee desires. Emberumanar also carefully uses the word ‘तन्मनीषितप्रकारॆण’ to subtly show that this is pointing at the archaa forms. After all, didn’t the veda vakya say ‘हृदा मनीषा मनसाभिक्लृप्तः’ as seen previously? The word maniishaa shows the desire of the devotee. This is exactly translated by Azhwar when he says ‘தமருகந்ததெவ்வுருவம் அவ்வுருவம் தானே’. So, going by context, this sloka clearly shows how Bhagavan’s divine auspicious qualities apply to archaa forms also. In other words, Emberumanar is also of the opinion that archaa forms are made of shuddhasattva dravyam only.

One may say that this particular sloka differentiates Bhagavan’s nature from the previous slokas, in the sense that Bhagavan takes up archaa forms as praakrta dravyam only. The basis for such argument is Emberumanar’s words ‘न कॆवलं’ and ‘अपि तु’. Their argument is that Emberumanar opines that Bhagavan not only descends with this shuddhasattva tirumeni, but also gladly accepts the praakrta forms as they are. But this argument cannot be accepted because there is no pramana for such an opinion. Emberumanar, who showed seven veda vakyas as pramanas for showing Bhagavan’s tirumeni as shuddhasattvamaya (in the ajopi sloka), should have ideally shown at least one pramana in support of the opinion that Bhagavan also accepts praakrta forms as they are. But there is no pramana taken in the commentary on this sloka at all. The entire commentary for this sloka is given above for one’s perusal. How, then, can we accept that Emberumanar is of such an opinion? If one argues in turn that this is clearly explained in Nampillai’s eedu when he takes this sloka as a pramana for seyyatamarai pasuram, then there is a fallacy of mutual dependency (अन्यॊन्याश्रयदॊषः). It doesn’t sound logical to argue that Emberumanar’s statement is the pramana for Nampillai, and Nampillai’s statement is the pramana for Emberumanar. We can accept that Emberumanar’s statement is a pramana for Nampillai, but there should be another external pramana for Emberumanar’s claims. Only then it can be accepted. Therefore, this line of argument stands refuted.
