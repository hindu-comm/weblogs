viśadavaak

Who all accept tiruvaradhanam in our daily worship?

Some of us may claim that we have the most elaborate collection of Azhwars and Acharyas tirumenis in our tiruvaradhanam, along with different and rare tirumenis of Perumal and his parivaram. Even some of the rare Acharyas or nityasuris might be found in some places. But if I ask you, “do you have the tirumenis of all of the 74 simhasanadhipatis, all of the 12000+ shishyas of emberumanar, all of the oraanvazhi acharyas, all of the ashta diggajas of mamunigal, all the nityasuris etc.”, I am sure no one will come forward to claim that they do possess all of these tirumenis in one place.

Then, one must say that they perform tiruvaradhanam only to the azhwars or Acharyas who are present at that time in that place. It is not wrong to say so, after all. But it is certainly incomplete in terms of worshipping all the servants of Emberumanar (or Azhwar). Isn’t it?

Sri Manavala Mamunigal precisely addressed this point in his grantham “jeeyarpadi tiruvaradhana kramam”. He showed us that we perform tiruvaradhanam to every single Acharyapurusha that ever existed. We perform tiruvaradhanam to every single Bhagavata that ever existed/exists. We perform tiruvaradhanam to every single muktatma and nityasuri that exists. Only then we go on to do tiruvaradhanam to Perumal and his consorts. Let us see how we do it.

In every step of the tiruvaradhanam, Mamunigal uses the word Sri along with the azhwar or acharya or even the nityasuri that we intend to do tiruvaradhanam to. For example, “Srimate ramanujaya namaha”, “SrimadbhyaH parankusha-parakaladibhyo namaH”, “Srimate vishwaksenaya namaH”, etc. This is how we include every single Acharya or Bhagavata in our tiruvaradhanam. The word ‘Sri’ usually refers to Srimahalakshmi in our sampradayam. Bhagavan is called ‘sriyahpati’, meaning ‘the husband of Sri’. However, the term doesn’t limit to Srimahalakshmi alone. It refers to anyone who serves. ‘shri~g sevaayaam’ is the dhaatu. The form ‘श्रयत इति श्रीः’ means that a certain person is called ‘Sri’ because they serve. In this context, every single shishya of an Acharya is called ‘Sri’, because they serve the Acharya in all possible ways. So, when we say “Srimate ramanujaya namaH”, for example, we are bowing down to Emberumanar – who is surrounded by his dear disciples. The word “srimat” shows this indispensible relationship of Emberumanar and his disciples. The ‘matup’ adjunct is a clear indicator of ‘eternal union’ (nityayoga). Emberumanar is eternally united with his dear disciples. They are his ‘Sri’. Therefore, he is called ‘srimat’. Such disciples are none other than Vaduganambigal, Mudaliyandan, Koorattazhwan, Embar, Anantazhwan, and so on. This is how we include all the 74 simhasanadhipatis and all the 12000+ disciples of Emberumanar (including disciples who were ladies). So there is nothing wrong in claiming that a person following the Jeeyarpadi tiruvaradhana kramam offers tiruvaradhanam to all these Acharyapurushas and bhagavatas, by being a limb to his own Acharyan. They do it while offering tiruvaradhanam to Emberumanar with the mantra “Srimate Ramanujaya Namaha”.

Alright, we can say that all direct disciples of Emberumanar are included in our tiruvaradhanam. But can we just randomly say, “I offer daily tiruvaradhanam to Nampillai (for example)”? He is not a direct disciple of Emberumanar in his vibhava form, after all! The answer to this is: It is certainly possible. Let us see how it happens.

The word ‘Sri’ was understood as the servitor in the previous paragraph. There is also another meaning to it. The form ‘श्रीयत इति श्रीः’ means that a certain person is called ‘Sri’ because they are being served. In the same example that we took earlier, let us say we call Tirumalai Anantazhwan as the ‘Sri’ who served Emberumanar. So the meaning that was applied earlier is that of being a ‘servitor’. However, the same word ‘Sri’ holds good, also because Anantazhwan had many such disciples for himself. He was being served by them all. Let’s say, Anantazhwan’s son was also one of his disciples, and therefore called ‘Sri’ (servitor or Anantazhwan). So, now, Anantazhwan is a servitor (Sri) of Emberumanar on one hand, and the one being served (Sri) by his disciple son on the other. In both ways, he is called ‘Sri’. The disciple of Anantazhwan is called ‘Sri’, because he would serve Anantazhwan on the one hand, and also be served by his own disciple at the next level. Just as Anantazhwan is forever in union with his Acharyan Emberumanar, so also he is forever united with his disciples. If we consider Anantazhwan as the paadukas of Emberumanar (as in Tirumala), then his disciples are also the paadukas of Anantazhwan, and so on. This heirarchy continues up to the current generation. In this way, every person in the heirarchy of disciples of Emberumanar is ‘Sri’ – both in the sense of a servitor as well as the one being served by the next line of disciples. This grows into a huge tree of Srivaishnava Acharyapurushas, which clearly extends all up to one’s own Acharya! So, there is absolutely nothing wrong in boldly claiming that one offers tiruvaradhanam to Parashara Bhattar, Nampillai, Nanjeeyar, Pillai lokacharyar, Vedantacharyar, or any of the Acharyapurushas that ever existed, whom one can name.

The same logic holds for the other Sri shabdas also. When we say “Srimadbhyah parankusha-parakaladibhyo namah”, we are including all the contemporaries of all Azhwars, who were their shishyas, as well as the later Acharyapurushas upto Emberumanar. Such Acharyapurushas include Nathamunigal and all his heirarchy of disciples up to Emberumanar. Similarly, when we say “Srimate vishwaksenaya namah”, we are including all the nityasuris and muktatmas upto Azhwars. In short, we are covering every single Srivaishnava in our daily tiruvaradhanam – be it any Acharyapurusha, or just a Bhagavata – starting from our own Acharya, upto the Nityasuris including Ananta-Garuda-Vishwaksena, et al. The sobre Jeeyarpadi tiruvaradhana kramam really covers such a vast group of Bhagavatas unto whom we offer tiruvaradhanam by being a limb to our own Acharyan! And all this is achieved with just a word ‘srimath’!
