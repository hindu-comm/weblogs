viśadavaak

We had a question in our circle of friends – whether it would be right to do kainkaryam to Tiruvengadamudaiyan, or to Periya Perumal. Each one had their own views and tastes, and hence started debating on those lines. I also wondered as to which one of these would be right. Here I record my train of thoughts.

Bhagavan Sriman Narayana has five forms, namely – para, vyuha, vibhava, antaryami and archaa. Of these, the archaa forms are considered the best of all due to his simplicity in those forms.

In the the Srivachanabhushanam, Pillailokacharyar shows that it is the archavataram where all Azhwars did prapatti, as Bhagavan showed all his auspicious qualities in the archavataram. The fullness of all his qualities is found in the archavatara.  
He says this in the mumukshuppadi:  
“இங்குச்சொன்ன ஸௌலப்யத்துக்கெல்லைநிலம் அர்ச்சாவதாரம்.  
…  
இவையெல்லாம் நமக்கு நம் பெருமாள் பக்கலிலே காணலாம்.”

This prapatti has two parts to it. This is very clearly shown in the dwaya mahamantram. The purvakhanda tells the first part, which is the act of surrendering to Perumal. The uttarakhanda tells us the second part, which is the fruit for such surrendrance. The fruit is nothing but eternal kainkaryam to the divine couple at all places and at all times, and in all states (of waking, sleeping, etc).

Our Acharyas show that all the Azhwars did prapatti in archavataram. Although Nammazhwar did one sharanagati in vibhava form as Krishna, the main sharanagati that fructified was with tiruvengadamudiyan – an archaa form of Bhagavan. One of the reasons for this is the fullness of auspicious qualities in the archaa forms. Pillailokacharyar shows this in his two sutras – “ஆழ்வார்கள் பலவிடங்களிலும் ப்ரபத்திபண்ணிற்றும் அர்ச்சாவதாரத்திலே. பூர்ணம் என்கையாலே யெல்லாகுணங்களும் புஷ்கலங்கள்.” So, their kainkaryam also would be to the archaa forms of Bhagavan, while spending the remaining days in this leela vibhuti. “இருக்கும் நாள் உகந்தருளின நிலங்களிலே ப்ரவணனாய் குணானுபவ கைங்கர்யங்களே பொழுதுபோக்காகையும்” is how Pillailokacharyar shows the characteristic of a Srivaishnava adhikari.

While there are 108 divyadeshams that are sung by Azhwars, our Acharyas have shown that it is Tirumalai where Perumal waits to accept kainkaryam of jeevatmas. That is where his completeness in accepting kainkaryam is shown. Periyavacchan pillai shows this in the shenniyongu vyakhyana avatarikai, thus:  
பர-வ்யூஹ-விபவ-அந்தர்யாமித்வ-அர்ச்சாவதாரங்களானவிடங்களிலெழுந்தருளி நின்றது – சேதனரைத்திருத்துகைக்கும், திருந்தினாரை யடிமைகொள்ளுகைக்குமிறே. அது பூர்ணமாகக்காணலாவது திருமலையிலெழுந்தருளி நிற்க்கிற நிலையிலேயிரே.

All azhwars sing tirumalai when it comes to seeking kainkaryam. For example, Nammazhwar’s ‘ozhivil kaalamellam’ decad is very popularly known for seeking such relentless kainkaryam toawrds Him. He submits that prayer to Tiruvengadamudaiyan. Kulashekharapperumal, who is very fond of Periya perumal, sings ‘ooneru selvam’ decad, seeking kainkaryam; and that is dedicated to Tirumalai. Tirumangai azhwar’s ‘taaye tandai’ padigam is a very moving decad, where he prays for eternal kainkaryam unto Tiruvengadamudaiyan. Then what is so special about Tirumalai? Why not sing the same pasurams to Tiruvarangam and seek that kainkaryam at the lotus feet of Periya perumal?

The answer to this lies in a simple statement of our Acharyas “ஸௌந்தர்யம் அந்தராயம்”. As seen in one of the previous blogposts here <https://visadavaak.wordpress.com/2023/03/29/agratah-prayayau-ramah/>, Bhagavan’s physical beauty comes in the way of kainkaryam. So our elders warn us to take care of ourselves and get into kainkaryam. After all, azhwar said “உயிர்காத்து ஆட்ச்செய்மின்”. If sufficient care is not exercised, then there is a chance that we will get washed away in the flood of his beauty. Our kainkaryam (and hence our swarupam) will be lost. For that not to happen, we must take sufficient care and keep ourselves away from the main distraction, which is His beauty!

Perumal’s beauty is unparalleled. However, the degree of his beauty differs with his posture. For example, there’s no doubt that he looks extremely beautiful while standing. But he looks even more beautiful while walking. That is why Sumitra had to warn her son Lakshmana about the beauty of Perumal while he walks. Even more beautiful is his posture when he is seated on his throne. The way he folds his legs, the way he places his hands, the way he holds the divine weapons – are all a sight to behold. Azhagiya Manavala Perumal Nayanar says this in the Tiruppavai vyakhyanam: “கண்வளருகிறபோதையழகும் உலாவுகிறபோதையழகும் கண்டால் இருக்கும்போதையழ்கும் காணவேணுமிறே”. He says, our eyes feel fulfilled only when we see Him seated in the throne! Just seeing the other postures will not suffice!

But his reclining posture is beyond all words! “श्रीमान् सुखसुप्तः” says the Sriramayanam. It is only when he reclines that he shows his real wealth of beauty! Tondaradippodi Azhwar says “கிடந்ததோர் கிடக்கை”, which means his reclining is unparalleled. Our Acharyas call it ‘अद्वितीयशयनम्’. In all other postures, we can reclaim our senses from His beauty. But that is never possible with his reclining form. That is why Tiruppanazhwar sung “என்னமுதினைக்கண்ட கண்கள் மற்றொன்றினைக்காணாவே”. It was just not possible for Azhwar to get his sight back from Periya Perumal. If Perumal goes on to show such mammoth quantities of physical beauty, then how would a jeevatma ever serve him without falling in that huge pit?

Namperumal’s beauty while in his standing position is sung beautifully by Parashara Bhattar in his Srirangaraja stavam. He says :  
अग्रॆ तार्क्ष्यॆण पश्चादहिपतिशयनॆनात्मना पार्श्वयॊश्च  
श्रीभूमिभ्यामतृत्प्या नयनचुलकनैः सॆव्यमानामृतौघम् ।  
वक्त्रॆणाविस्मितॆन स्फुरदभयगदाशङ्खचक्रैर् भुजाग्रैः  
विश्वस्मै तिष्ठमानं शरणमशरणा रङ्गराजं भजामः ॥

Bhattar says that Namperumal’s beauty is such that Vainateyar who was standing in front of him sat down, unable to contain his beauty! The two consorts Sri and Bhudevis on the two sides of Namperumal couldn’t get enough of his beauty, and they wanted to enjoy more. So they also sat down. After all, Namperumal is “munnilum pinnazhagiya perumal”. So his beauty on his backside is more than that on the front side. So He wanted to have that lion’s share in enjoying His own beauty, and therefore lied down at the back in the form of Periya perumal. All these people couldn’t get enough of Namperumal’s beauty. So they made themselves comfortable and started enjoying more and more.

If Namperumal’s beauty in his standing posture is so much, then what to say about Periya Perumal’s reclining posture? Bhattar says:  
“शॆषशयलॊचनामृतनदी-  
रयाकुलितलॊलमानानाम्  
आलम्बमिवामॊदस्थम्भ-  
द्वयमन्तरङ्गमभियामः “

The two pillars called ‘Amodastambha’s are there so that we can take their support when we feel we are being washed away by the flood of Periya Perumal’s kataaksham! The lochanaamrtam of Periya perumal means both – his anugraham towards us (kataaksham) as well as the beauty in his eyes (pundareekaakshatvam). It looks like Periya perumal already knew that people wouldn’t be able to stand the inundation of his reclining beauty, and therefore emerged along with the Amodastambhas (apart from the pranavakara vimanam)! But inspite of those pillars being present, it becomes quite difficult for people to gather themselves after having a vision of His reclining beauty! Such is the beauty of His sheshashaayitvam!

The truth of this statement can be seen from the Sriramayana sloka, which says:  
गतॆ पुरॊहितॆ रामः स्नात्वा नियतमानसः  
सहपत्न्या विशालाक्ष्या नारायणमुपागमत्

This is the first sloka in the sixth sarga of Ayodhyakanda. Just when Rama’s coronation was announced, Dasharatha sent Vasishta and others to Rama’s residence and made him take up some vows (deeksha). That night was spent by Rama, worshipping their kuladhanam Periya Perumal (who was called ‘Narayana’, back then). After all, it is a well known fact that Periya Perumal was the tiruvaradhana perumal for all Ikshvaku rulers until Rama, who then passed on the perumal to Vibheeshanazhwan, who in turn established him in periya kovil. While doing the tiruvaradhanam to Narayana thus, Rama was accompanied by his wide-eyed wife Seetha. What is to be noted here is the usage of the word ‘vishalakshi’ for Seetha.

Two questions should be answered at this point. One – why did Rama want Seetha to accompany him in his tiruvaradhanam? Two – why was Seetha wide-eyed during the tiruvaradhanam?

The reason for Seetha to accompany Rama in tiruvaradhanam: The popularly known reason is that she would assist Rama in the tiruvaradhanam by providing him with all the necessary things for the tiruvaradhanam, cooking bhogam for perumal, etc.

Why was she wide-eyed during the tiruvaradhanam? Again, the popular story that everyone knows is that Seetha was so engrossed in Sri Rama’s beauty and poise while he did the tiruvaradhanam.

However, Govindarajar comments a little differently in his commentary named “Sriramayana bhushanam”. He gives a different view for both of these questions. Let us examine them now.

First, the reason he gives for Seetha accompanying Rama in his tiruvaradhanam is that Rama wanted company in crossing over the ocean of Periya Perumal’s vigraha anubhavam. He says, Periya Perumal’s tirumeni anubhavam is like a huge ocean where one needs support and company of other people whom one can hold on to. Without such company or support, one is sure to drown in the ocean. सहपत्न्यॆति श्रीरङ्गविग्रहानुभवसागरतरणस्य सहायॊक्तिः \|

For the second question on Seetha being wide-eyed, Govindarajar says that Seetha was more enchanted with the beauty of Periya Perumal’s tirumeni. This enchanting beauty of Periya Perumal was much more than even Rama’s beauty. So, she didn’t want to blink her eyes, lest she loses that divine experience for a small fraction of a second. विशालाक्ष्यॆति रामविग्रहानुभवादपि श्रीरङ्गविग्रहस्य निरतिशयप्रियास्पदत्वॆन निर्निमॆषानुभव उक्तः ।

Alright, so Periya Perumal was more likeable to Seetha than Rama himself. What about Rama? Did he lose himself in experiencing Seetha’s beautiful eyes? Well, this could have been true at other times. However, when they were before Periya Perumal, Rama would not be caught in the trap of Seetha’s eyes. He would turn towards Periya Perumal to experience his divine beauty while reclining on the serpent bed. SO, Seetha was secondary to him at that time, and Periya Perumal was his primary attraction. विशालाक्ष्या नारायणमिति सीतानॆत्रवागुरानाकृष्टतया सीताया अप्राधान्यम् । नारायणस्य स्वाभीप्सिततमत्वॆन प्राधान्यं चॊक्तम् ।

From this, we learn that Periya Perumal is the one who enchanted even Rama and Seetha themselves, to the extent that they would not even look at each other while experiencing Periya Perumal! If this is the case with Rama and Seetha themselves, what is to be told about weaklings like us? We will certainly get trapped in his beauty and forget kainkaryam, beyond any doubt! So, Perumal – out of His unbounded compassion – stood in the Tiruvengadam to accept all kainkaryams. After all, He chose to remain in the posture that would least affect a seeking jeevatma, while He accepted their kainkaryam.

So, looking at it in this angle, kainkaryam to Tiruvengadamudaiyan makes more sense than kainkaryam to Periya Perumal.

PS: Just because I say kainkaryam to Tiruvengadamudaiyan makes more sense, it doesn’t mean that I neglect Periya Perumal. It just means that it makes more sense for me to stay safe and protect my identity. After all, Tiruvengadamudaiyan only lies down on the serpent bed as Ranganayakan! So, there you go.
