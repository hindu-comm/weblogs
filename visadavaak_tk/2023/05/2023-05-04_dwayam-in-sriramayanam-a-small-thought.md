viśadavaak

Yesterday was Chittirai hastham – Devapperumal’s tirunakshatram. His avataara utsavam is conducted with great pomp and glory at Perumal Kovil. Devapperumal is known as the one who shows the meaning of dwaya mahamantram. Alavandar instructed to his disciples during his last moments in this leela vibhuti that Periya perumal shows the meaning of tirumantram, Devapperumal shows dwayam and Tiruvengadamudiayan shows charamaslokam. Devapperumal is said to be showing the meaning of dwayam, because He is always seated with his consort Perundevi tayar. The way he accepts the utsavams along with Tayar is a sight to behold.

So here is a little blog for his tirunakshatram day, in an attempt to contemplate upon the dwaya mahamantram.

Sriramayanam is known as the extended version of dwaya mahamantram. You can find the details here: <https://m.facebook.com/story.php?story_fbid=pfbid0tKZHaWdDmdAamzMXAjv5VNJy3Wt2WpGUQAsMSR4iBNYdPaYbAztN72bw6XbykVcul&id=100055337926363&mibextid=Nif5oz>  
In this post, I had shown from Govindarajeeya vyakhyanam as to how the seven kandas correspond to the purva and uttara khandas of the dwaya mahamantram.  
Today we will see the meanings of dwaya mahamantram shown in the Sriramayanam in a single sarga. In the 31st sarga of Ayodhyakanda, Lakshmana pleads with Srirama to take him along to the forest. The entire sarga is a conversation between Srirama and Lakshmana, debating on whether or not Lakshmana should go to the forest with Srirama. Finally, Srirama agrees to take Lakshmana along. But what captivates our minds is the words of Lakshmana, which precisely show the meanings of the dwaya mahamantra.

Valmiki gives an account of what Lakshmana did, at the very beginning of the sarga. He says: “स भ्रातुश्चरणौ गाढं निपीड्य रघुनन्दनः । सीतामुवाचातियशा राघवं च महाव्रतम् ॥”  
It means that Lakshmana held on tightly to the lotus feet of Srirama, and looked at Seetha and talked to her. He then talked to Srirama also. This sloka itself shows the meaning in the purva khanda of dwaya mahamantra. To put it in short, holding on tightly to the feet of Srirama shows his total surrendrance, and shows Srirama as the sole refuge for everyone. At the same time, talking to Seetha before turning to Rama shows that he accepted her as the purushakara or the mediator. So, in effect, this shows the meaning of the purva khanda.

Similarly, Valmiki also quotes Lakshmana’s words towards the end of the sarga, where Lakhmana says “भवांस्तु सह वैदॆह्या गिरिसानुषु रंस्यते । अहं सर्वं करिष्यामि जाग्रतः स्वपतश्च तॆ ॥”  
In this sloka, Lakshmana says that he will follow Srirama and Seetha to the forest and serve them in all ways possible. He requests Srirama to allow him to serve them at all places, at all times and in all states (sleeping, waking, etc.). This is effectively the meaning of the uttara khanda of dwaya mahamantra.

All these points are popularly known. However, one point needs to be mentioned, which is not as popularly known as the meanings that we saw above.

The dwaya mahamantra is found in the kathavalli upanishat. Our Acharyas have shown that the two statements in the dwaya mahamantra are not consecutive in the upanishat. There are a few other statements in between the two. The other statements that are sandwiched between the two khandas are: “idam purnam adah purnam purnaat purnamudrchyate…”. These statements essentially show the completeness of Bhagavan both in terms of being easily accessible, as well as being the sheshi (the one who accepts servitude) at the same time. Our Acharyas show that this completeness of Bhagavan is shown in the archaa forms more than any other forms. So, now a question arises whether such a thing is seen in the Sriramayana sarga also, in between the two aforesaid slokas, to the same effect. The answer can be seen in another sloka that comes just before the “bhavaamstu…”. The beautiful sloka goes thus: “कुरुष्व मामनुचरं वैधर्म्यं नॆह विद्यते । कृतार्थॊहं भविष्यामि तवचार्थः प्रकल्पते ॥”  
This is also a statement of Lakshmana, where he shows the completeness of Srirama, just as in the kathavalli upanishat. The meaning of this sloka is: “Make me your servitor. There is no lack of dharma in us. I shall become accomplished by serving you for your pleasure alone”. The word ‘vaidharmyam’ is the key. Govindarajar comments on this word thus: वैधर्म्यम् – सॆव्यसॆवकधर्मराहित्यम् । तन्नास्ति ।  
He says, vaidharmya is essentially the lack of ‘servitude’ and ‘mastership’ in the two entities jeevatma and paramatma respectively. That lacuna is not present here. Servitude rests fully with Lakshmana, and mastership rests fully with Rama. This itself shows the completeness told in the kathavalli upanishat. Only after showing this completeness, he goes on to say “bhavaamstu…”. The rest of the slokas before this ‘kurushva…’ is just building the conversation up to this sloka.

So, these three slokas become the key in this sarga.  
1) sa bhraatuh = purva khanda  
2) kurushva maam = idam purnam…  
3) bhavaamstu = uttara khanda

All other slokas are only contextual, and lead to these slokas.

Together, this sarga (2-31) shows the meaning of the dwaya mahamantra in exactly the same way that the kathavalli upanishat shows us.
