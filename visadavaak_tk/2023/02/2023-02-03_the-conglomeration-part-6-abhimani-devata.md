viśadavaak

In the previous post, we saw that the first assumption of an ‘abhimani devata’ is that he/she should identify themselves with an achit. That means to say that the abhimani devata should be the one chetanan who is denoted by the “I”ness of that achit. This is much similar to saying that I – the jeevatma – am denoted by the “I”ness of my body. To examine the truth or falsity of this assumption, we should check the astra-bhushana-adhyaya of Sri Vishnu Purana.

In the first amsha 22nd chapter of Vishnu Purana, Parashara maharshi shows how Bhagavan bears all the sentient and insentient entities in His own divine body in the form of divine weapons and divine ornaments. That is how this chapter has come to be known as ‘astra-bhushana-adhyaya’. In this chapter, Parashara Maharshi says that Bhagavan bears all of these in His divine body through their respective abhimani devatas. Starting from the 68th sloka in this chapter, he draws a parallel between the astra-bhushanas and the twenty five tattvas (comprising the chit-achit mass). In the 68th sloka, he shows that the kaustubha mani that Bhagavan wears is the abhimani of jeevatma.  
The sloka goes thus:  
आत्मानमस्य जगतो निर्लेपमगुणामलम् । बिभर्ति कौस्तुभमणिस्वरूपं भगवान्हरिः ॥

Engalazhwan comments on this sloka, beginning thus: प्रथममात्मनोऽभिमान्यधिदैवतमाह – आत्मानमिति ।  
He says, “At first, the abhimani adhidevata of aatma (jeevatma) is told with the sloka aatmaanam etc”. Much to our surprise, Parashara Maharshi shows an achit ‘kaustubha gem’ as the adhidevata abhimani of a chit (jeevatma). If we apply our assumption at this place, then we should have ideally shown a chetana entity as abhimani of an achetana entity, whereas Parashara Maharshi shows the exact opposite of it. In no way we can accept that an achetana identifies itself with the chetana (or in other words, that an achetana entity is denoted by the “I”ness of the chetana entity). An insentient object can never claim to be the ‘self’ of another entity – sentient or insentient.

Also, if we are to accept Parashara Maharshi’s words as a whole fact along with the aforesaid assumption, the jeevatma would never identify itself as ‘I’ anymore; because, whenever a jeevatma says “I”, it would denote the kaustubha gem and not the jeevatma itself! Kaustubha is the abhimani adhidevata, you see? So, the whole point of calling a jeevatma as ‘chit’ or sentient entity, and claiming that the jeevatma has svasmai bhaasamaanatvam goes for a toss!

Then how are we to reconcile the fact that Parashara Maharshi calls kaustubha gem as an abhimani of jeevatma?

It is simple. We just need to accept the fact that our initial assumption is wrong. That is to say, abhimani devata does not necessarily show a jeevatma who is denoted by the “I”ness of an achit. I am not saying the assumption is completely wrong. I only mean that it is not universally true. When I say that Suryadeva is the abhimani devata of the orb called ‘sun’, then indeed he is a jeevatma who is denoted by the “I”ness of that achit orb. In that case, the assumption holds true. However, in case of kaustubha being the abhimani devata of jeevatma, the assumption doesn’t hold true. So, the assumption is not a complete one. It does not give a complete definition of an abhimani devata.

From this, we get to learn that abhimani devata is something/someone who represents another entity – sentient or insentient. For example, a bureaucrat can represent a whole nation in any international meeting. There, the bureaucrat becomes the abhimani of an entire nation, and anything told by them is considered as told by the people of that nation itself. Similarly, a sports person represents a nation. An MLA represents an entire area. In all these cases, the abhimani jeevatma represents an entire community, and identifies itself with that community. But it does not claim sole ownership of any achit entity. These “abhimani” people represent the nation/area/etc even while the people in those respective communities individually identify themselves with the term “I”. So abhimani jeeva can identify itself with an achit along with one or more jeevatmas identifying themselves with the same achit, simultaneously.

Secondly, we should see if the other assumption that an abhimani devata is essentially of a ‘deva’ birth. In the same example as shown above, the kaustubha mani is an achit, and not of any of these births at all. In fact, the deva-manushya-etc births are only for baddhatmas and not for nityatmas. Kaustubha is an apraakrta achit which does not have any of these births. Even if we take the chetana adhisthata of that kaustubha gem, that chetanan is also a nityatma, and therefore does not have a deva/manushya/other births. Yet, it is called by the name ‘abhimani adhidevata’. Then what does it mean? It simply means that “the one which is recognized with the expression ‘jeevatma'”. Here, the word devata only means something/someone that is recognized with a certain expression. “दिवु व्यवहृतौ ” is the dhatu that should be applied. Vyavahara means talking about something using a certain expression. If something can be known through an expression that is used as a name of another entity, then that thing can be called ‘abhimani devata’. That expression showing kaustubha in this case is “jeevatma”. Therefore, kaustubha is called abhimani devata of jeevatmas, as we can know kaustubha gem (among ornmaents) while speaking about jeevatmas. In the other examples, we know the bureaucrat/sportsperson/et al. through the expression “India” or any other nation. Therefore, they become the respective abhimani devatas. So, an abhimani devata is not necessarily of a deva birth. He/she/it can be of any birth (including deva), and yet be called ‘abhimani devata’.

Considering these arguments, we can safely claim that –  
a) an abhimani devata can identify itself with an achit even alongside another (or many other) jeevatma(s) identifying with the same achit simultaneously, and  
b) that the abhimani devata doesn’t necessarily mean a jeevatma of deva birth.

This invalidates the claim that all the achit that we see around is identified by an ‘abhimani devata’ only, and not an indwelling jeeva. Let us see further about this topic in the forthcoming posts.
