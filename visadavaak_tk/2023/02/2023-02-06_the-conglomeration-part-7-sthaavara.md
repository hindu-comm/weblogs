viśadavaak

Further to our discussions in the previous posts, we need to see what ‘sthaavara’ means, in our sampradayam. The word sthaavara is popularly translated as ‘plants’ or flora in general. This is how most of the other matasthas also translate ‘sthaavara’. In fact, when our Acharyas show that Bhagavan takes sthaavara avataras too, just like deva, manushya and tiryak, they indeed show a kubjaamra (dwarf mango tree). Agreed. Bhagavan takes birth in all yonis. As deva, He took birth as vamana and others. As manushya, He was born as Rama, Krishna and others. As tiryak, he was born as matsya, kurma, varaha and other animals. Similarly, as tiryak, he was born as kubjaamra ‘and others’. This is how our Acharyas have shown. Most people take this illustration and claim that Bhagavan came down only as kubjaamra while taking the sthaavara yoni. But what they miss to see is the phrase ‘and others’ (kubjaamraadi) that is shown by our Acharyas. If kubjaamra was the only sthaavara shareera, then they would not be using it to show ‘etcetera’. There would be no need to do so.

Then, you may ask, why would the Acharyas show only kubjaamra and not name others? Well, why would our Acharyas always say “ananta-garuda-vishvaksenaadi” for nityasuris? Is there a dearth of nityasuris? Not at all. Is it that our Acharyas are not aware of more than these three? I would suggest the reader to go through Emberumanar’s nityagrantham or Manavala Mamunigal’s commentaries on Mumukshuppadi and tattvatrayam to get a glimpse of what they knew. Then why is it that they always stuck to these popular names? It is because they are ‘popular’. The answer lies just there. There is no use in showing off one’s knowledge while the real focus should be on ensuring knowledge transfer. As long as they know, the purpose is fulfilled. That is the only reason they would stick to those names. The same holds here as well. Kubjaamra is known and used widely by Acharyas before our Guru paramparaa times, and hence the later Acharyas also promptly used the same.

Also, a word can derive its meaning in two ways – ruudhi and yoga. Ruudhyartha is the popular meaning, and yaugikaartha is the technical meaning. In practice, we call a lotus as pankaja. But technically even an insect or a worm growing alongside the same lotus should also be called panjaka, because that is also growing in marsh (panke jaatam pankajam). But people only understand pankaja as lotus, and not a worm/insect/mushroom/weed. That is called ‘ruudhi’ or ‘popular’ meaning. Same holds with sthaavara as well. The most popular meaning is ‘plants’, agreed. But nowhere have our Acharyas explicitly mentioned that it stops at that, and doesn’t indicate a rock or a wood.

Now comes another question. Is there an example of Bhagavan’s avatara in any other form apart from kubjaamra? I shall show you. Bhagavan took birth as a teertha pond in Pushkara kshetra. He took birth as a forest in Naimisharanyam. Most popularly, He is present even today in each of our homes. He is in the form of lakhs and crores of big/small/huge/tiny salagrama srimurthis found in the famous Gandaki river near Muktinath. Here He is present in the form of a stone itself. Isn’t that enough? NOTE: I am not equating srimurthy to a stone. But I am just explaining in laukika standard just to drive home the point that sthaavara is not just plants! Bhagavan should only kindly excuse me for using this phrase!!

Oh wait! Some say stone is not sthaavara! Only plant is sthaavara! Then how valid is my claim here? Let us examine.

First, let us get back to our refuge Sri Vishnu purana. In the sixth amsha seventh adhyaya (the same chapter that we saw earlier about keshidhwaja-khandikya samvaada), keshidhwaja says this: There are three bhavanas (mindsets). One is karmabhavana, another is brahmabhavana and the third one is ubhayabhavana. Karmabhavana is what most of the present day vaidikas possess. It is the mindset with which a person considers performance and officiating of vedic rituals as their sole duty. Since their focus is on karma or actions, it is called karmabhavana. The second type – brahmabhavana – is what great devotees of Bhagavan possess. It is the mindset with which such people never care about any worldly benefits and fixate their minds on Parabrahmam alone. Their sole aim is to contemplate upon that Supreme being, and preaching about Him. Nothing else matters to them. The third type of mindset – called Ubhayabhavana – is a mixture of karma and brahma bhavanas. Such people are like the devas; say, for example, the four faced brahma. He is continuing his ordained duties and yet contemplating upon Bhagavan at some times. Having mentioned about these three types of mindsets, Keshidhwaja continues to take examples of who possesses which type of mindset. In that context, he says this:  
सनन्दनादयो ये तु ब्रह्मभावनया युताः । कर्मभावनया चान्ये देवाद्यास्स्थावरावराः ॥ (६-७-५०)

Our Engalazhwan promptly comments on this sloka, thus:  
केषां का भावनेत्यत्राह – सनन्दनादय इति । स्थावरावराः – स्थावरचरमाः । स्थावराणां हिमवदादीनां हि कर्मस्वधिकारः श्रूयते । बिल्वा मार्दङ्गिका ह्यासन् क्रीडन्त्योषधयस्सदा इति च \|

Here, he gives the meaning of sthaavara-avara as ‘sthaavara and charama’. Amongst the sthaavaras, he says, himavan and others indeed have eligibility for karma, and therefore they have the karmabhavana. Wait, shouldn’t Engalazhwan be taking an example of plant alone? Why is he bringing a mountain himavan in the scene? Of course, he gives an example of plants in the same sloka, agreed. But if sthaavara meant only ‘plants’ in our sampradayam, then Engalazhwan, who does not deviate from the Sri Sampradayam, should ideally have quoted just the plants for example. But he says himavaan is sthaavara. Moreover, he says only a few such sthaavara-bodied jeevas have such eligibility (probably due to a previous great karma?). The key to get this right is in the phrase “स्थावरचरमाः”. Charama means movable entity. The opposite of it is sthaavara – a stationary entity. That is all. The root ‘shthaa’ (ष्ठा गतिनिवृत्तौ) gets the varach pratyaya, and after suitable modifications, it results in the form ‘sthaavara’. So it just means ‘anything which is stationary by default’.

Good for a definition. But how do we apply it in our case? Let us see in the next post.
