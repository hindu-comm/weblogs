Further, Nampillai explains the words of Parasara Bhattar “अर्च्यः सर्वसहिष्णुरर्चकपराधीनाखिलात्मस्थ्तितिः” to show the extent of Bhagavan’s soulabhyam in archaa forms. He also explains the “यॆ यथा मां प्रपद्यन्तॆ” sloka of Bhagavad Gita. He quotes Poigai Azhwar’s famous pasuram “தமருகந்ததெவ்வுருவம் அவ்வுருவம் தானே” at this point. He also quotes the Vishnupurana sloka “पापं हरति यत्पुंसां स्मृतं सङ्कल्पनामयम्”. All these show how Nampillai draws a parallel in all the said pramanas to show one thing – the divine qualities in archaa forms. Exactly at that place, Nampillai says this: Bhagavan resides in paramapadam with his divyamangala vigraha, which is called ‘asaadhaarana vigraha’ (this is the shuddhasattvamaya vigraha). When Bhagavan descends in vibhava avataras, he makes the same tirumeni similar to other forms that he is taking (deva, manushya, tiryak, sthaavara). That means, his divine forms remain shuddhasattvamaya in vibhava, but the forms are similar (in looks and behaviour) to the deva, manushya and others, according to his birth. Unlike these, if an aashrita requests Bhagavan to accept as his tirumeni a form that he has made with any substance that he desires (say, gold or silver or anything of his choice), Bhagavan accepts that itself as his divine form and considers that itself as his asaadhaarana vigraha. This is how Bhagavan comes in front of us (to accept us into his fold) in the archaa forms.

The exact words of Nampillai at this place are as follows: “பரமபதத்தில் அஸாதாரணவிக்ரஹத்தோடும் குணங்களோடுமிருக்கும்; விபவங்களில் வந்தால் அதுதன்னை இதரஸஜாதீயமாக்கிற்றாயிருக்கும். இங்கனன்றிக்கே, ஆஶ்ரிதன் தானுகப்பது ஏதேனுமொரு த்ரவ்யத்தை திருமேனியாகக்கோலினால் அத்தையே தனக்குத் திருமேனியாகக்கொண்டு அதிலே அஸாதாரணவிக்ரஹத்தில் பண்ணக்கடவ அபிமானத்தைப்பண்ணி இவ்வழியாலே முகங்கொடுக்கும்படியாயிறே இந்நிலையிருப்பது. “

The scholars who opine that griharcha forms are praakrta take this exact passage of Nampillai and say that Nampillai and other Acharyas are of a clear opinion that grihaarcha is different from the other shuddhasattvamaya vigrahas. For them, the interpretation of the word ‘abhimanam’ is ‘liking’. That means to say, Bhagavan just likes the praakrta forms offered to him by his devotees as much as (or even more than) the shuddhasattvamaya tirumeni in Srivaikuntha. It does not mean that the form itself becomes shuddhasattvamaya, but just that Bhagavan likes that form to that extent. Nampillai says “அதிலே அஸாதாரணவிக்ரஹத்தில் பண்ணக்கடவ அபிமானத்தைப்பண்ணி”, and not “அத்தை அஸாதாரணவிக்ரஹமாகவே கொண்டு”. They even quote the arumpadam to this passage, and say that the northern school of thought (of Vedantacharya and others) which says that Bhagavan descends in these archaa forms with a shuddhasattva tirumeni is thus refuted with these words of Nampillai. So, their conclusion is that Nampillai clearly differentiates between shuddhasattva tirumeni and grihaarchaa forms.

The arumpadam commentary to this passage is given here for your reference: “(அஸாதாரணவிக்ரஹத்தில் பண்ணக்கடவ அபிமானத்தைப்பண்ணி) என்கையாலே – “ஸவிக்ரஹனாய் பிம்பத்தை அதிஷ்டிக்கும்” என்கிற வடவாசார்யர்களிற்காட்டில் வ்யாவ்ருத்தி பலிக்கிறது…. “परव्यूहादि विग्रहवत् तत्तद्विग्रहानुप्रवॆशादर्चापि षाड्गुण्यप्रकाशकशुद्धसत्त्वद्रव्यमयीत्यर्थः” என்றிரே தூப்பிற்பிள்ளை கத்யவ்யாக்யானத்திலே அருளிச்செய்தது.”

We have to again examine the truth or falsity of this interpretation to this passage. For this, we should not independently interpret the passage, but take help of other works of our Acharyas and learned scholars. Nampillai says “இங்கனன்றிக்கே, ஆஶ்ரிதன் தானுகப்பது ஏதேனுமொரு த்ரவ்யத்தை திருமேனியாகக்கோலினால் அத்தையே தனக்குத் திருமேனியாகக்கொண்டு “. In explaining this concept, we had seen from Arthapanchakam in an earlier post that Pillailokam jeeyar writes these words: “….. ….. ஆஶ்ரிதருகந்தவையான ஸுவர்ண ரஜத தாம்ர தாரு சிலா ம்ருண்மயங்களான த்ரவ்யங்களாலே யாதொரு திருமேனியை கோருகிறார்களோ, அப்படியே விலக்ஷணத்ரவ்யமென்றும் அவிலக்ஷணத்ரவ்யமென்றும் பாராமல் தனக்குத்திருமேனியாக பரிக்ரஹிக்குமவனாய்…… தனக்கு இந்தத்திருமேனி வேணும் இந்தத்திருமேனி வேணும் என்கிற நிர்பந்தமில்லாமலேயே ஆஶ்ரயித்தவர்கள் ஆனவர்கள் தாங்களுகந்து ஏதேனும் ஒரு த்ரவ்யத்தாலே விக்ரஹத்தைப் பண்ண என்றால் நல்லது என்று அப்படியே அந்தவிக்ரஹத்தில் அஸாதாரணமான அதிஷ்டானவிஶேஷத்தை அதிஷ்டித்து அதுவே தனக்கு அஸாதாரணமான திருமேனியாக பரிக்ரஹித்தும்…. .. . …. ..”

Also, we saw in the previous posts how Shuddhasattvam Doddaiyangar, Shuddhasattvam Embavaiyangar, Tirumazhisai Annavappangar and other learned scholars show that Bhagavan’s archaa forms are indeed made of shuddhasattva material only, in all their arumapadams to Mumukshuppadi and Srivachanabhushana vyakhyanams.

So, the reason why Nampillai is differentiating para and vibhava forms from archaa forms is that the para and vibhava are originally shuddhasattvamaya, whereas the archaa forms are composed of praakrta materials turned into shuddhasattva. Unlike the para and vibhava forms where Bhagavan decides how his divine form should be, he accepts any form that a devotee offers and takes that itself as his shuddhasattva tirumeni in the archaa forms. He doesn’t demand any particular material like gold or gems (owing to his greatness), but accepts literally anything that his devotees offer with love (like mud, or even photos or drawings) as his tirumeni. This is the real difference that Nampillai intends to show. In that pursuit, he uses the word ‘abhimanam’. So, looking at the context, and going by the other commentaries in similar passages, we understand that the word ‘abhimanam’ is not to be taken as ‘liking’, but as ‘consideration’. It is similar to saying ‘dehatmabhimanam’. We don’t interpret dehatmabhimanam as ‘liking the body as much as or even more than atma’, but we say ‘considering body itself as atma’. There is a clear difference in these two statements. Similarly, archaa forms are considered by Bhagavan himself as his asaadhaarana vigrahas. Can we dare say that the material remains praakrta even after Bhagavan considers it as apraakrta? In other words, Bhagavan does a sankalpam that this vigraham is apraakrta, but the material refuses to change! oh come on, how on earth is it possible? One may ask, here, as to how a praakrta material turns into apraakrta. Where do the prthvi, ap, tejas and other elements disappear? The answer is in Bhagavan’s aghatita-ghatanaa-saamarthyam. It is his ability to make something possible which we otherwise consider as generally impossible. To give an example, vasudeva was a baddha jeevatma, and devaki was a baddha jeevatma. They consummated their marriage, and begot eight children, the eighth one being Krishna. Do we accept that Krishna came with an apraakrta tirumeni? Yes! Of course! Who dares not? Then I ask – how did Vasudeva’s veerya and Devaki’s shonita (both being praakrta) turn into apraakrta form of Bhagavan? Where did the praakrta elements disappear when Bhagavan came there? It is purely His sankalpam alone, which can make anything possible. Same is the case with archaa forms as well.

Then a last question for this part: how do we understand the arumpadam when he says that Nampillai’s view is different from vedantacharyar’s school of thought? It is simple. Vedantacharyar says that Bhagavan descends in archaa forms along with a shuddhasattva tirumeni. So, it is like an object in a container. The container is praakrta itself, but the object contained within it is shuddhasattvamaya tirumeni. Whereas, Nampillai just says that the container itself is shuddhasattvamaya tirumeni, and the object contained in it is Bhagavan’s divyaatma svarupam. That is all the difference!
