viśadavaak

Continuing from the previous post, we are to see how the technical definition of sthaavara fits in our context. Let us examine that now.

Are plants stationary by default? Yes, they are. Are stones stationary by default? Yes, unless they do not fly away like the great mainaka mountain! Are logs of wood stationary by default? Yes, I would say. Are pots, mats, clothes, plates, tumblers, laptops, cables etc stationary by default? Why not!! Anyone to contend this claim? If not, then we can safely accept that ALL of these objects come under the head ‘sthaavara’. And just as there are no deva-manushya-tiryak shareeras without a jeevatma indwelling them, so also, a sthaavara shareera doesn’t exist with a certain name and form without a jeevatma indwelling it. This is also what Emberumanar has clearly showed in the vedartha sangraha statement “…काष्ठं शिला तृणं घटः पटः इत्यादयस्सर्वे प्रकृतिप्रत्यययोगेन अभिधायकतया प्रसिद्धाः शब्दाः लोके तत्तद्वाच्यतया प्रतीयमानतत्तत्संस्थानवस्तुमुखेन तदभिमानिजीव-तदन्तर्यामिपरमात्मपर्यन्तसंघातस्यैव वाचकाः”. It couldn’t have been more clearer! He spells out ‘kaashtam’, ‘shilaa’, ‘ghata’, ‘pata’ etc. Furthermore, Shrutaprakashika bhattar gives more names – such as ‘loshta’, etc. What more can we ask for? The ‘sanghaata’ of three entities namely achit samsthaana, abhimani jeeva and antaryami paramatma that Emberumanar is showing in this statement is what I am calling as ‘conglomeration’ in all these posts. The achit samsthaana is the achit framework for the names ghata, pata, kaashta, lostha, etc. In other words, they are the physical objects with forms that we see with those respective names. Abhimani jeeva is the indwelling jeevatma (remember – not abhimani devata, as seen in previous posts), and antaryami is the all-indwelling paramatma.

Also, if sthaavara did not include anything other than plants, then why would Kulashekhara azhwar yearn to get a birth as a foot path, waterfall or a rivulet, or even the threshold of Tiruvengadamudaiyan on Tirumala hills? Did Azhwar not know that they are not ‘bodies’ for us – the chetanas? Of course that is not possible. Azhwars were blessed by Bhagavan with the greatest expanse of knowledge. So their words would not go against the shastras whatsoever. And Azhwar proudly sings in his ‘uuneru selvam’ decad that he yearns to take any of these births in tirumala, so that he can remain there forever. That is because, they are all covered under the ‘sthaavara’ category (technically, and not popularly. In other words, by yaugikaartha and not ruudhyartha).

So, saying sthaavara just means plants, and that sthaavara bodies do not comprise of rocks, wood, mats, pots etc is clearly a false (or incomplete) statement. And therefore, the other claim that they do not have jeevatmas indwelling them is also refuted.

Moral of the story: All insentient things we see around fall under the ‘sthaavara’ category, and jeevatmas definitely indwell such bodies. And only by this virtue, they get the ‘name’ and ‘form’, as per the vedas, as Bhagavan is the indweller of all such entities.

“Then, there is a big problem awaiting us, which needs to resolved”, they said.  
The problem statement is: if there is a brick, then a jeevatma indwells it. Alright. If several such bricks are used to build a wall, then that structure is no longer called a brick. It is now called a ‘wall’. Will a jeevatma indwell that wall, because the name and form are different? I say, “Oh yes! Why not!”. The next immediate question I am faced with is “then what happened to the jeevatma in the brick?”

Well, my friend – there is something called as avayava-avayavi sambandha. It is the relationship between a part and the whole possessing that part. IN that sense, as long as an object is an ‘avayava’ or a part, the jeevatma in that body is not used to identify the whole (avayavi). To put it in understandable language, lets say we have a nail, hair, etc. As long as they are attached to our body, we are indeed the controlling/identifying jeevatmas for those entities. However, they still possess a name and form. So, the question is: do they have jeevatmas already in them?

Countless jeevatmas are definitely there inside various parts of our body. Let us have no doubt about it. It is just that “I” the jeevatma am the controller of this entire body, and hence the other jeevatmas do not get that importance. Not seeing them as the main/important jeevatma doesn’t mean their absence in anyway. In that sense, even a nail, hair and every other cell will have one or more jeevatmas sitting there. But as long as they are the avayava or part of my body, I am the controller. THe moment they are cut off from my body, another jeevatma gains that importance in that severed part, and henceforth that is the adhishtata which considers that nail/hair/whatever as its new “sthaavara” body. Some people say a new jeevatma enters the falling hair or nail. Some others say one of the indwelling jeevas itself becomes the new shareeri. In either case, the fact is that a new jeevatma gains the adhishtata role in that severed part. It is no longer an ‘avayava’.

The same logic applies to the brick example as well. The brick doesn’t lose its name or form just because it is used in building a wall. So the jeevatma definitely remains in it. It however doesn’t become the main controller of the entity called ‘wall’. There is a different jeevatma for that purpose. However, if the same wall is broken down and if we are fortunate enough to get the brick in the same undamaged form, then that jeevatma still remains. That is how we continue to call it ‘brick’ (the same name). The adhishtata jeevatma exits only when it is broken. Once it is broken, it gets a different name and form.

Enough said about the conglomeration. Let us get back to the original question at hand in the next post!  
Bhagavan’s shuddhasatva tirumeni…

PS: Why did we delve so deep in knowing whether there is a jeevatma in every achit or not? The reason is twofold:  
a) We need to realize that many jeevatmas are locked in such bodies due to their karma. So it is a warning to the other chetanas (not just mumukshus) that they should abide by the scriptures to avoid such births.  
b) We also need to realize that irrespective of which jeevatma resides in a particular achit, every single entity shows the ultimate indweller Bhagavan alone at all times (through the jeevatma).
