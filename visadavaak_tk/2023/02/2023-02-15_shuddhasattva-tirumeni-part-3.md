viśadavaak

In a contextual topic, we saw in the previous posts how an achit is always associated with a jeevatma (shareeratayaa prakaarabhuta). The reason why we saw that topic in such depth is because we should first accept the fact that the “idols” that we offer to Bhagavan as tirumeni are all praakrta in the beginning and possessing a jeevatma within them for the sake of name and form. Only with Bhagavat sankalpam (following a pratishtha or abhimanam), it turns into shuddha sattva, and the jeevatma is removed. Bhagavan then occupies that form, directly, without a via medium of jeevatma.

If one doesn’t accept that the tirumeni of Bhagavan is shuddhasattva, then the naama-rupa-vibhaaga cannot be reconciled with the shaastras. That is because, the shaastras say that it is through a jeevatma that an object gets its name and form. So, if we have to identify the divine body of Bhagavan as a ‘rock’, then it should have a jeevatma giving it that name and form. Similarly, golden or copper idols, and so on. Without that adhisthata jeevatma, a praakrta achit would cease to exist. The only other way such an achit would have no baddha jeevatma in it, and yet exist with name and form, is by turning into shuddhasattva and being the divine body of Bhagavan (or a nityasuri). In that case, Bhagavan directly pervades that tirumeni and there is no jeevatma in between. In fact, that is the reason why the shaastras teach us not to think about the raw materials used in an idol. Even after pratishta, the only reason why we can still call that tirumeni as a salagrama tirumeni, sudha/daaru bimbam etc. is its previous state of being that material. In its current state, it is no longer that praakrta material, and hence it becomes a sin to identify it with a praakrta name.

Now, let us see some scriptural proofs for this claim that Bhagavan’s tirumeni is shuddhasattva only.

Arthapanchakam is a beautiful rahasyagrantha authored by Pillai Lokacharyar. It is one among the 18 rahasya granthas of Pillai Lokacharya. Pillailokam jeeyar has authored a descriptive commentary for this work. In the 23rd sutra of Arthapanchakam, Pillai lokacharyar speaks about archaavataara. He says, “அர்ச்சாவதாரமாவது – “தமருகந்ததெவ்வுருவம் அவ்வுருவந்தானே தமருகந்ததெப்பேர் மற்றெப்பேர்” என்கிறபடியே தனக்கென்ன ஓருருவம் ஓர் பெயருமின்றிக்கே ஆஶ்ரிதருகந்த வடிவே வடிவாகவும் அவரிட்ட பெயரே பெயராகவும்……… … .. . . எழுந்தருளியிருக்கும் நிலை.”  
This means, Bhagavan – in his archaavataara form – doesn’t have a name or a form of his own. His form is verily the form offered by his devotees, and his name is also what is offered by his devotees. In commenting on these lines, Pillailokam jeeyar says thus: “….. ….. ஆஶ்ரிதருகந்தவையான ஸுவர்ண ரஜத தாம்ர தாரு சிலா ம்ருண்மயங்களான த்ரவ்யங்களாலே யாதொரு திருமேனியை கோருகிறார்களோ, அப்படியே விலக்ஷணத்ரவ்யமென்றும் அவிலக்ஷணத்ரவ்யமென்றும் பாராமல் தனக்குத்திருமேனியாக பரிக்ரஹிக்குமவனாய்…… தனக்கு இந்தத்திருமேனி வேணும் இந்தத்திருமேனி வேணும் என்கிற நிர்பந்தமில்லாமலேயே ஆஶ்ரயித்தவர்கள் ஆனவர்கள் தாங்களுகந்து ஏதேனும் ஒரு த்ரவ்யத்தாலே விக்ரஹத்தைப் பண்ண என்றால் நல்லது என்று அப்படியே அந்தவிக்ரஹத்தில் ***அஸாதாரணமான அதிஷ்டானவிஶேஷத்தை அதிஷ்டித்து அதுவே தனக்கு அஸாதாரணமான திருமேனியாக பரிக்ரஹித்தும்***…. .. . …. ..”.  
The point to be highlighted here is “asaadhaaranamaana adhishtaanattai adhishtittu, aduve tanakku asaadhaaranamaana tirumeniyaaga parigrahittum”. He clearly mentions the “asaadhaarana adhishtaana vishesham”, which is the shuddhasattvamaya material. Bhagavan’s shuddhasattva tirumeni is also popularly referred to as “asaadhaarana vigraham” or “tirumeni”. Therefore, Pillailokam jeeyar clearly shows that the archaavataara form of Bhagavan is shuddhasattvamaya.

This is also shown in the arumpadams of Mumukshuppadi and Srivachanabhushanam. Let us examine these now.  
In the mumukshuppadi 139th sutram, Pillailokacharyar says “இங்குச்சொன்ன ஸௌலப்யத்துக்கெல்லைநிலம் அர்ச்சாவதாரம்.” This is in context of explaining the ‘narayana’ padam in purvakhanda of dvayamahamantram. Manavala Mamunigal comments on this sutra thus: “…..தமருகந்ததெவ்வுருவம் அவ்வுருவம் தானே என்கிறபடியே ஆஶ்ரிதர் உகந்ததொன்றைத் *திருமேனியாகக்கொண்டு ஸதா ஸந்நிதி பண்ணியிருக்கும்* அர்ச்சாவதாரமென்கை.” He also takes the pramana of Azhwar srisukti “tamarugandadevvuruvam”, and says that Bhagavan accepts the same form offered by his devotees. But then, one may ask, Mamunigal doesn’t seem to mention shuddhasattvamaya tirumeni in his commentary. Then how do we conclude that he indeed intends to convey the same? The answer is simple – Mamunigal is called ‘vishadavaak shikhaamani’, for the sole reason that he chooses his words very carefully. Here, he uses the words “tirumeni” and “sadaa sannidhi panniyirukkum”. A normal praakrta body would not have to be called ‘tirumeni’. Also, we do not use the word ‘sannidhi’ (divine presence) for an otherwise normal praakrta achit while showing its antaryami Bhagavan. We use the word ‘sannidhi’ for a special purpose, and that – here – is the shuddhasattva tirumeni.

This intent of Mamunigal becomes more clear with the study of Arumpadam of Shuddhasattvam Doddayangar swami for the same passage. It goes thus: “தமர் – பக்தர் ; உருவம் – திருமேனி ; அவ்வுருவம் – ஆஶ்ரிதருகந்த விக்ரஹம். தானேயாய் – ஆஶ்ரிதருகந்த திவ்யமங்களவிக்ரஹத்தையே தனக்கு அஸாதாரணமாய், ***அப்ராக்ருதமான விக்ரஹமாக உடையனாயிருக்கும்***.” This makes it free from all confusions, as the swami clearly mentions apraakrtamaana vigraham.

Also, Shuddhasattvam Embavaiyangar swami’s arumpadam for the phrase “sadaa sannidhi” goes thus: “ஆஶ்ரிதருகந்த திருமேனியைத் தனக்கு ***அஸாதாரணதிவ்யமங்களவிக்ரஹமாகக்கொண்டு ஸதா ஸந்நிதி பண்ணியிருக்கும் இவ்வர்ச்சாவதாரம்***….”

If not for these arumpadams, it would have been difficult to decode Mamunigal’s words with ease. Thanks to Shuddhasattvam Doddaiyangar swami and Shuddhasattvam Embavaiyangar swami that we are able to understand Mamunigal’s words in their entirety.
