viśadavaak

In the Srivachanabhushanam, Pillai lokacharyar deals with prapatti at great length, and then says that such prapatti is also done by Azhwars in various Archavataras, because the Archavatara is replete with all the required divine qualities for such prapatti. In that context, he gives an example of how the five forms of Bhagavan are relatable to us. Para is like the avarana jalam, Vyuha is like the ksheerabdhi, antaryami is like the underground water, vibhava is like the flash flood in a river, and archaa is like a waterbody that gets stored from such flash floods.

Manavala Mamunigal, in his beautiful commentary, shows why Pillailokacharyar gives this example of a waterbody that gets stored from flash floods. He says that since the divine qualities shown by Bhagavan during his vibhava avataras are all completely found in all the Archa places, and also since Bhagavan takes divine bodies in Archavatara that are very similar to the vibhava avataras, the archavataras can be called as water stored from flash floods (where flashfloods are the vibhava avataras). In this context, Mamunigal’s words are as follows:  
“அவதாரகுணங்களெல்லாம் அர்ச்சாவதாரத்திலே பரிபூர்ணமாயிருக்கையாலும், அவதாரவிக்ரஹங்களை அர்ச்சாரூபேண பலவிடங்களிலும் பரிக்ரஹித்துக்கொண்டிருக்கையாலும் (அதிலே தேங்கின மடுக்கள்போலே) என்கிறது.”

In the Arumpadam, Tirumazhisai Annavappangar Swami says that the archaa forms are an imitation of the Vibhava – with the limbs in the tirumeni placed exactly as they were during specific pastimes of the Lord in his vibhava avataras. “ராமக்ருஷ்ணாத்யவதாரவிக்ரஹஸம்ஸ்தாந-ஸஜாதீய-ஸம்ஸ்தாநயுக்த-அர்ச்சாவிக்ரஹபரிக்ரஹம் பண்ணுகை.” One may say that this statement doesn’t speak about the material comprising the tirumeni.

However, in summarizing the archavatara context, he says thus: “ஆக, அவதாரோத்தரகாலவர்த்தித்வே ஸதி அவதாரகுணவிக்ரஹஸஜாதீயகுணவிக்ரஹயுக்தத்வம் அவதாரத்திலே தேங்கினமை அர்ச்சாவதாரத்துக்கு விவக்ஷிதம்.” This means, the speciality of archavatara is that it has the same set of Gunas and the same kind of Tirumeni as Bhagavan had during his vibhava avataras, and that is why it is compared to ‘stored water from flash floods’. This clearly doesn’t stop at just positioning the limbs as they would have been in vibhavam (like, the hands holding a flute, or a bow and arrow, etc), but also extends up to the material comprising the divine body, which is shuddhasattva. That is very clearly shown in the words “avataara-guna-vigraha-sajaateeya-guna-vigraha-yuktatvam”.

Now let us come to the main pramana – Nampillai’s eedu. In the third centum, sixth decad, Nammazhwar sings the glories of Archavataram, and says “Archavataram is the easiest and most accessible place to surrender. So never doubt the Archavataram; go and surrender yourself at the lotus feet of Bhagavan in his archavataram.” The decad that we are talking about here is ‘seyyataamaraikannanaay’. In the pravesham, Nampillai shows how Nammazhwar talks about the easy accessibility of Bhagavan (saulabhyam). Then he raises a question as to why Nammazhwar is talking about soulabhyam again, at this point, when he has already expounded Bhagavan’s soulabhyam so beautifully starting from the first decad itself (pattudai adiyavarkkeliyavan and other tiruvaimozhis). Having raised this question, Nampillai himself answers it saying “Nammazhwar talked about Bhagavan’s soulabhyam in general earlier, and talking about the soulabhyam specifically in the archaavatara now”. He says that when the soulabhyam in archavatara and vibhava avataras are compared, the soulabhya in vibhava and other forms are like his supremacy or parattvam; and that the real soulabhyam to its maximum extent can be seen only in the archavataram. Immediately, the arumpadakarar raises another question here. He asks, “Nammazhwar already expounded the soulabhyam of Bhagavan in Kilaroli and Ozhivil kaalam decads. Then why is he repeating the same even now?”, and says “It is because he showed the soulabhyam in svayamvyakta kshetras in the other two decads, whereas he is showing the soulabhyam in other archa forms all up to griharcha forms in the present decad”.

At this point, some scholars opine that this is a clear indication that the griharcha is different from the archaa forms in divyadeshas and other temples. This is exactly where the difference arises – the difference between ‘archavatara having a shuddhasattva tirumeni’ (in temples with pratishta) and ‘archavatara not having shuddhasattva tirumeni’ (in households). They interpret this arumpada statement as saying ‘in the kilaroli and ozhivil kaalam decads, azhwar talked about soulabhyam in temples, whereas in seyyatamarai decad, azhwar is showing the soulabhyam in griharcha and other forms’. This means that the archaa forms in temples are different from the archaa forms in homes and other places. The exact words in the arumpadam at this point are as follows: “”ஆனால் பின்னை, கீழே தானும் கிளரொளியிளமை, ஒழிவில்காலம் முதலான திருவாய்மொழிகளில் அர்ச்சாவதார பர்யந்தமான ஸௌலப்யம் சொல்லிற்றில்லையோ என்ன – அங்குச்சொன்ன ஸௌலப்யம் ஸ்வயம்வ்யக்தமான அர்ச்சாவதாரவிஷயம், இங்குச்சொல்லுகிற ஸௌலப்யம் குடீகுஞ்ஜேஷு என்கிறபடியே க்ருஹார்ச்சா பர்யந்தமான அர்ச்சாவதாரவிஷயம்” என்று உத்க்ரந்த ஶங்காபரிஹாரங்களை அருளிச்செய்வர். “

Now we have to examine the truth or falsity of the words of the scholars who opine that there is a difference in the archavataras in temples and other places. In reading the arumpadam, it clearly means that the soulabhyam in svayamvyakta kshetras was told in earlier decads, whereas the soulabhyam of other archaa forms all up to griharcha is being told now. This is clear from the phrase “க்ருஹார்ச்சா பர்யந்தமான அர்ச்சாவதாரவிஷயம்”. The word ‘paryantamaana’ shows that it is not talking about a specific form as opposed to svayam vyakta, but only extending ‘up till’ griharcha. So when it is just the extension of soulabhya in different archaa forms, how can we claim that the archaa forms themselves are different in nature? Isn’t there a difference between ‘in extension to’ and ‘as opposed to’?

Also, going by logic, the arumpadakarar shows that the previous decads were about the soulabhyam in ‘svayam vyakta kshetras’ very explicitly. There is no word like ‘aadi’ (etc) to include archaa forms in other temples apart from svayamvyakta. So does that mean that the archaa forms in other temples are also not made of shuddhasattva, just like grihaarchaa? After all, all forms other than svayam vyakta are grouped alongside griharcha in the arumpadam, aren’t they (bhauma-niketaneshvapi-kutee-kunjeshu)? If it is accepted as a fact that such archaa forms in other temples are not shuddhasattvamaya either, then we are going against one of the biggest pramanas for us – the pancharatra agamas, which claim loudly and clearly that the archaa forms of Bhagavan are made of shuddhasattva material only. It is very evident during pratishta, as well as in daily worship rituals.
