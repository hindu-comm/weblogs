6\) Regarding the point where Dr.Rangan says that the first chapter is more reliable when compared to the 3rd or 4th chapters (which could be later additions): the reason he says that the first chapter is more reliable is because it ends with Rama’s coronation, just like the yuddha kanda. But one may peruse the first chapter of balakanda from any publication. It will be surprising to find these slokas, where it says that Rama will rule over this kingdom for eleven thousand years, and then reach his supreme abode Srivaikuntham. That is where the kavya ends. In between of the coronation and the return to paramapadam, there are several slokas that indicate how the Ramarajya would be, and the period for which Rama would be in this world. Interestingly, our Acharyas also show that Seetaviyogam is indicated here. For, it says “दशवर्षसहस्राणि दशवर्षशतानि च” – it splits up Rama’s period in this world as 10,000 + 1,000 years. The reason for this split is the difference between presence and absence of Seeta by Rama’s side. However, this is not in the popular commentaries to Sriramayana, and it is shown elsewhere. I just mentioned here contextually. In any case, the point of focus here is that the first chapter (which is reliable to the other party) does NOT end with coronation, but goes up to Rama’s return to paramapadam, which is perfectly in sync with the uttarakanda.

7\) Finally, talking about the traditional viewpoint of uttarakandam, Dr.Rangan says that the uttarakanda is not mentioned in any of the other ancient scriptures. Even if they are found, they are either added later by known authors, or the story itself is changed to have a better ending.

But our Acharyas have never subscribed to this viewpoint. They have always believed that the uttarakanda is an indispensable part of Sriramayana. They have given an equal and fair treatment to the uttarakanda, where applicable.

Dr.Rangan shows that the tilaka vyakhyana calls the uttarakanda as ‘khilam’ (quoting the ancient people). He agrees, at first, that the word ‘khilam’ has many meanings. But he chooses to go with a certain meaning shown by Sridhara swami in commenting on a sloka from Bhagavata purana. He also takes help from a western scholar, who seems to call the uttarakanda a supplement (added later, to complete the story). That western scholar probably got this idea from the word ‘khilam’, when he read the tilaka commentary. Oh! we don’t need a neighbour to tell us what our mother means, with her words; do we? We can know it ourselves, thank you. If one says that the tilaka vyakhyakara had an intention that the uttarakanda was a supplement, and not an original part of Sriramayana (or with equal weightage), and that is why he calls it a ‘khilam’, then behold – the same tilaka vyakhyakara calls the same uttarakanda as ‘sarvato vishishta kavya’ (see commentary on balakanda-3-39). Then what is ‘khilam’ according to the tilakavyakhyakara? It just means an ‘epilogue’, and a very special one at it. How does this discount uttarakanda as an inseparable part of Sriramayana? First of all, my friend! if the tilaka vyakhyakara had an intention of calling uttarakanda as an unimportant or supplemented kanda (with the word ‘khilam’), why would he comment on the uttarakanda at all? Why would anyone contradict their own statements like this? I fail to understand!

At the end, he also mentions that only Kulashekharazhwar (of all azhwars) has mentioned about some events from the uttarakanda, and that too- just once; and, therefore, concludes that uttarakanda is not authored by Valmiki! Come on! We trust our Azhwars more than any other scholar. Even if they tell something just once, it is nothing but the truth. Whereas, no matter how many times we tell something, it is mostly false! Azhwars have an exalted status due to Bhagavan’s grace, and nothing in their words are false. Not just our Azhwars, but our Acharyas also hold an equally exalted position for us. So, their words are also highly revered and accepted. Manavala Mamunigal, for example, quotes extensively from the uttarakanda in his commentary to the famous work “Srivachanabhushanam” of Pillai lokacharya. He, in fact, deals with Seeta’s banishment part only. Periyavachchan Pillai (the popular Srivaishnava Acharya whom the speakers in this video address as ‘Krishna Suri’, and claim to be one of the ancestors of Dr.Rangan) comments on Kulashekara Azhwar’s Perumal Tirumozhi, and explains the incidents from uttarakanda in a very undistorted way. He also accepts the Azhwars as parama-pramanikas, and accepts uttarakanda as an inseparable and indispensable part of Sriramayana. Govindarajar is one of the foremost commentators on the Sriramayana. His Sriramayana-bhushana is a widely accepted commentary on the southern recension of Sriramayana. He accepts the uttarakanda as an authentic kanda, and authored by Valmiki himself. I wonder why Dr.Rangan never chose to even mention this commentary atleast once during his entire interview. Probably because the speakers would only get into more trouble with his words?

We look to people like these Azhwars and Acharyas, who are most trustworthy, when we have such confusions. They are sure to clear our confusions completely.

So, when we look at all these points, it is very evident that uttarakanda is indeed an inseparable and indispensable part of Sriramayana. It is indeed authored by Valmiki, and the traditionalists have unanimously agreed on this. There are no contradictions or inconsistencies with the rest of the epic.

Do you know what true inconsistency is? See this:  
Dr.Rangan says these exact words in the video: “Bhavabhuti may be the greatest of poets, but cannot be considered authentic, and his works cannot be considered itihasa”. But he analyses that Bhavabhuti had some reason why he made two books – veerarama charita and uttararama charita, although he could have put them in the same book if uttarakanda was indeed a part of Sriramayana! The reason being – uttarakanda was a later addition.

The host asks (at around the 55th minute of the video) “can we say that Bhavabhuti is not writing itihasa, but writing a work of fiction in uttararama charitam?”, Dr.Rangan says “yes, because he changes the story”. What story is considered as the right version by Dr.Rangan and co.?

The irony is when Dr.Rangan narrates the story from uttararama charitam, and says, “who can believe all these things?”. My question is – if you consider it as fiction, who asked you to believe it? If not, what are you trying to compare it with?

What do these people consider as ‘itihasa’? and what are they comparing the uttararamacharita against, to know whether Bhavabhuti is authentic or not? An interpolated uttarakanda (which they call as itihasa here)?

This is just one inconsistency that I am pointing out. I can come up with many such statements – where the speaker contradicts his own statement in this one hour video, or where the speaker quotes from tilaka vyakhya as a main pramanam, but the tilaka vyakhya itself fails the speaker in another place, and so on. But I stop here, for, the post is already too long. May that supreme lord Rama bless us all with the true knowledge, and true devotion.

Jai Srimannarayana!
