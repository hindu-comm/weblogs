viśadavaak

Arthapanchakajnaanam is the knowledge about the five things that every mumukshu must have, in order to attain moksham. This is what our Acharyas have shown, while explaining the statement “jnaanaanmokshaH”. There was a small – but interesting – discussion in a certain group about how the Gopikas possessed the arthapanchakajnaanam. Here I have tried to record what was discussed in the group. Please be advised that this post is slightly lengthy.

Also, it is important to know that this post is not meant to teach the arthapanchakam to the uninitiated. It helps if the reader has some basic knowledge about the arthapanchakam through prior kalakshepams.

In order to understand the arthapanchakajnaanam of Gopikas, we can see a number of instances. But we chose to see just one instance – the raasa leela – where all the five things are shown beautifully. So here goes the explanation:

The raasa leela and many other glorious pastimes of Krishna have been described vividly in various places. But one of the finest places to enjoy these pastimes are in the ashtottara shatanaamaavali of Krishna, which is found in the Brahmanda purana. It is called “Krishna premamrta stotram”.

Five tirunamams in this Krishna premamrtam explain the beauty and philosophy of the renowned raasa leela of Kannan with the gOpikas. The first tirunamam in that series is ‘ajaH’.

The story of Kannan’s Raasaleela, in short:  
On a certain full moon night in the Sharadrutu (fall season, as we popularly call it), our Kannan desired to fulfil the desire of gOpikas – who were very keen on spending some happy time with Him. So He stood near yamuna and started playing His flute in order to invite everyone. Listening to this charming music of Kannan, the gOpikas started running towards Him in a trance, pushing aside the barriers of their parents/in-laws/husbands/etc. One gOpika was breastfeeding her newborn baby. She just left the infant lying right there and ran towards Kannan. Another gOpika was serving her husband and parents in-law. She just left them halfway through her service and ran. One more gOpika was decorating her eyes with kajal (anjanam). She also left her makeup halfway through and ran to Kannan. Thus, every gOpika who was otherwise engaged in other works could not resist the enchanting music of Kannan and ran towards Him. Since He is the one who provoked every gOpika to give up their daily chores and run towards Him, He is called ‘ajaH’. The word ‘ajaH’ comes from the root ‘aj’ (aja gati-kshepaNayoH; kshEpaNam=prEraNam). The root ‘aj’ is used in movement or provocation. Here, it means ‘to provoke’. Krishna is called ‘ajaH’ since He provoked the gOpikas to run towards Him – ajati prErayati iti ajaH.  
Periyavacchan Pillai says this in his beautiful commentary on Jitantaa stotram, in the very first sloka vyAkhyAnam. “5 laksham kuDiyil peNgaLil kATTil tannuDaiya murppADu tOtRugaikkAgavirE tirukkuzhalai vAyvaittaruLitRu”, meaning – it is to show His precedence over the women folk in the five lakh houses in vrindavanam that He played the flute. That is why He is called ‘pUrvaja’ – the one who takes the first step towards us. This was true in His rAsaleelai as well. Hence, He is called ‘ajaH’.

Here, we see that the Gopikas gave up on all material things/relations and ran to Kannan. This prakrti sambandham itself is said to be the greatest obstacle in attaining perumal. The gopikas never hesitated to leave those mundane things and run to Kannan. Thus, the knowledge of gopikas – that this samsaram is the virodhi – is shown.

ajAya namaha

The next tirunamam in the series:  
niranjanAya namaH

Kannan received the gOpikas cordially and happily danced with them that night. His manifestation for this dance was extraordinary. He took many forms simultaneously, and danced with every gOpika individually. The famous sloka ‘anganAm anganAm antarENa mAdhavaH mAdhavaM cha mAdhavaM cha antarENa anganA’ shows how beautifully they played the raasa dance. However, wouldn’t this act of dancing with others’ wives bring upon Him a great blemish of adultery? “No” says this tirunamam. The current tirunamam for perusal is ‘niranjanaH’ – meaning ‘the one who has no blemish’. “Anjanam” refers to the kajal that is kept to the eyes. In many contexts, it just means ‘black spot’. Niranjana, therefore, means the one without a black spot.  
There is a beautiful conversation between Krishna and the gOpikas recorded in Srimad Bhagavatam in this context. Krishna asks the gOpikas if it is right on their part to come to Him, leaving behind all their husbands. He chides them asking if they really have pAtivratyam (loyalty to one’s own husband alone). The response by gOpikas to this question is mind blowing. They say “Oh Krishna! The Vedas hail you as the husband of all worlds. ‘patim viSvasya’ hail the vEdas. Similarly, you are the indweller of all sentient and insentient entities and hence it is indeed you (the indweller of all our husbands) whom we have been loyal to, all these days. And now we still are loyal to you. Carnal pleasure with an outsider is wrong. But this is a sacred relationship between a jeevAtmA and paramAtmA. This is not carnal in nature. So please accept our pleas and take us into your eternal servitude”. These justifying words from the mouths of gOpikas themselves makes Krishna free from all blemishes. He is, hence, called ‘niranjanaH’ – the one who is free from blemishes.

Here, gopikas readily identify Kannan as the supreme lord. We normally say that the gopikas are ignorant about things. But their knowledge about Kannan was so accurate, which can be easily seen in this tirunamam. So, their knowledge about the paraswarupam is shown.

niranjanAya namaH

The next tirunAmam in the series:  
kAMajanakAya namaH
