viśadavaak

The next tirunAmam in the series:  
kAMajanakAya namaH

Most people interpret this tirunAmam as ‘the father of manmatha’, which is totally out of context here.  
Krishna is the one who instigated love towards Him in the minds of gOpikas. Hence, He is called ‘kAmajanakaH’. ‘kAma’ here doesn’t refer to carnal pleasure. It rather refers to unadulterated bhakti. “kaNNanukkE Amadu kAmam” says tiruvarangattamudanAr. The word ‘kAma’ itself simply means ‘desire’. Desire can be for any object. Here, it is a desire to serve and be with Kannan. Hence, it is called ‘kRuShNakAmaH’. This is the highest puruShArtha for any jIvAtmA. In the 40th pasuram of Iramanusa Nootrandadi, Amudanar shows this explicitly. Of the four puruShArthAs called dharma, artha, kAma and mOkSha, Emberumanar would always choose kAma over others. The reason being – it is very much the swarUpam or identity of a jIvAtmA to be desiring kainkaryam unto Bhagavan.  
Here, kaNNan is directly giving that kAmam also to the gOpikas, Himself. The question is whether we should develop that kAmam, or kaNNan should be giving. Azhwar says “mayarvara madinalam aruLinan”. Here, nalam refers to bhakti. Azhwar says the bhakti was also given to him by Bhagavan Himself. In another place in the Tiruvaimozhi, Azhwar says “uNarvil umbar oruvanai avanadaruLAl uralporuTTu en uNarvinuLLe iruttinEn, aduvum avanadinnaruLE” (I thought I kept that Bhagavan in my mind; but it is He who graced thus). Alavandar also prays “Bhagavan! Bhaktimapi prayacCha mE” (oh Bhagavan! Give me the pure bhakti as well). So it is indeed Krishna who has to give the bhakti towards Him, and we cannot build it all by ourselves.  
The next question is – why should krishna give this bhakti or kAma to the jIvAtmAs? It is simply because such pure love adds to His enjoyment. Then why doesn’t He give this kAma to everyone alike? It is because of their impressions! Not everyone would love Krishna as much as the gOpikas do. Sri Parasara maharshi says “kRuShNE nibaddhahRudayAH” while talking about gOpikas in the rAsakrIDA varNanam. That is to say, the gopikas had their hearts bounded to krishna.  
And therefore, He is called ‘kAmajanakaH’ – the one who instigates pure love towards Him.

Here, the identity of a jeevatma is shown. The gopikas came running to Kannan, seeking union with Him, and asked to spend time with Him. This kRuShNakAma itself is verily the identity of every jeevatma, which comes out of the exclusive servitude and total dependence on Bhagavan. The gopikas, therefore, show their knowledge about the jeeva swarupam in this tirunamam.

kAmajanakAya namaH

The next tirunAmam in the series –  
kanjalOchanAya namaH

kanja means lotus – that which is born in water. kanjalOchana refers to His punDareekAkShattvam – meaning the one with lotus-like wide and beautiful eyes. What has kanjalOchanattvam got to do with rAsaleelai?  
Krishna, out of great love towards gopikas, started dancing with them in the rAsa leelai. It was then that the gopikas started thinking – “there is no one to equal us in this world, because Krishna himself is dancing with us”. When there is such ego, krishna carefully removes it with His pastimes. Therefore, He just concealed Himself immediately, and disappeared. There is another interpretation of our Acharyas as well. A human cannot bear more than a certain quantum of pleasure or pain. When the gopikas were dancing with kaNNan, their joy knew no bounds, and it was as though they would give up their lives right there. Krishna saw this, and wanted to bring them back to normalcy before they could enjoy with Him again. So He disappeared. Immediately, the gopikas were overcome with passion and grief, and started singing the gOpikAgItam. They enacted as Krishna and other people. All this was in an effort to overcome the grief coming out of His separation. Notwithstanding this ardent plea of the gOpikas, krishna immediately appeared before them with such great beauty that their sorrow also did not take away their lives!! That is when the Srimad Bhagavatam hails His beauty thus: “tAsAm AvirabhUt shouriH smayamAnamukhAmbujaH pItAmbaradharaH sragvI sAkShAnmanmatha-manmathaH”. This state of His being like a manmatha to manmatha himself is hailed in this tirunAmam – kanjalOchanaH. His beautiful eyes captured the hearts of the gOpis, and instigated unending love towards Him in them (as seen in kAmajanakaH).  
Why is krishna called lotus-eyed? It is because His eyes are as beautiful, tender and red like just blossoming lotuses. The redness in His eyes shows His unending love towards the jeevAtmas. In the first place, it shows His vAtsalyam – by accepting the jIvAtmAs as they are, with all their blemishes. And then, it also shows His pure unending love – by the colouration. It is only when Krishna attained Azhwar that his body became dark hued and eyes became wide and red like lotuses (kaNgaL sivandu periyavAy…). Since Krishna joined with the gOpikas again, He was lotus-eyed, and hence “kanjalOchanaH”.

Alternately, smayamAnamukhAmbuja means the lotus like face with a beautiful smile. This lotus-like face is an indicator (upalakShaNam) for His lotus-like eyes as well. This is how Azhwar enjoys Bhagavan’s tirumeni in his tiruvaimozhi – “karumANikkamalaimEl maNittaDam tAmaraikkADugaLpOl tirumArvu vAy kaN kai undi kAl uDaiyADaigaL seyyapirAn”. Bhagavan united with Azhwar in the previous decad (until 8-8 kaNgaL sivandu), thereby regaining His original form with vibrant colours. Azhwar saw this, and immediately sang His beauty in 8-9, karumANikkamalai decad. He says Bhagavan is like a lotus pond. His divine chest, lips, eyes, hands, navel, etc are like individual lotuses, and He is like the lotus pond filled with such lotuses! At that point, Azhwar shows that His eyes, His lips etc are like lotuses, and hence His face itself is lotus-like! So mukhAmbuja also has other ambujas like nayanAmbuja and vadanAmbuja. It becomes an upalakShaNam. In this way also, krishna is hailed as “kanjalOchanaH”.

In this tirunamam, we see that the ego of Gopikas – who were enjoying with Kannan – was vanquished. Our Acharyas say “(kainkaryattil) kaLaiyaagiRadu tanakkennap paNNumadu”. The sense of enjoyment in our kainkaryam comes out of ahankaram, and that is the main obstacle to the main goal (of exclusive kainkaryam). Thus, this tirunamam shows the true nature of kainkaryam (‘chaturthi’ along with ‘namaH’); and hence, the gopikas show their knowledge about such highest goal in its purest form as well. This is called phalaswarupam.

kanjalOchanAya namaH

madhughnE namaH  
The last tirunAmam in the context of rAsaleela is “madhuhA”. In other words, it may be called “madhusUdana”. One might wonder what has slaying of the demon madhu got to do with rAsaleelai! But there is a very interesting fact that we can see in the Vishnu Puranam. Parasara Maharshi records the rAsaleelai, and says this:  
“ramyam gItadhvanim shrutvA santyajyAvasathAMstadA  
AjagmustvaritA gOpyO yatrAstE madhusUdanaH” – meaning, when the gOpikAs heard the enchanting flute music of krishna, they just abandoned their houses and ran to the place where Madhusudana stood. It is also interesting to note that Parasara Maharshi holds on to this divine name madhusUdana throughout the rAsaleelA adhyAya! Of all names, why does Parasara Maharshi choose this name?  
In the tiruvAimozhi, Azhwar sings a beautiful pasuram thus:  
“Unil vAzh uyirE nallippO unnai peTru  
vAnuLAr perumAn madhusUdan ennammAn  
tAnum yAnum ellAm tannuLLE kalandoLindOm  
tEnum pAlum neyyum kannalum amudum ottE”  
In this pasuram, Azhwar sings how Bhagavan united with him with all sweetness! Azhwar says, it was as though honey, milk, ghee, sugarcane juice and nectar (all sweet items) were mixed together! And then he hails Bhagavan as “madhusUdan ennammAn”. The commentator Nampillai asks “why this name madhusudana when Azhwar is uniting with Bhagavan?” and gives a splendid answer! He says, “madhuvAgira asuranaippOkkinArppOlE ennOTTai samshlEShavirOdhiyaippOkki ennai tOrppittu tanpakkalilE kainkaryattilE mUTTina tAnum”. That means, Bhagavan is the one who engaged Azhwar in His eternal service after defeating him, by removing the obstacles to their union, just as He removed the asura called madhu.  
Here, the name madhusUdana is used to illustrate the fact that He removes the obstacles. Madhu and kaiTabha were two asuras who stole the vedas from Brahma, thus obstructing his creation. Bhagavan took the matsyaavatara or the fish form and killed the two asuras and brought back the vedas. He then instructed those vedas again to Brahma in the form of Hayagriva. This shows that slaying of madhu is an indicator of removal of obstacles in their union. And hence, this name is used here as well.

Krishna is the one who removed all obstacles to His union with the gOpikas. Therefore, He is called ‘madhusUdana’!

The remover of obstacles is the called the ‘upAya’. upa-sameepE, ayate-gamyatE (anEna) iti upAyaH. Anything can be called an upAya only when it removes the obstacles in attaining a certain result. This is what kannan is showing in this tirunamam. Since the gopikas had complete faith in kannan in removing their obstacles, they also displayed this knowledge about upAyaswarupam.

madhughnE namaH

Thus, as we see here, the five tirunamams – ajaH, niranjanaH, kAmajanakaH, kanjalOchanaH, and madhuhA – show the knowledge of gopikas about the five things – virodhi swarupam, paraswarupam, jeevaswarupam, phalaswarupam, and upAyaswarupam – respectively. Thus, the gopikas are essentially showing their arthapanchakajnaanam. It is pertinent to note that they didn’t stop with just possessing that knowledge, but also put it into practice. This ‘anuShThAnaparyanta-arthapanchakajnaana’ is what brings us moksham (jnaanaanmokShaH).

How beautiful, isn’t it?

(concluded)
