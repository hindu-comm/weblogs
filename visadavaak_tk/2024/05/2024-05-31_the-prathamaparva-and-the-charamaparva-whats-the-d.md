viśadavaak

What is the difference between a prathamaparva and charamaparva? First, we have to understand what are these parvas. They are the stages of beliefs in which a person stands. The prathamaparva means the first stage of belief, which is to essentially realize that Bhagavan is the goal, as well as the means. The charamaparva means the ultimate stage of belief, which is to essentially realize that one’s own Acharyan (as the highest devotee of Bhagavan) is the goal, as well as the means.

While the prathamaparva is very easily relatable and understood well, the charmaparva is more easier said than practiced. It is very easy to give up the sense of enjoyment in worldly things and develop a taste for Bhagavan, because the worldly things are replete with blemishes, whereas Bhagavan is free from all blemishes. But the same doesn’t hold good for the charamaparva. It would have been easier to find more charamaparva nishtas only if it was possible for us to find blemishes with Bhagavan. Since there is none, it is not so easy to give up on Him, and reach out to His devotees. In fact, one sees the devotees as blemished, and Bhagavan as pure. Therefore, it is not at all an easy task.

However, we thankfully have a few examples from where we can learn the real mindset of both of these kinds of people. One of the best examples we could take is from the Sriramayana, where the entire world is servient to Rama, whereas Shatrughna alone is servient to Rama’s dear devotee Bharata. So, we can easily compare Lakshmana and Shatrughna – the twin brothers, to know the mindset of prathama and charama parvanishtas respectively.

Lakshmana says this in the Aranya kanda: “संन्त्यज्य विविधान् सौख्यानार्यं सर्वात्मनाश्रितः । जितः स्वर्गस्तव भ्रात्रा भरतॆन महात्मना ॥” When Rama, Lakshmana and Seetha are in the dandaka forest, they go to the Godavari river on a fine winter morning to bathe. All three of them are mightily pleased to witness the beautiful nature. That is when Lakshmana understands the heart of Rama, which kept longing for uniting with Bharata in order to enjoy these beautiful scenes, and starts praising him in order to make Rama happy. In the pursuit, he tells this aforesaid sloka, which means: “Oh Rama! That great soul Bharata, who is your dear brother, has given up on all enjoyments, and also won over the desire for attaining the swarga itself. He has completely surrendered to you with all his faculties.” In this statement, we should note how Lakshmana identifies Bharata as Rama’s dear brother (तव भ्रात्रा), while he was also equally related to him in the same way. This shows how the prathamaparva nishta Lakshmana considered a dear devotee of Bhagavan.

On the other hand, when Bharata and Shatrughna go to the forest in order to bring back Rama, they reach Shrungiberipura, from where they decide to cross the Ganga river on the next morning. They halt at Guha’s place that night. The next morning, Bharata wakes up Shatrughna, saying that it was already daybreak, and that they must leave immediately so that they could meet Rama at the earliest. That is when Shatrughna says these words: “जागर्मि नाहं स्वपिमि तमॆवार्यं विचिन्तयन्”, meaning – “I am awake, and not sleeping; I am meditating upon that same elder brother (Rama)”. We must analyze why Shatrughna says “that same elder brother” (तमॆवार्यं), and not just “our elder brother”? It is because, he knew exactly how Bharata would have meditated on Rama the whole night; and considered it as his foremost duty to please Bharata by meditating on the ‘same Rama’ whom Bharata loved the most. So, the words “the same elder brother” is indicative of Bharata’s direct relationship with Rama, and his own indirect relationship with Him.

In both of these cases, we see that both Lakshmana and Shatrughna spoke about or meditated upon Bharata and Rama, respectively, in order to please the person they liked the most. Lakshmana wanted to please Rama, and hence spoke highly about Bharata. Shatrughna wanted to please Bharata, and hence meditated on Rama. The only difference is – the prathamaparva nishta Lakshmana spoke about a bhaagavata Bharata, because he was a dear brother and devotee of Bhagavan Rama; whereas the charamaparva nishta Shatrughna meditated upon Bhagavan Rama, only because he was the dearest to the bhaagavata Bharata.

So, the prathamaparvanishta primarily worships the God, and praises the devotees because the God likes them. Whereas, the charamaparvanishta primarily worships the devotee, and meditates upon God because the devotee likes Him.
