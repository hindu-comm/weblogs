# Can You Risk Yourself?

### Human societies have always lived on the edge of danger but today, it has become easier to avoid ever experiencing it even mildly. It might seem like a good thing but is it really?

In his book, *Why We Sleep*, Mathew Walker asks a question that probably never occurs to most of us— how did we evolve to sleep at night when it is the time some of the deadliest predators are on loose? It sounds suicidal when you think about it yet Walker makes the case that is the reason why some people are night owls. Some of us have evolved to stay awake at night so that we could keep a watch to protect those who were fast asleep. Even today, despite our fortified modern housing, we still need watchmen to be on the look-out for anti-social elements who might come out of woodwork in the dark hours of night. We don’t want to be in danger, after all.

Yet, that is only one of the many manifestations of danger that lurks within the confines of our living environments. At the end of the day, anything that terrifies us is dangerous for us. For some, it could be a barking dog. For others, it could be expressing an unpopular opinion in a polite gathering. For the rest, it could be encountering lizards and other creepy crawlies suddenly. But whatever it is, we all have fears that are extremely real, dangerous, and life-altering for us.

For most part of human history, most of the things we now take for granted were quite fearsome. Before the advent of electricity, most of us did not venture out after dark. Before tourism, most of us did not even think of venturing outside of our own tribe, let alone our country. Before globalisation, the idea of pursuing education, work, or family in foreign lands was just as terror-inducing. Before air travel, the idea of flying from one place from another was something only god could do, if at all possible. All of these fears would have threatened our survival and most of us were right to heed them. But despite it all, we are here at the present moment in history where all of these fears have been rendered comical. Why did some of our ancestors push back on those fears then? Perhaps, they were acting out of desperation where survival was bleak either way. Perhaps, they sensed great opportunities and were able to overcome their biological and mental blocks as a result. Perhaps, that is just how nature intended it to be.

There is something rather fertile about fear. As much as it can freeze us into inaction when it has us in its grip, it can also free us to the same extent if we were to overcome it. But it can be tough to know which fear is meant to be overcome and which one is meant to be heeded. Because fear also exists to protect us from our worst instincts. If we had no fear, we might start jumping out of high-rise buildings, burn our hands in fire, or start punching anyone who annoys us in the slightest. To fear death, loss, and retribution, is not only sane but it is also what ensures our survival just as much as the watchmen of our societies.

But fear being what it is, it can also confuse us to no end. It never stops making a case for itself and in order to discern its truth from its lies, we need to be able to find stillness within ourselves. It is only in this stillness that the mud can settle and we can see if fear is trying to save us or stagnate us. Whatever it is and whatever we choose to do with this fear is for us to determine but it certainly decides the trajectory of our lives, for better and worse.

However, fear has had a strange evolution in the modernity. Where on one hand, streaming platforms are chock-a-block with dark, edgy shows that are often binge-watched by millions, on the other hand we have people who will let the algorithms dictate their lives in the most boring way possible. Where on one hand, you can get trolled and even lose your job for expressing a controversial opinion, on the other hand we live in suffocatingly politically correct times where even common-sense can be experienced as violence. Where on one hand, it is easier than ever before to live in a foreign land, on the other hand we have people who are increasingly more rigid about their cultural, religious, and linguistic identities. Where on one hand, we chase progress at breakneck speed, on the other hand we reject its attempts to evolve us in any meaningful way and we remain the same human beings that we have ever been.

Millennials and GenZ are now infamous for their unwillingness to interact with strangers in real life, despite almost exclusively interacting with strangers in their online avatars. Our version of Grimm Fairy Tales would probably include stories of losing our smartphones and not being able to distract ourselves with it every second of the day. Or perhaps having to negotiate prices with vegetable-sellers and cab drivers in a city where we don’t speak the local language. Or finding out an address by asking strangers for directions and still not being able to find it.

We desire to be free, independent, and resilient individuals of modernity, yet we are probably more enslaved, dependent, and fearful than our traditional ancestors in many ways. When fear does not metabolise into courage, it does not go away. It either numbs itself into stagnation or it twists into something far more sinister. Modern society suffers from both while allowing for lesser and lesser courage to manifest. It does not allow for risk because that would be dangerous for not just one individual but the collective society around the world.

Modernity lulls us all into submission with its non-stop comforts, conveniences, and distractions. Courage, then, becomes irrelevant and rather stupid. Who would trade a world of ease for a world of risk, after all? It never lets us see how ease too leads to entropy beyond a certain point. In fact, it never lets us see that entropy either. Modern society is not concerned with the evolution of our soul, rather it is content to feed our ego endlessly. It builds us an identity all too easily and busies us in the work of fortifying it as if we were never going to die. It never grants us stillness of its own accord, in fact it constantly intrudes upon any remaining vestige of it. To transform our fears into courage in such times, we might just need to risk losing and becoming our selves, bit by bit.
