I usually post about once a week and though I don’t manage to follow that routine for any considerable length of time, I do try to follow it. I usually write in a single go or so about a day or so before posting. This time I was looking to post the following poem with a lot longer introduction and explanation but I haven’t felt like writing anything for a few days now and so I’m posting it as it is. Learned readers will certainly be able to point out not only my inspirations but also possible errors, both grammatical and of taste, that I’ve probably committed in each line.

As for the reason, you probably have seen the apocalyptic videos of the floods in Nepal. The videos are heartbreaking enough and I don’t want to think more of death in such times. Some people I knew crossed the bridge you see in the video just five minute or so before this happened. They are fortunately safe but the same cannot be said of the hundreds dead and the thousands dead.

The metre is Gāyatrī but only in the loose sense of three lines of eight syllables each. The translation is fairly literal. Much of it came to me in a dream some months ago. Obviously I have added, subtracted or changed much but that was it’s genesis.

### Original

``` text yamáḥ prathamám īyivā́n ánu vénan pitṛ́bhyo naḥ । virāṣā́ḍ vikṣú rājate॥ ápehi somya putraka ná tvā́'nu tātá īyate । sá yáthā́'kar dyávidyavi ॥ pathíbhiḥ sugébhir gahi pitáro yā́n naś cakriré । amuyā́ jyótir aśnavaḥ ॥ mā́ bhaiḥ sārameyā́ tāta tā́ táva śúnakā iva । ná jambhata ánāgasam ॥ tīrṇá írāvatīṃ yāhi yāhi tváṃ mánasā dṛḍháḥ । nā́vaṃ gīrbhír dṛḍhā́ṃ karam ॥ supalāśā́d adhó vṛkṣā́t saraṇyvò rājate sutáḥ । saṃ píban pitṛ́bhiḥ sumát ॥ ágre gabhīrávepaso yamásya mā́ bhayáṃ gamaḥ । draṣṭā́ mṛl̤éna cákṣuṣā ॥ pitáraṃ tátra paśyāsi pitúṣ ṭe somya hŕ̥diyam । yó'krīḍat te purā́ sayúk ॥ ambā́ smarati tāta tvā  rurudānā́ divédive । ási me śírasi sthitáḥ ॥ ná mártiyeṣu te tātó vivide marḍitā́raṃ kám । śráddadhāna ṛtè'sti nū́ ॥ vraté vaivasvatásya yé víśve náro vayáṃ smási । té yāmas tátra tvā́m ánu ॥ aháṃ tváṃ tvám aháṃ céti purāṇā́ no ví cakṣire । ánuyāsyāvahé'ciram ॥ ```

### Translation



Text within this block will maintain its original spacing when published

``` text Yama, who first went searching for our fathers, man-subduer, lords it over the folk. Go away, dear son, your father doesn’t follow you, as he did day after day. Go by easy roads that our fathers have created. Thus you will find the light. Do not fear the sons of Saramā; like your puppies they don’t attack the guiltless. Cross Irāvati and go; go with a strong heart. I’ve made the boat strong with songs. Below the nice-feathered tree, Saraṇyū’s son rules, drinking with the fathers. Don’t be nervous before Yama of deep wisdom; He’ll look on you with gracious eyes. You’ll see the dear one there, father of your father, who used to play with you. Your mother remembers you, boy, crying each day. You are ever in my head. Your father found no mortal man to console him. He places faith in ṛta now. All of us men, bound in the law of Vivasvat’s son, shall follow you there. “I am you; and you I”, so the ancients have told us. We two shall follow you soon. ```
