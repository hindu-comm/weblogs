The stuti-s of the Mātṛ-yāga

I first heard of the Mātṛ-yāga or the Mātṛkā-pūjā from R1’s father and saw him perform it in Bhagānagari. He had acquired the same during the period he and his ancestors were in Nepal, which is also one of the last centers of the unadmixed practice of the Paścimāmnāya. The vidhi consists of the worship of the Mātṛ-s, which was probably originally an archaic standalone ritual associated with the worship of their images in the Mātṛkā panels and in the cemetery. However, in the form practiced by him and as expounded in a highly corrupt Nepalian manuscript (Aṣṭamātṛkā-pujā: NGMCP 1/596 Reel No: A 436/7), the ogdoad of Mātṛ-s forms the Mātṛ-cakra, in the center of which the supreme Bhairava and the Bhairavī are worshipped as the focal deities of the Kulāmnāya. In this note, we do not provide the mantra-s used in the yāga, but the stuti-s which are uttered after the deployment of the mantra-s. They are extremely corrupt in the above-mentioned manuscript; hence, the versions provided here are emended for meter and, where possible, grammar and semantics; however, from the Pāṇinian viewpoint, there will be some aiśa usages.

R1’s father pointed out that these stuti-s were sung in the cemetery during the sammelana (the equivalent of gaṇacakra-s) ritual and could be considered surviving versions of the spontaneously recited caryā-gīti-s or chandaka-s. We wondered if the Saundaryalahari, primarily affiliated with the Paścimāmnāya and the Dakṣiṇāmnāya, was an evolute of such compositions.

The supreme Bhairava and the Bhairavī are worshipped in the center of the Mātṛ-cakra with the following stuti-s. At the outset, the Bhairavī is worshiped as the central, transfunctional kaula goddess Cakreśvarī along with the Sapta-mātṛka-s (not eight Mātṛ-s as in the cakra itself) in śārdūlavikrīḍita stuti-s. The kaula goddess in the center of the maṇḍala is not specified with respect to her gharāmnāya. Modern practitioners of the Paścimāmnāya take it to be Kubjikā (see below). However, in terms of iconography, we may note the following: (1) Her red color (raktāṅgī); (2) premollāsa-rasārdra (moist with the joy/bliss of the erotic sentiment); (3) pretāsanā (the corpse throne); (4) dhṛta-śūla-śakti (holding a trident and spear). These features could apply, at least partially, to the focal goddesses of the other āmnāya-s, e.g., Tripurā. Hence, it is possible that the mātṛ-cakra was a remnant from the period where the focal kaula goddess was worshipped in the midst of the mātṛka-s prior to the differentiation/incorporation into āmnāya-s.

R1’s father took the deity to be the Mistress of the Dakṣiṇāmnāya; however, on occasions, he also worshiped her as Kubjikā:

brahmāṇī kamalendu-saumya-vadanā māheśvarī līlayā  
kaumārī ripu-darpa-nāśana-karī cakrāyudhā vaiṣṇavī \|  
vārāhī ghana-ghora-gharghara-mukhī aindrī ca vajrāyudhā  
cāmuṇḍā gaṇanātha-bhairava-yutā rakṣantu māṃ mātṛkāḥ \|\| 1 \|\|

pīṭhe madhya-nivāsinī bhagavatī pretāsanā sundarī  
raktāṅgī dhṛta-śūla-śakti-sukarā saṃsāra-sāra-pradā \|  
brahmopendra-surendra-kinnara-gaṇaiḥ saṃpūjitā sarvadā  
tāṃ vande śatadhā namāmi śirasā kāmārtha-siddhi-pradām \|\| 2 \|\|

brahmāṇy-ādibhir ambikā parivṛtā kṣetre sthitā yoginī  
śrī-vīreśi kuleśvarī samarasā śūlākṣamālā-dhṛtā \|  
unmattā dhṛta-śūla-maṅgala-yutā pīyūṣa-dhārāvṛtā  
premollāsa-rasārdra-bindu-sahitā cakreśvari tvāṃ bhaje \|\| 3 \|\|

1.  May the Divine Mothers protect me: Brahmāṇī, whose face is serene as
    a lotus and the full moon; Māheśvarī, who acts through effortless
    divine play; Kaumārī, who destroys the arrogance of enemies;
    Vaiṣṇavī, armed with the discus; Vārāhī, whose snout utters deep,
    terrifying snorts; Aindrī, equipped with the thunderbolt; and
    Cāmuṇḍā, joined by the Gaṇanātha and the Bhairava-s 2.  Residing in the very center of the sacred seat, the Supreme Goddess,
    seated upon the corpse-throne, exquisitely beautiful,
    crimson-limbed, bearing a trident and spear in her beautiful hands,
    the bestower of the ultimate essence of corporal existence.
    Worshipped by Brahmā, Upendra (Viṣṇu), Surendra, and the hosts of
    Kinnaras – I worship her, bowing my head a hundredfold, the granter
    of desires, wealth, accomplishments. 3.  Encircled by Brahmāṇī and the others, the Mother, the supreme Yoginī
    abiding in the sacred site, O Śrī Vīreśī, you are the mistress of
    the Kula, the essence of equivalence, bearing the trident and
    rosary, frenzied, bearing the trident, endowed with auspiciousness,
    enveloped in streams of nectar, united with the central Point, moist
    with the nectar of the bliss of love – O Cakreśvarī, I praise you!

When Cakreśvarī is worshipped as Kubjikā, she is further praised with the following sragdharā verse:

śyāmā raktā trinetrā stana-ghana-namitā matta-mātaṅga-gāmī  
bimboṣṭhī cārunetrā pṛthu-tara-jaghanā mekhalā-ratnaśobhā ।  
devāṅgā vastra-śobhā vara-vara-kusumā barbarā keśabhārā  
sā me śrī-kubjikākhyā vitaratu tarasā jñāna-divyaugham ekam ॥

Dark, ruddy, three-eyed, her heavy breasts causing her to bend, she moves with the gait of an intoxicated elephant; her lips are like bimba fruit, her eyes beautiful, her hips exceedingly broad, and she is beautiful with a jeweled girdle. Divine-limbed, splendid in her garments, adorned with the choicest flowers, with a luxuriant mass of curly hair – may that Lady Kubjikā swiftly bestow upon me the knowledge of the one divine transmission (divyaugha of the Paścimāmnāya).

In this practice, Kubjikā is mediated upon as having 6 faces:

| Face         | Direction |
|--------------|-----------|
| Hṛtkamalā    | Upward    |
| Kuladīpā     | East      |
| Barbaraśikhā | South     |
| Bahurūpā     | North     |
| Mahāntārikā  | West      |
| Koṅkaṇā      | Downward  |

Thereafter, the Bhairava is worshipped in the center of Mātṛcakra. When invoked as the lord of the Paścimāmnāya, the following sragdharā verse is first deployed, which is popular among practitioners of the Paścimāmnāya:

saṃvartā-maṇḍalānte krama-pada-nihitānanda-śaktiḥ subhīmā  
saṃsṛjyādyaṃ catuṣkam akula-kula-gataṃ pañcakaṃ cānya ṣaṭkam \|  
catvāraḥ pañcako ’nyaḥ punar api caturas tattvato maṇḍale ’dam \|  
saṃsṛṣṭaṃ yena tasmai namata guru-varaṃ bhairavaṃ śrī-kujeśam \|\|

At the end of the Saṃvarta-maṇḍala, established in the prescribed sequence, abides the exceedingly terrible Ānanda-śakti. Having brought forth the first four, the five belonging to Akula-Kula, and another six; four, another five, and again four – this \[arrangement\] is in the maṇḍala according to the tattva-s. To him by whom this was brought together, bow: to the excellent Guru, Bhairava Śrīkujeśa.

The great Bhairava (Mahābhairava) is worshiped with a śārdūlavikrīḍita verse. While several exemplars of it are found in different manuscripts, they are all badly mangled from corruption. However, based on R1’s father’s oral recitation and a comparison of the manuscripts, we have restored metrically perfect versions of the verse. There are two variants that can be discerned across manuscripts. The great Bhairava is termed śrī-Siddhayogeśvara, who is the male counterpart of the great Bhairavī of a very early stratum of the Pūrvāmnāya tradition, Siddhayogeśvarī (the goddess after whom the Siddhayogeśvarī-mata is named). Thus, under this name, the Bhairava performs his classical Rudrian duties of absorbing or destroying the manifest universe, congruent to the action of Navātman Bhairava or Samvarta in the Saundaryalaharī and the Paścimāmnāya.

kvākāśaḥ kva samīraṇaḥ kva dahanaḥ kvenduḥ kva viśvambharaḥ  
kva brahmā kva janārdanaḥ kva bhujagaḥ kvendraḥ kva devāsurāḥ ।  
kalpāntāra-mahā-naṭaḥ pramuditaḥ śrī-siddhayogeśvaraḥ  
krīḍā-nāṭaka-nāyako vijayate devo mahābhairavaḥ ॥

Where is space? Where is the wind? Where is fire? Where is the Moon, and where is the all-sustaining Sun\*?  
Where is Brahmā? Where is Janārdana? Where is the cosmic serpent (Śeṣa), where is Indra, and where are the Deva-s and Asura-s?  
Rejoicing as the Great Actor who oversees the cosmic eons, the Lord Siddhayogeśvara  
The great god Bhairava, the lead actor in the \[cosmic\] play, reigns supreme in triumph.  
\* could also mean Earth but translated as Sun for parity with the Moon. Consistent with this, in the version below, the Earth is separately mentioned.

kvākāśaḥ kva samīraṇaḥ kva dahanaḥ kvendraḥ kva devāsurāḥ  
kva brahmā kva janārdanaḥ kva dharaṇiḥ kvāpaḥ kva viśvaṃbharaḥ \|  
kalpāntāra-mahā-naṭaḥ pramuditaḥ śrī-siddhayogeśvaraḥ  
krīḍā-nāṭaka-nāyako vijayate devo mahābhairavaḥ \|\|

Where is space? Where is the wind? Where is fire? Where is Indra, and where are the Deva-s and Asura-s?  
Where is Brahmā? Where is Janārdana? Where is the Earth? Whither the waters? Where is the all-sustaining Sun?  
Rejoicing as the Great Actor who oversees the cosmic eons, the Lord Siddhayogeśvara,  
the great god Bhairava, the lead actor in the \[cosmic\] play, reigns supreme in triumph.

Thereafter Baṭukanātha, the ectype of Skanda worshipped in the Kulāmnāya-s, is praised using a famous stuti in the vasantatilakā chandas. These verses appear to have emerged in the worship of the deity alongside the mātṛ-s in the Paścimāmnāya (referred to as the Śrī-śāsana):

raktāmbaraṃ jvalita-piṅga-jaṭā-kalāpaṃ  
jvālāvalī-vitata-śakti-dharaṃ pracaṇḍam \|  
sūryodayārka-kanaka-tanu-kānti-gauraṃ  
gaurī-sutaṃ baṭukanātham ahaṃ namāmi \|\|

Clad in crimson robes, bearing a mass of blazing, tawny matted locks,  
Emerging from a wreath of radiant flames, wielding a spear, formidable in form,  
Fair-complexioned with a bodily luster glowing like pure gold and the rising sun,  
Unto Him, the son of Gaurī, Lord Baṭukanātha, I bow in adoration!

śrī-śāsane baṭukanātha iti prasiddhaḥ  
skandaḥ surāri-jana-kānana-dhūmraketuḥ \|  
gaurī-sutaḥ sakalam ujjvalitaṃ dadānaḥ  
pāyāt su-rakta-vasanaḥ śikhi-vāhano vaḥ \|\|

Renowned in the auspicious tradition (the Kulāmnāya) as Lord Baṭuka,  
He is Skanda, a comet \[of destruction\] unto the dense forest of demon-folks  
The son of Gaurī, who has put forth all-\[illuminating\] luminosity,  
Clad in bright red robes and mounted upon the peacock, may he protect you all!

Thereafter, the Herambanātha-stuti, a śārdūlavikrīḍita verse dedicated to the Kulagaṇeśvara Herambanātha, is used in the Mātṛ-cakra-pūjā:

gauraṃ tryakṣa-vibhāti-pañca-vadanaṃ siṃhastham ākarṣaṇam  
pāśaṃ mudgara-ṭaṅkam-akṣa-varadaṃ nāgeśvaraṃ taṃ dhṛtam \|  
pātraṃ modaka-pūritaṃ ca abhayaṃ \[1\] savyāpasavyaiḥ karair  
bibhrac chūla-manoharaṃ sukhakaraṃ \[2\] heramba-nāthaṃ bhaje \|\|

The fair-complexioned one, with five faces shining brightly with three eyes each, seated upon a lion, and captivating to behold, he holds in his hands a noose, a war-hammer, an adze, a rosary, the gesture granting boons, and the serpent-king, while bearing a bowl filled with modaka-s, the gesture of fearlessness, and a charming trident in his left and right hands – the maker of pleasures – Lord Heramba – I worship.

1\. ca abhayaṃ: resolves the saṃdhi to maintain a perfect śārdūlavikrīḍita. We are not attempting any emendation here. The alternative, with multiple manuscript attestations, reads:  
pātraṃ modaka-pūritam abhayadaṃ savyāpasavyaiḥ karair  
This reading removes the irregular saṃdhi but slightly departs from the śārdūlavikrīḍita.  
2. variant reading: sujaṭharaṃ – with a good belly.

Finally, the two-verse anuṣtubh praise of Mahākāla is uttered:

mahākālaṃ mahāraudraṃ mahā-kṛṣṇāñjana-prabham ।  
sthūla-rūpaṃ mahākāyaṃ kartrī-kapāla-dhāriṇam ॥ 1 ॥  
khaḍga-kheṭaka-dhāriṇaṃ tri-netraṃ jvalana-prabham ।  
namāmi mahākālaṃ taṃ śatru-saṃhāra-kāraṇam ॥ 2 ॥

1.  The immensely fierce Mahākāla, whose dark radiance resembles black
    collyrium, possessing a massive, imposing form, he wields a cleaver
    and holds a skull-cup. 2.  Holding a sword and shield, three-eyed and shining with the
    brilliance of blazing fire—I bow unto that Mahākāla, the cause of
    the destruction of all enemies.

Thereafter, the core pūjā of the Aṣṭamātṛkā-s is initiated. Each mātṛkā is associated with a direction in the ogdoad, a kṣetra, and a holy tree:

| Goddess        | Direction | Kṣetra   | Holy Tree |
|----------------|-----------|----------|-----------|
| **Brahmāṇī**   | E         | Prayāga  | Udumbara  |
| **Māheśvarī**  | SE        | Vārāṇasī | Tāla      |
| **Kaumārī**    | S         | Kolāpuri | Vaṭa      |
| **Vaiṣṇavī**   | SW        | Aṭṭahāsa | Kadamba   |
| **Vārāhī**     | W         | Jayantī  | Nimba     |
| **Aindrī**     | NW        | Caritra  | Kadamba   |
| **Cāmuṇḍā**    | N         | Ekāmra   | Aśvattha  |
| **Mahālakṣmī** | NW        | Devīkoṭa | Plakṣa    |

Further, mātṛkā is worshipped with her Bhairava consort (the “Bhairavaized” version of her standard male counterpart), two dūtī-s (one of them is zoocephalous), 8 kulayoginī-s and a Kṣetrapāla.

At the conclusion of each mātṛkā’s and her retinue’s and kṣetra’s worship, the following stuti-s are recited.

**1. Brāhmāṇī**  
The gīti of Brahmāṇī:  
brahmāṇī brahma-sāvitrī brahma-tattva-nivedinī ।  
catur-bhujā catur-vaktrā catur-veda-parāparā ॥  
carācara-jagat-vyāpī caturvarga-parāyaṇā ।  
haṃsa-yukta-vimānasthā saumya-rūpā pitāmahī ॥  
puṣṭakaṃ japamālāṃ ca varadābhaya-pāṇinī ।  
pīta-puṣpa-ratā devī pītāṅgī pīta-vāsinī ॥  
pītopahāra-santuṣṭhā pīta-gandhānulepinī ।  
udumbara-taru-sthitā prayāga-kṣetra-vāsinī ।  
pūrva-pīṭhe sthitā nityaṃ brahmaśaktyai namo ‘stu te ॥

1.  Brahmāṇī, Sāvitrī among the mantra-s, revealing the ultimate essence
    of mantra-s, four-armed, four-faced, the supreme embodiment of the
    four Vedas. 2.  Pervading the moving and unmoving universe, devoted to bestowing the
    four goals of life, seated upon a chariot drawn by swans,
    gentle-formed, the Grandmother \[of existence\]. 3.  Holding a book and rosary, displaying the gestures of granting boons
    and protection, the Goddess delights in yellow flowers,
    yellow-limbed, clad in yellow garments. 4.  Pleased by yellow offerings, anointed with yellow paste, abiding
    under the Udumbara tree, dwelling in the sacred field of Prayāga,
    ever established in the Eastern station, to the energy of Brahma,
    obeisance be unto You.

**2. Māheśvarī**  
The gīti of Māheśvarī:  
māheśvarī mahādevī moha-māyā-nirañjanī ।  
mahāvṛṣa-samārūḍhā mahā-huṃkāra-nādinī ॥  
hema-varṇa-nibhā-dehā kapāla-śaśi-śekharā ।  
trilocanā śūlākṣasūtra-kamaṇḍalu-dhāriṇī ॥  
hārālaṅkāra-sarvāṅgī dakṣiṇena varapradā ।  
sṛṣṭi-sthiti-vināśānāṃ sā vyāpinī ca śūlinī ॥  
śveta-puṣparatā devī śvetāṅgī śveta-vāsinī ।  
śveta-vastra-parīdhānā muktābharaṇa-bhūṣitā ॥  
vārāṇasyām mahākṣetre tāla-vṛkṣa-nivāsinī ।  
āgneye ca sthitā pīṭhe māheśvaryai namo ‘stu te ॥

1.  Māheśvarī, the Great Goddess, is untainted by delusion and illusion,
    she who is seated upon the great bull and utters the mighty sound of
    “Huṃ.” 2.  Her body shines with a golden hue, and her crest is adorned with a
    skull and the crescent moon. śhe is three-eyed and holds a trident,
    a rosary, and a spouted pot. 3.  Her entire body is adorned with necklaces and ornaments; with her
    right hand, she bestows boons. She, the wielder of the trident, is
    the all-pervading \[energy\] behind creation, sustenance, and
    dissolution. 4.  Residing beneath the Tāla (Palmyra) tree in the great abode of
    Vārāṇasī, and established in the South-East directional seat – unto
    Māheśvarī – salutations to you.

**3. Kaumārī**  
The gīti of Kaumārī:  
vīrabhāve mahāraudrī kaumārī raktalocanā \|  
rakta-vastra-paridhānā sindhūrāruṇa-vigrahā \|\| 1 \|\|  
catur-bhujā śakti-hastā siddhi-pātra-dharā śubhā \|  
kaumārī paramā śaktir mayūra-vara-vāhinī \|\| 2 \|\|  
rakta-puṣpa-ratā devī rakta-māṃsa-vasāśinī \|  
kolāpuryām mahākṣetre vaṭa-vṛkṣa-nivāsinī \|  
yāmyapīṭhe sthitā nityaṃ kaumāri ca namo ‘stu te \|\| 3 \|\|

1.  She who is exceedingly fierce in the heroic state, Kaumārī, the
    red-eyed one. Clad in red garments, her body glowing vermilion-red
    \[like freshly applied\] sindūra (orange pigment). 2.  Four-armed, wielding the spear in her hand, holding the magic-vessel
    of attainments, the auspicious one, the supreme energy of the god
    Kumāra, mounted upon the noble peacock. 3.  The Goddess who delights in red flowers, who partakes of \[ritual
    offerings\] of blood, flesh, and marrow, dwelling at the banyan tree
    within the great seat of Kolāpura, ever stationed at the southern
    seat, obeisance to you, O Kaumārī!

*\* The Kolāpura mahākṣetra is located in the eponymous city in modern Mahārāṣṭra.*

**4. Vaiṣṇavī**  
The gīti of Vaiṣṇavī:  
vaiṣṇavī viṣṇumāyā ca daitya-durgārti-nāśinī \|  
harita-śyāma-varṇāṅgī garuḍopari-saṃsthitā \|\| 1 \|\|  
śaṅkha-cakra-gadā-hastā catur-bāhu-vibhūṣitā \|  
haripuṣpaṃ ca gandhaṃ ca sadā haritadhāriṇī \|\| 2 \|\|  
svarga-martye ca pātāle sthiti-rūpeṇa saṃsthitā \|  
\<nānā-vastra-dharā devī nānā-bhūṣaṇa-bhūṣitā \|\| 3 \|\|\>  
aṭṭahāse mahākṣetre kadamba-vṛkṣa-vāsinī \|  
tiṣṭhasi pīṭha-nairṛtye nārāyaṇī namo ‘stu te \|\| 4 \|\|

1.  O Vaiṣṇavī, the very Māyā of Viṣṇu, destroyer of the terrible
    afflictions caused by the Daityas! Whose limbs shine with a verdant,
    dark-emerald complexion, seated majestically atop Garuḍa! 2.  Holding the conch, discus, and mace in her hands, adorned with four
    arms! Ever bearing fresh green garlands, fragrant unguents, and
    flowers sacred to Hari! 3.  Established throughout Heaven, Earth, and the Underworld as the very
    embodiment of cosmic sustenance: the Goddess wearing multi-hued
    garments, adorned with diverse ornaments! 4.  Dwelling by the Kadamba tree at the great sacred field of Aṭṭahāsa!
    Abiding in the south-western quarter of the sacred Pīṭha—O Nārāyaṇī,
    salutations unto thee!

*\* Aṭṭahāsa with a svAyambhuva liṅga is believed to be in the vaṅga country and place where chitranAtha, one of matsyendra’s sons, taught.*

The gīti of Vaiṣṇavī is corrupt in the manuscripts and 3cd restored from the oral recitation.

**5. Vārāhī**  
The gīti of Vārāhī:  
vārāhi ghora-raktāṅgī raktakeśī mahodarī ।  
daṃṣṭrā-karālā gambhīrā varārka-teja-rūpinī ॥  
kapāla-mālā-saṃyuktā vicitra-puṣpa-śobhitā ।  
mahā-mahiṣam ārūḍhā jvalitāgni-samaprabhā ॥  
mīnāṅkuśa-kartrikādyā hārābharaṇa-bhūṣitā ।  
tri-netrā jvalitā-dehā duṣṭa-darpa-vināśinī ॥  
jayantī-kṣetra-saṃsthānā nimba-vṛkṣa-samāśṛtā ।  
nāgapīṭhe sthitā nityaṃ kolarūpī namo ‘stu te ॥

1.  Vārāhī, terrifyingly crimson-limbed, red-haired and large-bellied,
    terrifying with tusks, solemn, and possessing the brilliance of the
    supreme sun. 2.  Adorned with a garland of skulls, decorated with various colorful
    flowers, mounted upon a great buffalo, radiant like the blazing
    fire. 3.  Holding a fish, a hook, and a cleaver (kartrikā) in her hands,
    adorned with jeweled necklaces. Three-eyed, with a blazing body, the
    destroyer of the arrogance of the wicked. 4.  Stationed in the sacred field of Jayantī, dwelling under the neem
    tree. Ever abiding in the Nāgapīṭha (the Western station), she of
    the form of a boar, obeisance be unto You!

*\* Jayantī is probably the kṣetra near Chandigarh.*

**6. Indrāṇī**  
The gīti of Indrāṇī:  
śakreśvarī sahasrākṣī kuṅkumāruṇa-vigrahā \|  
sureśvarī sadā devī sarvālaṅkāra-bhūṣitā \|\| 1 \|\|  
catur-bhujā viśālākṣī vajra-ghaṇṭā-vidhāriṇī \|  
mahāvajradharī devī sthitā airāvatāgragā \|\| 2 \|\|  
nānā-puṣpa-ratā devī nānā-ratna-vibhūṣitā \|  
nānā-gandha-viliptāṅgī nānā-vastra-virājitā \|\| 3 \|\|  
caritre ca mahākṣetre kadamba-vṛkṣa-vāsinī \|  
vāyu-pīṭhe sthitā nityaṃ śakreśvarī namo ‘stu te \|\| 4 \|\|

1.  O Śakreśvarī! O Thousand-eyed Goddess, whose form is crimson red
    like vermilion! O Queen of the Gods, eternal Goddess, adorned with
    every ornament! 2.  Four-armed, large-eyed, bearing the thunderbolt and the bell! O
    Goddess, wielding the great thunderbolt, seated atop Airāvata! 3.  Goddess who delights in various flowers, adorned with diverse
    jewels, whose limbs are smeared with varied fragrances, resplendent
    in multi-colored garments! 4.  Dwelling in the Kadamba tree at the great sacred site of Caritra,
    eternally abounding at the Vāyupīṭha: (Northwest) O Śakreśvarī,
    salutations unto thee!

*\* Caritra is believed to have been located in the Konkan-s.*

**7. Cāmuṇḍā**  
The gīti of Cāmuṇḍā:  
cāmuṇḍā caṇḍikā caṇḍa-caṇḍā caṇḍāsi-dhāriṇī ।  
caṇḍāṭṭahāsā caṇḍākṣī pracaṇḍa-hāsa-hāsinī ॥  
daṃṣṭrā-karālā raktāṅgī piṅgakeśī kṛśodarī ।  
kṛṣṇāṅgī bhīṣanī raudrī jihvābharaṇa-bhīṣaṇī ॥  
mahā-preta-samārūḍhā bhujā aṣṭā suśobhinī ।  
ari-carma-yutā-hastā ḍhakka-khaṭvāṅga-dhāriṇī ॥  
kartrī-kapāla-hastāṅgī varadābhaya-bhūṣitā ।  
nara-carma-dhṛtā devī dvipi-carma-kaṭi-sthitā ॥  
muṇḍa-mālādharī raudrī antrā-bharaṇa-bhūṣitā ।  
hārālaṃkāra-sarvāṅgī ali-phalgu-sadāpriyā ॥  
sahasra-sūrya-saṃkāśā romakupe prati prati ।  
jvālā-mālākulā-dehā tadit-koṭi-samaprabhā ॥  
bhūta-vetāla-ḍākinyaḥ parivāraṃ ca rakṣikāḥ ।  
ekāṃraka-mahākṣetre ‘śvattha-vṛkṣa-nivāsinī ॥  
pīṭhe uttara-saṃsthāne cāmuṇḍāyai namo ‘stu te ॥

1.  Cāmuṇḍā, O Caṇḍikā, the fierce of the fierce, who wields a terrible
    sword, with a terrible laughter, fiery-eyed, laughing with a
    formidable laughter. 2.  Terrifying with prominent fangs, crimson-limbed, tawny-haired, with
    a thin belly, dark-bodied, frightful, fierce, made awe-inspiring by
    the ornament of her \[protruding\] tongue. 3.  Standing atop a great corpse, splendid with eight arms, holding a
    shield of enemy-hide in her hand, wielding the hand drum and
    skull-topped staff. 4.  Holding a cleaver and skull-cup in her hands, adorned with gestures
    of boons and fearlessness, the Goddess holding up flayed human skin,
    with a leopard-skin wrapped around her waist. 5.  Wearing a garland of severed heads, dreadful, adorned with entrails
    as ornaments. Her entire body is bedecked with jeweled necklaces,
    ever delighted by ritual wine and wild figs. 6.  Resembling a thousand suns at every single hair-pore \[of her
    body\], her body enveloped in garlands of fire, possessing the
    radiance of ten million lightning flashes. 7.  She has ghosts, zombie-ghosts, and ḍākinīs as her entourage and
    female guardians. Dwelling at the Aśvattha tree in the great sacred
    field of Ekāmraka, stationed in the northern seat, to Cāmuṇḍā,
    obeisance be unto You.

*\* Ekāmra-kṣetra is identified with either Kāñcī in the Tamiẓ country or the eponymous kṣetra in Oḍiśa.*

**Mahālakṣmī**  
The gīti of Mahālakṣmī:  
mahālakṣmī mahādevī saubhāgya-sura-sundarī ।  
vaiḍurya-pādukārūḍhā siṃhāsana-paristhitā ॥  
caturbhujā viśālākṣī khaḍga-kheṭaka-dhāriṇī ।  
nūpura-hāra-keyūrā ratna-kuṇḍala-dhāriṇī ॥  
ratna-maṇḍita-sarvāṅgī cūḍāmaṇī-vibhūṣitā ।  
vicitra-puṣpa-ratnāḍhyā vastra-gandhānulepinī ॥  
trailokya-vyāpinī devī sarvastha-sacarācaraṃ ।  
siddha-gandharva-namitā vidyādhara-surārcitā ॥  
devīkoṭe mahāplakṣe saṃhārāṣṭa-kulākrame ।  
īśāna-pīṭha-saṃsthāne mahālakṣmyai namo ‘stu te ॥

1.  Mahālakṣmī, the Great Goddess, the divine celestial beauty of good
    fortune, she wears sandals inlaid with beryl and is seated
    comfortably upon a lion-throne (siṃhāsana). 2.  Four-armed and large-eyed, wielding a sword and a shield, she wears
    anklets, necklaces, armlets, and gem-studded earrings. 3.  Her entire body is decked with gems, and she is adorned with a
    crest-jewel. She is richly adorned with wondrous flowers and jewels,
    wearing \[fine\] garments and anointed with fragrance. 4.  The Goddess pervades the three worlds and everything abiding within
    them, both moving and unmoving; she is revered by Siddhas and
    Gandharvas, and worshiped by Vidyādharas and Devas. 5.  At Devīkoṭa beneath the great Plakṣa (fig) tree, in the absorptive
    kula sequence of the Eight, established in the Northeastern seat
    unto Mahālakṣmī, salutations unto You.
