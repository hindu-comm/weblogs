Some notes on the Parā tantra

This is by no means a comprehensive treatment of the text under consideration. Nor does it aim to perform a comparative analysis of other tantra-s and paddhati-s with a similarly synthetic outlook. It gives a broad overview of the text and considers a few points of interest in greater detail.

The Parā tantra teaches the Ṣaḍ-āmnāya system, which adds two additional āmnāya-s to the original four that comprise the archaic directional tetrad (e.g., as in the Ciñciṇī-mata-sāra-samuccaya). The 6 āmnāya-s taught by the Parā tantra with the respective central deities are: East: Pūrvāmnāya – Pūrṇeśvarī (see below); South: Niśeśvarī (see below); West: Kubjikā; North: Kālī; Zenith: Tripurā; Nadir: Vajrayāna deities.

The locus of the Parā tantra’s teaching is the cemetery of Karavīra (modern Kolhapur):

bahu-siddhi-samākīrṇe śmaśāne karavīrake \|  
tatra vīra-gaṇāḥ sarve mahā-hallāla-huṃkṛte \|\| 1 \|\|

In the cremation ground of Karavīra, completely saturated with many magical perfections, all the hosts of heroic initiates gather there, with thunderous roars and huṃkāra-s.

nivartita-mahācakre yoge tridaśa-maṇḍale \|  
mahāvṛnda-mahāsphāla-raṇa-ḍākinī yācite \|\| 2 \|\|

In the great wheel, conjoined with the assembly of the gods, is the great congregation and great throbbing vibration of the solicitation of ḍākinī-s of the battlefield.

brāhmyādyā mātaraś cāṣṭau kṣetreśa-bhairavādayaḥ \|  
gaṇeśa vaṭuka siddhā mātṛ-cakre tu melake \|\| 3 \|\|

The Eight mātṛ-s headed by Brāhmī, the Lords of the Field, the Bhairava-s, Gaṇeśa, Vaṭuka, and the siddha-s all converge for the union in the wheel of the mātṛ-s”

It distinguishes as the ultimate āmnāya (Ūrdhvāmnāya) the worship of Tripura-bhairavī or Tripura-sundarī (the Śrīkula goddess) from the goddess of the Dakṣiṇāmnāya. It identifies the Ūrdhvāmnāya with the geographic locale of Oḍḍiyāna and stations Tripurā with the 16 Nityā-s in the Śrī-cakra:

nityā ṣoḍaśa-vidyāyā navacakre vyavasthitā \|  
navacakreśvarī nityā nava-koṇāgrata-sthitā \|\| 17 \|\|

She is present in the nine-fold cakra with vidyā-s of 16 Nityā-s; she is the mistress of the nine-fold cakra, the Nityā stationed in the midst of the nine-triangles.

The Śrī-cakra is understood as the nine-fold, as it is comprised of the nine circuits: 1. Bindu; 2. the Baindava cakra or Antar-trikoṇa (the internal triangle); 3. the Vasu-koṇa (the eight-cornered cakra); 4. the Antar-daśāra (the inner 10-cornered cakra); 5. Bahir-daśāra (the outer 10-cornered cakra); 6. Manv-aśra (14-cornered cakra); 7. the Vasu-dala-padma (the 8-petalled lotus); 8. the Ṣoḍaśa-dala-padma (16-cornered lotus); 9. the Paridhi (the bounding structure of three circles and the Bhūpura).

Notably, it also frequently calls the other goddesses of its system “Nityā” (26 times in structurally similar phrases), perhaps signaling their identity with the nityā-s at the heart of the Śrīkula tradition.

It incorporates the Vajrayāna deities in the adhāmnāya and salvages some of the Bauddha traditions into the Hindu fold. Vairocanī, the central goddess of this āmnāya is visualized as three-headed, four-armed, holding a cleaver, a skull, a khaṭvāṅga and a ḍamaru, reddish in color, and standing on a corpse. She is accompanied by the bhairava-s, who are none other than Kālacakra, Saṃvara, Hayagrīva, Yogāmbara and Kāleśa. Thus, it presents a comprehensive plan of incorporating the entire kaula tradition within a single unified rubric.

The Uttarāmnāya of this system, as expected, teaches the Kālī-kula. The first form of Kālī taught there in Mahākālī Caṇḍayogeśvarī, who is explicitly associated with Viṣṇu:

koṭi-koṭi-prabhedo ‘sti kālikā tīvra-nāyikā \|  
ājñā-siddhi-pradā devī viṣṇu-tejopa-bṛṃhitā \|\| 2 \|\|

Kālikā, the intense leader, possesses crores upon crores of diverse modalities. She is the Goddess who bestows the successful execution of commands, continuously amplified by the luster Viṣṇu.

nārāyaṇī mahākālī jagad-rakṣaṇa-tatparā \|  
kāli kālī mahākālī caṇḍayogeśvarīti ca \|\| 3 \|\|

She is Nārāyaṇī, she is Mahākālī, intent on protecting the universe. She is repeatedly addressed as Kālī, as Mahākālī, and as Caṇḍayogeśvarī.

In this form, she is visualized as three-headed, nine-eyed, with a blue complexion, but her left head is orangish as a flame, and her right head is yellow. She wears a garland of 27 ( == number of nakṣatra-s) severed heads.

Thereafter, the Uttarāmnāya chapter mentions a form of Kālī seated in padmāsana with 9 faces. Finally, it mentions the 5-fold kālī-krama of the 5-faced Guhyakālī. While her zoocephaly is unmentioned, she is described as accompanied by pairs of falcons, jackals, crows, vultures, owls, eagles, bees and rhinos (kaṅkaṭin = armored animal). The kālī-kula is said to be promulgated by Durvāsas, Nārada, Vyāsa, Vasiṣṭha and Kauśika.

After teaching the 6 āmnāya-s, the Parā tantra moves on to the Kālī-krama (separately from the Uttarāmnāya: paṭala 7). The overarching deity of this system is Kālasaṅkarṣiṇī, whom Kuleśvara-Sadāśiva salutes in two verses before beginning his teaching:

brahmāṇḍa-koṭi-lakṣāṇi bhasma sā kurute yayā \|  
tāṃ kāla-grāsa-rasanāṃ kālasaṅkarṣiṇīṃ namaḥ \|\| 13 \|\|

Salutations to Her, Kālasaṅkarṣiṇī, by whom crores and lakhs of universes are reduced to ash; she whose very tongue is the ultimate devourer of Time itself.

yasyā krīḍārtham akhilaM brahmāṇḍaM kandukāyate \|  
tāṃ kāla-grāsana-ratāṃ kālasaṅkarṣiṇīṃ namaḥ \|\| 14 \|\|

Salutations to Her, Kālasaṅkarṣiṇī, for whose play the entire cosmos is like a mere ball; she who is perpetually delighted in devouring Time itself.

Her krama’s divine transmission is said to have been from Brahman down to Kubera. Thereafter, it was transmitted to the rākṣasa-s of Laṅkā, like Rāvaṇa, Kumbhakarṇa, Vibhīṣaṇa and Meghanāda; the daitya-s Hiraṇyakaśipu, Bāṇa, Bali, and Jalandhara; the Aryan monarchs Ambarīṣa, Māndhātṛ, Nahuṣa and Haihaya; the sages mentioned above, and among them Vasiṣṭha and Viśvāmitra, transmitted to Rāmacandra. In the world of men, it was introduced by the siddha Virūpākṣa and his successors. This krama, which it teaches (with several mantra-s), is the five-fold krama of the 5 kālī-s mapping onto the 5 elementals (solid, liquid, gas, radiation, space) and 5 stages of manifestation of the universe:

Sṛṣṭi-kālī (emergence); 2. Sthiti-kālī (stationary phase); 3. Saṃhāra-kālī (destruction); 4. Anākhyā-kālī devī (inexplicable); 5. Bhāṣā-kālī (pure light). The meditations of each of these 5 kālī-s are detailed further down in the text.

Then all five of these in a unified form are present together in the body of Guhyakālī. Here she is explicitly described in her 10-headed zoocephalic form (1. human; 2. lion; 3. jackal; 4. ape; 5. boar; 6. eagle; 7. crocodile; 8. elephant; 9. horse; 10. divine) with 54 arms and wearing a garland of 27 severed heads.

She is worshipped in a complex maṇḍala with several circuits comparable to those specified in the Guhyakālī section of the Mahākāla Saṃhitā:

bindu trikoṇa pañcāra-navakoṇa punar likhet \|  
aṣṭāraṃ cāṣṭa-dalakaṃ daśa-dvādaśakaṃ tathā \|\| 36 \|\|

One should draw the central point (1), the central triangle (2), the pentagram (3), and the nine-angled figure (4) thereafter. Following that, one should draw the eight-pointed star (5), the eight-petaled lotus (6), as well as a ten-petalled lotus (7) and the twelve-petaled one (8).

ṣoḍaśaṃ dalam saṃlikhya muṇḍa-prākāra saṃlikhet \|  
catuḥ pañcāśakaṃ devī kabandha samudāhṛtam \|\| 37 \|\|

Having inscribed a sixteen-petaled lotus (9), one must draw an outer rampart composed of \[27 – based on the iconography\] severed human heads (10). Beyond that, O Goddess, sits a \[ring\] composed of 54 headless trunks (11), as has been declared.

These iconographic sections of the long chapter are followed by a long section on the encodings of mantra-s.

In the next chapter of the Parā Tantra, Śiva teaches the Māhārtha system centered on the 17-syllabled mantra of Kālī (Saptadaśākṣarī). It is stated that the system was originally taught by Nārada to Kṛṣṇa and by him to Arjuna. It associates the Goddess with the utterance ha-ha, the first of five such short phrases (apparently of old Tamil-Kannada borderland provenance), which are uttered in the Māhārtha, one for each of the 5 krama-s (as in the above-stated Pañcakrama). It specifies the use of unclean substances in worship.

tatra bhede nāmabhedā ekā shaktiḥ sahasradhā \|  
maṅgalā ca mahāmāyā caṇḍa-kapālinī śivā \|\| 97 \|\|

cāmuṇḍā carcikā caṇḍī sṛṣṭi-sthity-anta-kālīnī \|  
anākhyā ca mahābhāsā guhyakālī jayā parā \|\| 98 \|\|

siddhilakṣmī viśvalakṣmī mahāpratyaṅgirā smṛtā \|  
sundarī tripurā cārvī ugracaṇḍā prakāśinī \|\| 99 \|\|

vibhinnā bahudhā jātā nānā-bhedam āśritā \|  
ekā sā bahurūpā ca jānīyād divya-cakṣuṣā \|\| 100 \|\|

The colophons of two of the chapters (7 and 9) present themselves as the teaching of the Śiraścheda, originally a Śaiva-Tantra of the Vāma-srotas, later incorporated into the voluminous Jayadratha Yāmala. The final chapter (9th paṭala) teaches the rules for mantra-practice to attain various siddhi-s. It ends with a list of the various forms of the Great Goddess:

**Pūrvāmnāya  ** In our opinion, the parā tantra remembers some unique features of a transmission of the Pūrvāmnāya distinct from that outlined in the Ciñciṇī-mata-sāra-samuccaya, and expounded in the Mālinī-vijaya and Trika. However, it is probably aligned to a degree with that of the Siddhayogeśvarī-mata. The central goddess of the āmnāya, Pūrṇeśvarī, is described thus:

pūrṇeśvarī mahogrā sā pūrvāmnāya-prakīrtitā \|  
cakreśvaryās tu vakṣyāmi sādhakānāṃ hitāya vai \|\| 55 \|\|

She, Pūrṇeśvarī, intensely fierce, is celebrated in the Eastern Transmission. Now, for the ultimate benefit of the practitioners, I shall describe the mistress of the cakra.

dhyānaṃ tu citte ‘dhiṣṭhāya dhyātavyā parameśvarī \|  
indra-nīla-samaprakhyāṃ daśa-dor-daṇḍa-dhāriṇīm \|\| 56 \|\|

Establishing this visualization firmly within the consciousness, one should meditate upon the Supreme Goddess. She possesses a complexion resembling a deep blue sapphire and wields ten staff-like arms.

varābhayakaraṃ ramyaṃ satriśūlaṃ kapālakam \|  
ḍamaruṃ cākṣamālāṃ ca śaktīṃ caiva karāmbuje \|\| 57 \|\|

In her lotus-like hands, she holds beautiful gestures granting boons and fearlessness, a trident, a skull, a hand-drum, a rosary, and a spear.

ikṣu-cāpāṃ śarāṃ caiva khaḍga-hastāṃ suśobhinīm \|  
mahāpretopari tatra candra-sūryāsanāsthitām \|\| 58 \|\|

She also holds a sugarcane bow, arrows, and brandishes a sword, appearing supremely beautiful. She stands atop a great corpse, seated upon a throne composed of the sun and the moon.

pratyālīḍhāṃ trinetrāṃ ca muṇḍa-mālā-vibhūṣitāṃ \|  
vāsukī-śaṅkhapālau tau karṇa-bhūṣaṇatāṃ gatau \|\| 59 \|\|

She is poised in the attacking stance (pratyālīḍhā), has three eyes, and is adorned with a garland of severed heads. The great serpents Vāsukī and Śaṅkhapāla serve as her earrings.

ananta-hāra-mālā ca hārālaṅkāra-bhūṣitāṃ \|  
viśāla-traya-netrāṃ ca candrārddha-kṛta-śekharām \|\| 60 \|\|

She is adorned with garlands, including a garland made of the serpent Ananta, possesses three wide eyes, and carries the crescent moon upon her crest.

eṣā pūrṇeśvarī devī hāhārāva-ravapriyā \|  
pūrṇeśvarīti vikhyātā sāhaṅkārā rajoyutā \|\| 61 \|\|

This is the Goddess Pūrṇeśvarī, who is deeply fond of the terrifying, roaring sound of hāhā. Renowned as Pūrṇeśvarī, she is imbued with cosmic ego and is charged with dynamic energy.

girau pūrṇālaye pīṭhe lakṣmī-vana-śmaśānake \|  
indreṇārādhitā nityā bahurūpāvatāriṇī \|\| 62 \|\|

Upon the mountain at the sacred seat of Pūrṇālaya (Pūrṇagiri), in the cemetery Lakṣmī-vana, she is eternally worshipped by Indra – the manifestation of the Bahurūpā ṛk (aghorebhyo ‘tha…)

pūrvāmnāyeti vikhyātā pūrva-devarṣipūjitā \|  
īśvarī sarva-vidyānāṃ mahā-bhāgya-bharāvahā \|\| 63 \|\|

Renowned as the personification of the Eastern Transmission, she was worshipped by the gods and seers of old. She is the absolute sovereign of all knowledge (mantra-s), the great bearer and vehicle of fortune.

nānā-rūpa-dharā nityā bahurūpāvatāriṇī \|  
jayā jayantī vijayā ajitā nārasiṃhikā \|\| 64 \|\|  
vyāghreśvarī karālā ca rājeśī bhuveneśvarī \|  
raktadantā vishālākṣī kāmeśī caṇḍamālinī \|\| 65 \|\|  
vidyujjihvā ḍāmareśī nāṭeśī nṛtyanāyikā \|  
nandī caiva mahākāla-bhṛṅgiṇī ca kulālikā \|\| 66 \|\|

She assumes diverse forms, is the nityā, and the manifestation of the bahurūpā ṛk. The following 20 devī-s (her manifold emanations) represent her retinue:  
1. Jayā, 2. Jayantī, 3. Vijayā, 4. Ajitā, 5. Nārasiṃhikā, 6. Vyāghreśvarī, 7. Karālā, 8. Rājeśī, 9. Bhuvaneśvarī, 10. Raktadantā, 11. Viśālākṣī, 12. Kāmeśī, 13. Caṇḍamālinī, 14. Vidyujjihvā, 15. Ḍāmareśī, 16. Nāṭeśī, 17. Nṛtya-nāyikā, 18. Nandī, 19. Mahākāla-bhṛṅgiṇī, 20. Kulālikā

nava-nāṭeśvarī tatra catuḥ-pañca-rasānvitā \|  
bahubhedā devadevī bhairavāṣṭaka-mātṛkāḥ \|\| 67 \|\|

In their midst, she is the Nine-fold Queen of the Dance, imbued with 4+5 rasa-s. Of many varieties, the supreme goddess, with the eight Bhairava-s and mātṛ-s.

nāṭeśvarīti vikhyātā bhairavī cakra-pālikā \|  
kṣetra-vighneśa-baṭuka-yoginī-mātṛkāṣṭakāḥ \|\| 68 \|\|

Celebrated as the Mistress of the Dance, the Bhairavī and the protectress of cakra, she is accompanied by Kṣetrapāla, Vighneśa, Baṭuka, the yoginī-s and the eight mātṛkā-s.

**Dakṣiṇāmnāya**  
While in the old Kulāmnāya system, the Śrī-Kula goddess Kāmeśvarī was the mistress of the Dakṣiṇāmnāya, with her being made the central deity (as Tripurā) of the Ūrdhvāmnāya in the ṣaḍāmnāya system, the Dakṣiṇāmnāyais occupied by a distinct, fierce goddess, Niśeśvarī, who is iconographically equivalent to Cāmuṇḍā rather than the Śrī-Kula goddess. Indeed, she is called Rakta-Carchikā and Rakta-Cāmuṇḍā(ī). She is also repeatedly termed Chaitanya-Bhairavī and Nityā. While associated with the crematory fire, she is interestingly enthroned on the throne of the four Veda-s. Her consort is implicitly identified as Aghora Bhairava. Her retinue features 24 deities, including Nīlā-Sarasvatī, Dakṣiṇa-Kālikā, some mahāvidyā-s and the ancient Kaumāra-mātṛ-s, like Jambhinī, etc. (who are also part of Vārāhī’s retinue). Below is the section from the Parā-Tantra on Niśeśvarī and the Dakṣiṇāmnāya as per the Ṣaḍāmnāya krama.

dakṣiṇāmnāya vakṣyāmi mahāvīryā maholbaṇā \|  
aghora-bhairavoddiṣṭā niśeśī ca niraṅgalā \|\| 1 \|\|

Now I shall describe the Southern Transmission, O one endowed with great virility and boundlessly vast. This stream was revealed by Aghora Bhairava \[has as its\] supreme goddess Niśeśī (The Queen of the Night), who is completely unfettered.

nirālambeśvarī nityā aghoraṃ tac chiraṃ viduḥ \|  
siṃhāsana-gatā nityā niśeśī rakta-carchikā \|\| 2 \|\|

She is the Sovereign Ruler who requires no external support, eternal, and the wise know that her crest/head is Aghora \[Bhairava\]. She is eternally seated upon a supreme lion-throne – she, Niśeśī is Rakta-Carchikā.

chaitanya-bhairavī kecid raktakālīti kecana \|  
sundarī kālikā kecid aghoreśvarī kecana \|\| 3 \|\|

Some call her Chaitanya-Bhairavī, while others call her Raktakālī. Some address her as Sundarī and Kālikā, and still others call her Aghoreśvarī.

dhyāna-rūpaṃ tu vakṣyāmi bhairavyā yāmya-dik-sthitā \|  
nityā niraṃśani-ratā niśeśī nirguṇāśrayā \|\| 4 \|\|

Now I shall state the visualization of this Bhairavī who presides over the quarter of Yama. She is eternal, delighting in undivided reality, the queen of the night, who is the foundation of the quality-less absolute.

kratunārādhitā devi dharmarājāya vai purā \|  
sampradāya-kareṇaiva devī chaitanya-bhairavī \|\| 5 \|\|

In ancient times, she was propitiated by a sacrifice for the sake of Yama, the god of justice. Through that very line of master-to-disciple lineage transmission, the goddess Chaitanya-Bhairavī \[became manifest\].

catur-vedāsanā devī mahā-phetkāra-saṃsthitā \|  
prajvalantī citā-madhye dākṣiṇya-dakṣadā parā \|\| 6 \|\|

The Goddess sits upon a throne composed of the four veda-s, yet she is established amidst the terrifying howls \[of jackals and ghosts\]. Blazing in the center of the funeral pyre, she is the supreme bestower of harmony and skill.”

alāta-cakravad yasyā bhramanti śira-mālikā \|  
snāyur-akṣā kṛśāṅgī ca asthi-carmāvaśeṣitā \|\| 7 \|\|

Her garland of severed heads rotates such that it appears like a continuous ring of fire from a whirling torch. With sinewy eyes, she is emaciated, reduced to \[a structural lattice of\] bare bone and skin.

vyāghra-carmā kaṭi-vṛtā nara-carmottarīyakā \|  
sahasrāditya-saṅkāśā tapta-kāñcana-suprabhā \|\| 8 \|\|

A tiger skin is wrapped around her hips, and a human skin serves as her upper garment. She is resplendent like a thousand suns with the glow of molten gold.

añca-vaktrā cātibhīmā tri-pañca-nayanair yutā \|  
IShad-dhAsya-lalaj-jihvA nimna-nAbhi-kR^ishodarI \|\| 9 \|\|

She possesses five faces (pañca-vaktrā) and is exceedingly terrifying (atibhīmā), endowed with thrice-five (fifteen) cosmic eyes. Her tongue rolls and lolls out amidst a subtle laugh; she has a deeply indented navel and a slender belly.

vikarālā-sanasthātā cakra-vaktrāsana-sthitā \|  
raudrī bhayāpahā ghorā candrārddha-kṛta-ketanā \|\| 10 \|\|

She is seated upon a terrifying throne, stationed on a seat composed of a wheel of visages. She is the śakti of Rudra, the destroyer of fear, the terrible one, who bears the crescent moon as her banner.

barbarārddha-mukta-keśī nāga-rāja-virājitā \|  
kabandha-uṣṇīya-yutā brahmā-śīrṣa-śikhā-maṇiḥ \|\| 11 \|\|

Her curly hair is half-loosened, resplendent with the kings of serpents. She wears a turban woven from headless trunks, and the severed head of Brahman serves as her crest-jewel.

sadyaś chinna-jagad-raktaḥ śiro-mālā-vibhūṣitā \|  
bhujā-ṣoḍaśa-saṃyuktā gaja-kañcuka-kiṅkiṇī \|\| 12 \|\|

She is adorned with a garland of severed heads dripping with the fresh blood of the universe. She is endowed with sixteen arms, wearing a jacket made of an elephant’s hide decorated with tinkling bells.

pāśāṅkuśa-kapālaṃ ca khaṭvāṅgaṃ muṇḍa-ḍiṇḍimaṃ \|  
khaḍga-kheṭa-bāṇa-dhanuṣ-cakra-daṇḍa-kamaṇḍalum \|\| 13 \|\|  
abhayaṃ varadaṃ caiva tarjanī vidhṛtā karaiḥ \|  
muditā siddhi-madhunā śaktibhiḥ parivāritā \|\| 14 \|\|

In her hands, she holds a noose, a goad, a skull, a skull-topped rod, a drum made of a skull, a sword, a shield, an arrow, a bow, a cakra, a staff, and a water-pot. She displays the gestures of fearlessness and boon-bestowing, while her index finger is raised in the threatening gesture. She is joyful by the honey of siddhi-s and is surrounded by her retinue of śakti-s.

hūṃ-hūṃ-hūṃ-kāra-nādena trāsayan devatā-gaṇān \|  
ṛg-yajuḥ-sāmātharvākhyā vedasyopari sthitā \|\| 15 \|\|

She oppresses the host of deities with her intensive huṃkāra as she is stationed atop the fourfold Veda, the Ṛg, Yajus, Sāma, and Atharva.

śavarūpī-mahādeva-hṛdayopari-saṃsthitā \|  
niśeśī ca mahogrā ca dakṣiṇācāra-varjitā \|\| 16 \|\|

She is firmly stationed upon the heart of Mahādeva, in the form of a corpse. She is Niśeśī, the very fierce one, and she has abandoned the right-handed path.

jyeṣṭhānujyeṣṭha-bhedāsti yāmya-siṃhāsanāśritā \|  
tasyāṅga-devatāḥ sarve bahu-bhedā maholbaṇā \|\| 17 \|\|

She who is seated upon the Southern Lion-Throne manifests in hierarchies of seniority and juniority. All her ancillary deities are of several types and are boundlessly vast.

jambhini stambhini naramohini andhini śivā \|  
mṛtyuñjayā carcikā ca siṃhī dakṣiṇa-kālikā \|\| 18 \|\|  
pracaṇḍā ugrachaṇḍā ca ugratārā kapālinī \|  
tvaritā chinna-śīrṣā ca bagalā vāk-prādayinī \|\| 19 \|\|  
nīlā-sarasvatī bhīmā trailokya-ḍāmareśvarī \|  
trailokya-vijayā caṇḍī rakta-cāmuṇḍā bhairavī \|\| 20 \|\|

\[They are:\] 1. Jambhini, 2. Stambhini, 3. Naramohinī, 4. Andhinī, 5. Śivā, 6. Mṛtyuñjayā, 7. Carcikā, 8. Siṃhī, 9. Dakṣiṇa-Kālikā, 10. Pracaṇḍā, 11. Ugrachaṇḍā, 12. Ugratārā, 13. Kapālinī, 14. Tvaritā, 15. Chinna-Śīrṣā, 16. Bagalā, 17. Vāk-Pradāyinī, 18. Nīlā-Sarasvatī, 19. Bhīmā, 20. Trailokya-Ḍāmareśvarī, 21. Trailokya-Vijayā, 22. Caṇḍī, 23. Rakta-Cāmuṇḍā, 24. Bhairavī

dakṣiṇāmnāya-deveśī nānā-bheda-visāriṇī \|  
brahmādyāṣṭa-bhairavāṣṭa-kṣetrapālāṣṭam eva ca \|\| 21 \|\|  
sva-kṣetra-nāthaṃ baṭukaṃ gaṇeśaṃ yoginī-gaṇān \|  
ekā vibhinnarūpā sā samayī pālanāya ca \|\| 22 \|\|

The Supreme Goddess of the Southern Transmission expands in diverse modalities. Though she is absolute Unity, she emanates these diverse forms for the protection of the sacred lineage: the eight Bhairava-s headed by Brahman (the “bhairavized” deities), the eight Kṣetrapāla-s, the octad (of mother goddesses), the specific lord of her own sacred site (sva-kṣetra-nātha), Baṭuka, Gaṇeśa, and the host of yoginī-s.

aghorasya gaṇāḥ sarve nānā-pāriṣadāvṛtā \|  
mahā-caṇḍāṭṭa-saṃrāva pūrayanti digantarāḥ \|\| 23 \|\|  
vetālā bahulā tatra ḍākinī-gaṇa-saṃvṛtāḥ \|  
bāla-līlā-samāpannā niśācara-gaṇāvṛtaḥ \|\| 24 \|\|

All the hosts of Aghora surround her as diverse regiments of attendants. They fill all directions with the thunderous roars of their terrifying laughter. In that space wander innumerable vetāla-s, surrounded by hosts of ḍākinī-s; enveloped by legions of rākṣasa-s, they engage in child-like play.

daṇḍa-pāśodyata-karāḥ sarva-sādhaka-siddhidā \|  
śāka-puṣkara-dvīpeṣu sādhakā-bheda-darśinī \|\| 25 \|\|  
cauhāre gahvare pīṭhe yoni-maṇḍala-mārga-gā \|  
vāmā-cāra-ratā pūjyā hy aṇimādy aṣṭa-siddhidā \|\| 26 \|\|

With hands raised high, wielding a staff and a noose, they bestow siddhi-s on practitioners. Even across the continents of Śāka (Iranic lands) and Puṣkara, she reveals herself to different types of practitioners. Within the grove sanctuary of the sacred seat of Cauhāra (near Shimla, Himachal Pradesh?), she is attained by those practicing that path of the yoni-maṇḍala. Delighting in the transgressive left-handed path, she is to be worshipped as the ultimate bestower of the eight siddhi-s beginning with Aṇimā.”
